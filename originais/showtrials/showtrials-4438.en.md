INTERROGATION RECORD

OF BAKAEV I. P., dated January 12, 1935.

QUESTION: Name further facts concerning the activities of the Moscow center.

ANSWER: I have already described the attempt to establish ties with the Ryutin counterrevolutionary grouping. I now want to dwell on the episodes connected with this that are known to me. Once, when I was at ZINOVIEV's apartment, he was called to the telephone. ZINOVIEV returned looking very upset. When I asked what had happened, ZINOVIEV said that he was being summoned to the Central Control Commission in connection with STEN's case and the document he had received from STEN, and that the document was a kulak counterrevolutionary platform.

At this point EVDOKIMOV arrived, and ZINOVIEV told him the same thing.

EVDOKIMOV and I went with ZINOVIEV to the Central Control Commission. I remained waiting by the Ilyinka Gates for ZINOVIEV to return from the Central Control Commission, while EVDOKIMOV went home, from where he soon returned with SAKHOV, whom we informed of the reasons for ZINOVIEV's summons to the Central Control Commission.

I do not remember which of us suggested that the affair involving the document had been engineered in order to finish ZINOVIEV politically.

We did not wait until ZINOVIEV came out of the Central Control Commission; as later became known, he had suffered a heart attack and had been sent to hospital.

EVDOKIMOV and I visited ZINOVIEV in hospital.

When we learned that he was being sent into exile, one of us members of the center — EVDOKIMOV, SHAROV, myself, BAKAEV, GERTIK — raised the question of whether it would be appropriate to see ZINOVIEV off, to which the majority answered in the negative, on the grounds that this would be taken as a demonstration against the Party.

QUESTION: Describe the meeting of members of the center held in connection with ZINOVIEV's exile.

ANSWER: In connection with the publication of the Central Control Commission's resolution expelling ZINOVIEV from the Party for his ties with the Ryutin counterrevolutionary group, at a meeting at GORSHENIN's attended by a considerable number of the participants in our central group, KOSTINA declared that she would speak at the cell in protest against the resolution; she disagreed with the fact that the resolution deprived ZINOVIEV of the right to reinstatement in the Party after a specified period, whereas UGLANOV, who was expelled from the Party by the same resolution and in the same case, had retained this right.

Those present tried to persuade KOSTINA not to take this step, but she stubbornly stood her ground.

At a second meeting, organized at my apartment specifically on this question, EVDOKIMOV, SHAROV, GERTIK, and I again tried to persuade KOSTINA that her decision was wrong.

Our reasoning was as follows: ZINOVIEV could not be justified; he had made a mistake and entered into ties with a kulak counterrevolutionary group; speeches at the cells would only complicate ZINOVIEV's position, since it would be obvious that they were organized by his associates. Despite our attempts at persuasion, KOSTINA nevertheless spoke out and was expelled from the Party for it.

QUESTION: How did events develop afterward?

ANSWER: I know, I think from GORSHENIN, that his son Stepan traveled from Moscow to visit ZINOVIEV in exile and brought greetings from ZINOVIEV to all of us, and also stated that he would soon return from exile and be reinstated in the Party.

Afterward I no longer met ZINOVIEV, apart from two chance, fleeting encounters in 1933 and 1934.

Of the participants in the center, EVDOKIMOV, GORSHENIN, and KUKLIN maintain ties with him. Meetings of participants in the center usually take place with the following present: EVDOKIMOV, GORSHENIN, SHAROV, GERTIK, and myself, BAKAEV.

They take place chiefly at GORSHENIN's apartment, which during 1930-1934 is our group's main headquarters; meetings do, however, continue at my apartment as well.

The assessments of the domestic and international situation and of the policies of the Party, the government, and the Comintern, known to the investigation from my previous testimony, remain fully in force during this period, imbued with the same hostility toward the leadership of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) and toward STALIN.

February 1933 — we gathered at GORSHENIN's apartment; besides him, there were EVDOKIMOV, myself, KOSTINA, and another two or three comrades whose surnames I do not now remember. We discussed the preceding arrests of GERTIK, M. NATANSON, and others, and the death of Dina GERTIK, Artem GERTIK's wife; we spoke in anti-Party terms about the Party leadership, accusing it of persecuting former oppositionists, which had resulted in GERTIK's arrest; we pointed out that Dina GERTIK was a victim of the existing regime. The conversation developed into the general conclusion that the Party leadership was suppressing opposition cadres.

Incidentally, when I learned of A. GERTIK's arrest, I tried to discover the reasons and help him, but when the first attempt failed I took no further steps.

Spring 1933 — we returned to the Ivanovo events of 1932: incidents that had arisen at a number of enterprises in the city of Ivanovo-Voznesensk in connection with the workers' difficult food situation.

Our group — EVDOKIMOV, myself, BAKAEV, GORSHENIN, KUKLIN, and other comrades — regarded these events as an indication of the extreme dissatisfaction of a considerable stratum of the working masses with the leadership of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), seeing in this fact irrefutable evidence for our general assertion that the Party leadership was inadequate.

Our assessment of the results of fulfillment of the five-year plan, formulated by GORSHENIN and taken by him from a conversation with ZINOVIEV, dates from early 1933. GORSHENIN characterized the successes of the five-year plan as inflated; in a number of branches the plan had not been fulfilled; as for fulfillment of the qualitative indicators, the plan had been completely wrecked; all this was the result of the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks)'s unsatisfactory direction of the national economy.

In 1933 the participant in the center FEDOROV Grigory was removed from his job; he had been directing a trust. We explained his removal as the settling of scores with FEDOROV for his opposition past, discussing this entire incident in anti-Party terms.

The information I received about the exile NATANSON also dates from this period: ANISHEV told me, and I told FEDOROV, about her arrest in exile for keeping the counterrevolutionary diary of the bourgeois nationalist ABDUL-RAKHMAN [1]; my attitude toward NATANSON continued to be one of group solidarity.

Autumn 1933 — a meeting at GORSHENIN's apartment; present were EVDOKIMOV, GORSHENIN, myself, KOSTINA, KOZHURO, and someone else whom I do not now remember. Those present began speaking about the grain and fodder balance: GORSHENIN and EVDOKIMOV sharply criticized the state of the balance, pointed to the exceptionally difficult situation concerning grain resources in the country, said that the situation was beyond repair, and that great excesses had occurred in Ukraine in carrying out grain procurements.

At the end of 1933 the funeral took place of ZINOVIEV's secretary BOGDAN, who had committed suicide in connection with the purge of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks); “he feared being humiliated during the purge, since he was ZINOVIEV's secretary,” his wife explained to me as the reason for his suicide; BOGDAN had remained in contact with ZINOVIEV until recently. At GORSHENIN's invitation I attended BOGDAN's funeral out of the same considerations of group solidarity.

Either in 1933 or in 1934, I happened to meet in the street the former oppositionist ZILBERMAN, who had been a military functionary during the years of the opposition; I invited him to visit me, wanted to establish ties with him, and gave him my address, but he did not come.

In 1934 I maintained ties with ANISHEV, about which I have already testified.

From GORSHENIN I learned of ZINOVIEV's removal from the editorial board of “Bolshevik”; GORSHENIN pointed out that ZINOVIEV supposedly could not understand why he had been removed, and that he was depressed and dejected.

GERTIK, who traveled to Leningrad in 1934, told me of his meetings there.

He met LEVIN Vladimir, RUMYANTSEV V., and others; he reported that these comrades were wholly on our side, that they asked him about the state of affairs in Moscow, requested information, and were distressed at ZINOVIEV's position, that he was not in the leadership.

Correctly recorded from my words. I have read the record.

I. BAKAEV.

INTERROGATED BY:

Assistant Head of the EKO of the GUGB of the NKVD of the USSR — DMITRIEV.

[1] This refers to Abdrakhmanov Yusup Abdrakhmanovich (1901–1938), a statesman and political figure of Kirghizia, Chairman of the Council of People's Commissars of the Kirghiz ASSR, member of the Central Asian Bureau of the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) and of the Central Executive Committee of the USSR. His diaries (published in 1991) contain entries about events in 1928-1932 (as well as his love affair with M. Ya. Natanson, whom he met during M. Natanson's exile in Tashkent). Yu. Abdrakhmanov was expelled from the Party on October 14, 1933, and arrested on April 4, 1937. He was sentenced to execution by shooting by a traveling session of the Military Collegium of the Supreme Court of the USSR on November 6, 1937, and shot on November 7, 1937.
