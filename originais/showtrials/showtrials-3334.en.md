INTERROGATION RECORD

OF ZINOVIEV, GRIGORY EVSEEVICH.

December 19, 1934.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that, after submitting your declaration to the leading Party bodies renouncing your counterrevolutionary views and struggle against the VKP(b), you instructed your like-minded associates to return to the ranks of the VKP(b) for double-dealing purposes.

ANSWER: I deny this.

QUESTION: Do you know V. V. RUMYANTSEV, former secretary of the Leningrad Provincial VLKSM Committee?

ANSWER: I know him.

QUESTION: Do you confirm that the letter to RUMYANTSEV dated June 30, 1928, bearing your signature, which has been presented to you, was written by you?

ANSWER: Yes, I confirm that the letter presented to me was written by me.

QUESTION: The letter presented to you, bearing your signature, was found during a search at V. V. RUMYANTSEV's home. The testimony of RUMYANTSEV, KOTOLYNOV, N. TSARKOV and other defendants establishes that your letter constitutes a directive to formally renounce counterrevolutionary views, return to the Party through deception, and preserve cadres and organizational connections to continue the struggle against the Party. Do you acknowledge this?

ANSWER: This letter cannot be called a directive, since I wrote it to one person who, moreover, as the text shows, had broken with me together with a group of other former like-minded associates immediately after the Fourteenth VKP(b) Congress. As for the substance of the letter itself, I see no instructions to return in its contents.

QUESTION: The investigation has established that, after submitting your declaration to the leading Party bodies renouncing the struggle against the Party, you, setting yourself the task of continuing the struggle against the Party and the Soviet government, embarked on creating an illegal counterrevolutionary organization.

ANSWER: I categorically deny this. I conducted no struggle against the Party or Soviet government from the moment I submitted my declaration after the Fifteenth VKP(b) Congress, and created no counterrevolutionary organization.

QUESTION: Do you acknowledge that, in late summer or early autumn 1928, while in Leningrad, you held an illegal meeting with a group of your active like-minded associates about further counterrevolutionary work against the Party?

ANSWER: I did indeed visit Leningrad in 1928 for LILINA's funeral. I categorically deny not only conducting, but even attending, any illegal meeting before or after the funeral.

QUESTION: RUMYANTSEV's testimony of December 14 establishes that, while in Leningrad for LILINA's funeral, after the funeral you held a conspiratorial meeting at Yakov SHAROV's apartment on the Petrograd Side, attended by V. LEVIN, S. MANDELSHTAM, REM \[1\], P. SAFRONOV, MIKHAILOV, V. RUMYANTSEV, NALIVAIKO and other like-minded associates of yours. At this meeting you gave instructions for creating a counterrevolutionary organization, setting the task of not losing connections between former participants in the so-called Zinovievist opposition and of preserving cadres. This meeting was organizational and, after the destruction of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc, marked the beginning of the counterrevolutionary organization's emergence.

ANSWER: I categorically deny this. I repeat that I attended no meeting in Leningrad in 1928.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that you headed the counterrevolutionary organization created by you, KAMENEV and other like-minded associates of yours.

ANSWER: I categorically deny this.

QUESTION: Do you know of the existence in Leningrad of a Center of the counterrevolutionary organization you created from former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc?

ANSWER: No, I do not.

QUESTION: EVDOKIMOV's testimony establishes that in 1929 KOTOLYNOV and GESSEN told him of the creation of a Center of the counterrevolutionary organization in Leningrad. Did EVDOKIMOV tell you about this?

ANSWER: No, he did not.

QUESTION: The investigation has testimony from several members of the Center of the Leningrad counterrevolutionary organization that this Center was in constant contact with, and directed by, the existing Moscow Center of the counterrevolutionary organization, to which you belonged. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: I know of no Leningrad or Moscow Center of a counterrevolutionary organization.

QUESTION: The testimony of members of the counterrevolutionary organization's Center in Leningrad — RUMYANTSEV, KOTOLYNOV, MYASNIKOV and MANDELSHTAM — establishes that a Center of the counterrevolutionary organization existed in Moscow, consisting of yourself, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV and others. Do you acknowledge this?

ANSWER: I categorically deny this; there was no Center.

QUESTION: Do you know MYASNIKOV, Nikolai Petrovich, and his role in the counterrevolutionary organization in Leningrad?

ANSWER: I apparently know MYASNIKOV, though I remember him poorly. I know nothing of his membership in a counterrevolutionary organization.

QUESTION: Did you have a meeting with MYASNIKOV in Moscow in 1929, and what was its character?

ANSWER: I allow that I may have met MYASNIKOV, but I do not remember it.

QUESTION: The testimony of MYASNIKOV, a member of the Center of the Leningrad counterrevolutionary organization, establishes that in 1929 he met you at your apartment in Moscow on the instructions of V. LEVIN, also a member of the Center of the counterrevolutionary organization in Leningrad. At this meeting MYASNIKOV informed you in detail about the mood in the Party organization and among Leningrad workers, about the counterrevolutionary organization's cadres, and so forth. You inquired about relations in the leading circle of the Leningrad Regional Committee. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: I allow that I had such a meeting with MYASNIKOV, and that he may have told me about the mood in the Party organization and among workers, but this took the ordinary form permissible when one Party member meets another. I also allow that I may have inquired about relations in the leading group of Party workers in Leningrad. There could have been no talk of any counterrevolutionary organization.

QUESTION: Do you know V. LEVIN and his role in the counterrevolutionary organization in Leningrad?

ANSWER: I know Levin well; I know nothing of his membership in a counterrevolutionary organization.

QUESTION: Did you meet LEVIN in Moscow in 1932, and what was the character of this meeting?

ANSWER: In the summer of 1931 or 1932, LEVIN came with GERTIK to my dacha in Ilyinskoye on his own initiative. The conversation was entirely ordinary; I also asked him about the situation in Leningrad, construction and so on.

QUESTION: V. LEVIN's testimony establishes that during his meeting with you, you told him about a visit by STEN, explained to LEVIN the positions of the platform STEN had brought to you, and spoke of the possible unification of all your supporters around you, referring in this connection to such a step by SAFAROV. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: As far as I remember I did not speak to him about the platform, but I allow that, if I already knew about it at the time, I might have spoken to him about it; I might also have spoken of SAFAROV in connection with a meeting I had with him after several years of estrangement, but not in the context to which LEVIN testifies.

QUESTION: The investigation knows that, after renewing connections with SAFAROV in 1932, you agreed with him to submit a collective letter to the VKP(b) Central Committee about the country's allegedly difficult economic situation and the absence of information on this to the Party from the VKP(b) Central Committee.

ANSWER: I had no conversations about composing a letter to the Central Committee, either with SAFAROV or with anyone else.

QUESTION: EVDOKIMOV's testimony establishes the fact of your negotiations with SAFAROV about composing the above-mentioned letter. Do you continue to deny this fact?

ANSWER: I deny it.

QUESTION: What do you know about negotiations with SHATSKIN, who represented the Center of a counterrevolutionary organization and raised the question of exchanging information between his organization and the organization in which you, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, GERTIK and others participated?

ANSWER: As I have already testified, I did not belong to a counterrevolutionary organization and know nothing of one. I recall that, during my illness before my exile to Kustanai \[2\], EVDOKIMOV told me of SHATSKIN's proposals to establish whether there was agreement in assessing questions of current policy and to exchange information. EVDOKIMOV told me then that he had told SHATSKIN's proposal to go to hell.

QUESTION: EVDOKIMOV testifies that SHATSKIN asked to be informed of your decision to send a collective letter to the Central Committee.

ANSWER: EVDOKIMOV told me that there were people who wanted to address a letter to the Central Committee requesting reconsideration of the decision to exile me and KAMENEV. SHATSKIN's name may have been mentioned in this connection. I advised against sending a letter to the Central Committee.

QUESTION: What do you know about GESSEN's connections with SHATSKIN and LOMINADZE, and SHAROV's and FEDOROV's connections with ROVINSKY or UGLANOV?

ANSWER: I know that there is an old personal friendship between GESSEN, SHATSKIN and LOMINADZE. I know nothing of connections between them on a counterrevolutionary, anti-Party basis. SHAROV is an old friend of UGLANOV from Leningrad. I know nothing of political anti-Party connections between them, that is, SHAROV and UGLANOV. I know nothing about connections with ROVINSKY.

QUESTION: What can you testify about meetings held at your apartment with EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, GERTIK, SHAROV, KUKLIN, FEDOROV and others, and about counterrevolutionary anti-Party conversations that took place at such meetings?

ANSWER: In 1928 the people listed in the question periodically visited me in Kaluga at different times. After my return to Moscow and in early 1929 these visits continued, but there were no meetings.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that meetings of the above-mentioned people were periodically held under the guise of family evenings, anniversary celebrations and so forth, at which Party measures were criticized in a counterrevolutionary anti-Party spirit, accompanied by malicious counterrevolutionary attacks on the leaders of the VKP(b). What do you know about this question?

ANSWER: Gatherings of the character indicated in the question took place through the end of 1928 and in early 1929; after that period all this ceased.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that you and people connected with you, while officially speaking for the Party, held hostile attitudes toward the Party line and Party leadership within your own circle. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: No, I do not. I and a substantial majority of former participants in the so-called Zinovievist opposition whom I met resented not being given responsible work.

QUESTION: Do you not allow that such dissatisfaction might have developed into a hostile attitude toward the Party leadership and Soviet government and led to a counterrevolutionary path of struggle against the Party?

ANSWER: In me and those close to me this aroused a desire to earn the Party's trust and obtain responsible work. For the last year or year and a half, work at the journal “Bolshevik” more or less satisfied me. Of those people I was able to observe, I cannot conclude that their dissatisfaction developed into hostility toward the Party.

QUESTION: Was the question raised in your circle of bringing RYKOV, TOMSKY, BUKHARIN, ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV into the Central Committee Politburo, in connection with the general question raised by you and your like-minded associates of replacing the leadership of the VKP(b) Central Committee?

ANSWER: During 1929 this idea was vigorously discussed, and many then considered it very popular. Since then, however, the question was no longer raised in the circles with which I came into contact.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that over a number of years and until recently you systematically expressed sharply hostile attitudes toward the leaders of the Party and Soviet government and educated your like-minded associates in the same spirit, particularly members of the Leningrad counterrevolutionary organization composed of former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc.

ANSWER: After 1929 I did not and could not permit myself hostility toward the leaders of the Party and Soviet government. Before that time, in the heat of struggle, I did indeed display the most disgraceful attitude toward the leaders of the Party and Soviet government.

QUESTION: According to the investigation's information, the sharply hostile attitude toward the leaders of the Party and Soviet government which you instilled in the consciousness of your like-minded associates generated terrorist sentiments among members of the counterrevolutionary organization in Leningrad headed by you, leading to the murder, organized by members of this organization, of one of the leaders of the Party and Soviet government, comrade KIROV.

Do you acknowledge responsibility for the fact that your hostile attacks on the leaders of the Party and Soviet government led to comrade KIROV's murder by members of the counterrevolutionary organization you headed?

ANSWER: No, under no circumstances can I consider myself responsible for the counterrevolutionary degenerates who resorted to a fascist crime; but I do not deny that my struggle and that of my former like-minded associates against the Party leadership until 1929, which assumed its sharpest forms in Leningrad and was accompanied by unbridled persecution and discrediting of the VKP(b) leaders, especially comrade STALIN, may have influenced degenerate elements among my former supporters who descended to fascist methods of struggle — terror.

G. ZINOVIEV.

I consider it necessary to add the following:

A number of people about whom I was questioned (RUMYANTSEV, KOTOLYNOV, MANDELSHTAM and others) broke with the group of the former Leningrad opposition already after the Fourteenth Congress and then formed a separate group of the “leaderless” or “Leftists”. —

G. ZINOVIEV.

Interrogated by:

DEPUTY PEOPLE'S COMMISSAR OF INTERNAL AFFAIRS OF THE USSR — AGRANOV.

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD — LYUSHKOV.

SUPPLEMENT TO THE INTERROGATION RECORD

of December 19, 1934,

of ZINOVIEV, GRIGORY EVSEEVICH.

QUESTION: The investigation has established that over a number of years and until recently members of the Moscow Center of the counterrevolutionary organization you headed — GERTIK, KUKLIN, EVDOKIMOV and others — periodically traveled to maintain contact with the Leningrad Center of the counterrevolutionary organization.

State what instructions and assignments concerning the counterrevolutionary organization's affairs you gave, and to whom; with whom exactly they made contact in Leningrad; and what exactly they reported to you about the counterrevolutionary organization's cadres and activities upon returning to Moscow.

ANSWER: I know nothing about a counterrevolutionary organization or about Centers of such an organization, and therefore could give no one any assignments connected with the work of a counterrevolutionary organization. I know that EVDOKIMOV went to Leningrad on dairy factory business; I have not seen GERTIK for more than two years and do not know whether he went or not; I do not know whether KUKLIN went or not; I think he did not. —

G. ZINOVIEV.

June 30, 1928.

Comrade RUMYANTSEV!

I decided to take advantage of the occasion (Z. I.'s journey \[3\]) to write you a few lines.

I was told that you are still delaying submitting a declaration to the Central Control Commission. Truly, you are making a great mistake. What further proof is needed that without this one is left entirely without a path?

“Bargaining” with the Party now over the wording of the declaration is inadmissible. What the congress decided must be accepted. The declaration must be written in such a way that it is recognized as satisfactory.

New questions and new difficulties have arisen for the Party. Do not waste time needlessly. I know that you are a true Bolshevik. Such people now more than ever must be in the Party.

Every day's delay now is a lost day. Do you really not see this?

If you are in Moscow I shall be very glad to see you.

I venture to give you this advice although we parted ways in December 1927. I hope our former joint work and friendship give me the right to do so.

It is time for you to leave this pathlessness behind, enter the Party and fight there for Leninism.

Sincere greetings.

Yours, G. ZINOVIEV

Certified correct: Kruskop
