INTERROGATION RECORD

OF SOKOLOV Georgy Vasilyevich, dated December 25, 1934.

(Supplementary).

QUESTION: Tell us about your father's social and political background.

ANSWER: My father, Vasily Nikodimovich SOKOLOV (he is about sixty), worked before the revolution at various factories in Leningrad (the Obukhov Works, the former Aivaz factory, and others). From what my mother told me, I know that my father was a Menshevik whose membership dated back to before the revolution. According to the same information, and also my father's own accounts, he was a member of the Petrograd Soviet of 1905. My mother also told me that before the revolution my father had been exiled to the town of Kungur. I remember that after the February Revolution my father was some kind of commissar in the Lesnovsky district; unless I am mistaken, he was in charge of sanitary affairs and remained a Menshevik. He then worked at the former Aivaz factory again. He has been a pensioner for several years. He works as an instructor at some factory apprenticeship school. He separated from my mother many years ago, and I see him very rarely. Ivan Ivanovich DMITRIEV, an elderly man who works as a foreman at the “Bolshevik” factory and was also a member of the Soviet of 1905, knows my father well. My father now lives in Lesnoy, on Kozlovskaya Street.

Recorded correctly from my words; I have read it.

SOKOLOV.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF SECTION I OF THE SPO OF THE UGB LULOV.

Certified true: Churbanova
