INTERROGATION RECORD

Conducted on December 20, 1934, by RUTKOVSKY

KAMENEV, LEV BORISOVICH (supplementary)

QUESTION: The investigation has established that after submitting a statement to the leading Party bodies renouncing the struggle against the Party, you, setting yourself the task of continuing the struggle against the Party and the Soviet government, embarked on the creation of a counterrevolutionary organization. What testimony can you give on this question?

ANSWER: I categorically deny this.

QUESTION: The testimony of RUMYANTSEV, formerly secretary of the Leningrad Regional Komsomol Committee, has established that you edited the text of the duplicitous statement to the leading Party bodies. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: I do not remember this incident.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that until recently you headed the organization created by you, ZINOVIEV and others sharing your views. What testimony can you give on this question?

ANSWER: I gave testimony on this question on December 17, 1934.

QUESTION: Were you in Leningrad in late 1928 or early 1929, and where did you stay?

ANSWER: After returning from Kaluga in the autumn of 1928 I obtained work at the NTU and visited Leningrad on the institution's business. I stayed at some hotel; I do not now remember exactly which one.

QUESTION: During your visit to Leningrad did you meet a group of your former associates, and what did you discuss with them?

ANSWER: I admit the possibility that one of the former Zinovievists may have called on me at that time. If conversations took place, I could have told them only one thing: one must earn the Party's trust through work.

QUESTION: The testimony of V. V. RUMYANTSEV has established that Aleksandr MOROZOV, Foma NALIVAIKO, MANDELSHTAM and RUMYANTSEV gathered at your room in the Europe Hotel and accused you and ZINOVIEV of renouncing your views. You replied that it was necessary to “grit our teeth, submit and maintain chain connections,” adding that you loved the Party leadership no more than they, RUMYANTSEV and your other interlocutors. Do you confirm this fact?

ANSWER: I do not admit the possibility of such a conversation about establishing chain connections and a hostile attitude toward the Party leadership. I could only have urged them to follow the example of the majority of the former opposition: disarm and work honestly within the Party.

QUESTION: The testimony of KUKLIN and GORSHENIN has established that in 1929 you and ZINOVIEV inculcated counterrevolutionary anti-Party convictions among members of your organization, leading one of the seminars you had created for these purposes. What testimony can you give on this question?

ANSWER: After returning to the Party I did not inculcate counterrevolutionary anti-Party convictions in anyone. As for the seminars, I remember that after my return from Kaluga a group of comrades approached me with a request to study the economic policy of Soviet power with them. I initially agreed, but after the first or second session I discontinued them, having lost all interest in the matter.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that the seminars were dissolved because of the large gatherings, of 30–40 people, and fear that the anti-Party activities would be exposed. Do you confirm this fact?

ANSWER: No, I do not. Six or seven people attended the seminars and met once or twice. There was no criticism of the economic policy of Soviet power; we set ourselves the task of understanding current questions of economic policy.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that you, ZINOVIEV and persons connected with you, while officially speaking for the Party line, were hostile toward Party policy and the Party leadership within your own circle. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: No, I do not. During this period I changed my negative attitude toward the line pursued by the Party leadership to full recognition of its correctness.

QUESTION: The testimony of NAUMOV, GESSEN and others has established that you and your organization periodically held meetings of the organization's members disguised as family evenings, anniversary celebrations and the like, at which Party measures were criticized in a counterrevolutionary anti-Party spirit, accompanied by hostile counterrevolutionary attacks on the leaders of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks). What testimony can you give on this question?

ANSWER: I do not deny that we held meetings, large and small, during our struggle against the Party and for a short time afterward, in 1928. After that I know nothing of meetings of this kind.

QUESTION: The testimony of EVDOKIMOV, NAUMOV and others has established that, setting yourselves the task of replacing the Party leadership, you and your associates circulated various rumors about supposed disagreements among members of the Central Committee Politburo, about dissatisfaction among military personnel with the Party leaders, and so forth. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: This occurred only during the period of acute struggle against the Party. Then these rumors circulated among us in connection with the Rightists' struggle against the Party Central Committee in 1928. After that I passed no such information to anyone and heard none from anyone. I never heard anything about supposed dissatisfaction among military personnel with the Party leadership.

QUESTION: GESSEN's testimony has established that you discussed action against the Party leadership with him in 1932. You told GESSEN: “As long as the top leadership is united we must not move. We made a mistake once and must not make a mistake a second time.” Do you confirm the date and content of this conversation?

ANSWER: I do not remember this conversation with GESSEN. In any event, my answer could only have meant that we were defeated in the struggle and that I did not intend to begin a struggle a second time or recommend it to anyone. I consider the very formula about breaking unity within the Party leadership absurd.

QUESTION: Was the question raised in your circle of bringing RYKOV, BUKHARIN, TOMSKY, KAMENEV and ZINOVIEV into the Central Committee Politburo, in connection with you and your associates raising the question of replacing the leadership of the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks)?

ANSWER: I consider conversations on such subjects absolute stupidity.

QUESTION: EVDOKIMOV's testimony has established that after 1929 you and your associates returned to this question several times. In particular, was this question raised in a conversation with SAFAROV in 1932?

ANSWER: Personally, after 1932 I had no such conversations with anyone, and I have not seen SAFAROV since 1927.

QUESTION: What testimony can you give about negotiations by you and members of your organization with SHATSKIN, STEN, LOMINADZE and other Rightist-Leftists concerning joint counterrevolutionary activities?

ANSWER: I met STEN at the dacha in 1932, not knowing that he was a member of a counterrevolutionary organization. At my request STEN wrote articles about GOETHE, and our conversations were on literary subjects; I conducted no negotiations with him about joint actions. In the summer of that same year, 1932, I met LOMINADZE and SHATSKIN entirely by chance in Gagra. Once, when alone with me, SHATSKIN and LOMINADZE proposed exchanging political information, and LOMINADZE replied that the organization was himself and SHATSKIN. I told them that I considered any contacts between former anti-Party groups harmful and rejected their proposal. I have not met them since.

QUESTION: NAUMOV's testimony has established that around 1930 you constantly spoke with your associates about replacing the government and about a new composition of the government that you had planned. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: I deny this malicious slander. I have not seen NAUMOV for at least five years. I neither spoke to anyone nor thought about replacing the government, since in 1930 and earlier I considered such conversations criminal and stupid.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that, displaying hostility toward the Party leadership and the Soviet government, you continued until recently to educate your associates in that same spirit, in particular members of the Leningrad counterrevolutionary organization composed of participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc. Do you plead guilty to this?

ANSWER: I conducted propaganda against the political line of the Party leadership during the anti-Party struggle, without giving that struggle any personal character. From the moment my disagreements with the leadership's political line ceased, I fully supported that leadership, since I became deeply convinced that only that leadership ensured and ensures correct leadership of the Party and the country.

I know nothing of the existence in Leningrad of the organization named by the investigation. Had I known or suspected the existence of such an organization, I would have made every effort to expose and destroy it.

QUESTION: According to the investigation's information, the sharply hostile attitude toward the Party leadership and the Soviet government that you inculcated in the minds of your associates engendered terrorist sentiments among the members of the counterrevolutionary organization you headed in Leningrad. These sentiments led to the murder of Comrade KIROV organized by members of your organization. Do you acknowledge responsibility for the fact that your hostile attacks on the leaders of the Party and the Soviet government led to the murder of Comrade KIROV by members of the counterrevolutionary organization you headed?

ANSWER: I cannot plead guilty to the most abominable crime committed by villains with whom I had and could have had no connection. But I acknowledge all the responsibility that rests on me, first, for participating in the struggle against the Party and, second, because, having returned to the Party and overcome my disagreements with it, I did not fight actively and energetically enough against the degeneration that resulted from that struggle and on whose soil a gang of bandits drawn from the dregs of the former anti-Party organization could arise and commit its crime.

The record was read to me. Recorded correctly from my words.

L. KAMENEV.

INTERROGATED BY: RUTKOVSKY.
