INTERROGATION RECORD

Of Mikhail Semenovich REM — December 24, 1934.

In addition to my testimony of 22/XII, I recall that at the meeting ZINOVIEV held at SHAROV's apartment (autumn 1928), ZINOVIEV also spoke about the decisions of the Plenum of the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks). He, ZINOVIEV, expressed satisfaction at the disagreements with the Rightists; he believed then that the difficulties caused by disagreements in the leadership could be used to bring him, ZINOVIEV, into a leading position in the party.

QUESTION: Describe your meetings with SHAROV and the content of your conversations.

ANSWER: I met SHAROV in various years; when visiting Moscow, I would call at his apartment on Tikhvinskaya Street. At every meeting, SHAROV expressed sharp dissatisfaction and hostility toward the party leaders.

1929. Following the Central Committee's appeal on self-criticism, a number of abuses were exposed in Leningrad, and Control Commission officials DESOV, REKSTIN and others were consequently removed from their jobs. SHAROV spoke negatively about this, claiming that Comrades STALIN and KIROV had removed these officials supposedly for personal reasons.

1932. While on a business trip to the Railway Workers' Central Committee, I visited SHAROV. His mood was somewhat better, but on this occasion too he made critical remarks about conditions in the country, bread supplies and shortcomings in public catering. He said that in Kalinin there had been a work stoppage among workers because of the difficult conditions. SHAROV then asked whether I saw V. LEVIN; he scolded me for not attending SHURYGIN's funeral.

In March 1934, I was on a business trip, met SHAROV near the former theater and then went with him to his apartment. SHAROV then expressed dissatisfaction that he, an experienced textile worker, was being employed improperly and had been given clerical work. SHAROV expressed satisfaction at ZINOVIEV's readmission to the party, but at the same time said that he would not be given important work: “They will probably send him to ‘Vechernyaya Moskva’ or to compile the ‘Agitator's Notebook.’” SHAROV saw Comrade STALIN as the reason ZINOVIEV was being denied leading work in the party, and spoke sharply and negatively about Comrade STALIN in this connection, giving me a slanderous explanation of this policy of the party leadership.

SHAROV also expressed negative sentiments toward Comrade L. M. KAGANOVICH, particularly concerning his selection of his closest assistants; SHAROV said that Moscow was being given too much money at the expense of the interests of the state as a whole, blaming Comrade KAGANOVICH for this.

Possibly some of these conversations — about ZINOVIEV's readmission to the party and SHAROV's personal grievances concerning his allegedly improper employment — belong to the previous conversation, in late 1932 or early 1933.

QUESTION: Do you acknowledge the counterrevolutionary character of all these conversations of SHAROV's?

ANSWER: Yes, I acknowledge it. All these conversations were aimed at discrediting the party line and the party leadership, but at the time they seemed to me simply the grumbling of an “aggrieved” man.

QUESTION: What objective was SHAROV pursuing when, in conversations with you, he depicted the country's situation in a counterrevolutionary spirit and expressed hostility toward the leadership of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks)?

ANSWER: SHAROV knew me as an active participant in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc, and through these conversations he apparently wanted to draw me once again into active struggle against the party.

QUESTION: Consequently, do you acknowledge that these conversations of SHAROV's with you are one method of organized subversive work within the party?

ANSWER: Yes, I acknowledge it.

REM

Interrogated by: HEAD OF THE SIXTH SECTION OF THE OO OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB — KORKIN

Certified true. — Potrokhova
