STENOGRAPHIC TRANSCRIPT.

INTERROGATION RECORD

OF NIKOLAEV, Leonid Vasilyevich

16 December 1934. —

Question: When did you become acquainted with YUSKIN Ignaty?

Answer: I have known YUSKIN since 1927; I met him at the apartment of my sister Ekaterina Vasilyevna, who lives in the same building as I do. YUSKIN's wife is my sister's childhood friend. At that time our meetings were fairly frequent.

In 1928, during a meeting at my sister's apartment when we were alone, YUSKIN began speaking to me about the opposition; at the end of the conversation he tried to induce me to undertake opposition work, but I did not agree. Some time later he left Leningrad for Moscow, and I only began meeting him again in 1931, when he returned. In conversations he displayed dissatisfaction with the party leaders, condemned conditions in the country and said that under Soviet conditions it was difficult for an able person to advance.

Some time later YUSKIN went to study and became a student at the Industrial Academy.

Our relations became still more friendly. YUSKIN quite quickly discerned my political sentiments.

During 1934 we had private meetings — in the evenings and on days off — at which we discussed various political subjects. In October 1934 YUSKIN told me of his membership in the Zinovievist organization, into which he had been recruited by RUMYANTSEV. Around the same time I had a conversation with him about preparations for the terrorist act against KIROV; he then indicated that a terrorist act against STALIN must also be organized.

I subsequently told KOTOLYNOV about my conversation with YUSKIN. YUSKIN knew from me that I too belonged to the Zinovievist organization.

I consulted YUSKIN about how suitable the Smolny building might be as a place for carrying out the terrorist act. He said that this location would ensure the greatest success, since KIROV spent most of his time there; a suitable moment might be his return from a meeting; on the other hand, committing the act inside Smolny also had the advantage that it could be portrayed as an attempt by a former Smolny employee, devoid of political content.

From my conversation with YUSKIN I learned about ERMOLAEVA what I testified in the interrogation record of 14 December of this year; he told me that she had terrorist sentiments and that it would be expedient to use her in preparing the act against STALIN in Moscow, since she had many acquaintances there.

Finally, YUSKIN agreed to participate in preparing the terrorist act against STALIN and expressed a desire to travel with me to Moscow to ascertain the possibilities for carrying it out.

In his statements on political subjects YUSKIN showed himself a supporter of ZINOVIEV, preferring him to TROTSKY.

Correctly taken down in shorthand from my words. I have read the record.

NIKOLAEV.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE EKO, GUGB, NKVD OF THE USSR — MIRONOV

ASSISTANT TO THE HEAD OF THE EKO, GUGB, NKVD OF THE USSR — DMITRIEV.
