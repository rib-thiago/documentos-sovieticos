INTERROGATION RECORD

On January 1, 1935, I, assistant head of the EKO of the GUGB of the NKVD of the USSR—DMITRIYEV, interrogated citizen MINAYEV S.P. as an accused person.

MINAYEV Semyon Pavlovich, born in 1900, originally from the village of Sekirino, Zaryansk district, former Ryazan prov\<ince\>; father a poor peasant, mother a worker; Russian, USSR citizen. Res\<ides\> in Moscow, Kolpachny Lane, building 6, apartment 93; second address: town of Kalyazino, Mosc\<ow\> reg\<ion\>, Elektrozavod rest home. Director of the Elektrozavod rest home in Kalyazino. Married; wife—Zinaida Nikitichna, 34 years old, librarian at the Elektrozavod factory apprenticeship school; daughter—Raisa, 13 years old, a pupil. Without property. Elementary education; member of the VKP(b) since 1918; belonged to the VLKSM from 1917 to 1926. Held various elective offices, mainly in the VLKSM; was a member of the Bureau of the CC of the VLKSM in 1925; in 1926, was a member of the district committee and uezd committee of the VKP(b). In 1919, was arrested on a charge of authorizing the illegal removal of flour. Middle-ranking reserve command and political personnel.

QUESTION: The investigation has information establishing that, although you submitted a statement of withdrawal from the Zinovievist opposition in 1927, you actually preserved old political connections with oppositionists and until recently were a member of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: Yes, I confirm that, despite the formal renunciation of participation in the opposition that I made in 1927 while working in Oryol, I actually preserved old political connections with Zinovievists and until recently was a member of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization.

I joined the opposition in 1925. My first vacillations in political views occurred while I was studying at the political school attached to the Northwestern Bureau of the VKP(b); SAFAROV directed the school, which was organized on ZINOVIEV's instructions. In early 1925, I took part in the work of the plenum of the CC of the VLKSM held in Moscow; at that time, I was a member of the CC of the VLKSM and belonged to the Leningrad delegation of Central Committee members. At ZINOVIEV's instigation, this plenum adopted a decision to remove TROTSKY from the Politburo; the decision was a direct demonstration against the CC of the VKP(b). There followed a whole series of opposition actions in which I took an active part, namely: preparation for the Seventh Komsomol Congress, where I spoke in the debate; at the congress, we made a great fuss over including ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV in the congress presidium; together with TARASOV, I fraudulently obtained from the Secret Department of the CC of the VLKSM the resolution adopted by the congress's council of representatives on the report of the CC of the VLKSM; we needed this document to prepare the opposition's interventions at the congress during discussion of the resolution on the Komsomol CC's report. Some time later, the CC of the VKP(b) sent us to work in Khabarovsk, where I met oppositionists TUNTAL and KUZNETSOV; in Vladivostok I met RUMYANTSEV and MUSHTAKOV; the meetings were political in nature. Having contracted tuberculosis, I left the Far Eastern Territory for treatment in Crimea and returned from there to Moscow.

In September 1927, I left for Rostov-on-Don to take up work. Aleksandr BATASHOV and BARDIN assigned me to take opposition literature to Rostov and Baku: to Rostov for SARKIS, who was conducting opposition work there, and to Baku on SARKIS's instructions; I carried out the assignment. I soon returned to Moscow because the North Caucasus Territorial Committee of the VKP(b), having learned that I was an oppositionist, gave me no work. The CC of the VKP(b) sent me to work in Oryol, where I stayed for over two years.

In Oryol, I submitted to the City Party Committee a statement of withdrawal from the opposition; the statement was published.

Besides myself, oppositionists MIRONOV and his wife submitted statements of withdrawal. Before submitting the statement, I consulted with MIRONOV: we were deciding what to do. The fact was that we were cut off from the opposition centers and had to decide for ourselves how to respond to the decision of the Fifteenth Congress of the VKP(b) on the incompatibility of remaining in the party and participating in the opposition. We considered it tactically expedient to announce our withdrawal while retaining our old oppositional views, since we understood that there was no other way out in those circumstances.

In early 1930, at the summons of the People's Commissariat of Communications, I moved to Moscow.

I lived in Moscow throughout 1930, 1931, 1932 and 1933. In the first days of April 1934, I was appointed director of the Elektrozavod rest home in Kalyazino, and throughout the subsequent period I systematically visited Moscow.

Beginning in 1930, I had my closest connections with Zinovievists through Andrei TOLMAZOV.

In 1930, we discussed questions of party policy in the countryside together; we were unanimous in our assessment that the party had adopted an incorrect pace of collectivization, that is, we repeated the opposition's familiar arguments on this issue. From that same year, I knew of RUMYANTSEV's double-dealing: in words he declared loyalty to the party line, but in practice he conducted underground work against the party.

In 1932, I was in Leningrad and attended a party organized by Leningrad Zinovievists on the occasion of the fifteenth anniversary of the VLKSM. The party was organized directly by TSEITLIN Yakov; those present were LUKIN, ALEKSANDROV, TSEITLIN, myself, SUROV and other\<s\>; I remember anti-party speeches by other participants, who said that the party was pushing them out of work; sharp dissatisfaction with the existing leadership of the VKP(b) was expressed.

In 1933, KHANIK came to my apartment in Moscow; I was in hospital at the time; he told my wife that the group of former oppositionists existing in Leningrad authorized me, as an old Komsomol member, to approach the CC with a complaint that they were being given no work. My wife relayed this conversation to me; I understood the senselessness and, above all, the danger of the proposed undertaking—to appear before the CC on behalf of a group, an organization—and, of course, did not agree to it.

Throughout this time, I maintained connections with the following members of the Zinovievist organization: BULAKH, Ivan NAUMOV, ROZOVSKY, SHVARTS, TARASOV, DRYAZGOV, BATASHOV and other\<s\>.

I acknowledge my guilt in double-dealing, in having sworn loyalty to the party in words while in practice doing its opponents' dirty work, and in bearing responsibility for the failure to expose the counterrevolutionary organization in good time, which would have prevented its further development and the murder of Comrade Kirov.

The record has been read to me. Recorded correctly from my words—

MINAYEV.

INTERROGATED BY:

ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE EKO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR (DMITRIYEV)

Certified correct: V. Stoiko
