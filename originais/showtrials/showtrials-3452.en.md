INTERROGATION RECORD

Of BRAVO Boris Lvovich.

December 26, 1934.

QUESTION: The investigation knows that in the testimony you have given so far you are concealing highly significant facts about the anti-Party counterrevolutionary activity of the group of former Zinovievists headed by EVDOKIMOV and your personal role in this group. What can you testify on this matter?

ANSWER: I acknowledge that until now I concealed from the investigation the true political character of the group of former Zinovievists headed by EVDOKIMOV, of which I myself was also a member. My personal relations with EVDOKIMOV, KUKLIN, and GERTIK prompted me to conceal from the investigation facts of their anti-Party activity known to me. I have now realized the need to tell the investigation the whole truth, concealing nothing.

First of all, on the political assessment of our group: besides personal friendship and our joint struggle against the Party in the past, we were bound by common dissatisfaction with the present Party leadership and furious malice against STALIN as the Party's leader.

When meeting one another, all of us used every opportunity, every rumor, even unverified and sometimes improbable, to discredit the Party leadership, sow and strengthen distrust, especially toward STALIN.

In essence, we were disintegrating the Party, undermining its monolithic character and cohesion from within.

I recall the following fact: during my stay at ZINOVIEV's dacha in Ilyinskoye in August 1932, where EVDOKIMOV, KUKLIN, KAMENEV, and FEDOROV were present besides myself, someone present related that Yemelyan YAROSLAVSKY had recently published some article in “Pravda” concerning one of the “stoppages” in Ivanovo-Voznesensk and that STALIN was removing him from the editorial staff of “Pravda” for that article.

Having heard this report, ZINOVIEV declared: “STALIN does not need people who worked together with LENIN, and he will gradually remove such people.”

All of us silently agreed with this assessment by ZINOVIEV.

When the Comintern appeal concerning the Shanghai events was published in 1932, EVDOKIMOV declared in conversation with me: “Would it have been conceivable under LENIN to address the working people of China over the heads of the USSR's working masses? Only STALIN can think that he will succeed in outwitting the international bourgeoisie and concealing the Comintern's connections with the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) and the Soviet Union's working class.”

On another occasion, after publication of the Seventeenth Party Conference resolutions on the transition to a classless society in the second five-year period, EVDOKIMOV reproached the Party leadership for presenting this resolution, historical and pivotal in significance for the further development of socialism in our country, to the Party and all working people as a surprise, without any prior preparation, preventing the masses from gaining a deep understanding of its substance and significance.

Hence the conclusion about the Party leadership as a bureaucratic machine stamping its resolutions through the Party apparatus and presenting them ready-made to the masses, about general “repression” and the absence of internal Party democracy. KUKLIN and GERTIK shared the same sentiments. Assessing the atmosphere in which the Seventeenth Party Congress took place, EVDOKIMOV spoke of STALIN's person in highly malicious and unflattering terms.

QUESTION: What do you know about the connections of your Moscow group with the remnants of the former Zinovievist opposition in Leningrad?

ANSWER: I know that individual persons belonging to EVDOKIMOV's group maintained contact with remnants of the former Zinovievist opposition in Leningrad. For example, LEVIN Vladimir and BASHKIROV came to Moscow from time to time and met with EVDOKIMOV, GERTIK, and myself.

GERTIK frequently traveled from Moscow to Leningrad, and I, BRAVO, went several times. I have already testified at previous interrogations about my meetings in Leningrad. In addition, I know from EVDOKIMOV's words of several trips he made to Leningrad during 1933–1934. I remember from his accounts that in Leningrad he met with BOGRACHEV and some employee of regional supply whose surname I do not know.

QUESTION: What further facts can you report about your anti-Party connection with ZINOVIEV?

ANSWER: In late summer 1932, the “ZAGOTZERNO” trust sent Inspector Obelov to Ukraine to examine fulfillment of the sowing plans. On his return, I approached him, OBELOV, asking him to provide a report for the journal I edited, “On the Agricultural Procurement Front.” Instead of a report for publication, OBELOV gave me a copy of the memorandum he had drawn up, depicting an extremely difficult situation in the countryside, a lack of food among the population forcing people to eat substitutes and consume seed reserves (the matter concerned one of the districts of Odessa region).

When I later visited ZINOVIEV at Ilyinskoye, I informed him about the copy of OBELOV's memorandum I had received concerning the difficult food situation in Ukraine. ZINOVIEV expressed a desire to have a copy of this memorandum and asked me to pass it to him. Some time later, I met EVDOKIMOV's wife in Moscow, who was temporarily living at ZINOVIEV's dacha and reminded me that ZINOVIEV was waiting for the promised memorandum. I then sent the copy of OBELOV's memorandum through EVDOKIMOVA in a sealed envelope addressed to ZINOVIEV, who did not return it to me.

Taken down correctly from my words and read by me: BRAVO.

INTERROGATED BY THE ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE SPECIAL DEPARTMENT OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD — GENDIN.

INFORMATION: OBELOV died in December 1934.

CERTIFIED TRUE: Kuznetsov

RGASPI, fond 671, inventory 1, file 121, leaves 187–189.
