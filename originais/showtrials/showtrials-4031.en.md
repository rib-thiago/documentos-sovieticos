INTERROGATION RECORD

Of YAKOVLEV, Moisei Naumovich (supplementary).

Dated January 3, 1935.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that you are the leader of the counterrevolutionary organization in the Vasilyevsky Ostrov district. Describe its membership and activities?

ANSWER: After the 15th Party Congress, I headed a counterrevolutionary organization in the Vasilyevsky Ostrov district until the end of 1928; it had arisen with the disintegration of the Zinovievist group.

The following persons belonged to this organization:

1. NOTMAN, formerly a teacher at LILI.

2. FOKIN, A., a worker at the “Elektroapparat” plant.

3. SEMYONOV, Semyon, a worker at the “Comintern” plant.

4. KONDRATYEVA, Yevdokia, a worker at the Uritsky factory.

5. SEMYONOV (nickname “Skobar”).

6. PANKO, Adam, a worker, now an employee somewhere in publishing.

7. SHEINBERG [i], former secretary of the party committee at the Kozitsky plant.

Our counterrevolutionary organization took a negative attitude toward both SAFAROV’s and ZINOVIEV’s positions.

We considered that the principal cause that had led us so far along the path of counterrevolutionary struggle against the party was ZINOVIEV’s unprincipled bloc with TROTSKY (the demonstration, “linkups,” etc.); we were therefore opponents of this bloc on principle.

Regarding the decisions of the 15th Congress as correct, we considered it necessary to return to the party to work for the cause of the proletariat; however, on the question of the 14th Party Congress, particularly on the question of building socialism in our country, we held counterrevolutionary Trotskyist positions, and in this we deceived the party.

During that year the organization held seven or eight illegal meetings in private apartments, mainly mine, at which the theory of building socialism in our country was criticized from counterrevolutionary positions, the leadership of the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) was criticized, and the positions of ZINOVIEV, SAFAROV, and TROTSKY were discussed.

By the end of 1928 or the beginning of 1929, I came to the conviction that the LENIN–STALIN theory of building socialism was the principal link in the party’s general line. I therefore decided to participate in party work, discontinued all meetings and organized gatherings, and went to the district committee to ask for work.

However, even after 1928 and up to the very latest days, I had a number of meetings with participants in the former United Opposition, which is now a counterrevolutionary organization.

I met KASPERSKY, I. F., about twice in 1930 and twice in 1934; SEMYONOV, S., and his wife KONDRATYEVA, Yevdokia, twice in 1929–30 and once in 1934 at SHEINBERG’s funeral; FOKIN, A., once in 1930 or 1931 and once in 1934; SHEINBERG, D., once in 1931; SEMYONOV (“Skobar”), two or three times over the entire period; and PANKO, Adam, several times. The meetings occurred by chance, and no conversations against the party took place.

Nevertheless, these meetings, while containing nothing anti-Soviet in form, objectively served to preserve the old counterrevolutionary threads.

I have read the testimony; it has been recorded correctly from my words —

M. YAKOVLEV

INTERROGATED BY:

OPERATIONS OFFICER OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD — FEDOROV.

Certified correct: Kazakova
