INTERROGATION RECORD

Of AVDEEV, Mikhail Nikolayevich, dated January 5, 1935.

AVDEEV, Mikhail Nikolayevich, born in 1885, native of Kazan, Leningrad, 5 Solyanoy Lane, apartment 7, Russian, citizen of the USSR. Low-current engineer at the “Skorokhod” factory, unemployed since December 19, 1934. Son of an office employee. His father taught at a trade school and worked as an artisanal shoemaker. His mother was an unskilled laborer. Before the revolution a metalworker; after the revolution a worker until 1921. Since 1921 an office employee. Wife: AVDEEVA, Olga Venediktovna, aged 40, housewife, non-party; daughter Yelena, aged 14; AVDEEV’s brother: Pyotr Nikolayevich, aged 44, metalworker, works at a canning factory in Pyatigorsk, member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks); sister: VISHNEVSKAYA, Yekaterina Nikolayevna, aged 45–46, lives in Nizhny Novgorod (now Gorky), non-party; specialized higher education, graduated from the Lenin Leningrad Electrotechnical Institute in 1930. Non-party, belonged to the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) from 1917 to 1927, expelled for membership in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc and factional activity in 1925–27. Readmitted to the party in 1927 and expelled again in the purge in January 1934 as a double-dealer and opportunist who did not fight for the party’s general line. No convictions or prosecutions before or after the revolution. No awards. Reserve military registration category: T-8. Was registered with the Murmansk District Military Commissariat; since October 1934 has not registered. Did not serve in the Red Army. Did not serve in the White Army. Did not participate in gangs, counterrevolutionary organizations, or uprisings. Member of the Petrograd District Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) in 1922–23; currently performs no public work. —

Question: When did you join the Zinovievist opposition, and what form did your practical participation in factional work take?

Answer: I joined the Zinovievist opposition in 1925. At that time I was studying at the Electrotechnical Institute and was connected with its group of oppositionists: NATANSON, Maria, ANTONEVICH, PAVLOV, Ivan, TSUNSKY, Konstantin, LEIBEL, Boris, ALEKSANDROV, Sergei, and others. My participation in opposition activities consisted of supporting the opposition platform, recruiting new opposition participants, and attending oppositionists’ meetings. I continued factional work until the 15th Party Congress. After the 15th Congress I was expelled from the party by the Petrograd District Control Commission; soon afterward I submitted a declaration withdrawing from the opposition and was readmitted to the party.

Question: With which participants in the Zinovievist opposition did you maintain contact after the 15th Party Congress?

Answer: Until 1930, while continuing my studies at the Electrotechnical Institute, I met students of this institute, the former Zinovievists NATANSON, ANTONEVICH [1], PAVLOV, ALEKSANDROV, and others. All meetings took place at the institute and in the street. Since 1930 I have met none of the former Zinovievists except for a chance meeting with PAVLOV in 1932 at the apartment of a mutual acquaintance, an engineer named Misha (I have forgotten his surname).

The testimony has been recorded correctly from my words and read by me.

AVDEEV.

INTERROGATED BY

OFFICER OF THE 2ND SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE UGB, GOLUBEV

Certified correct: Churbanova
