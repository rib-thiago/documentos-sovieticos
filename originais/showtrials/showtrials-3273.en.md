INTERROGATION RECORD

On December 16, 1934, I, ZAIKO, authorized officer of the SPO of the GUGB, interrogated citizen SHIRYAEV, Dmitry Ignatievich.

(SUPPLEMENTARY)

QUESTION: The investigation has uncovered the existence of a counterrevolutionary Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization and your participation in it. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: Yes, I confirm that I did indeed belong to the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization and that, when giving testimony on December 15, I tried to conceal its existence from the investigation.

QUESTION: What do you know about the emergence of the Zinovievists' counterrevolutionary organization?

ANSWER: ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's renunciation of their anti-Party views and their struggle against the Party in the period following the Fifteenth Congress, and, on their instructions, the renunciation by all of us Zinovievists, was in fact a maneuver designed to deceive the Party and preserve our forces from destruction. The connections that existed between members of the Zinovievist group before the Fifteenth Congress persisted until recently and, shortly after the congress, took the form of an illegal counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization that existed throughout all these subsequent years.

After the congress, regular meetings and gatherings of Zinovievists took place at the apartment of EVDOKIMOV, with whom I was most closely connected: GESSEN, BAKAEV, NAUMOV, Maria NATANSON and others; among the younger people, RUMYANTSEV, Pyotr TARASOV, Sergei MANDELSHTAM and others. Questions connected with the situation in which the Zinovievists found themselves were extensively discussed there, and their course of conduct was worked out. There were major debates over returning to the Party. I remember that GESSEN, who hesitated at first, then began arguing the necessity of returning to the Party regardless of one's views. His position was that, retaining our views and concealing them from the Party, we should return to the Party to preserve our forces for future struggle. In confirmation that the Zinovievists' return to the Party, especially that of their “leaders” and active members, was plainly deceptive and double-dealing, I consider it necessary to report the following facts to the investigation:

1\. After the return to the Party, efforts to influence and educate Zinovievists, especially the young, in an anti-Party and outright counterrevolutionary spirit continued. At one gathering at EVDOKIMOV's apartment in the winter of 1928, attended by the Zinovievists SHADRIN, NATANSON, TARASOV, myself, SHIRYAEV, and others whose surnames I do not remember, GESSEN recited counterrevolutionary poems, praised their authors and let us copy them. Incidentally, I kept some of them until my arrest, together with other anti-Party and counterrevolutionary documents (the platform of the “Thirteen”, “Sotsialistichesky Vestnik”, the newspaper “Dni” and others), as an archive.

2\. After the Zinovievist “leaders” returned to the Party, instructions were given to preserve factional documents. For example, when I asked EVDOKIMOV what to do with the factional documents (the platform of the “Thirteen”, ZINOVIEV's appeal to workers concerning the demonstration outside the Uritsky Palace during the anniversary session in 1927, and others) which I had received from him before the congress, he instructed me to preserve them. The same instructions were given to other Zinovievists.

3\. After EVDOKIMOV moved to Moscow, active Zinovievists gradually began converging there, and gatherings at his apartment resumed as before. On almost every visit I made to Moscow, I observed that BAKAEV, KUKLIN, GESSEN and others visited him regularly. Political matters were discussed at these gatherings and meetings, usually with criticism of Party measures on one issue or another. GESSEN was particularly active in this respect. I remember his maliciously sarcastic remarks about an advertisement he had read in a book under the general heading “The Classics of Marxism on Collectivization”, which listed works by comrades STALIN and YAKOVLEV alongside works by MARX and LENIN.

4\. As had been agreed by the so-called Zinovievist “leaders”, EVDOKIMOV and others, having already returned to the Party, engaged in outright double-dealing activities within it.

Shortly after ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV were expelled from the Party in connection with the SLEPKOV case and others, I had a conversation with EVDOKIMOV in which, plainly regretting what had happened, he argued that the decision of the VKP(b) Central Committee was unjust. However, when he learned that BAKAEV's wife, KOSTINA, had openly spoken in defense of ZINOVIEV at the Party meeting in the factory where she worked, he attacked both her and BAKAEV for speaking openly and voting against ZINOVIEV's expulsion, and instructed them not to speak openly on the subject, so as not to reveal their connections with ZINOVIEV and support for him. This conversation took place in my presence at EVDOKIMOV's apartment.

QUESTION: Name those who belong to the Leningrad Center of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization.

ANSWER: For reasons of secrecy, our organization was structured so that its members could not all know one another. Personally, I was connected with MYASNIKOV, Nikolai Petrovich, as a representative of the leading Leningrad Center. From MYASNIKOV I know that Vladimir LEVIN plays a prominent role in the organization.

QUESTION: Whom do you know among the members of the organization's Moscow Center?

ANSWER: Among the members of the organization's Moscow Center I know: 1. G. E. EVDOKIMOV; 2. I. P. BAKAEV; 3. A. KUKLIN; and I assume that SHAROV belonged to the Center. If he is not a member of the Center, he is in any case close to it.

QUESTION: The investigation knows of your close connection with EVDOKIMOV, a member of the counterrevolutionary organization's Moscow Center. Describe the nature of this connection.

ANSWER: Since I was close to EVDOKIMOV and frequently visited Moscow in the course of my work, I naturally served as a kind of link between the Leningrad and Moscow people. On my visits to Moscow I received relevant information from EVDOKIMOV about what ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV were doing, their state of mind, and so forth. On arriving in Leningrad I passed all this on to MYASNIKOV.

In turn, MYASNIKOV conveyed through me everything that might interest EVDOKIMOV. In particular, EVDOKIMOV asked how Zinovievist cadres were distributed in Leningrad — where each person was; where the people with whom I had participated in factional activity during my student years were living and working. He was interested in SHADRIN, ZAKHAROV and others, asking about my meetings with them; he asked about the mood among Leningrad workers and the situation in the Party organization to which I belonged.

QUESTION: Whom do you know, or whom do you suspect, of participating in the Zinovievists' counterrevolutionary organization?

ANSWER: Apart from the members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization I have already listed — EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, GESSEN, KUKLIN, SHAROV, MYASNIKOV and LEVIN — I can name participants in the organization whom I know: SAKHOV, who works in Moscow at the Procuracy; Pyotr TARASOV, who works at Kuznetskstroy as head of the rolling shop of the Stalinsk Metallurgical Plant; Pyotr ZALUTSKY, who works in Moscow; M. V. SHADRIN, who works at Kuznetskstroy as chief power engineer of the Stalinsk Metallurgical Plant. In addition, I know as members of the counterrevolutionary organization in the period after the Fifteenth VKP(b) Congress: NAUMOV, who works in Moscow; RUMYANTSEV, secretary of the Vyborg District Soviet; S. MANDELSHTAM; Nikolai ZAKHAROV, who works in Stalingrad; Ilya MEERSON, who works in Leningrad at some hydrological or hydraulic engineering institute; SAVCHENKO (a woman whose first name I do not remember), who in 1928 was a student at the Polytechnic Institute; YAKOVLEV, whose first name, I believe, is Moisei (SAVCHENKO's husband), who was a teacher at the Polytechnic Institute. I do not know whether the people listed currently belong to the counterrevolutionary organization. I think some of them have not changed their anti-Party views and remain participants in the counterrevolutionary organization.

QUESTION: What can you testify concerning the murder of comrade KIROV, committed by Leonid NIKOLAEV, a member of the Zinovievists' counterrevolutionary organization, on the organization's instructions?

ANSWER: The systematic education of the organization's members in a spirit of hatred for the leaders of the VKP(b), and especially comrade STALIN, naturally created fertile ground for terrorist sentiments. NIKOLAEV's murder of comrade KIROV is a direct consequence of these terrorist sentiments among members of the organization. Our organization, and each of its members individually, must bear political and moral responsibility for the murder of comrade KIROV. I did not know NIKOLAEV personally; nor did I know that he was a member of the Zinovievists' counterrevolutionary organization.

I have read the record; it has been written correctly from my words.

D. SHIRYAEV

INTERROGATED BY:

AUTHORIZED OFFICER OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB — ZAIKO.
