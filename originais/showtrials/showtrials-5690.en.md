INTERROGATION RECORD

OF LIPSHITS, Pavel Abramovich (also known as ZALTSER Pavel).

of July 10, 1936.

1902, y\<ear\> of b\<irth\>, a native of the town of Druskeniki (Poland), Jewish by nationality, from the family of a prominent commission merchant. From 1926 to 1931 he was a member of the Communist Party of Germany (KPD); from 1931 to 1935, a member of the AUCP(b). On December 26, 1935, he was expelled from the AUCP(b) for passivity. Until January 1931 he lived in Berlin, where he graduated from the Higher Technical School; by profession, an electrical engineer. Before his arrest he worked as an engineer at the Dynamo factory in Moscow.

Question: You are accused of membership in and active work for an underground counterrevolutionary Trotskyist organization. Give testimony about the facts known to you concerning the activities of this organization and your personal role in it.

Answer: Yes, I plead guilty to membership in an underground Trotskyist organization, in whose activities I have participated since 1928, having been recruited back in Berlin by the medical student Nathan LURIE. Until January 1931 I was in contact with Nathan LURIE in Berlin and, on his instructions, distributed Trotskyist literature among members of the KPD and the Union of Soviet Students whom I knew.

Question: For what reasons did you leave Berlin for the USSR, and what organizational assignments did you receive from the Trotskyist organization in connection with this departure?

Answer: In December 1930 I graduated from the higher technical institute in Charlot\<t\>enburg and at the beginning of January 1931 I went to the USSR to work in my specialty. Before I left, Nathan LURIE gave me a clandestine contact in Moscow, the Trotskyist Erik Karlovich KONSTANT. I was to visit KONSTANT at the home address Nathan LURIE gave me. In connection with my departure, Nathan LURIE instructed me, upon arrival in Moscow, and also KONSTANT, to establish contacts among Trotskyists and people expelled from the Party and work on recruiting them into the organization. KONSTANT, for his part, was to penetrate circles of German political émigrés and recruit supporters among them.

Question: State with whom, among the Trotskyist leadership, Nathan LURIE was in contact in his turn.

Answer: From Nathan LURIE's words I know that in Berlin and later in the USSR he was organizationally connected with and received directives from the prominent Trotskyist Moisei LURIE.

Question: What did you do in Moscow to carry out Nathan LURIE's assignment?

Answer: In late April or early May 1931 I visited Erik KONSTANT in his apartment in the commune house on Shabolovka and established contact with him. Subsequently I met him at the foreign workers' club. At our first meeting I passed KONSTANT the directives I had received from Nathan LURIE before my departure. KONSTANT fully agreed with Nathan LURIE's instructions, and I understood that these directives were not new to him, since he was already carrying out similar work in Moscow.

Question: What did the illegal Trotskyist activity you carried out in Moscow after establishing contact with Erik KONSTANT consist of?

Answer: During our first meetings Erik KONSTANT began asking me about the status of my Party membership. When I informed him that I had no documents confirming my membership in the KPD, KONSTANT said that obtaining a Party card had to be expedited in the interest of organizational work. To that end, in his own name he issued a certificate to the Party organization at the Dynamo factory stating that he, KONSTANT, supposedly knew me as a KPD member since 1926. In fact KONSTANT could not know that, since we had known each other only since February 1929 through Nathan LURIE. We became acquainted when Erik KONSTANT came to Berlin from the USSR on leave.

Question: What organizational connections of Erik KONSTANT in Moscow do you know about?

Answer: During one of my meetings with Erik KONSTANT at the foreign workers' club on Herzen Street in September or October 1931, he introduced me to the German architect Franz VAITS. VAITS was a German national and worked in the group of the architect MAY \[1\]. However, I learned only in 1933 from KONSTANT and Nathan LURIE that he was a frequent visitor to the German embassy in Moscow. Also from KONSTANT and Nathan LURIE I learned in 1933 that VAITS was a member of Germany's National Socialist Party.

Question: Testify about the nature of the connection that developed between you and VAITS.

Answer: At the end of 1931 and throughout 1932 until the m\<onth\> of July I visited VAITS several times in his room at the Grand Hotel. At these meetings VAITS had frank counterrevolutionary Trotskyist discussions with me, making it clear that he sympathized with Trotsky and that only Trotsky's return to power would alter the entire policy pursued by the AUCP(b) and the Soviet government. VAITS continually insisted to me that employed intellectuals in several capitalist countries, including Germany, lived incomparably better than in the USSR. Considering VAITS a member of our Trotskyist organization, I often visited him to exchange opinions on current political and economic matters. During one conversation VAITS told me that if Germany declared war on the USSR because of the wounding of Tvardovsky \[i\], an uprising would inevitably occur and Soviet power would be overthrown. I objected that the Trotskyists' task was not the downfall of Soviet power but Trotsky's return to power. On that occasion VAITS told me that the best way to bring Trotsky back to power would be a shot at Stalin. In later conversations VAITS repeatedly stressed that the Trotskyists needed a resolute man to eliminate STALIN. As a result, VAITS, seeing that I entirely shared his views, raised the question of my participating in a terrorist attempt against Stalin.

Question: Did you agree to commit a terrorist act against Stalin at VAITS's request? What assassination plan did you develop?

Answer: Yes, I gave VAITS my consent. This was at the end of March 1932. VAITS then proposed that I attempt to assassinate Stalin during the May Day demonstration of 1932 on Red Square by firing from the column of demonstrators in front of the mausoleum. For that purpose he gave me a medium-caliber Browning pistol with 32 live rounds.

Question: Where and when did he give you this pistol?

Answer: VAITS gave me the pistol at the end of March or beginning of April 1932 in his room at the Grand Hotel. It was in perfect working order, as I judge from having test-fired it on three occasions in a secluded place in Sokolniki. During the three test sessions I fired 19 rounds at an Osoaviakhim small-caliber rifle target. On the third session, three out of five rounds landed within the target. I informed VAITS of the results, and he was satisfied.

Question: When was your last meeting with VAITS before May 1, 1932, and what instructions did you receive from him?

Answer: I met him on the evening of April 30 in his room at the Grand Hotel. VAITS checked once more whether I was ready to carry out his assignment and commit the terrorist act against Stalin. I firmly assured him that I would not retreat from my decision and would do everything possible to carry out the attempt. There VAITS also instructed me to commit suicide at the scene after shooting STALIN. He explained that this was necessary to avoid revealing my connections. I fully agreed with his instructions.

Question: What steps did you take to implement VAITS's directives concerning Stalin's assassination?

Answer: On May 1, 1932, carrying in my pocket the pistol VAITS had given me, fully loaded with seven live rounds (one round chambered and the safety engaged), I crossed Red Square with the Dynamo factory column from the Proletarsky d\<istrict\> on the side nearest GUM. The considerable distance separating me from the mausoleum, where the government was located, forced me to abandon the terrorist act, because it was impossible to get closer to the mausoleum due to the extensive security cordon between the ranks of demonstrators. On the evening of May 1, 1932, I visited VAITS and told him that it had not been possible to carry out the planned assassination of STALIN.

Question: What new directives did you receive from VAITS concerning this matter?

Answer: VAITS instructed me to find better circumstances that would allow me to see STALIN at closer range. He advised me to try to ascertain, wherever possible, information about STALIN's life, his schedule and his whereabouts.

Question: Whom did you inform about the substance of VAITS's assignments and the actual preparations for a terrorist act against STALIN?

Answer: I informed Erik KONSTANT and Nathan LURIE, who had arrived from Berlin by then. This was between April 25 and 27, 1932, at Nathan LURIE's apartment.

Question: You did not visit VAITS only by yourself. With whom, besides KONSTANT, did you meet VAITS?

Answer: In mid-May 1932 KONSTANT invited Nathan LURIE and me to visit VAITS at his hotel. It was the first meeting of the three of us with him. At this meeting KONSTANT introduced Nathan LURIE to VAITS as a prominent member of the organization, newly arrived from Berlin, who could report on the Trotskyist organization abroad and on Trotsky's plans and directives concerning illegal activities in the USSR. Nathan LURIE gave a report, at the end of which he declared that the task of all Trotskyists should be to lead discontented elements in the country onto the path of struggle against STALIN by propagating the need for his physical elimination. Nathan LURIE stated that this would help restore Trotsky to the leadership of the Party and the country.

Question: Why did Nathan LURIE speak so openly upon meeting VAITS for the first time?

Answer: I am convinced that immediately upon arrival KONSTANT informed Nathan LURIE of VAITS's active role in preparing a terrorist act against STALIN. At the time I did not perceive any lack of caution \[2\] on the part of Nathan LURIE or VAITS.

Question: What questions were discussed and what specific measures were planned at this meeting?

Answer: KONSTANT was instructed to expand and intensify work among German political émigrés to find out through them who would be sen\<t\> to Germany on instructions from the Executive Committee of the Communist International (ECCI). At the same time KONSTANT was instructed to establish cells among the émigrés. I, LIPSHITS, was instructed to begin work among Soviet students who had returned from Germany, in order to select among them militants capable of being used for terrorist acts. Nathan LURIE was to settle in Moscow and take charge of the group. At this meeting VAITS also declared that he intended to leave the USSR in the near future.

Question: When did VAITS leave the USSR?

Answer: VAITS left the USSR in the autumn of 1932. I personally saw him for the last time in June 1932. After his departure I continued meeting Nathan LURIE and saw KONSTANT twice: once in 1932 and once in 1933.

Question: What measures to prepare terrorist acts did your Trotskyist fighting group plan after VAITS left Moscow?

Answer: On Nathan LURIE's direct instructions, during a session of the All-Russian Central Executive Committee (VTsIK) in the autumn of 1932, I obtained a guest pass from the factory Party organization to attend a meeting of that session in the Kremlin. Nathan LURIE's assignment was for me to enter the Kremlin and commit a terrorist act against Stalin during the VTsIK session if he was present. I agreed to carry it out. At that time I still had the pistol VAITS had given me, with 13 live rounds. Throughout my attendance at the session I carried it in my pocket.

Question: From whom did you personally receive the pass for the VTsIK session, and who else from your factory attended that session?

Answer: I received the guest pass for the VTsIK session from the Party organizer of the Dynamo factory's production-preparation department, the engineer ZHOLDAK. With me in the Kremlin was the engineer Viktor OSTANIN, who knew nothing of my terrorist intentions.

Question: During the meeting, were you seated with OSTANIN?

Answer: No. I left him on a plausible pretext because he might have noticed my agitation and prevented me from carrying out the terrorist act. When I left, I told him I was going to see the exhibits.

Question: How did you intend to carry out the terrorist attempt at that VTsIK session?

Answer: Since the only Politburo member at that session was KALININ, I left OSTANIN and went to the exhibition hoping to encounter Stalin there. However, I met neither Stalin nor any other Politburo member. Nor was I able to attempt an assassination of Kalinin because the guest seats were too far from the presidium table, and I did not consider it worthwhile to fire without any chance of success. After spending several hours there, I left the session. The next day I visited Nathan LURIE and told him about the failure of the assassination plan. Nathan LURIE said, word for word, “What a pity, such opportunities are rare,” and immediately added that a more convenient and suitable occasion had to be sought.

Question: Did Erik KONSTANT know about the terrorist assignments you received from Nathan LURIE?

Answer: I know nothing about that.

Question: Until what time did you continue meeting Nathan LURIE?

Answer: I met with Nathan LURIE until July 1933, that is, until his departure for Chelyabinsk. Before leaving, Nathan LURIE told me that Moisei LURIE, who directed him, had arrived in Moscow on a special assignment from Ruth FISCHER and MASLOV. At that time Nathan LURIE also told me that active work on organizing terrorist acts against members of the Politburo of the AUCP(b), particularly against STALIN, now had to unfold. From Nathan LURIE's words I know that he informed Moisei LURIE of the preparations for terrorist acts that I had carried out up to that point, but which had ended without result.

Question: Did you personally meet Moisei LURIE in Moscow?

Answer: No, I never met him here.

Question: What do you know about Nathan LURIE's intention personally to commit a terrorist act against the Party and government leadership?

Answer: From Nathan LURIE's words I know that on Moisei LURIE's instructions, with the assistance of his relative, Dr. TAITS, he was to obtain employment at the Kremlin hospital, so as to gain access to the Kremlin and thereby secure the opportunity to attempt an assassination of Stalin \[3\]. However, he did not succeed.

Question: Whom did you personally recruit from among the militants for terrorist activities?

Answer: I attempted to recruit Aleksandr Samoilovich OKHRIMSKY, whom I had known since Berlin and who had met Nathan LURIE there and sympathized with the Trotskyists, into our terrorist group. I knew OKHRIMSKY as a man of strongly counterrevolutionary views. However, I could not recruit him because during my attempt he declared that he already belonged to a terrorist group composed of Soviet students who had returned from Germany to the USSR. He did not name its participants, and for reasons of secrecy I did not ask.

Question: Where and when did you meet OKHRIMSKY?

Answer: I usually met OKHRIMSKY at my apartment during 1933.

Question: Did you inform Nathan LURIE about your attempt to recruit OKHRIMSKY, and how did he react?

Answer: I recounted my entire conversation with OKHRIMSKY to Nathan LURIE in detail. Nathan told me that he knew him well from Berlin and considered him a resolute man suitable for terrorist activity.

Question: Who continued to maintain contact with VAITS after he left the USSR?

Answer: According to Nathan LURIE, Erik KONSTANT maintained contact with VAITS throughout that period.

Question: Where did you keep the pistol until the time of your arrest?

Answer: At the beginning of December 1933, in my apartment, I handed OKHRIMSKY the Browning pistol I possessed, with 13 live rounds. I did so because I intended to marry and did not wish to arouse my wife's suspicions by unlawfully keeping a weapon.

Question: You have so far given no testimony about your terrorist activities during 1934–1936. Give exhaustive testimony about all your connections and your specific work in preparing terrorism during the past two years.

Answer: I must add the following to my testimony: in January 1936 Nathan LURIE arrived in Moscow from Chelyabinsk on his way to Leningrad. I met him at his wife's apartment on Kropotkin Street; I do not remember the house number \[ii\]. During that meeting we talked alone, having asked Nathan LURIE's wife to leave us by ourselves. Nathan LURIE told me that during his stay in Chelyabinsk he had actively prepared a terrorist attempt against Sergo ORDZHONIKIDZE.

Question: What exactly did Nathan LURIE tell you concerning the preparation of the terrorist act against Sergo ORDZHONIKIDZE?

Answer: Nathan LURIE told me that in 1935 Sergo ORDZHONIKIDZE had come to Chelyabinsk on an inspection tour of factories. Nathan LURIE decided to exploit that visit to commit an attempt against him at the Chelyabinsk factory. According to Nathan LURIE, he had a revolver for that purpose. From what Nathan LURIE said, the attempt was prevented by the heavy security around Sergo ORDZHONIKIDZE, which made it impossible to get close to him.

Question: What further plans for terrorist activity did you and Nathan LURIE discuss?

Answer: At the same meeting, after Nathan LURIE told me about his unsuccessful attempt against Sergo ORDZHONIKIDZE, he said he intended to take steps to return to Moscow in connection with the forthcoming resumption of admission and transfer of members into the AUCP(b), so that by joining the Party and obtaining a Party card he could create favorable conditions to prepare and commit a terrorist act against one of the leaders of the AUCP(b), above all Stalin.

Question: Did Nathan LURIE tell you about organizational assignments connected with his departure to study in Leningrad?

Answer: Nathan LURIE let me understand that he had organizational instructions from Moisei LURIE to establish contacts in Leningrad. He did not disclose whom he was to contact.

Question: What terrorist plans did Nathan LURIE describe to you in connection with his departure for Leningrad?

Answer: Nathan LURIE told me that, after settling in Leningrad, he also intended to take steps to prepare and commit a terrorist act against ZHDANOV, secretary of the Leningrad Committee of the AUCP(b). He justified the need for an attempt against ZHDANOV by saying that he was one of the prominent Party leaders and headed the Leningrad Party organization.

Question: What assignments of a terrorist nature did Nathan LURIE leave you?

Answer: Nathan LURIE instructed me to be ready by the time of his return to Moscow to participate in a terrorist attempt against Stalin or another Party leader. He believed it would be difficult for me alone, no longer being in the Party, to commit a terrorist act, so he made this dependent on his return to Moscow. Nathan LURIE said he intended to attempt to assassinate Stalin, and I was to help him in this undertaking.

Question: What do you know about whether Nathan LURIE had weapons when he was leaving for Leningrad?

Answer: Nathan LURIE never personally showed me the weapon he had, but from his own words I knew that he possessed a weapon.

Question: When did Nathan LURIE intend to return to Moscow?

Answer: No exact date had been fixed, but it was assumed that he would return to Moscow immediately upon completing the courses for advanced medical training and would visit Moscow during vacations.

Question: Did you meet Nathan LURIE after that meeting in January?

Answer: No, I did not see him again before my arrest.

Question: Which other elements with active terrorist inclinations do you know?

Answer: In the middle of the m\<onth\> of March 1936, I met the Trotskyist MUSHINSKY in the premises of the Party Control Commission (KPK), on the second floor, in the corridor to the left. Before that I had not met him in Moscow. I knew him by sight and by surname from Berlin, but not closely. From my conversation with MUSHINSKY I understood that he knew about me and my activities, as I suppose, from Moisei LURIE. A conversation began in which MUSHINSKY told me he had been an active Trotskyist in Germany and therefore had great difficulty transferring into the AUCP(b). He also told me that he was now expelled from the Party. I informed him in turn that I too had been expelled. In the further conversation MUSHINSKY displayed bitterness and hostility toward the leadership and policies of the AUCP(b), saying that the situation in the country and the Party could be corrected only if STALIN were physically eliminated. I agreed with him. I did not meet MUSHINSKY again after this encounter.

The foregoing was correctly recorded from my words, corresponds to reality, and was read to me.

P. LIPSHITS.
