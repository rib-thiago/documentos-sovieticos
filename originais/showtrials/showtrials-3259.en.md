INTERROGATION RECORD

On 15 December 1934, I, SHAKULEV, officer of the 2nd Section, SPO, interrogated Aleksandr Ivanovich ALEKSANDROV as an accused.

ALEKSANDROV, Aleksandr Ivanovich, born in 1899, native of Leningrad, from a working-class family, Russian, USSR citizen; resides at 4 Dzerzhinsky Street, apartment 46. Deputy manager of Lenkozhtrust. Married; wife Maria Ivanovna ALEKSANDROVA, 32, non-Party, homemaker; daughter Vera, 10; daughter Zoya, 2; brother Nikolai Ivanovich ALEKSANDROV, 37, VKP(b) member, responsible official at Soyuztabaktorg, 118 Fontanka, apartment 38; brother Boris, 31, VKP(b) member, last employed as director of the Pulkovo workings; mother Maria Grigorievna ALEKSANDROVA, 70, lives at 118 Fontanka, apartment 38. No real estate; elementary education. VKP(b) member since 1918; expelled in 1927 for belonging to the Zinovievist opposition, reinstated in January 1929; and until 1917–1923 worked in the Komsomol; in 1922 a VLKSM Central Committee member; in 1924–1926 secretary of the “Krasny Putilovets” factory Party committee. Never tried or investigated. Military registration category: senior instructor of the Military District Political Directorate; did not serve with the Whites.

QUESTION: With which former participants in the counter-revolutionary Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc did you maintain contact?

ANSWER: After reinstatement in the VKP(b) in 1929, while living in Moscow in 1929–1931, I met the following Zinovievist opposition participants: 1) Mikhail Erastovich BARANOV, working in the People's Commissariat of Agriculture system; Boris Semionovich BLINKOV, in the People's Commissariat of the Timber Industry system; Ivan NAUMOV, then working at Sovkino; Aleksandr ZILBERMAN, then employed on the “Trud” newspaper editorial staff. Of these, I maintained closest contact with BARANOV and BLINKOV. I repeatedly met the latter during his visits to Leningrad. Living in Leningrad, I met Yakov TSEITLIN, Georgy POPOV, Vladimir RUMYANTSEV, Lev KHANIK, SOSITSKY, Ivan KOSTRITSKY, Vladimir LEVIN, Aleksandr MOROZOV, Aleksandr SEREDOKHIN, Pyotr TARASOV, Foma NALIVAIKO, Vladimir MATVEEV and Semion MINAEV. All were former active participants in the Zinovievist opposition. In spring 1933, at the Smolny District VKP(b) Committee premises, I met Sergei MANDELSHTAM, a Zinovievist oppositionist visiting from Moscow. In early 1934 I met Vladimir SOBOL, visiting Leningrad, who had also formerly adhered to the Zinovievist opposition.

QUESTION: Which former leaders of the Zinovievist opposition did you meet?

ANSWER: I met EVDOKIMOV several times. In addition, in summer 1934 I met BAKAEV, with whom I stayed at the Frunze sanatorium in Sochi.

QUESTION: Which former participants in the Zinovievist opposition did you visit at home?

ANSWER: In 1932, on the anniversary of the Leningrad VLKSM organisation, TSEITLIN or KHANIK, I do not remember exactly, telephoned my workplace at the Regional Supply Department, where I then worked, inviting me to an “anniversary banquet”, as he put it, where, he said, only old Komsomol members would gather. I was given an address either on Roshal Embankment or Red Fleet Embankment. Arriving there, at what turned out to be SUROV's apartment, I found that this “anniversary banquet” had gathered mainly former members of the Zinovievist opposition. Present were myself — ALEKSANDROV — RUMYANTSEV, SUROV, TSEITLIN, KHANIK, POPOV, MINAEV, DEMIDOVA, ILYINA, PANOV, SHERKOVA and several others whose surnames I cannot now recall. About twenty people were present altogether.

QUESTION: For what purpose did you keep counter-revolutionary literature at your apartment?

ANSWER: I acknowledge my guilt in keeping this literature.

The record has been correctly taken down from my words and read by me —

A. ALEKSANDROV. —

INTERROGATED BY: SHAKULEV

CERTIFIED TRUE: M. Glazkov
