INTERROGATION RECORD.

Of POZDEEVA, Maria Vasilyevna, dated December 23, 1934.

POZDEEVA, Maria Vasilyevna, born in 1898, member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) since 1917; received a Party disciplinary penalty in 1926 for participation in the Zinovievist opposition. Instructor at the Moscow Regional Institute for Advanced Training of Public Education Personnel.

Question: Inform the investigation about the nature of your connections with the Zinovievists in Moscow.

Answer: Unlike the Trotskyists, who were defeated ideologically and organizationally, the Zinovievists preserved their cadres until recently both in Moscow and in Leningrad. Despite repeated assurances given by the Zinovievist “leaders” before the entire Party of their full acceptance of the Party line, the Zinovievists as a whole—both the “leaders” and their supporters grouped around them—had until recently not dissolved into the general Party membership. They lived within the Party as a separate caste, had their own special interests distinct from those of the Party, and continued to occupy a special position. I am most closely acquainted with Zinovievist circles in Moscow. I do not know the situation in Leningrad. But I know that GERTIK, for example, often traveled to Leningrad, that people from Leningrad were often in Moscow, and therefore I allow that a similar situation existed in Leningrad too. My testimony of December 22 shows that, returning to the USSR in December 1931 after four years' absence, I found in Moscow the entire leading group of Zinovievists headed by ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV. Already at my first meeting in Moscow with BAKAEV, GORSHENIN and GERTIK I sensed that they did not live by the Party's interests, that they had turned, as it seemed to me then, into narrow-minded private individuals. Subsequently I saw and learned how bitterly mistaken I had been: these people who seemed to me mere private individuals had not ceased their counterrevolutionary struggle against the Party. GERTIK was arrested and exiled; the grouping of Zinovievists did not cease. The joint struggle against the Party with all of them and the personal relationships that had developed—all this pushed me into this milieu: I remained connected with the Zinovievists until recently. I must frankly declare to the investigation that the cultivation within the Party of a separate Zinovievist milieu, special groups with their anti-Party sentiments, critical and hostile attitude toward the Party leadership, and their position as the aggrieved, turned this milieu, these groups, into an essentially counterrevolutionary organization. I was directly connected with the people heading this counterrevolutionary organization, although I was not informed of the true essence of all their counterrevolutionary designs. Together with all Zinovievists I bear political responsibility for the murder of KIROV, since this murder grew on the soil of our struggle against the Party, on the soil of the bitter attitude toward the Party leadership that we sowed.

Recorded correctly from my words and read by me.

POZDEEVA.

INTERROGATED BY: HEAD OF THE 4TH SECTION OF THE SPO, L. KOGAN.

Certified correct: Churbanova
