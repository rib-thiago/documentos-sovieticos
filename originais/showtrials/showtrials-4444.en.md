To the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), Comrade I. V. STALIN.

From L. Ya. FAIVILOVICH.

I appeal to you as the great leader, the head of the Party, and a sensitive comrade who will hear me, understand me, and help me return to the Party path. I have committed a great crime against the Party and the working class: in fact, I partially preserved and continued old opposition ties and allowed a counterrevolutionary conversation with I. TARASOV and I. KOTOLYNOV about bringing ZINOVIEV into the Party leadership; in connection with this we harshly abused you as the leader and head of the Party. I remember how, some nine or ten years ago, you told me: “Watch out, FAIVILOVICH, lest you find yourself together with the Trotskyists”; yes, indeed, those words of yours have been fully borne out. I began a struggle against the Party and the Central Committee, accused the Central Committee of “semi-Trotskyism,” after which, together with the entire counterrevolutionary Zinovievist group, I was an active oppositionist in the so-called united opposition. After the Fifteenth Congress I honestly performed the economic work entrusted to me by the Party in Central Asia and Kazakhstan, and then in Moscow. It seemed to me that I had broken completely with my opposition past. But in reality it only “seemed to me”; by preserving and continuing certain old ties and, moreover, allowing what was essentially a counterrevolutionary conversation about the Party leadership, I in fact became a participant in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist group, which, like myself, bears moral responsibility for the base, treacherous, vile murder of one of the finest fighters and leaders of our Party, Comrade KIROV. After all, the group that organized and carried out the murder consists of members of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization; after all, it is essentially a fascist gang that emerged from the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist group and permitted itself to commit a vile murder. This is the vile counterrevolutionary swamp into which I have fallen; I have become fully conscious of my crime.

I ask you, Comrade STALIN, to help me get out of it. I have understood the full extent of my crime; I am only 34 years old, of which I spent almost 17 in the Party.

My background: I am the son of a Leningrad worker who worked at a factory for some 30-35 years; my entire family is Communist, even my father, who is over 70, belongs to the Party. I ask you to give me the opportunity, through honest work for the benefit of the Party and the working class, to wash away the shameful stain upon me and become a devoted fighter of the great Party of LENIN–STALIN. —

FAIVILOVICH.

January 12, 1935.

Certified correct: Churbanova

RGASPI, F. 671, Op. 1, D. 134, fols. 239-240.
