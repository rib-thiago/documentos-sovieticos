INTERROGATION RECORD

of TARASOV, Ivan Ivanovich, dated December 19, 1934.

(Supplementary)

QUESTION: Do you acknowledge that you returned to the Party with a double-dealing purpose in accordance with ZINOVIEV's instructions in his letter to RUMYANTSEV dated June 30, 1928, which you read and discussed at RUMYANTSEV's apartment in July 1928?

ANSWER: Yes, I do. I do not now remember whether I read ZINOVIEV's letter instructing us to deceive the Party, but I knew of ZINOVIEV's instruction to return to the Party with double-dealing intentions in order to preserve the cadres. And we entered the Party, myself in particular, not as a result of disarmament before it, but on the basis of ZINOVIEV's instruction, to deceive the Party. I recall that during the very process of our reinstatement we displayed double-dealing toward the Party. I, I. KOTOLYNOV and V. RUMYANTSEV visited comrade YAROSLAVSKY, and from him went to KAMENEV; we informed him, and he, KAMENEV, directed us and helped draft the final text of our declaration.

QUESTION: What do you know about the existence of a Zinovievist-Trotskyist counterrevolutionary organization?

ANSWER: The double-dealing return to the Party, about which I testified above, and the preservation of connections between participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc led to the preservation, or rather creation, of a counterrevolutionary organization from cadres participating in the struggle against the Party in the ranks of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc. As I have already testified, ZINOVIEV's directive amounted to preserving cadres and entering the Party through double-dealing; this task was successfully accomplished.

During the open struggle against the Party, our “leaders” instilled in us a sharply hostile attitude toward comrade STALIN and his closest associates, such as KAGANOVICH and KIROV. We were systematically told various counterrevolutionary fabrications directed against Stalin. Comrade STALIN was called a Bonaparte who used violence against the Party; it was said that he was leading the Party toward a split and that “it would be better if he were not there”.

Having no political program (at any rate, I know of none), members of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization, when meeting one another, continued to cultivate and spread counterrevolutionary talk against the Party leaders, especially comrade Stalin, speaking of the necessity of bringing ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV and their supporters into the Party leadership.

I recall a conversation a year ago at Faivilovich's apartment, attended by myself, KOTOLYNOV and FAIVILOVICH's wife, TEREMYAKINA. In this conversation hostile sentiments toward comrade Stalin were expressed: counterrevolutionary attacks from the same arsenal the oppositionists had used in their time for slanderous statements against comrade Stalin. There was also talk about who could replace comrade Stalin, but I do not remember exactly how or in what connection this question arose. KAGANOVICH's name was mentioned. Faivilovich then spoke about the possible inclusion of ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV in the Party leadership.

I acknowledge that hostile talk directed at the Party leaders, conducted by organization members when they met, objectively fed, and could not fail to feed, the terrorist sentiments of certain particularly aggressive elements. —

TARASOV.

Interrogated by: HEAD OF THE 6TH SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD — KORKIN.
