INTERROGATION RECORD

Of LELEVICH, Labory Gilelevich,

January 22, 1935.

QUESTION: In your testimony of January 19 of this year, you spoke about the connections of participants in the Zinovievist organization with Trotskyists and about attempts to establish a bloc with Shlyapnikov's followers. What further testimony can you give on this question?

ANSWER: A striving for association and contact, attempts to establish a bloc with representatives of various anti-Party counterrevolutionary groupings, was extremely characteristic of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization, of which I was a member until recently. When meeting Trotskyists or participants in the Right-Leftist bloc, we felt ourselves in a kindred environment, among our own.

In my previous testimony, I spoke about my connections with Trotskyists; in the same testimony, I spoke of the connection of MEDVEDEV and SHLYAPNIKOV with VARDIN and ZINOVIEV, which had the character of probing the possibility of a counterrevolutionary bloc. In this connection, the following is also significant: in 1932, in the spring or summer, the Zinovievists myself, LELEVICH, VUYOVICH, and BUDZINSKAYA gathered at VUYOVICH's apartment, along with the leaders of the so-called Leftist grouping, subsequently participants in the Right-Leftist bloc: SHATSKIN Lazar, LOMINADZE, and STEN. I do not remember who initiated this meeting. At this meeting, I remember, the conversations revolved around the state of the ideological front; in particular, STEN and I characterized in counterrevolutionary terms the influence of Comrade STALIN's historic letter to the editors of “Proletarskaya revolyutsiya” on the Party's ideological work, slanderously asserting that this letter had led to the extermination of theoretical cadres and to the stagnation of the Party's theoretical work. No general political and organizational conversation took place, since SHATSKIN and LOMINADZE arrived very late, but the very fact of our meeting with three “Leftist” leaders is characteristic as an attempt to probe the possibility of a counterrevolutionary bloc.

QUESTION: Did you have meetings with KAMENEV? Inform the investigation of the nature of your conversations with him.

ANSWER: In 1931, 1932, and 1933, I met with KAMENEV several times on the business of the “Academia” publishing house. I remember an extraordinarily vivid detail: around August 1932, I visited KAMENEV at his apartment on Neglinnaya. During the conversation, KAMENEV asked me how the case accusing me of Trotskyist contraband in connection with my book “The Poetry of the Revolutionary Raznochintsy” had ended. I replied that the Moscow Control Commission had noted a number of errors in the book, but had dropped the accusation of Trotskyist contraband. KAMENEV smiled and, making a malicious counterrevolutionary attack on the law of August 7, issued shortly before, remarked: “Well, that is a very humane decision, when a peasant is shot for a stolen ear of grain!”

In general, with regard to meetings in 1933, KAMENEV displayed great caution. When, on arriving in Moscow in October 1933, I called on him at the publishing house, KAMENEV warned me as we parted not to visit his apartment, saying that this would be better for him and for me. This testified to increased secrecy in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization after ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV returned from exile.

Taken down correctly from my words and read by me —

LELEVICH.

Interrogated by —

Head of the 1st Section of the SPO of the UGB — LULOV.
