INTERROGATION RECORD

Dated January 5, 1935, conducted by RUTKOVSKY, assistant head of the SPO of the GUGB.

LEBEDEV, Konstantin Fedorovich, born in 1882, from Kostroma; father was a stove builder; residing in Leningrad, 10 Tchaikovsky Street, apartment 42; head of the special department of Kultpromsoyuz; wife: Yelizaveta Sergeyevna KUKLINA, born in 1892, member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) since 1919, worked as head of the Central District Health Department, now removed from work; without property; home education; member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) since 1900. From 1900 to 1903 in Sormovo belonged to an illegal circle. Was arrested and dismissed from the plant. In 1904–1905 reentered the Sormovo plant and continued revolutionary work until 1908. Then, after dismissal from the plant, left for Chita and Vladivostok, and from there moved to Leningrad at the end of 1909. Continuously in Leningrad since then. Before the revolution belonged to illegal circles there and participated in organizing strikes at the “Nobel” plant. Removed from military registration.

QUESTION: The investigation has established that you participated in a meeting of Zinovievists at your apartment organized by KUKLIN, A. S., who came from Moscow in 1933. Do you admit your guilt in this?

ANSWER: Yes, I admit my guilt in the fact that Zinovievists invited by KUKLIN gathered at my apartment and I did not inform anyone of this in good time. KUKLIN gathered the Zinovievists at my apartment without my knowledge.

QUESTION: Name the Zinovievists who gathered at your apartment?

ANSWER: The following gathered at my home: KUKLIN, Alexander Sergeyevich, who had arrived from Moscow and was temporarily staying at my apartment; SOLOVYOV, Konstantin Stepanovich, chairman of ROSAT (Rostov Fittings Trust); KUSHNIKOV, Pavel Ivanovich, who had come to Leningrad from Rostov on business; and RUMYANTSEV, V. V., whom I did not know.

QUESTION: The investigation has established that you participated in conversations hostile to the party and party leadership among the Zinovievists gathered at your home?

ANSWER: I allow the possibility that those gathered might have had conversations of a hostile, anti-Soviet character before my arrival. On coming home I found all the persons listed above already there, and in my presence there were no conversations hostile to the party and Soviet power.

QUESTION: Was your wife present at this meeting the whole time?

ANSWER: My wife was at home when I arrived from work.

QUESTION: Tell the investigation with which Zinovievists you maintained regular contact?

ANSWER: I had contacts with SOLOVYOV, K. S., KUKLIN, A. S. (my relative), and KUSHNIKOV, P. I., during the latter’s visits from Rostov to Leningrad. The meetings with them were not in the nature of organizational-political contact but were simply friendly.

K. LEBEDEV.

INTERROGATED BY: RUTKOVSKY.

Certified correct: A. Svetlova
