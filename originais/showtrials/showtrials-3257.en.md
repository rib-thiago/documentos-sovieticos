INTERROGATION RECORD

CONDUCTED ON 15 DECEMBER 1934 BY THE SPO, GUGB OFFICER — ZAIKO.

SHIRYAEV, Dmitry Nikitich, born in 1902, native of Pavlodar, former Semipalatinsk Province; father a peasant, Russian, USSR citizen; in Leningrad since 1925, 8 Paradnaya Street, apartment 24. Responsible head of the die-steel group at the Central Institute of Metals. Wife: Elena Vasilievna SHIRYAEVA, née SHISHKINA; daughter Svetlana, three and a half; son Boris, one and a half; mother Olga Semionovna SHIRYAEVA, 60; brother Pyotr Nikitich SHIRYAEV, 39, works at the “Bolshevik” factory as a distribution foreman in the shaped-casting shop. Father owned a homestead with a house before the revolution. Higher education: Leningrad Kalinin Polytechnic Institute; VKP(b) member since 1920. In early 1928 received a Party penalty, a severe reprimand from the Vyborg Control Commission for factional work; adhered to the Zinovievist group during the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc; posted proclamations of a group of individual Bolsheviks in Pavlodar under Kolchak in 1918. Arrested in Pavlodar during Kolchak's rule, not brought to trial. Military registration: reserve private. Was on territory occupied by Kolchak.

QUESTION: Which Zinovievists do you know?

ANSWER: I know many Zinovievists because in 1927–1928 I shared an apartment with the well-known Zinovievist Grigory Eremeevich EVDOKIMOV, a relative of mine: our wives are sisters. I shall name those with whom I had the closest contact through joint factional work and shared political views:

1\. Ivan Petrovich BAKAEV; 2. Mikhail Mikhailovich LASHEVICH; 3. NAUMOV, first name forgotten; 4. Sergei GESSEN; 5. Maria Yakovlevna NATANSON; 6. Aleksandr KUKLIN; 7. Vladimir RUMYANTSEV; 8. Pyotr Nikitich TARASOV; 9. Mikhail Vasilievich SHADRIN; 10. Nikolai Aleksandrovich ZAKHAROV; 11. Ilya MEERSON; 12. Fedor Savvich SIDORENKO; 13. Vladimir LEVIN; 14. Nikolai Petrovich MYASNIKOV and others.

EVDOKIMOV and BAKAEV exerted considerable influence in drawing me into active factional work.

QUESTION: Relate your recent meetings with the Zinovievists named.

ANSWER: I meet EVDOKIMOV and MYASNIKOV systematically. In particular I visit EVDOKIMOV whenever I go to Moscow; I last saw him in Leningrad before his departure for a resort in July–August 1934. I see MYASNIKOV even more often. Our last meeting was on 5/XII, when I visited his apartment. I last saw GESSEN and KUKLIN in 1934 at EVDOKIMOV's apartment. I saw SHADRIN in early 1934 during his business trip to Leningrad, when he called at my apartment. SHADRIN works at Kuznetskstroy as the factory's chief power engineer. I saw TARASOV, EVDOKIMOV's son-in-law, in late 1933 at EVDOKIMOV's in Moscow. I met ZAKHAROV in late October 1934, when he came specially to Leningrad for a ten-day holiday. ZAKHAROV works in Stalingrad at the “Krasny Oktyabr” factory as head of the wire-rolling shop. During the same period I met SIDORENKO, who came on the occasion of ZAKHAROV's arrival to our mutual comrade SUMIN, with whom ZAKHAROV stayed. I happened to meet MEERSON on 4/XII at a demonstration. I have not met NAUMOV or LEVIN for the last three years. I have not seen RUMYANTSEV for a long time either, but know he once left Leningrad, returned and recently worked as secretary of the Vyborg District Soviet. Our wives know each other and sometimes meet; RUMYANTSEV's wife also knows my wife's sister, EVDOKIMOVA, wife of G. E. EVDOKIMOV.

QUESTION: What was the nature of these meetings?

ANSWER: These meetings were mainly political in character. The statements many Zinovievists submitted renouncing factional work were in substance nothing but a tactical manoeuvre.

Renouncing their views, formally acknowledging the Party's general line and agreeing entirely with it verbally, a considerable part of the Zinovievists in fact retained their former views, did not share Party policy on fundamental questions, and essentially entered the Party for self-preservation, acting as double-dealers within it. Even during the mass submission of statements of withdrawal, a group of Zinovievists who were Leningrad Komsomol workers — V. RUMYANTSEV, — — — —., [i] P. TARASOV, NAUMOV and others — sharply opposed withdrawal and renunciation of further factional struggle, but agreed to submit a statement while actually retaining their views hostile to the Party on every fundamental question of Party life. I adopted exactly the same line when submitting my statement withdrawing from the opposition. My withdrawal was purely formal; I retained my former views and disagreed with Party policy on several questions, while agreeing with the congress's decisions on organisational questions. Naturally all subsequent meetings with Zinovievists after the Fifteenth Congress could have no other character, since, as I stated, many had not overcome their former views and remained Zinovievists after formally withdrawing. This is the light in which to consider the fact that a certain closeness, more or less regular meetings, mutual support and so on persisted until the last days among both the Zinovievist leadership and the others. I consider it entirely non-accidental that EVDOKIMOV maintains systematic contact with Zinovievists and regular meetings occur at his home.

Personally, when meeting other Zinovievists, particularly SHADRIN, I frankly shared my anti-Party sentiments, and together we condemned the VKP(b) Central Committee for ignoring and excluding from leading bodies the former Leningrad leadership, ZINOVIEV, EVDOKIMOV and others. In conversations with SHADRIN we concluded that the Central Committee actually used individual opposition slogans and propositions to resolve economic problems, particularly in agriculture.

The record has been correctly taken down from my words and read by me.

SHIRYAEV

INTERROGATED BY — ZAIKO
