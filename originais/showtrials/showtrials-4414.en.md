INTERROGATION RECORD

Of SAFAROV G.I. — dated January 11, 1935.

(supplementary)

In addition to my testimony I inform the investigation of the following:

After returning from a vacation spent in Kazakhstan, in September 1932 I met ZINOVIEV for the second time in Ilyinskoye—following the July meeting. This meeting, like the previous one, was arranged by MAGYAR, with whom ZINOVIEV agreed on the time. This second meeting was devoted to discussing two questions: the state of affairs in the Comintern and its sections, and my “information” about Kazakhstan. At this time the question of the possibility of Hitler seizing power was already acutely posed. ZINOVIEV was the first to start talking about this. He assessed the situation in Germany as follows. The Communist Party of Germany was completely confused and disoriented by the slogan of “struggle against social fascism as the main danger”; through its blindness the KPD was helping Hitler come to power. The leadership of the KPD Central Committee, headed by Thälmann, did not realize what was happening and was taking no measures even for the eventuality of Hitler coming to power. The inevitable crushing of the KPD, considered the Comintern's strongest section in the West, lay ahead. The leadership of the VKP(b) in the Comintern was pursuing a line that doomed the party to defeat. Lenin's united-front tactics had been forgotten, thrown overboard. The parties—the CI sections—were unable to exploit the process of the working masses' revolutionary radicalization in the West, since they were merely an appendage of the VKP, deprived of independence, with no inner-party democracy or self-criticism whatsoever. Mediocre people appointed by the CC did everything at its bidding, incapable of thinking. Thus spoke ZINOVIEV, asking me whether I knew or had in mind people from foreign parties who had an “independent opinion”—of course, in the sense of struggling against the line of the VKP(b) and the Comintern. I answered in the negative and proposed talking to MAGYAR, who traveled abroad and had the opportunity to communicate with a broader circle of people in Germany, France and Poland.

My “information” about Kazakhstan was just as counterrevolutionary in character as the discussion of the state of affairs in the CI. I spoke of famine in Kazakhstan and of the fact that among the local Kazakhs only “Goloshchekin's yes-men” were brought into the leadership. ZINOVIEV remarked that he himself was well informed about Kazakh affairs. “And here they go spinning tales about industrialization: they destroyed agriculture and livestock raising and built industrial enterprises in the desert. GOLOSHCHEKIN did his best, but the CC knows all about this too,” said ZINOVIEV. At this meeting ZINOVIEV again recommended seeing MRACHKOVSKY and I.N. SMIRNOV, as well as SMILGA.

“A great deal goes into all sorts of preliminary meetings and probing,” ZINOVIEV complained in reply to my remark that “if there were a little less diplomatic maneuvering, it would already be possible to submit a collective document to the CC.” The meeting ended there.

Written in my own hand.

G. SAFAROV.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD: PETROVSKY.

Certified correct: Kazakova
