REPORT

On December 4, 1934, at the request and in the presence of the ch\<ief\> of the medical service of the NKVD Directorate, Comrade MAMUSHIN, and the oth\<ers\> undersigned, a commission of experts consisting of Professor V. A. NADEZHDIN, the responsi\<ble\> forensic-medicine instructor N. I. IZHEVSKY, Professor DOBROTVORSKY, and Prof\<essor\> ROZANOV performed a forensic autopsy on the body of citiz\<en\> BORISOV Mikhail Vasilyevich, with the following findings:

EXTERNAL EXAMINATION:

The deceased was about 60 years of age, with a body length of 172 cm and a chest circumference of 91 cm. He was of normal build and moderate nourishment; the skin was pale, and on the back of the body there were pronounced red-violet postmortem lividity patches that blanched under pressure. Rigor mortis was present. The hair on his head was gray and stained with blood. In the left parietal region, somewhat below the parietal eminence, there was a horizontally oriented abrasion measuring 2 × 1.5 cm. The face was pale, the eyes were closed, the conjunctivae pale with several pinpoint hemorrhages, and the corneas somewhat dull. The left pupil was 0.55 cm wide and the right 0.50 cm. The auricles were bluish. Drops of blood were flowing from the right auditory canal. There were small blood clots in the nostrils. The nasal bones and cartilages were intact on palpation. The mouth was closed, and the lip mucosa was pale. The teeth were partly closed together. The gums were slightly stained with blood; no foreign substances were found in the oral cavity. The neck showed no signs of injury. The chest was adequately developed. The abdomen was not distended and had a greenish tint in the iliac regions. The external genitalia were normally developed; a drop of clear fluid could be expressed from the urethral opening. The anus was closed and clean. The bones of the limbs were intact. The following additional injuries were found on the body: on the back, in the area of the upper third of the lef\<t\> scapula, an abrasion measuring 4 × 2.5 cm, oriented vertically; on the front of the upper third of the left thigh, three small abrasions, the largest measuring 2 × 0.5 cm and the smallest 1.5 × 0.2 cm, oriented vertically, slightly from left to right. No other signs of violence were found.

The deceased was dressed in a warm white shirt, a white undershirt, and white long underpants. The collars of both shirts and the chest area of the white undershirt were stained with blood.

B. INTERNAL EXAMINATION:

The abdominal organs were in their normal positions. The peritoneum was smooth and shiny. No particular odor was noted from the abdominal cavity. There were up to 7 cubic centimeters of clear fluid in the pericardial sac. The heart measured 10.5 × 10.5 × 6.5 cm. Its external surface was moderately covered with fat, shiny, with several pinpoint hemorrhages

beneath the outer covering. All chambers of the heart contained a moderate amount of dark liquid blood, with somewhat more in the right atrium. The aortic valves were watertight. The valve apparatus had a small amount of whitish deposits. Similar deposits were present near the coronary openings in the aorta; the coronary vessels admitted a probe. The heart muscle had a small quantity of grayish streaks. The stomach contained up to 100 cubic centimeters of liquid blood; the gastric mucosa showed slightly dilated vessels, without hemorrhages. No smell of alcohol was detected in the gastric contents or the blood. The mucous membranes of the pharynx, esophagus, and respiratory tract were covered with a small amount of blood with a bluish tint; the tonsils were unchanged. The descending aorta bore a few white plaques. The lungs had moderate focal adhesions to the parietal pleura. At the lung apices there were hardened nodules with scarring, no larger than peas, arranged in small groups; on section, caseous material was present in their centers. At the left apex, between the indurated areas, there was slight breakdown of tissue; elsewhere the lung tissue was dark red, without induration, and contained a moderate amount of frothy, blood-stained fluid. On the surfaces of the lungs, in places, there were groups of renal subpleural hemorrhages. The lower margins of both lungs contained hemorrhages within the lung tissue, symmetrical on both sides. The spleen was of medium size; the tissue was dark red, with distinguishable trabeculae and follicles, and only slight scrapings. The liver was not enlarged, its edge was somewhat blunt, and its cut surface showed a clearly defined pattern; the bile ducts were patent, and the bile was liquid and olive-colored. The intestinal mucosa had slightly dilated vessels, as did the pancreas, whose tissue otherwise showed no changes. The adrenal glands were unchanged. The kidneys were not enlarged; their capsules could be removed with some difficulty, the kidney surfaces were slightly granular with isolated small cysts, and the renal tissue had a slightly bluish tint. The boundaries were blurred, and the cortical layers were not thickened. The bladder contained 150 cubic centimeters of clear urine, its mucosa was unchanged, and the prostate was slightly enlarged and rather firm. Hemorrhage was found in the skin and muscle coverings of the skull corresponding to the abrasion in the left parietal region, as well as extensive hemorrhages in the right occipitoparietal region. Above the dura mater, in the right parieto-occipital region, there was liquid blood. The dura mater was tense. Upon its removal, a considerable quantity of continuous, loose clots was found beneath it on both the right and the left. In the left frontal and temporal regions, on the inferior and lateral surfaces of the brain, pinpoint hemorrhages extending into the cortical substance were found. There were several drops of clear fluid in each of the lateral ventricles of the brain. The fourth ventricle contained liquid blood. The brain substance was pale, with a symmetrical pattern and no distinctive odor. The following injuries to the skull were found: in the right parietal region, at the boundary between the lower and middle thirds, there was a longitudinal fracture extending through the full thickness of the bone, continuing in the same direction through the right portion of the occipital bone; in its lower portion the fracture branched, extending from the right toward the foramen magnum, toward the right temporal bone and its surface, dividing the petrous portion transversely in half and continuing toward the sella turcica. From the end of the longitudinal fracture, in addition to those described, there was a fracture of the occipital bone to the left, curving around the foramen magnum and ending at the lower edge of the petrous portion. The course of the fractures is approximately shown in the attached schematic drawings. The pelvic bones, spinal column, and ribs were intact. The fingernails were bluish.
