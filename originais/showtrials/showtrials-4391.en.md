Leningrad, January 10, 1935.

Dear Lazar Moiseevich\!

More than any other leader of our party, you know that even before the Fourteenth Congress I had serious doubts about whether I was doing the right thing by following the Zinovievist opposition. When I was removed from the Urals and immediately afterward, on Comrade Stalin's proposal, the PB decided to retain me in party work and sent me to Saratov as secretary of the Provincial Committee, I did not understand or take into account that the greatest trust had been shown in me and that I had been given an opportunity to return to the party track. When, at a PB meeting just before the Fourteenth Congress, you personally wrote me a note and advised me as a comrade not to enter the swamp of the Zinovievist opposition, I did not listen then either. And afterward, as always happens, when someone, having made a small mistake (although my conduct at the Fourteenth Congress was already an extremely grave mistake), fails to stop in time, recognize and correct the mistake, it grows into a very great one, which is then very difficult to correct… This is not the time to recall the entire past. Yesterday I wrote Comrade Stalin a letter and hope he will believe in my sincerity. I also ask you, L\<azar\> M\<oiseevich\>, to believe in my absolute sincerity when I declare that approximately 3–4 years ago I definitively broke with Zinoviev and counterrevolutionary Zinovievism, both politically and physically. During these years I have been wholly and unreservedly devoted to the party, its leadership and personally to Comrade Stalin.

The facts of life and my journeys through the districts in 1929–1930 long ago convinced me that Stalin's path is the fully developed, victorious continuation of Lenin's path. I simply cannot forgive myself for not writing such a letter 3–4 years ago, when I eliminated all remnants of my former anti-party positions and views, when I definitively broke with Zinovievism, when Stalin's name became as dear to me as Lenin's had once been: why did I not then, 3–4 years ago, expose Zinoviev's counterrevolutionary slanderous exercises? I write of 3–4 years, rather than 5 or 6, because before that I myself had not yet entirely freed myself of my former anti-party views. From the time I was reinstated in the party, the process of overcoming \*them\* \{the former opposition views and positions\} continued for approximately 2–2½ years.

I ask you, Laz\<ar\> M\<oiseevich\>, as chairman of the Party Control Commission and personally as a comrade, to help me escape this terrible situation in which I now find myself through my own fault, through my own stupidity. Do not deprive me of the honorable title of member of the VKP(b); do not take away my opportunity to continue fighting for the cause of Lenin–Stalin.

With com\<munist\> greet\<ings\> —

M. Kharitonov.

RGASPI F. 17, Op. 171, D. 206, fols. 95–95v. Autograph.
