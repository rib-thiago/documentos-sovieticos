INTERROGATION RECORD

OF BAKAEV, Ivan Petrovich,

14/XII/1934.

BAKAEV, Ivan Petrovich, born in 1887, native of the former Saratov Province, Kamyshin Uezd, Gusel Volost, Smorodino village. Administrator of the GLAVENERGO–NKTP network. Wife: KOSTINA, Anna Porfirievna, 39; children: Anastasia, 21, married, husband Vladimir SOLOMIN, both studying in Leningrad; son Pyotr, 18, works as a fitter in Moscow; daughter Tatiana, 14, studying; son Aleksandr, 12, studying; wife's mother, Olimpiada Kuzminichna, 64. Brother's family lives in Leningrad; brother's wife Natalia Sergeevna BAKAEVA is ill. Daughters: Nina, 15; Tanya, 10; Manya, 8, studying. They are my dependants. I own no real estate. My parents had a poor peasant holding. Completed village school and elementary vocational school (metalworking section), then self-education. VKP(b) member since 1906. Interruption from the Fifteenth Party Congress until June–July 1928. Information on public and revolutionary work: in the revolutionary movement since 1905, in the Kamyshin organisation until 1906. Participated in the revolutionary uprising in Kamyshin, then left for Baku. Worked in the Balakhany District. Arrested in 1907; expelled from Transcaucasia in 1909. Returned illegally to Baku, soon arrested and exiled to my native place a second time. Left for Nizhny Novgorod, thence Astrakhan, and thence Leningrad in 1910. Participated in the “Social Science” organisation and various Party work. In 1911 arrested in the street with a leaflet and sentenced to three years' fortress imprisonment. After the fortress, intended to legalise my status in Kamyshin. Again arrested and tried. The arrest was acquitted by the Judicial Chamber; returned to Leningrad and took a job at Novy Lessner; dismissed for a strike, entered the Aeronautical Factory on Vasilievsky Island. In 1916 arrested again and sentenced to exile, but did not leave. Participated in the February and October revolutions in Leningrad.

QUESTION: Were you at I. K. NAUMOV's apartment on 13 November 1929, where Boris KUSHNER informed those present of the Rightists' activity?

ANSWER: I do not remember the date, but such an incident did occur. I called at NAUMOV's apartment with FEDOROV. Sergei GESSEN and KUSHNER were already there. KUSHNER was speaking about something, and on our arrival LEPESHINSKAYA suggested that he repeat what he had said. The substance of his information was that at the last Central Committee plenum the Rightists had submitted a statement which, in KUSHNER's opinion, was a tactical manoeuvre.

KUSHNER allowed for the possibility that the Rightists would put forward the slogan of a “LENINIST CENTRAL COMMITTEE”, that is, a Central Committee including Bolsheviks who had been Central Committee members under LENIN.

He said this slogan would strengthen the Rightists' influence in the Party and find a response among considerable sections of the Party. I remarked that nothing would come of this scheme, since the Party would ask the Rightists what they wanted, and their wishes had long been known from their leaders' formulations, particularly BUKHARIN's. I do not remember GESSEN's or the others' views on this matter.

QUESTION: Relate your visits to ZINOVIEV and the conversations at his apartment.

ANSWER: Conversations generally began with everyday questions about personal life and work, then moved on to political news and the appointments and transfers of major political officials.

I remember visiting ZINOVIEV's apartment after publication of the resolution on grain deliveries or possibly another resolution of equally major political and principled significance, around 1933. ZINOVIEV asked whether I had read it and immediately said it deserved consideration at the recently held Central Committee plenum, that this case was no exception and that STALIN managed to do this. I remarked that under STALIN this was not dangerous, but somebody else might not get away with it. There were other people at ZINOVIEV's besides myself, but I cannot now recall who. Somebody present said STALIN was ill; I confirmed I had heard this.

I was also with ZINOVIEV when he was summoned to the Central Control Commission concerning RYUTIN's platform. A few minutes after my arrival ZINOVIEV was called to the telephone; I noticed that after the call his face changed sharply and he was visibly very upset. I asked: “What is the matter?” He answered that he was being summoned to the Central Control Commission over the Rightists' platform. Asked what platform this was, he briefly replied that it was a kulak counter-revolutionary document.

At this point EVDOKIMOV came in, and ZINOVIEV repeated what he had told me. One of us, myself or EVDOKIMOV, asked whether he had reported the document to the Central Control Commission. ZINOVIEV said he had telephoned Comrades KAGANOVICH and YAROSLAVSKY several times but could not find them, and now did not know whether to attend the Commission because he felt unwell. We advised him to go without fail.

A few days later, having learned of ZINOVIEV's illness, EVDOKIMOV, SHAROV and I went to his apartment to inquire after his health. As far as I remember, GERTIK came later. Somebody in his family told us there about ZINOVIEV's impending exile. Somebody present asked whether it would be appropriate to see him off. I do not remember whether EVDOKIMOV or I answered that seeing ZINOVIEV off could be interpreted by the Party as a demonstration. I do not know whether any comrades formerly in the opposition saw him off, since I had gone on holiday by the time he was exiled.

QUESTION: What do you know of the former Zinovievists' intention to speak at Party meetings about ZINOVIEV's expulsion from the Party in the RYUTIN case?

ANSWER: I discussed this question with my wife, KOSTINA, who considered UGLANOV no less guilty before the Party than ZINOVIEV and deserving no lesser punishment. That evening EVDOKIMOV and SHAROV called on me, and later, during our conversation, GERTIK arrived.

In their presence KOSTINA said she would express her opinion at the cell. Those present disagreed with her decision to speak on this question at the meeting.

QUESTION: How do you assess ZINOVIEV politically?

ANSWER: His ties with the Rightists give grounds to suppose he might enter an unprincipled bloc with individual persons, groups and organisations fighting against the Party leadership.

The record has been read to me. Correctly taken down from my words — BAKAEV.

INTERROGATED BY — RUTKOVSKY.

On KOSTINA's opinion about speaking, I add that, finding no support for her opinion, she declared that she would speak nonetheless, since there was no faction and the view of the majority present was not binding on her.

Supplementing my answer concerning ZINOVIEV's political assessment, I report that it was precisely because of such doubts about him that I stopped visiting him.

Additions made in my own hand — Iv. BAKAEV.
