INTERROGATION RECORD

of citizen KORSHUNOV, Efim Konstantinovich,

dated December 17, 1934.

QUESTION: During the search a letter signed “Ivan PETROV”, from Vyatka, dated July 6, 1928, was found at your home. Who wrote this letter?

ANSWER: The author of this letter is BAKAEV, Ivan Petrovich. I received this letter from BAKAEV by post in July 1928. At that time I was in Kurgan.

QUESTION: In this letter to you, BAKAEV urges you to “knock at the Party's doors” more energetically. BAKAEV writes: “...The times we are living through are difficult. And the prospects for the coming years — especially for next year — promise us nothing better. There are many serious reasons to expect worse. Things are bad with bread, as you know. And that drags exports and imports along with it. Need it be said that exports and imports determine not only the pace of industrialization, but our entire economy, and consequently the policy of our Party and state?”

“...After all, political differences have fallen away. They have genuinely been overcome, I would say; but for how long will be determined by the balance of class forces in the country and the decisive states of the world, which ultimately determines our Party's policy too. Knock, my friend, knock! Do not try to obtain the ‘favor’ of the local ‘leaders’, but earn the goodwill of ordinary Party members by your deeds and example, and ‘it shall be well with thee’, as the ‘Scripture’ says.”

How did you assess and understand these passages quoted above from BAKAEV's letter?

ANSWER: I understood this letter as an instruction that, at the present stage, one should give up the struggle against the Party and join the Party even without fully sharing its positions; but at the same time one should proceed from the fact that, as BAKAEV writes, the Party still faced great difficulties, that differences had been set aside only briefly, and that a time might come when these differences would arise again; opposition cadres in the Party should be prepared and preserved for that time.

I received this letter from BAKAEV by post. This explains, in my view, why BAKAEV wrote all this in a partly veiled form. I wish to note in this connection that such an instruction to enter the Party and preserve opposition cadres within it was quite widespread among supporters of the Zinovievist opposition at that time.

QUESTION: What do you know about the activities of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization?

ANSWER: Since the time of the Leningrad opposition I had been closely connected with Vladimir LEVIN, who, as a member of the Central District Committee during the Leningrad opposition, was an active oppositionist. As I have already testified above, when joining the Party I had instructions both from BAKAEV and from other oppositionists that one should enter the Party even if differences had not been overcome, in order to preserve our opposition cadres within it. When subsequently encountering LEVIN and meeting SOSITSKY, MYASNIKOV, ZILBERMAN, BASHKIROV, DMITRIEV and a number of other oppositionists at his home, I became convinced that the Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization had not in fact been liquidated, but, having gone deeply underground, continued to preserve its cadres and to exist as an illegal organization uniting former Zinovievist-Trotskyists who had not abandoned their previous views.

I knew from LEVIN that until recently he had been connected with ZINOVIEV, BAKAEV and EVDOKIMOV. He informed them about the work of the Leningrad organization of Zinovievist-Trotskyists and received from them a number of guiding instructions for his illegal work.

During meetings at LEVIN's apartment, in the presence of myself, MYASNIKOV, BASHKIROV and SOSITSKY, we repeatedly discussed that ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV should be brought into the Party leadership; that not only they, but all those who had been on the Politburo under LENIN, that is, also BUKHARIN and RYKOV, should be brought onto the Politburo; that circumstances would yet arise in the country and the Party in which one could hope that ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV would be brought into the Party leadership; and that we needed to gather our forces to secure ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's inclusion in the Party leadership.

Recorded correctly from my words and read to me:

KORSHUNOV.

Vyatka, July 6, 1928.

Dear comrade! Only the other day I returned from Moscow, where, together with other “expellees” who had gathered there in quite considerable numbers at the start of the “reinstatement process”, I read your congratulatory telegram. Thank you for the greetings and congratulations. Well, how are your Party affairs? Have you knocked at the Party's doors? Knock, my friend, knock with your old energy! The times we are living through are difficult. And the prospects for the coming years — especially for next year — promise us nothing better. There are many serious reasons to expect worse. Things are bad with bread, as you know. And that drags exports and imports along with it... Need it be said that exports and imports determine not only the pace of industrialization, but our entire economy, and consequently the policy of our Party and state? It is difficult in a short letter to say everything important that could be said about our country's situation. We are going through a very difficult situation now. True, there have been worse times; the Party and state have coped with great difficulties, and of course we shall cope now too. But for a proletarian revolutionary to be outside the Communist Party's ranks at this time is impermissible. Therefore, knock and keep knocking! After all, political differences have fallen away. They have genuinely been overcome, I would say; but for how long will be determined by the balance of class forces in the country and the decisive states of the world, which ultimately determines our Party's policy too.

Knock, my friend, knock! Do not try to obtain the “favor” of the local “leaders”, but earn the goodwill of ordinary Party members by your deeds and example, and “it shall be well with thee”, as the “Scripture” says. Our “little Georges”, however much they strutted and made trouble, have nevertheless, as you know, taken the only correct path, and they too are knocking at the Party's doors...

A couple of words about your proposal to establish business relations with Kozhtrest, contact, as they say. Nothing will come of it, my dear fellow! The Trust hands over all its production to the leather syndicate under contract and has not a single square foot to dispose of at its own discretion. Because the existing contracts severely restrict the trust's commercial activities, it cannot accept your second proposal either, about marketing our cooperative's products. Try writing to the leather syndicate — perhaps something will come of it. Greetings!

Ivan PETROV.

In a few days I shall have to go to Moscow again, where I shall again stay for about two weeks. Others find such business trips very much to their liking, but I would gladly avoid these journeys.

And it is not that I do not want to go to Moscow because I have become very attached to the local people (on the contrary, relations are such that I would gladly say goodbye to the people of Vyatka!), but because one has to handle very delicate matters in Moscow almost without support from the local “bigwigs”, although both the local economy and the state are extremely interested in a favorable resolution of “my” questions.

Ivan.

This letter from BAKAEV was found at my home during the search.

December 17, 1934.

P.S. BAKAEV's letter found in KORSHUNOV's possession.
