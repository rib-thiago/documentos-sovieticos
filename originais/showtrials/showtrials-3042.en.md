STENOGRAPHIC TRANSCRIPT

INTERROGATION RECORD

OF NIKOLAEV Leonid Vasilyevich

15 December 1934.

QUESTION: When did you become acquainted with SHATSKY Nikolai?

ANSWER: I have known Nikolai SHATSKY since 1923; he was the responsible secretary of the Komsomol organization at one of the large enterprises in the Vyborg District and simultaneously a member of the District Komsomol Committee, while I was that Committee's business manager. I met him repeatedly at District Committee meetings and on business within its apparatus; friendly, quite close relations gradually developed between us.

Later SHATSKY took an active part in opposition work; in 1927 he was expelled for factional work; he repeatedly left Leningrad to work in other cities.

Meetings with SHATSKY resumed in 1934.

QUESTION: In what month and where did you first meet Nikolai SHATSKY during 1934?

ANSWER: It was approximately in February–March of this year, at the Istpart building on the Moika, when quite by chance I met SHATSKY, who had come on some business to the “Bureau of the Komsomol Combat Fraternity,” located in the same building.

QUESTION: Describe the content of your conversation with SHATSKY at this meeting.

ANSWER: As I have already testified, I already held openly anti-party and counterrevolutionary views on the principal questions of party policy. From the conversation I established that SHATSKY shared these sentiments. He indicated that contacts must be established with old comrades — former oppositionists — saying that we must organize for work against the party and that he personally considered it necessary to infiltrate the party to facilitate this work. Knowing that the Komsomol Combat Fraternity was effectively in former oppositionists' hands, SHATSKY indicated that first of all we must contact comrades working in that organization; he displayed great bitterness against the party leaders. I answered in the same terms; we agreed to keep in touch and attempt to assemble a nucleus of loyal comrades for the work.

I did not gain the impression that SHATSKY already belonged to the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization at that time.

QUESTION: When did your next meeting with Nikolai SHATSKY take place?

ANSWER: My next meeting with Nikolai SHATSKY took place in August 1934. I met him by chance on Krasnykh Zor Street, not far from KIROV's apartment.

QUESTION: How do you explain having no meetings with SHATSKY between February–March and August? It would seem that, having detected the same counterrevolutionary sentiments in each other, each of you should have sought meetings with the other.

ANSWER: I personally still needed an impetus to begin counterrevolutionary work; as for SHATSKY, I find it difficult to explain why he did not seek a meeting with me during this interval.

QUESTION: Describe your conversation with SHATSKY in August of this year.

ANSWER: Since in previous interrogations I did not tell the whole truth about my counterrevolutionary work and the work of those with whom I was connected, in the records of 4 and 5 December and several others I did not testify to a number of points important to assessing the August conversation.

SHATSKY took me into his confidence and said that a counterrevolutionary organization of Zinovievists existed in Leningrad, including himself, KOTOLYNOV, SOKOLOV and several others whom I knew from Komsomol work. He stated that the organization favored the forcible removal of STALIN and KIROV, noting that he and KOTOLYNOV were authorized on the organization's behalf to carry out a terrorist act against KIROV. He said that he was watching KIROV's apartment, ascertaining all his movements, for the purpose of preparing the terrorist act.

At that moment a car passed with CHUDOV in it, heading toward Sestroretsk. SHATSKY said that CHUDOV was an unsuitable target for a terrorist act and that an act must be committed against STALIN.

From this conversation I discovered that SHATSKY was preparing an act against STALIN and conducting this work independently of preparations for KIROV's murder.

SHATSKY complained that he was registered with the OGPU and, as a former oppositionist displaying sharply anti-party views, had repeatedly been removed from work. I expressed my agreement to join the counterrevolutionary organization and said I was ready for anything, including terrorist acts. SHATSKY recommended contacting KOTOLYNOV, whom I also knew well from work in the Vyborg District Komsomol Committee and who was then studying at the Polytechnic Institute in Sosnovka.

During the October days of 1934 I again encountered SHATSKY near KIROV's apartment, watching it. I did not speak to him because I did not want in any way to hinder him from carrying out an act against KIROV. Since SHATSKY was watching KIROV's apartment, my and SOKOLOV's observations of KIROV were transferred to Smolny, where I sought an opportunity to kill him, which I succeeded in doing on 1 December of this year.

After August 1934 I did not meet SHATSKY again.

QUESTION: What did SHATSKY tell you about preparing the terrorist act against Comrade STALIN?

ANSWER: Everything I know about this question I stated in the preceding answer. I shall only note that SHATSKY regarded carrying out the terrorist act against STALIN as the next task, to be undertaken after KIROV's murder.

Correctly dictated from my words and read by me —

L. NIKOLAEV

INTERROGATED BY:

DEPUTY PEOPLE'S COMMISSAR OF INTERNAL AFFAIRS — AGRANOV

HEAD OF THE EKO, GUGB, NKVD OF THE USSR — MIRONOV

ASSISTANT TO THE HEAD OF THE EKO, GUGB, NKVD — DMITRIEV
