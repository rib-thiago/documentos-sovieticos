INTERROGATION RECORD

of FEDOROV, Grigory Fedorovich

December 18 \[1\], 1934.

FEDOROV, Grigory Fedorovich, born in 1891, a native of Leningrad; father and mother textile workers; Russian, a citizen of the USSR; Moscow, 6 Pankratyevsky Lane, apartment 132. Manager of the All-Union Cartographic Trust. Wife: Betti Grigoryevna, non-Party, housewife; children: Nina, aged 22, non-Party, design draftsman at Soyuz-stanko-instrument, lives at 7 Armyansky Lane, apartment 8; Oida, aged 19, housewife, lives on Merzlyakovsky Lane; Zorya, aged 10, a student; Elena, aged 8; Svetlena \[2\], aged 6. Former wife: Betti Mikhailovna, non-Party, housewife, lives at 7/6 Staro-Pimenovsky Lane, apartment 16.

Without property. Completed a three-class zemstvo school. A member of the VKP(b) since 1907. Expelled in 1927 for belonging to the opposition. Reinstated in 1928. In 1911 was under investigation under Article 102 \[3\]. Military registration: category II. Did not serve with the Whites.

QUESTION: Do you plead guilty to having engaged in counterrevolutionary, double-dealing and anti-Party activities?

ANSWER: Yes, I admit that until 1931 I engaged in anti-Party and double-dealing activities. When meeting ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, NAUMOV, BAKAEV, KUKLIN, SHAROV, GESSEN and GORSHENIN, I participated in discussions of a number of fundamental political questions in a plainly anti-Party spirit. These meetings usually took place at the apartments of ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, NAUMOV and GERTIK. Those listed above usually attended in varying combinations.

As I have already testified, at these meetings we discussed a number of fundamental political questions in an anti-Party spirit.

Of these questions I remember that we discussed the problems of collectivization and increases in consumer goods prices. In discussing collectivization, we proceeded from the view that, on the one hand, the Party leadership had been late in raising this question and, on the other, collectivization was being carried out with very great pressure on the peasantry.

On the question of rising consumer goods prices, we proceeded from the view that prices were growing wildly while wages lagged far behind.

In addition, these meetings discussed the possibility of a bloc with the Rightists, as a result of which the Zinovievists and Rightists would demand that the Party leadership create a so-called “Leninist Politburo”, that is, bring ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV into the Politburo.

At almost all these meetings, rumors were passed on and discussed that serious disagreements were taking place among Politburo members, which might result in personnel changes in its composition.

At all these meetings the Party leadership was subjected to the most severe and harsh criticism. STALIN in particular was subjected to especially harsh criticism and attacks. ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, SHAROV and GESSEN were the harshest and most irreconcilable in assessing STALIN's activities.

I continued meeting all the participants in the former opposition listed above until my arrest, but after 1931 I did not participate with these people in anti-Party activities or conduct anti-Party conversations with them.

Recorded correctly from my words and read to me.

FEDOROV.

INTERROGATED BY: HEAD OF THE 3RD SECTION OF THE EKO OF THE GUGB OF THE USSR NKVD — CHERTOK.
