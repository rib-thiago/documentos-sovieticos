INTERROGATION RECORD

conducted on January 20, 1935 by the head of the 1st Section of the SPO, GUGB, NKVD of the USSR,

PETROVSKY

NASONOV, Nikolai Mikhailovich, born in 1902, native of the village of Streletskoye, Voronezh Region, residing in the town of Bar, Vinnitsa Region, Ukrainian SSR, Russian, citizen of the USSR. Passport issued by the Moscow Regional Militia Directorate. Head of the Political Department of the Machine and Tractor Station in the village of Bar. Father a kulak, rented up to 70 dessiatinas of land. Before the revolution, studied. After the revolution, served in the Usman Department of Public Education. Family composition: father Mikhail Antonovich NASONOV, where he currently lives I do not know; brothers Konstantin Mikhailovich NASONOV and Sergei Nikolayevich NASONOV; sisters Raisa Mikhailovna and Sofya Mikhailovna; where my brothers and sisters live I do not know. Wife NASONOVA, Lidia Andreyevna, residing in the town of Bar, Vinnitsa Region. Education: three years at the Institute of Red Professors of World Economy. AUCP(b) member since 1919. Military registration category: k-67. Sociopolitical activity: party and Soviet work.

In answer to the questions put to me by the investigation I testify: difficulties in the countryside in the autumn of 1931 caused me dissatisfaction with the party line. At that time I heard from a number of people, LOMINADZE, KAKHIANI, and others, that there was outright famine in some regions, that there was enormous livestock mortality in Kazakhstan, and that it had been decided not to publish statistical data on the state of livestock farming in the country because of the large percentage decline in livestock. Finally, the anti-Soviet conversations of certain comrades (KOPP, CHAPLIN) strengthened my view that party policy on rural questions had to be changed. My detachment from practical work and dissatisfaction with the Central Committee decision in the LOMINADZE case prevented me from understanding the nature of the difficulties; I found myself captive to the counterrevolutionary swamp, since I did not see kulak sabotage in the collective farms. Anti-party sentiments concerning the collective farm movement created the ground for a negative attitude toward the leadership. I was also dissatisfied with certain questions of Comintern work. In 1932 I heard from MAGYAR that it would be useful for the party to bring ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV into the party leadership. MAGYAR also considered it useful to bring other former oppositionists into Comintern work. I know LOMINADZE from work in the Young Communist International; I began meeting him frequently again after my return from abroad. Some time later LOMINADZE was called to account before the party for his bloc with SYRTSOV. I considered the Central Committee decision concerning LOMINADZE exaggerated. At this time LOMINADZE began to detach himself from party life; he was dissatisfied with the general situation in the party, and expressions slipped out that brought him closer to TROTSKY. In the autumn he once declared that the difficulties had accumulated and piled up so much that they could be resolved only in the international arena. During this period I found VARTANIAN [1] at LOMINADZE's. Lominadze was also friendly with STEN. After receiving an appointment as party organizer at a factory, although he began to get involved in the work, his anti-party sentiments nevertheless persisted. LOMINADZE considered that the German party was trailing behind events, that its basic tactic should be an offensive against the fascists, and that it was necessary to forestall the fascists and seize power. LOMINADZE considered that the Comintern was pursuing an extremely irresolute line on the German question. LOMINADZE is on close terms with MAGYAR; MAGYAR passed newspapers to him and informed him about the state of affairs in the Comintern, shortcomings and breakdowns in industrial work, and so on. In 1931 LOMINADZE was acutely dissatisfied with Comrade STALIN in connection with the campaign of criticism of the “leftists” as an anti-party group. I know that LOMINADZE most frequently met VARTANIAN, CHAPLIN, FOKIN, LEVAN, YASHVILI, and others. My comrades with whom I discussed all questions were VARTANIAN, party organizer at the automobile factory in Gorky; Iosif MAZUT, now LOMINADZE's deputy in Magnitogorsk; and ZYAMA (PIERRE) RABINOVICH, currently studying at the Institute of Red Professors of World Economy and Politics.

Recorded from my words. Read to me — NASONOV.

Interrogated by:

Head of the 1st Section of the SPO, GUGB, NKVD of the USSR — PETROVSKY.

Certified true: V. A.
