INTERROGATION RECORD

OF SAFAROV G.I.

January 3, 1935.

In response to the questions put by the investigation, I testify:

Georgy GORBACHEV—a former Menshevik, active Zinovievist, friend of VARDIN and LELEVICH, as well as TATAROV. I happened to meet him a number of times during 1928–1934. He never missed an opportunity to testify that he stood in solidarity with ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV and “suffered with them on account of their oppressed position,” as he put it. GORBACHEV is a fairly striking representative of the thoroughly degenerated “party-adjacent,” that is, anti-party literary bohemia; for the Zinovievists, he served as one channel for spreading counterrevolutionary gossip and provocative rumors about “disagreements in the CC, a possible leadership crisis” and the like. At every meeting he was interested precisely in “news” of this kind, and with his fondness for drinking and countless love affairs, he spread gossip acquired in one way or another throughout his wide circle of literary and philistine acquaintances. On the literary front, he held the most Rightist positions, was invariably hostile to building socialism in one country, and explained “all literary misfortunes” by saying that “you cannot remake a peasant country in Arakcheyev's manner.” — “There is none of that creative culture found in the West, and where would it come from?” After KAMENEV's appointment to the Institute of Literature, he was very pleased at the possibility of “close contact” with him, and in a meeting in Leningrad in 1934 he expressed pleasure that “VORONSKY too had been appointed director of the Institute of Arts.” In that conversation, he “prophesied” that KAMENEV would soon be made an academician.

KREINOVICH—from the group of former Zinovievist Komsomol members. As early as 1925–1927, RUMYANTSEV spoke of his “suspicious social origin,” but he was very close to Rumyantsev's group, carrying out above all all kinds of organizational and technical assignments. He continued to maintain his connection with Rumyantsev's group until recently, and I think TOLMAZOV mentioned his surname in a conversation at the Comintern in 1933.

NOTMAN and GAIDEROVA \[1\]—old active Zinovievists. Moisei YAKOVLEV spoke of them in a conversation in Leningrad in 1933. All indications suggest that they were closely connected with him.

MATORIN—a Zinovievist, more closely connected with GAIDEROVA, tried to conceal his Zinovievist connections through his “professorial” activity.

BOLSHAKOV—director of the “Leningradskaya Pravda” printing house; in 1925–1927, printed anti-party documents for me and Vl\<adimir\> MATVEYEV. I assume that he continued his connection with MATVEYEV until recently.

Written in my own hand—G. SAFAROV.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR: PETROVSKY
