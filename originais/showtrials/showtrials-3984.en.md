INTERROGATION RECORD

OF SAFAROV G.I., January 2, 1935.

In response to the question put to me, I testify:

Nikolai Ivanovich UFIMTSEV—a prominent Trotskyist who enjoys Sergei MRACHKOVSKY's unlimited trust and is closely connected with I.N. SMIRNOV. I saw him and A.P. SIMASHKO in mid-1932 at MRACHKOVSKY's apartment. Over tea, I told them in a counterrevolutionary spirit about the situation in the Comintern; I spoke of the party's “decline,” the Comintern leadership's unfitness to lead the revolutionary struggle, the lack of “independent” cadres in foreign parties, and so forth. MRACHKOVSKY, UFIMTSEV and SIMASHKO received all this with great sympathy and approval, and MRACHKOVSKY said: “SAFAROV has tried the experiment, and you see what conclusions he has reached.” UFIMTSEV and MRACHKOVSKY undoubtedly maintained uninterrupted relations with each other. Everything showed that MRACHKOVSKY attached great importance to UFIMTSEV's holding a central leadership post in Moscow, his professional connections \<in\> economic circles and the possibility of using them both to obtain information on economic affairs and for counterrevolutionary Trotskyist work. In particular, UFIMTSEV and MRACHKOVSKY spoke to each other about connections in the Urals and his visit to the Urals. In his views, UFIMTSEV remained the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist he had always been. His apartment was a permanent refuge, especially for Urals Trotskyists. In 1932, Matvei BELYAYEV was there almost every day; S. BARANOV visited, as did others.

A.P. SIMASHKO—UFIMTSEV's wife, like him an old Trotskyist since 1923, no less close to MRACHKOVSKY than UFIMTSEV himself. Not only did Trotskyists meet at her and UFIMTSEV's apartment, but SIMASHKO herself was connected with Zinovievists. When in the autumn of 1932 I told EVDOKIMOV about KNORIN's information at a meeting of responsible officials of the CI concerning the Ryutin affair, the next day, arriving at SIMASHKO's and UFIMTSEV's apartment for a meeting with MRACHKOVSKY that failed to take place, I learned that SIMASHKO knew all this in the most exact terms. This could only have happened through the direct transmission of that information. As far as I remember, I later spoke to MAGYAR about it, expressing dissatisfaction with EVDOKIMOV's “chatter” and “violation of elementary conspiratorial precautions.” Another fact pointing to close connections with the Zinovievists is as follows. When GERTIK's wife died, SIMASHKO telephoned me with an insistent invitation to attend the funeral, at which, as I subsequently learned, many members of the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc were present. I was so surprised that she, rather than one of the Zinovievists, was conveying this invitation that I asked a couple of times whether it was really she who was calling. I last saw SIMASHKO when she came to my apartment to say goodbye, I think in 1933, before leaving for Irkutsk, after UFIMTSEV had already been arrested. I offered her financial assistance on the occasion of her “exile,” but she refused.

Written in my own hand—G. SAFAROV.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR—PETROVSKY
