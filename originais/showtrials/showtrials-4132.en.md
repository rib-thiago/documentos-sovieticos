INTERROGATION RECORD

Of January 6, 1935, conducted by the head of the 1st Section of the SPO, LULOV.

ABRAMSON, Eskusiel Bentsianovich, born in 1898, native of Leningrad, Maiorov Avenue, building No. 8, apartment 10, Jewish, USSR citizen, director of the P. Anisimov spinning and weaving factory, father a craftsman, married, wife Fenya Isaevna MEZHOVA-ABRAMSON, nonparty, son aged 11, supports his mother and father. Incomplete secondary education, member of the AUCP(b) since 1914. Arrested in 1915 for belonging to the Social-Democratic Party and prosecuted under Article 102. Released in February 1917. In the reserve. In the Red Army from 1919 to 1921, doing political work. Held responsible Party and Soviet posts.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that you and the former oppositionists associated with you, beginning with the period of the Fifteenth Congress and until recently, worked to gather and unite participants in the Zinovievist opposition in the interests of reconstituting the Zinovievist organization. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: Yes, I confirm it. Throughout all these recent years (despite my formal renunciation of factional work), until recently I did indeed work to establish contacts with, gather, and unite former Zinovievists. This work led to the reconstitution of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization, of which I was a member until my arrest.

QUESTION: Tell the investigation about your practical work to unite the Zinovievists' forces and the individual stages of this work.

ANSWER: The first stage belongs to the period 1926–27. Because of my opposition work, I was removed from my job in Leningrad and sent to Saratov. There we formed a group of Zinovievists which continued active factional work. The group included myself, ABRAMSON, Naum ROTENBERG, LELEVICH, and LEIKIN. Our group's activity was directed by the Moscow center of the Zinovievist organization. Contact with the Moscow center was maintained through trips to Moscow by LEIKIN and ROTENBERG. I personally also traveled to Moscow, receiving directives personally from ZINOVIEV. The instruction we received from the Moscow center (which guided us throughout the subsequent years) to preserve our cadres also dates from that time.

The second stage belongs to 1928–1931. I remained in Saratov. Besides myself, SAFAROV, ZALUTSKY, and Nikolai DMITRIEV gathered there. We constituted (comprising the persons listed) an anti-Party, counterrevolutionary group of Zinovievists which existed until the end of 1931, that is, until my departure, SAFAROV's, and others' from Saratov. Contact with Moscow was maintained during this period too. In particular, ZALUTSKY, when visiting Moscow, where he met EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, and other members of the Zinovievist leadership, on returning to Saratov informed us of Moscow news, where and which former oppositionists were to be found, and so forth. SAFAROV also traveled to Moscow. I do not remember which former oppositionists he met there, but I remember that he spoke about his meetings with TARKHANOV.

Finally, the third stage of my work to weld together the former opposition forces belongs to 1931–1934 in Leningrad. On arriving in Leningrad at the end of 1931, I reestablished contact with a number of Zinovievists, members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization.

QUESTION: Name the members of the Zinovievist organization with whom you were in contact in Leningrad.

ANSWER: In Leningrad, after my return in 1931, I established contact with the following members of the Zinovievist organization: V. BRODSKY, BOGRACHEV, Alexei SOKOLOV, and SHENBERG (the latter died last year). Together with the persons listed, we constituted a closely knit group of Zinovievists. We met most often at my apartment and at BRODSKY's and BOGRACHEV's. BOGRACHEV maintained close contact with Vladimir LEVIN. I consider it necessary to inform the investigation of the following circumstance: when, following the newspaper report that KIROV's murder had been committed by the dregs of the Zinovievist opposition, I drew BOGRACHEV's attention to this report (I had gone to see him at work), he immediately began telephoning Vladimir LEVIN without saying anything.

Besides the persons listed, over the past few years I occasionally met a number of Zinovievists. In particular, Vladimir LEVIN, SUROV, MANIKAINEN, and TSVIBAK. At SHENBERG's funeral, which I attended, I met the Zinovievists M. YAKOVLEV, Semyon SEMENOV, CHERNY, and KASPERSKY. These meetings continued the work of consolidating the former oppositionists' forces. This applies especially to the gathering of Zinovievists at SHENBERG's funeral.

QUESTION: Inform the investigation of the circumstances of your return to Leningrad from Saratov in 1931.

ANSWER: Before answering this question, I consider it necessary to draw attention to the following circumstance: in recent years a number of oppositionists have returned to Leningrad, and some of them even managed to take leading positions in Leningrad Soviet institutions. In particular, this applies to the group of former active Zinovievists who worked at Smolny until recently: BOGRACHEV, RUSANOV, MUSHTAKOV, MALETSKY, and MYASNIKOV. I believe this is no accident. I am certain that in Leningrad there is some group of people among the old Leningrad hands who bring them (the oppositionists) back and patronize them. I know, in particular, that RUMYANTSEV also returned to Leningrad; when something went wrong with his work at the house of culture, he went to the district Soviet. The same applies to TOLMAZOV's transfer from Sevzapsoyuz to work at the Putilov factory's ORS. The circumstances of my return to Leningrad, it seems to me, may shed some light on this question. They were as follows: in November 1931 I began my efforts to return to Leningrad. It was perfectly clear to me that appealing to the Central Committee was useless. I did not even try to do so, since I had no chances (positive ones) with the Central Committee, only negative ones (my involvement in the opposition). Leningrad was another matter. There I could count on the old Leningrad crowd, on past connections, appeal to feelings, saying, as it were, “we have fought, that is enough,” shed a tear, and so on. It was in precisely this frame of mind that in November 1931 I approached Comrade OSIPOV at the regional committee. At first OSIPOV told me that since my transfer to Leningrad involved the need to approach the Central Committee, nothing would come of it. However, yielding to my request, and learning from me that PTUKHA was secretary of the territorial committee in Saratov, OSIPOV agreed to write him a letter. The contents of OSIPOV's letter were as follows: “Comrade ABRAMSON has spent 5–6 years in Saratov; is it not time for him to return to Leningrad?” A month later I returned to Leningrad. I was appointed director of the Highway Institute and had already taken over, but since the Central Committee did not confirm me as a former oppositionist, the regional committee sent me to work as head of the Leningrad City Housing Department.

Recorded correctly from my words and read by me.

ABRAMSON.

INTERROGATED BY: LULOV.
