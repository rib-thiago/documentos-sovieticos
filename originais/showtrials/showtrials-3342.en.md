INTERROGATION RECORD,

Conducted by PETROVSKY, Head of the 1st Section of the SPO of the GUGB.

Dated December 19, 1934.

Of EMELYANOV, Kondraty Nikolaevich, born in 1901, of working-class origin, city of Sestroretsk, father a worker, Russian, citizen of the USSR. Resides in Moscow, 1st Koptelsky Lane, building 9/a, apartment 30. Assistant to the Chief Engineer of Mosgorzhilstroigorod. Father: Nikolai Aleksandrovich EMELYANOV; mother: Nadezhda Kondratyevna; brother: Aleksandr Nikolaevich EMELYANOV; brother: Nikolai Nikolaevich; brother: Lev Nikolaevich; Georgy Nikolaevich and Anatoly Nikolaevich; wife: Genuefa Aleksandrovna EMELYANOVA. Expelled from the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) in 1927 for active work in the Zinovievist group. In the Red Guard and the army until 1931. Since 1931 worked on various construction projects. Under investigation in 1928 for an anti-Soviet statement. Military registration category: senior, category 8/a.

Question: Tell us who brought you the archive of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization for safekeeping, and when.

Answer: In the autumn of 1927, at the dormitory of the Military Technical Academy in Leningrad, Nikolai Aleksandrovich MAKSIMOV, a member of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization, currently working in the Administration of the Chief of Engineers of the Red Army, told me that the archive of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization had to be hidden. I agreed to hide the archive, after which MAKSIMOV told me that I should go with “Marusya” or “Tonya” (I do not know her surname) to a clandestine meeting place in the Moika district of Leningrad. That same evening “Marusya” or “Tonya” took me to an old man and an old woman (I do not know their surnames) in the Moika district, where the organization's archive was kept. When I arrived at the “old folks'” home the archive was packed (I do not remember how it was packed). I took it, boarded a tram and went to Finland Station, from where I traveled by train to Razliv station.

Question: What documents were in the archive of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization handed over to you for safekeeping?

Answer: The archive handed over to me for safekeeping contained the following documents of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization: Lenin's testament, the platform of the “Bolshevik-Leninists,” an article by Ustryalov and the reply to it; the archive contained one complete set of all documents issued by the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization. Besides the set of the organization's documents, the archive contained an envelope sealed with sealing wax; I remember that the envelope contained a handwritten letter to the “Leningrad comrades” and the agreement between the Zinovievists and the Trotskyists.

Question: Where and with whom did you hide the archive belonging to the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization?

Answer: After my brother Aleksandr Nikolaevich EMELYANOV had acquainted himself with the archive, I put it in my room, where it lay for two or three days; then I divided the archive into small portions and hid the portions in a small dacha and a large house at Razliv station. I hid the archive between the roof and the roof lining. My brother Aleksandr Nikolaevich EMELYANOV knew where I had hidden the archive.

Question: The investigation has information that members of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization came to see you from Leningrad and Moscow and inquired about the archive's fate. Tell us who visited you, when, and what documents you handed over to the visitors.

Answer: In 1928 I received instructions from Mikhail Ivanovich KASHCHEEV, currently serving in the Red Army in the Far East, and from Nikolai Aleksandrovich MAKSIMOV to destroy the archive; I remember that I destroyed some of the documents. No one came to see me from Leningrad or Moscow concerning the archive, since I was absent from Leningrad Region for four years. I do not know whether anyone visited my brother Aleksandr Nikolaevich.

Question: Name to the investigation all the persons who knew that you were keeping the archive of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization.

Answer: The following knew that I was keeping the archive of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization at Razliv station in Sestroretsk District: Nikolai Aleksandrovich MAKSIMOV, Mikhail Ivanovich KASHCHEEV, KUZMINSKY—the latter a former student of the Military Political Academy of the Red Army—and my brother Aleksandr Nikolaevich EMELYANOV.

Question: What weapons have you hidden, where, and to whom do they belong?

Answer: I hid a “Nagant” and a “Parabellum”; the revolvers are hidden under the floor on the upper story of the small house; the Nagant belongs to KASHCHEEV and the Parabellum to KUZMINSKY. Both belonged to the Zinovievist-Trotskyist counterrevolutionary organization.

The record was taken down from my words and read to me, in confirmation of which I sign.

EMELYANOV

Interrogated by: PETROVSKY, Head of the 1st Section of the SPO of the GUGB.
