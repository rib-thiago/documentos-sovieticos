INTERROGATION RECORD

OF SAKHOV, Boris Naumovich—

January 1, 1935.

QUESTION: Describe the tasks of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization.

ANSWER: The tasks of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization were: a) to preserve the Zinovievist cadres, and b) to continue their political education in a spirit different from that in which the party and its CC educate their members. All this was subordinated to a single aim: advancing ZINOVIEV to the leadership of the party and the Comintern.

All the counterrevolutionary conversations of 1929, 1930 and early 1931, about which I have already testified, ultimately came down to the assertion that “the party leadership is not coping with its tasks.” In this connection, all kinds of rumors about “disagreements” in the Politburo were spread and sustained. Systematic attacks of a counterrevolutionary nature cultivated within the organization a hostile attitude toward the party leadership, especially Comrade STALIN and his closest associates. I therefore acknowledge that the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization bears full responsibility for the murder of Comrade KIROV, since it was from its active members that the scoundrels emerged who descended to fascist methods of struggle against the party leaders.

QUESTION: DMITRIYEV T. testified that in 1931 in Rostov you met him and GORDON and informed them about the organization's situation; in particular, you spoke of the need to preserve ties among Zinovievists and said that the task of advancing ZINOVIEV to participation in the party leadership had not been abandoned. What can you testify about this?

ANSWER: I visited Rostov on official business in 1930–1931 and 1932. In 1931, I think in April, I met T. DMITRIYEV and N. GORDON. I cannot now reconstruct from memory the content of my conversations with DMITRIYEV and GORDON, but they could have had precisely this content, since it corresponded to my sentiments at the time.

QUESTION: Tell us which former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc you met in Leningrad, and the content of your conversations.

ANSWER: As I have already testified, whenever I visited Leningrad I called on SHEINBERG; I told him everything I knew of Moscow news and naturally did not conceal my connections with EVDOKIMOV, KUKLIN and other\<s\> among the Zinovievists. SHEINBERG took a strongly negative view of my maintaining these connections and directly advised me to stop meeting them. In 1932, SHEINBERG and I met KUKLIN, and then SHEINBERG told me once again: “Stop meeting them. Isn't he carrying on some sort of work against the party?”

At SHEINBERG's home at various times, I met three or four of his colleagues from the Vasileostrovsky dist\<rict\>. Of these I remember SOKOLOV Aleksei, who works somewhere as a factory director (he is a thoroughly sick man). Some of my conversations with SHEINBERG also took place in his presence; he showed greater interest than SHEINBERG in questions concerning the former “leaders” of the opposition: how they lived, who worked where. But I heard no anti-party talk from SOKOLOV, just as I heard none from SHEINBERG. SOKOLOV visited me in Moscow two or three times.

In 1931, I called once on REPNIKOV Pyotr Mikhailovich; there were no anti-party conversations between us. In 1929–1930, while living in Moscow, REPNIKOV visited GERTIK's apartment; I do not remember him being active there.

In 1932, I saw ABRAMSON in Sestroretsk; there were no political, anti-party conversations between us.

At various times, no later than 1932, SOLOVYOV called on me two or three times; he also visited someone besides me, probably KUKLIN. With SOLOVYOV, there were conversations about the advisability of employing ZINOVIEV and other\<s\> in major political work. On one of my visits to Leningrad, I think in 1930 or 1931, SHEINBERG and I visited SHURYGIN during his illness.

QUESTION: Did you have a conversation with EVDOKIMOV concerning the proposal that you leave Moscow to work in the provinces?

ANSWER: Yes, I did. In the second half of 1932, I was summoned to the CC and offered a post as assist\<ant\> prosecutor in Yakutsk. I stated at the CC and then to KRYLENKO that this appointment was prompted by no professional considerations whatsoever, but constituted entirely unjustified political distrust.

That same day, I called on EVDOKIMOV at the “Paris” hot\<el\> and told him of the proposal made by the CC. EVDOKIMOV showed clear interest in this matter, asked how and what I had been told at the CC, and then advised me to object to the transfer (this also matched my own sentiments) and asked me to tell him how the matter ended. —

SAKHOV.

INTERROGATED BY: KORKIN.
