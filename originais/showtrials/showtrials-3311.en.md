INTERROGATION RECORD

of SHIRYAEV, Dmitry Nikitovich,

dated December 18, 1934.

Conducted by the authorized officer of the SPO of the GUGB — ZAIKO

QUESTION: Describe the nature of your connection with EVDOKIMOV, a member of the Moscow Center of the Zinovievists' counterrevolutionary organization.

ANSWER: At the first interrogation, on December 15, 1934, I already testified that EVDOKIMOV and BAKAEV exerted considerable influence on the formation of my views hostile to the Party and on drawing me into active factional work. As a result of this influence, I found myself among the participants in an illegal counterrevolutionary organization.

After submitting the declaration renouncing factional struggle in 1928, throughout the subsequent period I was closely connected with EVDOKIMOV, and naturally his anti-Party influence and systematic efforts to instill hatred and discontent toward Party policy and the leadership of the VKP(b) Central Committee consolidated my anti-Party views and pushed me into the hostile counterrevolutionary camp. Throughout all these recent years, EVDOKIMOV deliberately raised political topics in our meetings, developing plainly anti-Party, counterrevolutionary views. Thus, during collectivization, in assessing the situation EVDOKIMOV characterized the policy of the VKP(b) Central Committee as halfhearted, a policy, in his words, of “now forward, now backward”, declaring that it would lead to a catastrophic situation in the country. When discussing matters connected with industry, EVDOKIMOV, speaking of shortcomings in construction, approached criticism of these shortcomings not as errors and failures of particular individuals or organizations, but always portrayed and emphasized them as the result of incorrect leadership by the Central Committee: “they do not know how to do things properly”, and so forth.

In conversations about the international revolutionary movement, EVDOKIMOV always pointed out that the VKP(b) Central Committee paid little attention to this question, that questions of leading the international movement and of Comintern work were neglected, assessing this as a conscious policy of the Central Committee. In essence, this criticism continued the Zinovievist opposition's old theory about the impossibility of building socialism in the USSR.

In 1932 in Moscow, at EVDOKIMOV's apartment, we had a conversation about a document of the People's Commissariat of Foreign Affairs concerning “USSR–Japan–China” relations (I find it difficult to say exactly which document was involved). EVDOKIMOV sharply criticized Central Committee policy and said: “How can one confine oneself to a single NKID letter in such matters? A very broad campaign should have been launched everywhere in the USSR: at rallies, at workers' meetings.” When I tried to object that in such cases one must not forget the other, negative side of mass discussion of such questions, taking diplomatic considerations into account, as well as the USSR's increased role as a state throughout the world, EVDOKIMOV said I was already “spoiled” by Central Committee policy and was not reasoning in a Leninist way. In the end I agreed with his arguments.

QUESTION: What did EVDOKIMOV tell you about his connection with ZINOVIEV?

ANSWER: As I already testified in the interrogation record of December 16 of this year, EVDOKIMOV outwardly kept his connection with ZINOVIEV secret. In addition to my testimony about the incident involving KOSTINA, when she openly spoke in defense of ZINOVIEV at the factory Party meeting and received a reprimand from EVDOKIMOV for it, I must also report the following to the investigation: at the purge in 1933, EVDOKIMOV subjected the policy of visits “for a cup of tea” among former Zinovievists to “criticism”. He told me that from this standpoint he had condemned his meetings over tea with ZINOVIEV during the purge. Later, however, in 1934 (in March, I think), he gave me detailed information about ZINOVIEV, saying that ZINOVIEV was working at “Bolshevik” and that apparently nothing would come of it because they would not let him work there. He told me such a detail as that ZINOVIEV's telephone had been removed under the pretext of repairs and was not being reinstalled. EVDOKIMOV conveyed all this in a tone of displeasure with the Central Committee's treatment of ZINOVIEV. EVDOKIMOV's account clearly revealed his meetings with ZINOVIEV. During that same visit of mine to Moscow, EVDOKIMOV recommended that I read a preface written by ZINOVIEV for a short book about war (I do not now remember which book contained this preface), saying that the preface was wonderfully written, that such things should be published in large print runs and not only in Russian, but that here, of course, this would not be done because ZINOVIEV was sidelined, and so forth.

QUESTION: At the previous interrogation, on December 16 of this year, you testified that within the counterrevolutionary organization in Leningrad you were directly connected with MYASNIKOV, Nikolai Petrovich. Describe the nature of your connection in greater detail.

ANSWER: Through me MYASNIKOV maintained contact with the Moscow Center. As with EVDOKIMOV, at every meeting we constantly discussed questions of Party policy; MYASNIKOV entirely shared EVDOKIMOV's assessment of the situation in the country, accusing the Central Committee of incorrect policy. At the same time, MYASNIKOV instructed me that at all open meetings one should speak in a spirit of approval and agreement with the Central Committee's line, since we Zinovievists, and he in particular, were under constant suspicion.

QUESTION: What assignments did you receive from EVDOKIMOV and MYASNIKOV?

ANSWER: My duties mainly consisted of keeping the Leningrad and Moscow people mutually informed (through EVDOKIMOV and MYASNIKOV) about the activities of the “leaders”, ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV, about the Leningrad people's state of mind, where everyone was, and so forth. There were individual assignments to find employment, or rather to bring Zinovievists from the provinces to the center. In the autumn of 1932, on EVDOKIMOV's instructions, I specifically contacted ZALUTSKY about transferring the Zinovievist SHADRIN from Kuznetskstroy to work in Moscow.

I have read the record; recorded correctly from my words.

SHIRYAEV.

INTERROGATED BY:

AUTHORIZED OFFICER OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD — ZAIKO
