INTERROGATION RECORD

OF NIKOLAEV L. V., 14 December 1934.

Question: Under what circumstances did you join the Zinovievist organization, when did this happen, and by whom were you recruited?

Answer: From spring 1934 serious fluctuations began in my political outlook. Assessing the country's internal situation and the difficulties of construction, I gradually slid toward the positions of counterrevolutionary Zinovievist criticism; this concerned assessments of the countryside, the food question, the progress of the country's industrialization, and all other questions of internal conditions on which the Zinovievists held sufficiently well-known distinctive views, formulated in various documents of the former opposition.

I sought support for these sentiments in the outlook of my old comrades from Komsomol work; I soon established that my sentiments were shared by a number of comrades whom I knew as former oppositionists, some of whom had returned to the party. In spring 1934 I met SHATSKY at the ISTMOL premises at 59 Moika. I found in SHATSKY great bitterness against the party leaders; this conversation gave considerable impetus to the development of my sentiments.

ISTPART, where I worked until April 1934, occupied the same building as ISTMOL and the “Bureau of the Komsomol Combat Fraternity.” The latter was headed by KHANIK Lev and POPOV Georgy, in whom I had already observed views and sentiments openly hostile to the party before joining the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization. I observed the same sentiments among ISTMOL's leaders. It gradually became clear to me that anti-party and counterrevolutionary elements were grouping around both organizations and that the organizations themselves were under the undivided influence of the Zinovievists.

The existence of such surroundings sustained and intensified counterrevolutionary sentiments in me.

These sentiments created readiness for decisive action. I saw that the leading figures of the former opposition — ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV and others — had been pushed out of the leadership, and found in this an explanation for the decline of the national economy. I gradually absorbed the idea that a solution could be found in the departure of the present leading figures of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), to be brought about by all available means, and in the return of ZINOVIEV and his group to the leadership.

In August 1934 I met SHATSKY, who knew about my vacillations. Having established that I was sufficiently prepared to undertake counterrevolutionary work, SHATSKY took me into his confidence and said that a counterrevolutionary organization of Zinovievists existed in Leningrad, to which he himself, KOTOLYNOV, and several others whom I knew from Komsomol work belonged. Continuing the conversation, SHATSKY stated that the organization favored the forcible removal of STALIN and of KIROV as the fighting leader of the Leningrad party organization who had directly organized resistance to the former opposition in the place where it considered itself sufficiently strong.

SHATSKY recruited me into the organization and to participate in the terrorist act against KIROV, stating that he and KOTOLYNOV were authorized on the organization's behalf to carry out the terrorist act.

Question: Did you meet during this period with YUSKIN, a member of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization?

Answer: Yes. I am on close friendly terms with him, and in one conversation he told me of his membership in the organization. YUSKIN told me that the time had already come for the organization to begin acting actively and that a terrorist act against KIROV must be carried out.

Question: What conversations did you have during this same period with SOKOLOV Georgy, a member of the counterrevolutionary organization?

Answer: SOKOLOV G. said that KOTOLYNOV was in the organization's leadership in Leningrad, that the idea of the need to commit a terrorist act against KIROV had long been maturing in the organization, and that it must be carried out that winter, which, according to him, would be a very difficult period for the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks).

Question: How did you establish contact with KOTOLYNOV, a member of the counterrevolutionary organization?

Answer: KOTOLYNOV knew from SHATSKY, YUSKIN and SOKOLOV that I was already effectively an organization member. Visiting him at the Institute, I observed former oppositionists gathering around KOTOLYNOV who, as he confirmed to me, belonged to the counterrevolutionary organization. KOTOLYNOV said that the organization had two terrorist groups, named their members and stated that he headed one group and SHATSKY the other.

KOTOLYNOV confirmed SHATSKY's statement that I must be involved in carrying out the terrorist act against KIROV, worked through its technical execution with me, and assigned SOKOLOV to assist me in preparing it.

L. NIKOLAEV.

DEPUTY PEOPLE'S COMMISSAR OF INTERNAL AFFAIRS OF THE USSR — AGRANOV
