INTERROGATION RECORD

Of Petr Petrovich SEVEROV, dated 25/XII/1934.

(supplementary).

Question: What can you testify about Georgy MIRONOV's counterrevolutionary activities?

Answer: As early as the period of the Fourteenth Congress, MIRONOV was one of the leaders of the factional struggle against the party in the Petrograd district. He initiated illegal meetings, printing of illegal documents and so forth. He showed particular energy in counterrevolutionary indoctrination of young people and drawing them into struggle against the party. As one of the opposition's most prominent activists, he was directly connected with the then oppositionist secretary of the District Committee, FLIOR. After we were transferred out of Leningrad for opposition work, MIRONOV and I ended up in Pskov. This was in February 1926. There MIRONOV continued factional work with renewed energy. Together with MATORIN [1], he assembled a group of Zinovievists and became one of the leaders of the Pskov Zinovievist underground. This group included myself, SEVEROV, David PEKAR, ZVEZDOV, MATORIN and a number of others. Here too MIRONOV distinguished himself by his irreconcilability. I remember this incident: learning that I had begun to waver, MIRONOV traveled specially to Kholm (where I then was) to influence me to overcome these hesitations. Throughout his stay

in Pskov, MIRONOV never ceased factional work. MIRONOV returned to Leningrad, in 1928, I believe, and began renewing his old opposition connections. Even before moving permanently to Leningrad, MIRONOV began sounding out connections on visits from Pskov. Among others, he renewed contact with me. In conversations dating to this period, MIRONOV explained the need to return to the party in a somewhat veiled manner, citing the need to preserve his cadres from ideological and organizational disarray. Later, I believe, he maintained connections with M. NATANSON [2], with the active oppositionist TSUNSKY and others whose surnames I do not remember. I met MIRONOV again in 1930. He asked me a series of questions about my work, personal life and mood; generally, I gained the impression that he was sounding me out.

Question: The investigation possesses information that at one meeting of members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization, the question of ZINOVIEV's removal from the editorial board of “Bolshevik” was discussed. Do you confirm this?

Answer: Yes, I confirm it. This was in the autumn of 1934; organization members were present at my apartment: myself, SEVEROV, Georgy POPOV and ANTONOV. During the conversation I raised the question of ZINOVIEV's exclusion from the editorial board of “Bolshevik.” We assessed this fact as a continuation of the party leadership's old course of cutting ZINOVIEV off from leadership and removing him from any work whatsoever.

Correctly recorded from my words.

P. SEVEROV.
