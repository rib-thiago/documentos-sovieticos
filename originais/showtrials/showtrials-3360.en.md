INTERROGATION RECORD

Conducted on December 20, 1934, by the Head of the 1st Section of the SPO,

LULOV

VINOGRADOV, Vasily Alekseevich (supplementary).

Question: What further testimony can you give about the practical work of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization to which you belonged?

Answer: In my testimony of December 12, 13 and 14 I already testified about the organization's membership, its structure and political directives. All our practical work was subordinated to the main task: replacing the Party leadership headed by Comrade STALIN. Having suffered defeat during the Fourteenth Congress and subsequent years, when we acted openly, we returned to the Party with duplicitous aims in order to adapt our work to the changed situation. From the tactical and organizational standpoint this changed situation required: first and foremost preserving ourselves at all costs within the Party as an independent force; working in practice to preserve and restore, wherever they had been broken, connections between participants in the Zinovievist opposition; maneuvering in every way around circumstances that might lead to the slightest dispersal of cadres. Precisely such a maneuver was the vote by many of us to expel ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV from the Party in connection with the Ryutin case. The slogan “join the Party and work honestly,” which circulated among us during the period of declaratory renunciations, was also a duplicitous tactical step and essentially meant for each of us the directive: take part in the practical work of the Party, Soviet and economic apparatus in order to smuggle in our directives, to preserve ourselves within the Party as an independent force capable of acting as soon as even a somewhat favorable domestic or external situation arose. It was toward this situation that we steered our course. We proceeded from the assumption that war with the imperialist states, which would come sooner or later, and the situation within the country engendered by war would create favorable conditions for broad actions demanding the return to leadership of Zinoviev, Kamenev and other leaders of Zinovievism. Thus our Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization took distinctly defeatist positions in this respect. Meanwhile, in the hope of this situation, in the course of our meetings and conversations and our practical work to preserve and rally Zinovievist cadres, we shaped, particularly among the Zinovievist youth, such attitudes toward the Party leaders as ultimately led to NIKOLAEV's shot. Every even somewhat significant event in the Party's internal Party, domestic or foreign policy was a subject of discussion within our organization and was commented on in the spirit of Zinovievist directives, subjected to a counterrevolutionary assessment. The systematic cultivation of hatred toward the Party leadership and particularly Comrade Stalin constituted the essence of our activities. We particularly emphasized that STALIN dealt harshly with former oppositionists, that STALIN had abolished internal Party democracy and replaced it with the undivided domination of the apparatus, that the old Bolshevik cadres had been removed from the Party leadership. We bitterly noted that because of STALIN, MOLOTOV, KAGANOVICH and KIROV, ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV had been cut off from leadership; we set them against STALIN and described them as LENIN's closest pupils; that EVDOKIMOV, ZALUTSKY, BAKAEV and others had been removed from leadership. On this basis Comrade KIROV's murderer NIKOLAEV grew up and was educated, connected with the members of our Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization through a joint struggle against the Party and its leaders over a number of years. I testified about the individual membership of our counterrevolutionary organization in the record of my interrogation of December 14. —

VINOGRADOV.

Interrogated by: LULOV.
