INTERROGATION RECORD

OF NIKOLAEV, L. V.

8/XII/1934.

QUESTION: How did you prepare the terrorist act against Comrade KIROV?

ANSWER: With information about KIROV's habits obtained from Regional Committee employees, I decided first of all to attack KIROV near his apartment at 28 Krasnykh Zor Street. It was not difficult to discover KIROV's home address at the Regional Committee, since all the employees knew it. Admittedly, I initially made a mistake and began watching the apartments of CHUDOV and KODATSKY [1], which are in the same building but face another street. I intended to murder KIROV with a Nagant revolver which I had possessed since 1918, when I received it from a worker who was a Red Guardsman.

QUESTION: Did you have a permit to carry and keep the Nagant?

ANSWER: No; the permit I had possessed several years earlier expired in 1932, and I have not renewed it since then.

QUESTION: How many cartridges for the Nagant did you have on 1 December?

ANSWER: I had 25 cartridges altogether, 7 in the revolver's cylinder, with the remainder at home.

QUESTION: Where did you obtain these cartridges?

ANSWER: I obtained the cartridges in 1932, purchasing them at the “Dynamo” shop upon presenting my permit to carry the revolver.

QUESTION: Had you used the Nagant during the whole period you possessed it?

ANSWER: Yes, I fired at practice targets outside the city. In July 1934, having decided to kill KIROV, I decided to check the revolver's serviceability, for which purpose I went to Shuvalovo near Leningrad and test-fired it there. I established that the Nagant was in perfect working order.

QUESTION: How did your preparations for the act proceed thereafter?

ANSWER: In August 1934 I began preparing the act more energetically. For several days running I did not encounter KIROV and, deciding that he had gone out of town, went to the Regional Committee to check my assumption. There I met the woman who distributes newspapers and mail, about whom I wrote in today's record; she told me that KIROV was on holiday and would be in Leningrad only in two weeks. During September and October I encountered KIROV repeatedly, postponing the moment of attack for a time. At one point I selected as the place of attack the tram stop nearest KIROV's apartment, where cars usually have to slow down; I calculated that shooting KIROV in public would excite the crowd and lead to anti-Soviet disturbances, which in turn might develop into a serious counter-revolutionary outbreak.

Besides watching the apartment, I spent some time watching the Leningrad Soviet's garage on Kalyaev Street, where the Regional Committee's cars and KIROV's personal cars were kept. I observed whether KIROV's cars were always called at the same time and what route they took — I knew the cars' numbers.

Finally, I watched the arrival of KIROV's cars at Smolny, establishing the time of arrival and the frequency of their departures and arrivals.

All these observations, conducted in various directions, accumulated comprehensive information about KIROV's movements in my hands.

QUESTION: How were you detained by the NKVD authorities on 15 October 1934?

ANSWER: A city Party meeting was to take place on 15 October. I came to Smolny an hour before the meeting began because I had failed to obtain a ticket beforehand and hoped to obtain one immediately before the meeting. I do not remember from whom I learned that KIROV and CHUDOV would not be at the meeting — Ugarov was conducting it — and that they had just set off on foot for home on Krasnykh Zor Street. I thought I should use the favourable opportunity for an attack and followed them. I followed KIROV and CHUDOV right to building No. 28 without firing; I refrained from attacking because I thought I would have to shoot both of them, which was not in my plans. At that moment I was arrested by KIROV's guards, who found my behaviour suspicious. I was driven to the building of the NKVD Directorate for Leningrad Region and brought before the officer on duty.

QUESTION: Do you know the duty officer's surname?

ANSWER: No, I do not.

QUESTION: At the moment of arrest, did you have the Nagant and anti-Soviet notes on you?

ANSWER: Yes, I had both the Nagant and the anti-Soviet notes. Everything was in the briefcase I carried.

QUESTION: Were you searched when detained and upon being brought to the NKVD Directorate building?

ANSWER: No; at the NKVD Directorate building the duty officer asked whether I had a weapon and whether I had been searched. I answered both questions in the negative, yet I was not searched and was released.

Correctly taken down from my words. I have read the record.

L. NIKOLAEV

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE EKO, GUGB, NKVD OF THE USSR — MIRONOV

ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE EKO, GUGB, NKVD OF THE USSR — DMITRIEV
