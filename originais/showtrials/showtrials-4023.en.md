INTERROGATION RECORD

OF SAFAROV G.I., January 3, 1935.

In response to the question put to me, I testify:

SOKOLOV—an active Zinovievist who in 1925–1927 was one of the principal leaders of the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc. He preserved his Zinovievist views and sympathies, as well as his connections. V. RUMYANTSEV said in Moscow in 1931 that he had seen him “in a prosperous condition.” Until recently, he maintained contact with other Zinovievists from Vasileostrovsky, in particular SHENBERG, SAKHOV, SHAKHNOVICH and Moisei YAKOVLEV. SEMYON SEMYONOV and NIKOLAYEV—both from the main group of Vasileostrovsky Zinovievists—not only took the most active part in the struggle against the party in 1925–1927 but afterward continued counterrevolutionary work under cover of their party cards. Both are well known to Moisei YAKOVLEV and were connected with the deceased SHENBERG.

ABRAMSON was an active organizer of the Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc in the Vasileostrovsky district. In 1928–1929, he was in Saratov, sinking into complete philistinism and seeking to “withdraw from politics.” He later returned to Leningrad, using old acquaintances. SAKHOV boasted that he had helped ABRAMSON “extricate himself” from a dirty affair involving abuse of official position in the Housing Department headed by ABRAMSON. I assume that he reestablished his Zinovievist connections; in any case, he contacted SAKHOV, who supplied Zinovievist “information,” primarily to the Vasileostrovsky Zinovievists. ABRAMSON is an extremely weak-charactered man who considers himself “wronged” and on this basis turned to seeking “old friends.”

REPNIKOV—one of the principal organizers of the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc in the former Narva district, who later studied at the Transport Academy in Leningrad. I know that he constantly maintained a close friendship with Nikolai DMITRIYEV, who returned to Leningrad and is a very active Zinovievist. The continuation of this “friendship” would have been impossible had REPNIKOV not held the same counterrevolutionary views as DMITRIYEV.

REM—a leader and organizer of the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc in the former city district. In the summer of 1931 in Kislovodsk, KORSHUNOV spoke of his continued “friendship” with him; KORSHUNOV, in turn, maintained contact with V. LEVIN. I know from the past that REM had connections with participants in the Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc among male and female textile workers. REM was distinguished by great “devotion” to ZINOVIEV. I assume that through their old work together he continued to maintain contact with Artyom GERTIK and Yakov SHAROV.

Written in my own hand—G. SAFAROV.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR: PETROVSKY
