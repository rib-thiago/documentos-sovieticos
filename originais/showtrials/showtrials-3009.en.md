INTERROGATION RECORD

OF RUMYANTSEV Vladimir Vasilyevich, 13 December 1934.

Question: Tell us what you know about the existence of a counterrevolutionary Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization.

Answer: The party's defeat of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc did not, however, eliminate the independent existence of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization. From the moment of our return to the ranks of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), we were essentially carrying out ZINOVIEV's directive to deceive the party, given in his letter of 30 June 1928 found during the search of my premises. In particular, when we — I, KOTOLYNOV and TARASOV I. — spoke with Comrade YAROSLAVSKY in summer 1928 about returning to the party, we were simultaneously in contact with KAMENEV, who directed our actions and edited our statement. Gradually, as Zinovievists returned to Leningrad and the “leaders” to Moscow, organizational connections were restored, and after a while the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization was reestablished.

Question: How was the counterrevolutionary organization to which you belonged structured, and who belonged to it in Leningrad?

Answer: The counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization in Leningrad split into separate groups connected to the leading center through individual active organizers. Among the separate groups known to me I can name: a) The group headed by LEVIN Vladimir — chairman of Lenzhilsoyuz — around whom former members of the “83” group are concentrated. From this group I know: MYASNIKOV — deputy head of the Organization Department of the Leningrad Soviet; SOSITSKY — director of the automobile repair plant in the Petrograd District; KOSTRITSKY — director of some economic organization in the Narva District; LEVIN Mikhail — I do not know where he works, apparently teaches somewhere; MOROZOV Aleksandr — director of the Leningrad Soviet's funeral trust; BOGOMOLNY — works in GOROGO; ANISHEV — works in some institute; BOGRACHEV — deputy chairman of the Leningrad Soviet, now working somewhere as a factory director; ALEKSANDROV Aleksandr — deputy director of the leather trust; SEREDOKHIN Aleksandr — manager of the Oblryba trust; TARASOV Pyotr — now departed for the Kuzbass; NALIVAIKO Foma — works as deputy manager of Brodtrest; MATVEEV Vladimir — manager of the Leningrad branch of Soyuzfoto.

b) The group at the Industrial Institute headed by KOTOLYNOV I., from which I know ANTONOV Nikolai, NADEL Mikhail, ZVEZDOV Vasily, SUROV Pavel (currently in exile), TSUNSKY Konstantin — after graduating from the institute he left for some power station in the Urals — and PANFILOV Mikhail — left for somewhere.

c) The “Combat Fraternity” group at Istomol, headed by KHANIK Lev — instructor of the Kronstadt District Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) — consisting of BURMISTROV Pavel — former anarchist, director of the Intermediary Bureau of the Leningrad Soviet's Housing Department; FADEEV Pyotr \[i\] — chairman of the Road Construction Workers' Union; POPOV Georgy — director of the Uritsky tobacco factory; TSARKOV Nikolai — formerly employed at the aluminum combine; TOLMAZOV Andrei — assistant director of the Putilov Works for the ORS; EGOROV Sergei — assistant director of the VARZ factory-kitchen.

I shall name the remaining participants subsequently.

d) The group of former “leaderless” oppositionists headed by MANDELSHTAM Sergei, consisting of me — RUMYANTSEV V.; MIKHAILOV Mikhail — recently deceased, latterly a pensioner; TARTAKOVSKAYA — an engineer at the Northern Shipyard; TIMOFEEV Georgy — her husband, former agitation and propaganda official at the Krasny Vyborzhets factory; two former students of the Tolmachev Military-Political Academy whose surnames I do not remember, one of whom works at the Baltic Shipyard. MANDELSHTAM recently told me that he maintains contact with these two former students of the Tolmachev Academy.

The organization used members working in various party and Soviet bodies in its own interests. In particular, I — RUMYANTSEV — rendered various services to the counterrevolutionary organization, using my official position as secretary of the Vyborg District Soviet. I found jobs for ANTONOV Nikolai and BAKAEV's daughter; on a note from the latter I tried to find a job for TSARKOV Nikolai, but the Vyborg District Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) prevented this; at various times I provided financial assistance to organization members KOTOLYNOV, TSARKOV Nikolai and ANTONOV Nikolai from the funds of the May Day or October commissions, and to BURMISTROV Pavel and EGOROV Sergei from the partisan commission's funds; I obtained a resort voucher for ANTONOV's mother and arranged for KOTOLYNOV to use the party District Committee's dining hall. I do not remember other organization members to whom I provided material assistance, nor the amounts of financial aid.

Question: Who belonged to the center of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization in Leningrad?

Answer: LEVIN Vladimir, I — RUMYANTSEV —, MANDELSHTAM Sergei, KOTOLYNOV Ivan, MYASNIKOV Nikolai and SOSITSKY Lev belonged to the center of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization in Leningrad.

For reasons of secrecy, center members very rarely gathered together; contact was normally maintained personally between individual center members.

Question: What do you know about the existence of a center of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization in Moscow, and who belongs to it?

Answer: The center of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization in Moscow includes ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, KUKLIN, GERTIK and SHAROV Yakov.

Question: Was ZALUTSKY Pyotr a member of the counterrevolutionary organization's center?

Answer: In autumn 1934 KUKLIN came to Leningrad and personally invited me to the apartment of LEBEDEV Konstantin, who is married to KUKLIN's sister and lives at 10 Chaikovskogo Street. I arrived in the evening after work and found SOLOVYOV Konstantin, manager of Tochmashtrest, LEBEDEV K., KUKLIN and one other person whose surname I do not know, but whom I knew as a Zinovievist. KUKLIN then told me that the mood of the “leaders,” that is, ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV and the other center members, was firm and that they were determined to fight the present party leadership. KUKLIN also told me that for reasons of secrecy he rarely saw ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV, while EVDOKIMOV and BAKAEV maintained contact with them. When I asked about ZALUTSKY Pyotr, KUKLIN told me that ZALUTSKY's former disagreements with ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV had been eliminated and that he was again connected with the center and personally with ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV. KUKLIN told that me, that he would soon come or possibly move permanently to Leningrad. I believe this visit to Leningrad was intended to strengthen the leadership of the counterrevolutionary organization. I presume that KUKLIN met many members of the organization during this visit, although he did not tell me so. I infer this from such a fact as his meeting with MYASNIKOV, about which the latter told me personally.

Question: Name the members of the counterrevolutionary organization who are directly connected with or close to its center in Moscow or to individual center members.

Answer: Among members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization who are in Moscow, NAUMOV Ivan, ZORIN Sergei, GESSEN Sergei, FEDOROV Grigory, TARASOV Ivan, KHARITONOV and FAIVILOVICH Leonid are connected with the center.

Question: Explain how and through whom contact was maintained between the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization in Leningrad and the center in Moscow.

Answer: Our Leningrad counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization maintained direct contact with the center in Moscow through periodic journeys by individual center members or trusted persons from both the Leningrad and Moscow centers. The main contact with the Moscow center was concentrated in the hands of LEVIN Vladimir. LEVIN periodically traveled to Moscow, where he contacted GERTIK Artyom, whose home BAKAEV visited. LEVIN was also connected with EVDOKIMOV. The strength and frequency of LEVIN's contact with the center can be judged from the fact that he traveled to Moscow almost every month. On the youth side of the organization, KOTOLYNOV maintained contact with the Moscow center through TARASOV Ivan. KOTOLYNOV traveled to Moscow in December 1933, where he met TARASOV I. and FAIVILOVICH. In summer 1934 TARASOV I. came to Leningrad and contacted KOTOLYNOV. I know that among the Moscow center members, GERTIK was directly connected with TARASOV I., and KHARITONOV with FAIVILOVICH (when he was in the USSR). In 1933 and 1934 MYASNIKOV traveled to Moscow and contacted GERTIK; in 1933–1934 SEREDOKHIN and TOLMAZOV traveled to Moscow and contacted KUKLIN and FAIVILOVICH. They also contacted the prominent Zinovievist MINAEV, a former KSM Central Committee official. In summer 1934 TOLMAZOV told me of his conversation with FAIVILOVICH, who said that ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV and all center members were in a fighting mood, determined to struggle against the present party leadership and particularly hostile toward Comrade STALIN. From spring 1934 visits to Leningrad by representatives of the Moscow center increased; thus KUKLIN came in spring and autumn 1934, and I described the nature of my conversation with him above; after KUKLIN, FEDOROV Grisha came in autumn 1934 and tried to contact me, calling on me at the District Soviet, but could not talk to me because outsiders were present. I presume he contacted somebody else, since he left without seeing me. In summer 1934 GESSEN came to Leningrad, stayed with MUSHTAKOV and contacted organization members; he was also supposed to contact me but did not. MUSHTAKOV later told me that he had very much wanted to see me and had telephoned me at the Soviet several times, but since he did not give his name he apparently failed to establish contact. I remember that at the end of 1932 ROTSKAN came from Moscow and contacted LEVIN V. I must add that in 1933–1934 MYASNIKOV Nikolai also traveled to Moscow and contacted GERTIK on LEVIN V.'s instructions. After his trip in 1933 MYASNIKOV told me of GERTIK's arrest. Some twenty days before my arrest I met LEVIN V. at Smolny; he told me that GERTIK had been in Leningrad a few days earlier and had brought news, and that he, LEVIN, would contact me specially and pass it on. I could not speak with LEVIN in detail at Smolny because of the circumstances. In 1932 ZALUTSKY Pyotr also came from Moscow and tried to contact me by telephone, but confused me with my namesake, a tobacco factory director, and evidently for that reason our meeting fell through.

Question: Explain what directives the counterrevolutionary organization's center issued from Moscow through representatives of the Leningrad organization and the Moscow center.

Answer: As I have already testified, contact with the center was concentrated mainly in the hands of LEVIN V., who was the center's representative for the Leningrad counterrevolutionary organization. LEVIN received instructions for the organization's work and then passed them on in his own name. From conversations with LEVIN and my occasional contacts with the center's Moscow representatives (KUKLIN), it is quite clear that the counterrevolutionary organization's center in Moscow sounded out the mood in Leningrad, collected information on conditions at enterprises, gave organizational and political directives, and outlined the tactics the organization was to follow. I have already cited in my testimony individual instances of directives being transmitted from Moscow to the Leningrad organization through KUKLIN, GERTIK and others. I can cite several more facts: when ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV were expelled from the party at the end of 1932, our youth in the organization were in an extremely aggressive mood. LEVIN V. conveyed the Moscow center's directive to vote at party organization meetings in such a way as to preserve the organization's cadres from being smashed; at the end of 1933, after the funeral of the Zinovievist SHURYGIN, a clandestine meeting took place at SOSITSKY Lev's apartment on the Obvodny Canal, near the slaughterhouse, attended by me, MANDELSHTAM Sergei, LEVIN V. and SOSITSKY. At this meeting LEVIN conveyed Moscow's instruction that we must preserve our forces. LEVIN stated that the “leaders” — ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV — were irreconcilably opposed to the party leadership, that STALIN's remaining at the head of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) was absolutely intolerable (LEVIN expressed himself maliciously about him), and that the need to overthrow this party leadership with STALIN was clear. However, ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV did not yet have any definite political platform, since the old platform required amendments owing to various changes in the situation; therefore, LEVIN said, there was an instruction to follow waiting tactics and preserve our forces until a favorable moment. LEVIN concluded that the time would soon come when ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV would renew the struggle against the party leadership.

After returning from Moscow in January 1934, KOTOLYNOV I. told me about his meeting with FAIVILOVICH and TARASOV I., during which they told him, KOTOLYNOV, that ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV were irreconcilably hostile toward Comrade Stalin and the other Politburo members, that sooner or later this party leadership would have to be overthrown, and that forces must be gathered and preparations made to begin the struggle at a favorable moment. I must also testify about a meeting with KAMENEV in 1929 at the European Hotel, which clearly shows that this hatred of Comrade Stalin had long been systematically implanted in the consciousness of the counterrevolutionary organization's members and came directly from its center. In 1929, when KAMENEV came to Leningrad, MOROZOV Aleksandr, SAFRONOV Pyotr — works at Magnitostroi —, I, Foma NALIVAIKO and, I believe, MANDELSHTAM gathered in his hotel room. We all expressed indignation at ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV's renunciation of their views and declared that we must fight the party leadership. KAMENEV said that “we must submit, grit our teeth and keep in touch with one another along a chain.” KAMENEV added: “Do not think that we love Stalin more than you do.”

Question: Can you provide the investigation with further facts about the counterrevolutionary organization's connections with the center in Moscow?

Answer: In autumn 1933, during SEREDOKHIN and TOLMAZOV's trip to Moscow, about which I testified above, they also met SAFAROV. TOLMAZOV, who told me about the trip, said that SAFAROV informed them in detail about the situation in the Comintern and discussed international and domestic policy with them. TOLMAZOV said that SAFAROV was dissatisfied with the party leadership's attitude toward him and complained that he was not trusted or given work that would satisfy him. At the end of 1933 I had a conversation with MATVEEV Vladimir, who is directly connected with SAFAROV. He displayed a malicious attitude toward Comrade Stalin and told me that Comrade Stalin was intolerable at the party's head, and that the best people in the party, such as ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV and SAFAROV, had been removed from its leadership. I believe MATVEEV reflected SAFAROV's sentiments.

Question: Was the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization connected with the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist organization, and through whom?

Answer: I know from LEVIN Vladimir that the Zinovievist organization is connected with the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist organization. In particular, when NATANSON Maria was arrested, LEVIN V. told me that contacts with the Trotskyists went through her. I believe that contacts with the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist organization are now concentrated in the hands of LEVIN Mikhail, who also maintained contact with the Trotskyists during the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc in 1927.

Question: In your testimony you note manifestations of the most malicious counterrevolutionary attitude among organization members toward the leadership of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) and Comrade Stalin. Explain how these sentiments developed.

Answer: A number of facts cited in my testimony show that the most malicious, most sharply hostile sentiments toward the leadership of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), and especially toward Comrade Stalin, were cultivated in the counterrevolutionary organization. These sentiments did not so much arise among ordinary members as they were inflamed from above and came directly from the Moscow center. In such a heated atmosphere of intense hatred toward the party leadership and especially Comrade Stalin, sentiments of a terrorist character could not fail to be fostered.

Question: What can you testify concerning the terrorist act against Comrade KIROV committed by NIKOLAEV L., a member of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization?

Answer: Our counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization, every one of its members, and I in particular bear political and moral responsibility for Comrade KIROV's murder committed by NIKOLAEV. As I have already testified, the atmosphere of malicious hatred toward the leaders of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) prevailing in our counterrevolutionary organization educated NIKOLAEV in the spirit of terror.

I have read the record; it has been correctly taken down from my words:

V. RUMYANTSEV.

Interrogated by: Deputy HEAD OF THE SPO, NKVD OF THE USSR — LYUSHKOV.
