INTERROGATION RECORD

Conducted by the head of the Fourth Section of the SPO of the UGB — L. KOGAN.

Of Maria Vasilyevna POZDEEVA.

24/XII/1934.

Question: Inform the investigation of the facts known to you concerning the counterrevolutionary activities of the Zinovievist organization.

Answer: I have already stated that my principal connections with the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization were through members of the center in Moscow, chiefly GERTIK. I met GERTIK frequently until recently. In one conversation with me, GERTIK instructed me to conceal my affiliation with the Zinovievists from the party. In 1934 I intended to enroll for studies and wrote in my questionnaire about my struggle against the party in 1926–27. GERTIK told me that this should not be written. After GERTIK returned from exile, I asked him to tell me the reasons for his arrest. He told me that his exile was the result of slander by the Trotskyists. In his words: “They accuse me of traveling to Leningrad with directives from the center.” According to him, this was untrue. In the summer of 1934, KUKLIN came to visit my husband, GERTSBERG. My husband was not at home. We talked for several hours. KUKLIN asked me how I had passed the purge, and in doing so expressed a number of counterrevolutionary views. In response to remarks by the commission chairman, KORCHAGIN, which I considered correct, that I had not yet overcome Zinovievism within myself, KUKLIN burst into slanderous attacks on the purge, accusing KORCHAGIN of taking revenge on Leningraders because we Zinovievists had held him back in our time. On the whole, KUKLIN struck me as an embittered philistine. He declared, for example, that he did not want to work, that he lived on his pension, and complained that “they, the bastards, do not give him a responsible official's ration.”

In 1934 I met ZINOVIEV. He said that he worked at “Bolshevik.” When I asked whether he was satisfied with his work, he answered affirmatively. “The journal has a very large circulation. The Black Sea Fleet alone subscribes to 500 copies,” he said. I never understood what he meant by this: whether he was emphasizing what a large platform he had been given or was being ironic about the readers of “Bolshevik.” In any event, this remark of ZINOVIEV's must have had some hidden meaning.

Question: Describe the nature of your connection with SAFAROV.

Answer: In my previous testimony I forgot to name SAFAROV, whom I did indeed meet. SAFAROV first visited our home when we (GERTSBERG and I) returned from abroad in 1932. I told SAFAROV about cases known to me of dissatisfaction within the German Communist Party with its then Central Committee, particularly with Heinz NEUMANN. In connection with my report, SAFAROV criticized the Comintern line. I do not now remember exactly what he said, but the main point was that he disapproved of the Comintern's policy toward the German Communist Party. I also saw SAFAROV in 1934, but on this occasion I had no conversation with him of interest to the investigation.

M. POZDEEVA

INTERROGATED BY L. KOGAN.

Certified true: Churbanova
