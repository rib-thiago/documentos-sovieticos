INTERROGATION RECORD

Of ANISHEV Anatoly Isayevich

January 9, 1935.

On the question put to me by the investigation concerning terrorist tendencies among members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization, I additionally testify:

I have already testified that, from all that work of discrediting and fostering a spirit of hatred toward the Party leaders, particularly Comrade KIROV, it is entirely clear that NIKOLAEV's shot is connected with all our counterrevolutionary work.

This proposition is not merely a general political assessment of the counterrevolutionary essence of our organization. Knowing the situation in the Zinovievist organization, its connections, and so forth, I cannot imagine that the leading officials, particularly those members of the organization's center whom I know well and with whom I personally associated throughout all these years (EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, KUKLIN, GERTIK), did not know that the hatred of the Party leadership cultivated among us nourished terrorist sentiments.

This hatred of the Party leadership flowed from, and was conditioned by, the counterrevolutionary, restorationist political position of our organization. I have already testified that nothing remained of “Left” phrases and sentiments in the organization. A negative attitude toward collectivization was constantly displayed: at the gathering at BAKAEV's in 1933 (present were BAKAEV, KUKLIN, ELKOVICH, GESSEN, myself, and KOSTINA), during GERTIK's and ELKOVICH's visits to Leningrad, and so on. The same applied to the negative attitude toward the rates of industrialization adopted by the Party. It is characteristic that we tacitly agreed not to mention the platform for the Fifteenth Congress, that is, not to criticize it. As I have already testified, the platform itself was a concession to the need to utter “Left” phrases: during its drafting, ZINOVIEV approved an openly pro-kulak version of the chapter on agricultural policy, written by SMILGA and based on the orientation of granting complete freedom to the development of capitalism in the countryside.

The hostility toward the Party leadership that we cultivated was the hostility of a handful of capitalist restorationists toward the Party leadership that was successfully carrying out socialist construction.

This hatred of the Party leadership, which had been nurtured and cultivated among us for ten years, was bound to grow into terrorist sentiments also because it was cultivated in the form of hatred and malice personally directed at the Party leaders, especially Comrade STALIN and Comrade KIROV.

When, at the gathering of members of the organization at BAKAEV's mentioned above, we discussed the program to produce eight million tons of pig iron, this was accompanied by the most malicious and slanderous attacks on Comrade STALIN. Our defeatist assertions, at the same gathering, concerning the impossibility of fulfilling the rates of industrialization adopted by the Party were likewise accompanied by hostile attacks on Comrade STALIN. Repression against individual members of the organization (the arrests of GERTIK and NATANSON, KOSTINA's expulsion from the Party) was received by us — myself, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, KOSTINA, RUMYANTSEV — with especially malicious hatred of Comrade STALIN.

This hatred of Comrade STALIN was constantly fanned and inflamed by BAKAEV, KUKLIN, GERTIK, and other members of the organization. This incitement of counterrevolutionary hatred toward the leadership, especially Comrade STALIN, was transmitted to us in Leningrad as well.

I too brought this back from my trips to Moscow, from my association with members of the organization's center.

And ELKOVICH, who came to Leningrad in early 1933 (or late 1932), undoubtedly as a representative of the center, gave us instructions in connection with the Ryutin–Zinoviev case in a spirit of hostility toward Comrade STALIN, explaining this case as a desire to divert attention from failures on the collectivization front. The assessment of Comrade STALIN given by ELKOVICH and the information he gave us about the situation in the countryside differed in no way from the campaign of vilification against the USSR and Comrade STALIN conducted by the White Guards and fascists. His conclusion that collective farmers would not go to defend the USSR in the event of military clashes, and his attitude toward this, directly recalled the counterrevolutionary thesis concerning CLEMENCEAU.

And GERTIK, during one of his visits to Leningrad to maintain contact with members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization in 1932, also drew a direct counterrevolutionary conclusion regarding Comrade STALIN from information about famine in Ukraine. He generalized this conclusion concerning Comrade STALIN, permeated with bestial hatred, into the general problem of leaders in the Comintern, speaking of the leadership's culpability, especially Comrade STALIN's, for the defeats of fraternal Communist parties. Members of the Zinovievist organization in Leningrad — LEVIN, myself, NATANSON, with whom GERTIK met, MANDELSHTAM, RUMYANTSEV, KOTOLYNOV — all of us fed on this hatred of the Party leaders.

It is difficult for me now to recall a number of additional facts, but what I have testified above, like the entire atmosphere prevailing in our organization, is sufficient to conclude that terrorist sentiments grew precisely from the hatred incited among members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization toward the Party leaders.

Comrade KIROV occupied a special place in our organization as an object of this counterrevolutionary hatred from the very moment of his appointment to Leningrad. A counterrevolutionary lampoon in verse about Comrade KIROV circulated from hand to hand, written, as far as I know, by GESSEN. Comrade KIROV's biography was maliciously distorted. Comrade KIROV was the object of vile slander. Members of the Zinovievist organization depicted Comrade KIROV as a person elevated to leadership by Thermidorian degeneration. The counterrevolutionary assessment of the situation in Leningrad in the most recent period, about which I have already testified, both in my conversation with RUMYANTSEV and in conversations with GERTIK, was also invariably accompanied by hostile, malicious attacks on Comrade KIROV.

Written in my own hand — ANISHEV.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE UGB OF THE NKVD OF THE LENINGRAD REGION — LULOV.

Certified true: A. Svetlova
