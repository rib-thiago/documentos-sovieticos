STENOGRAPHIC TRANSCRIPT

INTERROGATION RECORD

Of KAMENEV, Lev Borisovich,

dated January 10, 1935.

QUESTION: The testimony of EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, SHAROV, FEDOROV, GERTIK and others establishes that the return of the Zinovievists to the party after the Fifteenth Congress of the VKP(b) was duplicitous in character, and that simultaneously with their return to the party an underground organization of Zinovievists was created, whose activities were directed against the existing party leadership and the Soviet government. The above is also confirmed by ZINOVIEV's testimony \[1\].

Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: I do. I gave detailed testimony on this subject on January 9, 1935.

QUESTION: Do you acknowledge that the organization of Zinovievists created after the Fifteenth Congress of the VKP(b) is counterrevolutionary?

ANSWER: Yes, I do; I gave detailed testimony on this question on January 9, 1935.

QUESTION: From the testimony of a number of the persons listed above, the investigation has established that after the Fifteenth Congress of the VKP(b) the counterrevolutionary organization of Zinovievists was directed by a Moscow center of which you were also a member.

Do you confirm the existence of the Moscow center and your membership in it? Name all the participants in the center.

ANSWER: I know of no formally constituted center of the organization, but I know a number of persons who met and conferred on current political questions. They all belonged to the above-mentioned organization of the former Zinovievist opposition. They were ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, KUKLIN, SHAROV.

QUESTION: According to the investigation's information, the counterrevolutionary organization of Zinovievists, in particular its Moscow center, existed until recently. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: I cannot confirm this. In any event, my information about the existence of the above-mentioned group, which directed the organization of f\<ormer\> participants in the Zinovievist opposition, ends in the middle of 1930. After that date I do not recall any systematic meetings of the above-mentioned group of a political character, although I do not deny chance meetings with the above-mentioned persons.

In my view, the cessation of the organization's activity resulted from its having lost any program whatsoever, having no definite goal and contemplating no concrete actions.

It was entirely clear to me personally that preserving any organization whatsoever was directly harmful to the party and would only hinder a return to party work, which I desired.

I personally favored ending the struggle against the party.

QUESTION: The investigation has established that the organization existed until recently; meanwhile you maintain that the counterrevolutionary organization of Zinovievists ceased to exist after the middle of 1930. The investigation calls on you to give a truthful answer to this question.

ANSWER: In this I proceed from the fact that I know of no meetings of the organization or of the above-mentioned center from the second half of 1930 onward. Nor do I know of decisions taken by the above-mentioned leading group of persons, or of any actions dictated by that center after that time.

QUESTION: You testify that beginning in the second half of 1930 you considered the preservation of any organization whatsoever directly harmful to the party, and that you therefore favored ending the struggle against the party. As one of the organizers and leaders of the counterrevolutionary organization of Zinovievists, did you take measures to liquidate that organization, and precisely what measures?

ANSWER: I acknowledge my guilt in having taken no steps within the organization itself to bring its existence to an end.

I considered that my personal conduct—my withdrawal from the organization and articles in the party press—sufficiently demonstrated that I had ended my struggle against the party, and considered that these facts should influence my like-minded associates to end their struggle.

QUESTION: Do you know LEVIN Vladimir, RUMYANTSEV Vladimir and KOTOLYNOV Ivan?

ANSWER: I met LEVIN very rarely in the period before the Fifteenth Congress. After the Fifteenth Congress I do not remember meeting him; it is possible that he was among the people who visited me in Leningrad in 1928.

I also met RUMYANTSEV before the period of the Fifteenth Congress; he visited me at the hotel in 1928, at a conference of which I spoke in my testimony of January 9, and later visited me in Moscow with a draft declaration for admission to the party.

I do not recall KOTOLYNOV at all; I do not deny that he may have visited me before the Fifteenth Congress, but I do not remember.

QUESTION: What do you know about LEVIN's, RUMYANTSEV's and KOTOLYNOV's membership in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization in Leningrad?

ANSWER: I knew that all three persons mentioned belonged to the opposition before the Fifteenth Congress. I also knew that after the Fifteenth Congress RUMYANTSEV and KOTOLYNOV opposed the tactics of the twenty-three and belonged to the left-wing group, the so-called “leaderless.” I know nothing about the conduct and political attitudes of all three after 1928.

QUESTION: Do you know MYASNIKOV, Nikolai Petrovich?

ANSWER: I do not know him at all.

QUESTION: According to MYASNIKOV's testimony, in 1928 he visited your apartment in Moscow, and during your conversation with him you instructed him—MYASNIKOV—on the need to gather forces for the forthcoming struggle against the party leadership, referring in this connection to the fact that “the CC's policy will fail anyway.”

Do you acknowledge this?

ANSWER: Since I do not remember a person named MYASNIKOV, I ask to be shown his photograph; perhaps I shall remember him.

QUESTION: ZINOVIEV's testimony establishes that the organization of Zinovievists which existed in Leningrad after the Fifteenth Congress was headed by LEVIN V. and RUMYANTSEV V. As a member of the organization's leading group, you knew of the existence of the organization in Leningrad and of its leading center. Do you acknowledge that you knew of the existence of the organization of Zinovievists in Leningrad, directed by the Moscow center, and of the persons heading the Leningrad organization?

ANSWER: I acknowledge that I knew of the existence of an illegal organization of Zinovievists in Leningrad after the Fifteenth Congress of the VKP(b). I did not know the names of the persons heading that organization. I knew of that organization's existence until the first half of 1930. Whether it continued to exist after that time, I do not know.

QUESTION: Do you know of ZINOVIEV's meeting in 1928 with the f\<ormer\> leader of the Right, BUKHARIN, and if so, precisely what? Where and in whose presence did it take place, and what was its character?

ANSWER: ZINOVIEV personally told me of this meeting. According to his account, it took place in the Kremlin. No one besides BUKHARIN and ZINOVIEV was present. ZINOVIEV told me that BUKHARIN believed that we, that is, ZINOVIEV and I, would be enlisted by the party leadership in the struggle against the Right; that the Right were very sharply opposed to Comrade STALIN personally; and that they believed it was only under his influence that they had at one time taken such a “stupid” step as helping to remove us from the leadership.

ZINOVIEV's account gave me the impression that BUKHARIN had summoned him chiefly to dissuade him from possibly assisting Comrade STALIN and the CC if we were enlisted in the struggle against the Right. I remember that for our part we then considered our enlistment in the struggle against the Right the most desirable outcome for us, and that we would, of course, have agreed to it wholeheartedly despite BUKHARIN's exhortations. ZINOVIEV conveyed to me that the general impression from his conversation with BUKHARIN was that the Right were prepared to disguise their true views in any way in order to remain on the Central Committee and retain their leading posts.

QUESTION: According to ZINOVIEV's testimony, the principal political thrust of the meeting was a proposal for a bloc between the Right and you Zinovievists.

Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: Yes, I do.

QUESTION: Tell the investigation how you reacted to ZINOVIEV's information about the negotiations with the Right.

ANSWER: I spoke against a bloc with the Right, considering that the most desirable outcome for our group was to be enlisted by the Central Committee in the struggle against the Right. My reasons were as follows:

1\. the line of Comrade STALIN, the principal target of the Right's struggle, was much closer to me than BUKHARIN's line;

2\. I did not believe in the Right's success and expected no improvement in our party position from a bloc with them, whereas our enlistment by the Central Committee for the struggle against the Right seemed possible to me and at the same time the only thing capable of restoring our role in the party. For these reasons I spoke against a bloc with the Right, but considered it very valuable to be informed of their steps, and therefore useful to receive information from them and not lose contact with them.

QUESTION: What do you know of ZINOVIEV's meeting with TOMSKY in Sochi in 1929, in whose presence did it take place and what was its character?

ANSWER: ZINOVIEV told me of this meeting, but all I retained in my memory from his accounts was that TOMSKY rebuked him for attacks against the Right in individual articles, telling him that ZINOVIEV would achieve nothing by this, since the Right were already tactically defeated and STALIN had no intention of enlisting the Zinovievists for that purpose.

QUESTION: What did ZINOVIEV tell you about his agreement with TOMSKY to establish permanent contact with him through FEDOROV?

ANSWER: I do not remember.

QUESTION: How did you react to ZINOVIEV's information about his meeting with TOMSKY?

ANSWER: I do not remember exactly, but probably simply took note of it.

QUESTION: Tell the investigation what you know about attempts at political collusion among various anti-party groups in 1932 and about your and ZINOVIEV's role in it.

ANSWER: I can now recall three facts in this area:

1\. conversations with STEN;

2\. my conversation with LOMINADZE and SHATSKIN;

3\. the affair of the Ryutin platform.

a) As for conversations with STEN, it seemed to me then that STEN represented no one but himself and had no firm political views. He was very oppositional toward the leadership and spoke of his personal break with STALIN, but all this was in the nature of personal remarks. I talked with him more on literary and philosophical topics than on political ones.

b) SHATSKIN—LOMINADZE. I met LOMINADZE by chance at the TsIK rest home in Gagra. LOMINADZE was already staying there when I arrived. I knew very little about his position in the party or his stance, and had no political conversations with him.

But once SHATSKIN came from Sochi to visit him. One day on the beach they both approached me, expressing their dissatisfaction with the situation inside the party and proposing the need to establish some contact among opposition groups. I replied that I had no group, that I considered their proposed connection harmful, and rejected their proposal. In doing so I asked them whom they actually represented. I was told that three of them were closely connected—SHATSKIN, LOMINADZE and, I believe, REZNIKOV \[2\] in Voronezh.

Their reply that the entire group consisted of three people followed my statement that I considered their proposal for mutual contact harmful.

After that I did not see SHATSKIN except once, by chance, on a Moscow street in November 1932. LOMINADZE remained at the rest home for some time longer, but the conversation on the subjects mentioned was not resumed. After that I never met LOMINADZE again. I remember that during the conversation LOMINADZE expressed very great dissatisfaction with the change in the party leadership in the Caucasus and attributed it personally to STALIN.

c) The affair of the Ryutin platform. I have already given detailed testimony about this.

QUESTION: What do you know about ZINOVIEV's negotiations with SAFAROV in 1932?

ANSWER: ZINOVIEV once—I cannot recall precisely when—told me of his meeting with SAFAROV, and all that remains in my memory is that SAFAROV, according to ZINOVIEV, assessed the Comintern's activities very pessimistically.

What do you know about EVDOKIMOV's negotiations with the Trotskyists through SMIRNOV in 1932?

ANSWER: I do not remember.

QUESTION: What do you know about negotiations with SHLYAPNIKOV, when and through whom were they conducted?

ANSWER: I recall, although I cannot determine exactly in which year it was—in 1931 or 1932—that ZINOVIEV told me SHLYAPNIKOV had met him and told him about some of his affairs at the TsKK, and had given him the impression of a bitter, demoralized man.

I know of no other meetings or negotiations with SHLYAPNIKOV.

QUESTION: What negotiations did you conduct with SMILGA in the autumn of 1932, and where?

ANSWER: I met SMILGA in Gagra, but conducted no political negotiations with him.

QUESTION: SMILGA's testimony establishes that in the autumn of 1932 you informed him of the existence of a platform of the Right. What precisely did you tell SMILGA about this, and how did he react to the information?

ANSWER: When meeting SMILGA in Gagra, I told him, among other things, about the Ryutinists' document which ZINOVIEV had shown me before my departure. What precisely SMILGA said about it, I do not remember.

I also met SMILGA later as the editor of one of the departments of the “Academia” publishing house; on his visits to Moscow he usually called at the publishing house. Our conversations were strictly businesslike, concerning the department's plans and the books he edited.

QUESTION: ZINOVIEV's testimony establishes that in 1932 various anti-party groups attempted to reach a political agreement with him, with you and with other leaders of the Zinovievist underground with the aim of organizing a bloc to struggle against the party leadership, and that the following circumstances prompted such attempts:

a) his, your and your close associates' anti-party attitudes;

b) the certainty on the part of representatives of anti-party groups that you would not expose them before the party;

c) the anti-party groups saw in ZINOVIEV and you people with political standing who could lead the struggle against the party leadership.

Do you acknowledge this?

ANSWER: I know only of those facts of conversations and negotiations with anti-party groups which I have set out above. As for the motives that drove these groups toward us, I set them out in point 3 of my testimony of January 9, 1935.

QUESTION: What do you know about ZINOVIEV's meeting with TOMSKY in 1932, shortly before ZINOVIEV's exile? Where did this meeting take place, what character

did it have, who was present, and how did you react to ZINOVIEV's information about this meeting?

ANSWER: I remember ZINOVIEV telling me that he had a meeting with TOMSKY and had arranged literary work with him. Whether ZINOVIEV also conveyed political statements by TOMSKY, I do not remember; perhaps I paid no attention to this.

QUESTION: Tell us what you know about MAGYAR's ties with the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization.

ANSWER: I knew only that MAGYAR, after visiting us in Kaluga, called on ZINOVIEV from time to time, informing him about the state of affairs in the Comintern. I considered MAGYAR personally connected with ZINOVIEV.

QUESTION: What do you know about attempts in 1932 to send a letter to the CC protesting against the exile of ZINOVIEV and yourself from Moscow? Who initiated this and how did you and ZINOVIEV react to the proposal?

ANSWER: I recall ZINOVIEV telling me that comrades close to him had put forward a proposal to send a letter to the CC concerning the exile of myself and ZINOVIEV. I expressed an entirely negative opinion of this, considering that it would be a revival of the Zinovievist organization, since by this time I was already opposed to this.

I suppose that the initiators of the submission to the CC were EVDOKIMOV and BAKAEV. At approximately this time BAKAEV visited me and said that KOSTINA had decided to speak out openly against my and ZINOVIEV's exile. He himself disapproved of this. So did I.

QUESTION: Tell the investigation everything you know about the Moscow center's systematic connection with the organization of Zinovievists in Leningrad. Name concrete instances of journeys by representatives of the Moscow center to Leningrad and, conversely, by representatives of the Leningrad center to Moscow, the nature of the information and directives conveyed, and so forth.

ANSWER: I know absolutely nothing about this.

QUESTION: What do you know about ZINOVIEV's anti-party attitudes in 1934?

ANSWER: ZINOVIEV's mood in 1934 was mixed. Alongside acknowledgment of the correctness of the party line and the party leadership, anti-party assessments of various party measures, dissatisfaction with his position and irritation with the party leadership repeatedly broke through.

KAMENEV.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE EKO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR — MIRONOV.

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR — LYUSHKOV.

\[1\] Concerning Zinoviev's testimony, which supposedly confirmed the existence of an underground organization, the Report on the “Moscow Center” prepared by IMEL and the KPK under the CC of the CPSU states: “Addressing investigative methods, Deputy People's Commissar of Internal Affairs Ya.S. Agranov stated the following at an operational meeting of NKVD USSR personnel on February 3, 1935: ‘Our tactic for crushing the enemy consisted of making all these scoundrels clash with one another and quarrel among themselves. And this was a difficult task. It was necessary to make them quarrel because all these traitors were closely bound together by a ten-year struggle against our party. We were dealing with hardened double-dealers, highly experienced deceivers. In the course of the investigation we succeeded in making Zinoviev, Kamenev, Evdokimov, Safarov, Gorshenin and others actually clash with one another.’ How these ‘clashes’ took place can be seen in the example of L.B. Kamenev's interrogation on January 10, 1935, when NKVD personnel demanded that he acknowledge the existence of an underground counterrevolutionary organization, asserting that G.E. Zinoviev was already confirming this circumstance. Yet the latter had not at that time given such testimony” (quoted from “Rehabilitation. Political Trials of the 1930s–1950s,” Moscow, Political Literature Publishing House, 1991, p. 157).

\[2\] It was precisely B.G. Reznikov who wrote the denunciation of S.I. Syrtsov that initiated the Syrtsov–Lominadze case in 1930. According to O. Khlevnyuk, “B.G. Reznikov worked with Syrtsov in Siberia, studied at the Institute of Red Professors in Moscow in 1929–1931, and then worked on the editorial staff of the newspaper ‘Pravda.’ During the years of the ‘Great Terror’ he was arrested. No information has been found concerning his execution or sentencing to imprisonment.”
