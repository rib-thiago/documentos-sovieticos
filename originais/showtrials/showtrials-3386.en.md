INTERROGATION RECORD

Of REM, Mikhail Semenovich, dated December 22, 1934.

QUESTION: Do you know about the duplicitous return to the Party by participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc, and for what purpose this was done?

ANSWER: Yes, I know about it. After the Party's ideological and political defeat of the opposition, participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc entered the Party not as a result of repentance, but to continue covert subversive work within the Party. This was also a maneuver to preserve their cadres.

ANSWER: Duplicity as a method of struggle against the Party and its leadership was cultivated by the opposition leaders even during the first period of open attack against the Party. Back in 1926, in conversation with ZINOVIEV, I expressed to him the idea that Party decisions should be sincerely carried out; he replied: “Never mind, they will be afraid to expel us from the Party, and when there is a new wave of difficulties in the country we shall carry out our line more easily.” Later, in January 1928, on the question of the need to enter the Party, ZINOVIEV personally told me: “The Bolsheviks in their time went into such a pigsty as the Duma. BADAEV kissed the cross, and you want to look squeaky-clean in the struggle.” He also recommended that I use this argument in agitation among oppositionists for returning to the Party. I recall that GERTIK, in TOGUNOV's presence, spoke of disagreements in the Politburo during preparations for the Fourteenth Congress; at that time TOGUNOV submitted a statement that factional preparations for the congress were taking place, and in this connection GERTIK told me: “If you are summoned to Comrade SHVERNIK, you undertake to conceal from him the conversation that took place between us.”

After the Fourteenth Party Congress GORSHENIN told me: “We must seek to keep our supporters in Leningrad and go to work primarily in factories, among the workers, and there cautiously explain ZINOVIEV's counter-report and KAMENEV's speech.” The directive to go to work in factories directly among the workers was emphasized more than once in subsequent years too.

In August 1928 a conference was held at Vladimir LEVIN's apartment on Nadezhdinskaya Street, attended by V. LEVIN, N. MYASNIKOV, KOSTRITSKY and myself, REM; GERTIK or SHAROV was then there from Moscow. GERTIK (or SHAROV) conveyed the following: “The ‘leaders’ have issued a directive for everyone to return to the Party, thereby preserving the cadres.” Particular emphasis was placed on the need for the Zinovievist youth to return to the Party. At the same time the Moscow representative spoke of rosy prospects in the sense that soon, in connection with probable grain difficulties in the country, since “the peasant will not give up the grain,” ZINOVIEV's line would triumph and become the Party's official course. Subsequent conferences, the maintenance of organizational connections between Zinovievists, and continued activities against the Party and its leadership characterize the activities of the Zinovievist organization.

QUESTION: What do you know about the emergence of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization composed of former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc?

ANSWER: I know that participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc, having entered the Party for tactical, duplicitous purposes, formed a counterrevolutionary organization.

The beginning of organized subversive activities within the Party was laid at a series of conferences held illegally in 1928. The first conference I happened to attend took place in August 1928 at V. LEVIN's apartment. I have already testified about the conversations at this conference; only the following need be added: on Vladimir LEVIN's proposal the organization's members were divided up to conduct work in separate districts. I recall that Efim KORSHUNOV was assigned to lead the organization's work in Central District, KOSTRITSKY in Moscow-Narva District, and Moisei YAKOVLEV and myself in Vasilyevsky Ostrov District (YAKOVLEV was not at the conference; V. LEVIN maintained contact with him). Specifically, I was assigned to organize work at two factories; LEVIN then gave me the surname of the worker SEMENOV, with whom I was to establish contact. Soon after the conference SEMENOV came to my apartment with two other workers. I do not now remember which factory these workers were from or who exactly sent them to me; apparently V. LEVIN. I never saw these workers again and had no connection with the district.

The second conference took place in the autumn of that same year, 1928, after LILINA's funeral, at Ya. V. SHAROV's apartment (Petrograd Side, former Arkhiereiskaya Street). Of those attending this conference I remember ZINOVIEV, KOSTRITSKY, V. LEVIN and KATAI (a worker from the Vyborg Side, from the STALIN factory or Krasny Vyborzhets); I do not remember whether anyone else was present. This conference chiefly raised the same questions that were discussed at the conference at LEVIN's apartment. On all questions ZINOVIEV personally spoke and gave instructions: preserving cadres from destruction, preserving connections between the organization's members; as at LEVIN's apartment, ZINOVIEV paid particular attention to the need for the youth to return to the Party and to preserve them from destruction.

In November 1928 there was a conference at KOSTRITSKY's apartment attended by KOSTRITSKY, V. LEVIN, N. MYASNIKOV and myself, REM. We discussed the question of speaking at the activists' meeting on A. I. RYKOV's report about the results of the November plenum of the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks). At approximately the same time I attended another conference, at which V. LEVIN, SOSITSKY and KOSTRITSKY were present; the conference was held on 8th Krasnoarmeiskaya Street at some worker's apartment.

QUESTION: What do you know about the counterrevolutionary activities of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization?

ANSWER: It is known that the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc directed the thrust of all its activities against the leadership of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), particularly Comrade STALIN. Personally, in Moscow in 1926, ZINOVIEV told me (in a conversation while celebrating the New Year): “Either I or STALIN”; in keeping with these sentiments of ZINOVIEV's, every kind of counterrevolutionary fabrication was spread against Comrade STALIN, and the bitterest hatred of Comrade STALIN was cultivated among the Zinovievists. Having formed a counterrevolutionary organization, the former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc, without any positive program, likewise directed all their activities against Comrade STALIN, continuing to cultivate and spread hostile sentiments against Comrade STALIN and his closest comrades-in-arms: Comrades KAGANOVICH, KIROV and VOROSHILOV. As in previous years, members of the organization spread all sorts of counterrevolutionary talk and rumors against the Party leadership, reducing everything to the need to promote ZINOVIEV and his supporters to the Party leadership, scorning no means to do so. The Zinovievist-Trotskyist counterrevolutionary organization, spreading hostility against the Party leaders, thereby cultivated terrorist sentiments among the most active and aggressively disposed young people. Therefore, sincerely repenting my crimes, I must declare to the investigation that our organization bears full political responsibility for the murder of Comrade KIROV by NIKOLAEV, a member of the Zinovievist organization. NIKOLAEV's shot showed that counterrevolutionary Zinovievism had descended to fascist methods in its struggle against the leadership of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks). Although I personally do not know NIKOLAEV and have never seen him, I likewise do not absolve myself of responsibility for his crime.

QUESTION: What do you know about the leaders of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization?

ANSWER: Among the leading officials of the Leningrad organization I know Vladimir Levin, about whom I have already testified above. Close to V. LEVIN were Timofei DMITRIEV (I do not remember when he left Leningrad), Moisei YAKOVLEV, N. MYASNIKOV and L. I. SOSITSKY.

I have read the record; it was taken down correctly from my words.

REM.

Interrogated by: HEAD OF THE 6TH SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB, KORKIN

Certified correct: Potrokhova
