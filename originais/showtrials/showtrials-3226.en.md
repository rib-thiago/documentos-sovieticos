INTERROGATION RECORD

OF NIKOLAEV L. V., dated December 25, 1934.

QUESTION: When exactly did you first visit the Latvian consulate?

ANSWER: It was on September 21 or 22, 1934. Knowing the consulate's telephone number — I had obtained it from the information bureau — I called the consulate from the Central Post Office and asked whether visitors were being received and whether the consul was in. I spoke to a woman. When I arrived at the consulate, I realized that she was the consul's secretary.

Having received the answer that the consul was in and receiving visitors, I went to the consulate.

The consulate is on Herzen Street; I do not now remember the building number. I went up the main staircase to the third floor, rang the bell, and a doorman opened the door. He is of medium height, thin, and elderly.

There is a wooden coat stand in the entrance hall. I waited in the waiting room for about ten minutes, after which the consul received me. I forgot to mention that all this happened at about noon.

The consul received me in his office, which is on the left as one goes from the entrance hall to the office.

The office is medium-sized; as far as I remember, there are two desks in it. I cannot recall the other furniture.

When I entered the office, the consul's secretary, a woman, was already sitting there. I concluded that she was the same woman I had spoken to on the telephone. She appeared to be thirty to thirty-five years old. She speaks Russian well.

I gave the consul my real surname, first name, and patronymic, and also my home address. The consul introduced himself during our very first conversation, but I cannot remember his surname; it is typically Latvian.

QUESTION: How many meetings did you have with the consul altogether?

ANSWER: There were three or four meetings altogether; all took place in the consulate building, over a period of ten days, at intervals of two or three days.

Apparently I arrived before the general reception of visitors began, since I encountered no outsiders on the consulate premises; I usually arrived at ten in the morning or shortly afterward.

I wish to report one more detail: when I was leaving the consul's office, on the very first occasion, I noticed curtains hanging at the windows of a room whose door the consul's secretary happened to open in my presence. From this I concluded that there were living quarters in the consulate.

Recorded correctly from my words; I have read the record.

L. NIKOLAEV.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE EKO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR MIRONOV.

ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE EKO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR DMITRIEV.
