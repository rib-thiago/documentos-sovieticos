INTERROGATION RECORD

Conducted by LULOV, HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO, on December 23, 1934.

TARTAKOVSKAYA, Yulia Borisovna, born in 1900 in the small town of Chudnov, former Volhynia Province; father a shop assistant; Jewish, citizen of the USSR, resides at Slutsky Street, building no. 5, apartment 22. Profession: engineer. Until October 1934, head of the turbine workshop at the Lenin factory; husband: TIMOFEEV, Evgeny Timofeevich, member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), engineer at the Lenin factory; daughter aged seven. Salary. Higher education: graduated from the Leningrad Polytechnic Institute. Member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) since 1917. Expelled from the Party by the VX Congress for active factional work. Readmitted by the Central Control Commission in 1928. No convictions.

Question: What do you know about the existence of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization?

Answer: After the ideological and political defeat of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc, participants in this bloc, participants in the Zinovievist opposition, began submitting statements renouncing their views and requesting readmission to the Party. At this stage the immediate task was to penetrate the Party at all costs and organize our work in accordance with the new conditions. To accomplish this immediate task we successively applied a series of maneuvers. The essence of these maneuvers is clearly visible in several applications for my readmission to the Party discovered during the search of my home. In my statement of April 18, 1928, in which I declared that I renounced factional work, I simultaneously emphasized “that the resolution of internal Party disputes is permissible only within the Party” and that “the Leningrad opposition, despite all its tactical mistakes, proved, I think, politically correct.” This maneuver, calculated to penetrate the Party while leaving room for struggle, proved insufficiently deep. The situation demanded a deeper maneuver. On May 19 of the same year I again appealed to the Central Control Commission for readmission, now declaring agreement with the Party line. In this same statement I emphasized that “the course toward continuing the development of the offensive against the kulak is unconditionally Leninist,” that “the line adopted toward establishing internal Party democracy is unconditionally Leninist.” This last theme was widely commented on among us in the sense that on the questions of fighting the kulak and internal Party democracy the Party was taking the path of the opposition's platform. But this tactical step too proved insufficient. We were forced to go further. In my and TIMOFEEV's statement of June 10 we said that the opposition had been mistaken on a number of questions of principle. This series of successive maneuvers pursued the task of preserving our cadres from destruction and uniting them within the Party as an independent political force. All the practical work throughout subsequent years and up to the present moment, systematic meetings and the establishment of connections among participants in the Zinovievist opposition, and discussion of all current questions of the country's political life led to the re-creation of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization in Leningrad, which existed until recently.

Question: What do you know about the Moscow center of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization?

Answer: From contact with a number of prominent Zinovievists I know that the Moscow center of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization includes ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, GERTIK, KUKLIN, BAKAEV, SHAROV, myself. Whether Olga RAVICH belongs to the center I do not know, but knowing her exceptional closeness to this group of people, I believe she is associated with the center.

Question: What testimony can you give about the connection between the Moscow center of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization and the Leningrad organization?

Answer: This connection was maintained through periodic visits. Aleksandr ALEKSANDROV, A. TOLMAZOV and V. LEVIN were personally connected with members of the Moscow center. These people are closest to ZINOVIEV. Personally, while in Moscow in November of this year, I met EVDOKIMOV and SHAROV.

Question: What was the nature of your meeting with EVDOKIMOV and SHAROV in Moscow in November 1934?

Answer: This meeting had the character of sounding out sentiments and connections, and on SHAROV's part, an almost undisguised instruction to intensify work. I met EVDOKIMOV at the People's Commissariat of the Food Industry. He emphasized that the task remained to gain the trust of Party leaders. We ascertained the sentiments and whereabouts of like-minded people. I asked about BAKAEV. He informed me that KOROLEV had recently visited Moscow and stayed with him. During this same trip to Moscow I visited SHAROV at his apartment, having telephoned beforehand. Present during the conversation at SHAROV's were myself, SHAROV, SHEPSHELEVA (SHAROV's wife), and Olga Ilyinichna ILYINA, who had also come from Leningrad. I had been connected with SHEPSHELEVA and ILYINA through joint opposition work back in Leningrad. During the conversation SHAROV spoke in sharply negative terms about the Party leadership, the internal Party situation and the persecution of former oppositionists. On the question of the Zinovievists' tactical step of entering the Party to preserve cadres, SHAROV declared that at the present stage this tactic, correct in itself, was no longer sufficient; enough waiting, work must be intensified. In this same connection he made a number of bitter and hostile attacks on the Party leadership. After asking me about sentiments in Leningrad, SHAROV proposed that I strengthen my connection with him. In SHAROV's statement about the need to intensify work, in connection with his sharply negative attitude toward the leadership, one sensed definite resolve and direction, I would say the desperation of a person capable of extreme actions.

Question: With which participants in the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization were you connected?

Answer: I have already testified about my meeting with EVDOKIMOV and SHAROV in 1934. During the Fourteenth and Fifteenth Congresses, actively participating in factional work, I was connected with a number of prominent Zinovievists. In 1927 I directed underground work in the Khamovniki District of Moscow and was connected with ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, GERTIK and others. Later, having been sent to Semipalatinsk, TIMOFEEV and I maintained contact there with SERE<B>RYAKOV and KHANIK. In Leningrad I was connected with Vladimir LEVIN.

Question: What testimony can you give concerning the murder of Comrade KIROV by NIKOLAEV, a member of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization?

Answer: Responsibility for the murder of Comrade KIROV by NIKOLAEV, a member of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization, rests entirely and fully on each of us, on me in particular. It was we, participants in counterrevolutionary Zinovievism, who for a number of years instilled in the youth that the Party leadership had degenerated and was supposedly pursuing a disastrous policy. In this spirit of slander and hatred toward the Party leaders we in turn were educated by the older generation of counterrevolutionary Zinovievism—ZINOVIEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, SHAROV and others—with whom we, including myself, associated and jointly conducted counterrevolutionary work against the Party. When we were defeated in open struggle against the Party, we returned to its ranks with concealed bitterness against the Party leadership in order to continue our demoralizing work within the Party. Comrade KIROV earned the greatest hatred from the members of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization through his consistent struggle to defend the Party's general line against the encroachments of counterrevolutionary Zinovievism, through his irreconcilable struggle against the Leningrad opposition and its remnants. I have already testified above about the nature of SHAROV's conversation with me in 1934; it testifies that the cultivation of hatred toward the Party leadership and Comrade STALIN not only did not cease throughout these years but, on the contrary, in the most recent period assumed sharper and more extremist forms. The leaders and participants in the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization, seeing the collapse of their platform and having lost any even remotely serious support among the masses, in their bestial hatred of the Party leadership and its policy degenerated into rabid enemies of Soviet power, taking the path of fascist methods of struggle. TOLMAZOV, KHANIK and other former Komsomol oppositionists, ideologically educated by ZINOVIEV and other “leaders” in a spirit of hatred toward the Party leadership, particularly Comrade KIROV, were organizationally connected with these “leaders” through RUMYANTSEV throughout all recent years. NIKOLAEV's shot is the direct and immediate result of the practical counterrevolutionary work conducted by the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization against the Party and its leaders.

Recorded correctly from my words and read by me:

TARTAKOVSKAYA

Interrogated by: LULOV.
