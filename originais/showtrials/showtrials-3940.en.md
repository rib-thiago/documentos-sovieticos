INTERROGATION RECORD

OF PERIMOV, Aleksei Viktorovich, 31 December 1934.

QUESTION: Do you acknowledge guilt in joining the party with double-dealing purposes together with the other so-called Right Zinovievists? ANSWER: Yes, I acknowledge guilt.

A characteristic example of the Right Zinovievists' double-dealing sentiments after the Fifteenth Congress, alongside conversations about maneuvering and preserving cadres, was the saying widespread in your milieu: “the faction is dissolved — the meeting continues.”

Before and after the Fifteenth Party Congress, until 1930, I was in Tashkent; although I visited Moscow several times during this period, I cannot say in detail what politically characterized this interval in the Zinovievist organization's activity. I knew ZINOVIEV had written a document entitled “YUZUS,” which he gave me to read at his own apartment considerably later than its composition. Its essential meaning was that the Zinovievists had been right at both the Fourteenth and Fifteenth Party Congresses.

QUESTION: After returning to Moscow in 1930, did you continually maintain contact only with the narrow circle of Zinovievists about whom you testified earlier?

ANSWER: Besides those named earlier, in the first period after arriving in Moscow I met EVDOKIMOV (while he lived at the Paris Hotel), BRAVO — I visited him once —, GESSEN, whom I saw twice, and CHERNY, a close personal friend. CHERNY effectively broke with the Zinovievists immediately after the Fifteenth Congress.

QUESTION: Which Zinovievists living in Leningrad did you maintain contact with?

ANSWER: On my first visit to Leningrad in autumn 1930 I met Mikhail LEVIN, Timofei DMITRIEV and KORSHUNOV and spent one night at MATORIN's. \[1\] I remember no political conversations with them; I had no assignments from the Zinovievists for Leningrad. My second visit was in summer 1931. I saw only Mikhail LEVIN. LEVIN Mikhail was a Trotskyist in 1923 and subsequently a Zinovievist. In conversations with me on both occasions M. LEVIN was loyal toward the party line. I had no assignments from Moscow Zinovievists. I then visited Leningrad in summer 1932. I called at TSVIBAK's, from where I went with GERTIK to Vladimir LEVIN's. Besides us, BASHKIROV and Gosha FEDOROV, apparently a student whom I had not previously known, were there. I quickly became drunk at LEVIN's and remember no conversations. I last visited Leningrad in September 1934. Of the Zinovievists I saw Mikhail LEVIN and TSVIBAK. I had no time to speak with LEVIN; I spoke at length with TSVIBAK, touching on political subjects in loyal tones and censuring ZINOVIEV for his afterword to Engels's letter. \[2\] DMITRIEV Nikolai visited me in Moscow, first from Saratov and then from Leningrad, in 1932 or early 1933. I do not now remember the precise substance of our political conversations, but judging by my sentiments then, they could not have been loyal. I had no other meetings with Zinovievists living in Leningrad, apart from a chance encounter with ANISHEV in a Moscow street in May 1934.

PERIMOV.

INTERROGATED BY:

ASSISTANT TO THE HEAD OF THE SPO, GUGB — RUTKOVSKY.

Certified correct: A. Svetlova

RGASPI, fond 671, inventory 1, file 126, folios 164–166.

\[1\] The text incorrectly reads “Motorin” (Моторина).

\[2\] This concerns G. Zinoviev's editorial note about Engels's letter to Ion Nădejde (Bolshevik, No. 13–14, 31 July 1934). The note was used as a pretext for removing G. Zinoviev from the magazine's editorial board by a Politburo decision of 16 August 1934. The decision stated: “Comrade Zinoviev's comments express a Trotskyist-Menshevik orientation that does not recognize Lenin's new contribution to the treasury of Marxism.” Essentially, the Politburo decision echoed I. Stalin's letter to Politburo and Bolshevik editorial board members dated 5 August 1934. For details see Stalin and Kaganovich. Correspondence. 1931–1936. Moscow: Rosspen, p. 716.
