INTERROGATION RECORD

conducted on December 19, 1934, by the HEAD OF THE 4TH SECTION OF THE SPO, L. KOGAN.

MURAVYOV, Mikhail Vasilievich.

QUESTION: The investigation's information has established that Leonid NIKOLAEV, comrade KIROV's murderer, is a member of your counterrevolutionary organization. What can you testify about this?

ANSWER: Personally, I remember L. NIKOLAEV from 1923. I was head of the House of Communist Education of Youth in the Vyborg district; at the time he was, I believe, a political education worker at the “Krasnaya Zarya” factory. The Komsomol workers of the Vyborg District Komsomol Committee all belonged to the Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc and actively struggled against the Party. Leonid NIKOLAEV, as a District Committee worker, also belonged to our counterrevolutionary organization. The hatred toward the Party leaders, especially STALIN, instilled in us by many years of struggle against the Party leadership found its logical culmination in NIKOLAEV's shot. Therefore political responsibility for comrade KIROV's murder by the organization's member Leonid NIKOLAEV rests with our entire organization, which first introduced within the Party the idea of struggling against the Party leadership through street clashes bordering on armed insurrection, and instilled hatred for the Party leadership into our consciousness. In the struggle against the Party leadership we did not shrink from using any methods. RUMYANTSEV told us that STALIN wanted to turn Leningrad into a provincial town, that we must defend the traditions of Leningrad, the first city of the revolution. To us young workers this seemed correct, and we obediently carried out all his instructions. We were in favor of war because we thought that war would bring about the collapse of STALIN's leadership. I recall an occasion when RUMYANTSEV, after returning from Shanghai, brought photographs of workers beheaded by Chiang Kai-shek; he showed them among our group's Komsomol members and non-Party people, accompanying these photographs with the explanation that responsibility for the shooting of Shanghai workers lay with the policy of the Comintern and STALIN personally, who had placed his bets on Chiang Kai-shek.

RUMYANTSEV made a roughly similar statement in connection with Cook's betrayal \[1\]. —

Recorded correctly and read to me, in confirmation of which I sign:

MURAVYOV. —

INTERROGATED BY: KOGAN. —
