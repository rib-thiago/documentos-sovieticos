RECORD

Of the supplementary interrogation of MUSHTAKOV, Andrei Andreevich, dated December 19, 1934.

Question: Tell us which of the Zinovievists you named in the record of December 18 expressed dissatisfaction with Party policy and displayed intense bitterness toward the leadership of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) when meeting you, and how this was expressed.

Answer: In 1929 I met G. E. EVDOKIMOV at the Paris Hotel in Moscow. In conversation with me EVDOKIMOV expressed the following view on collectivization: it is now too late to carry out collectivization, since the kulak has become substantially stronger and grown considerably. Collectivization should have begun after the Fourteenth Congress; the current international situation will not permit collectivization, and with such a course, intervention may find support in the countryside. In conclusion EVDOKIMOV drew the conclusion that the Party leadership did not take account of the concrete situation and could lead the country to ruin.

In 1932 I met Sergei GESSEN in Sochi. In conversation with me on the beach GESSEN criticized Comrade Stalin and the Party leadership in extremely bitter tones. GESSEN said that Stalin was a sole dictator, that there was no collective decision-making in the Party leadership, that Stalin did not tolerate those who thought differently, and therefore the Party was making serious mistakes. There GESSEN also cited the following fact as an example of the Party regime within the Party: “The regime in the Party is oppressive; the old Latvians gather at STUCHKA's home and weep, saying: what have we come to.” During the conversation GESSEN made a series of counterrevolutionary attacks on Comrade STALIN in a bitter and wholly unacceptable manner, declaring that a regime of one-man rule existed in the Party. In the spring of 1934 GESSEN came to Leningrad for the Greater Dnieper conference.

During the conference GESSEN called at my workplace and in conversation with me touched on the Seventeenth Congress.

Regarding the Congress, GESSEN again made a counterrevolutionary attack on Comrade STALIN. In doing so he displayed intense bitterness toward the leadership of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks). That same day GESSEN telephoned Vladimir LEVIN and arranged to meet him.

In 1933, during my meetings with Sergei MANDELSHTAM, he told me that a dreadful food situation had developed in the cities, that supplies to the workers had been reduced to a minimum, and that the peasant had been alienated; from this MANDELSHTAM concluded that the Party leadership was leading the country to ruin. I know that MANDELSHTAM has held such sentiments until recently.

Recorded correctly from my words and read to me:

MUSHTAKOV.

Interrogated by:

HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR, PETROVSKY.
