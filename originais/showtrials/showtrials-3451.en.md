INTERROGATION RECORD

Of SAFAROV G. I., December 26, 1934.

To the questions put to me by the investigation, I testify:

GERTSBERG belonged and belongs among the active Zinovievists. He frequently and regularly met with GERTIK and other participants in the Moscow Zinovievist organization to exchange gossip and information and converse. Some Zinovievists arriving in Moscow from other places were sent to GERTSBERG for the appropriate briefing. In addition, as I remember from a conversation during my visit to the GERTSBERG household in the summer of 1932, he maintained contact with the group of German Trotskyists who at the end of 1927 formed the immediate circle of Ruth FISCHER and MASLOW. He and POZDEEVA then asked me whether one of these Trotskyists had called on me, since he had come from abroad to the Comintern. I said that I had seen no one. The GERTSBERG household's German Trotskyist connections were undoubtedly revived and maintained for a number of years through a former Bundist who worked in the Agitprop department of the Central Committee of the German Communist Party \[1\] under the name EMEL and returned to the USSR in 1932. On returning, the latter found a place at the foreign workers' publishing house and then teaching ancient history somewhere at a higher educational institution. This “EMEL” is an old Trotskyist, and I remember him from November 1927, when I organized an “international conference” in Berlin with the participation of TREK \[2\], Suzanne GIRO, MASLOW, R. FISCHER, KALZAN, and others.

POZDEEVA also belonged to the Zinovievist group until recently, being, like GERTSBERG, particularly closely connected with Artyom GERTIK. Like GERTSBERG, she is informed about the Berlin Trotskyists. At times she had fits of “repentance,” a desire to “offset” her anti-Party activity with diligent work (she worked in the People's Commissariat of Light Industry), but in general she remained throughout in the circle of old Zinovievist connections, counterrevolutionary “information,” and gossip.

Pyotr ZALUTSKY was undoubtedly connected through the KOSTINA sisters with BAKAEV, one of the Zinovievists' permanent central organizers. A man with deeply wounded pride and an extremely high opinion of himself, he was always inclined to spread all kinds of counterrevolutionary slanders against the Party and its leaders, and his return to the Party was most probably dictated by considerations of personal safety, career, and life's comforts rather than a real break with the past. He was always especially eager to engage in petty personal incitement against Party leaders, considering all of them “far beneath” himself.

During MRACHKOVSKY's visit to Moscow in the autumn of 1932, he told me that the BOBYLEV brothers, who had always been with him, also worked on his road construction project. He spoke very approvingly of them as “reliable” people in the anti-Party sense, ready to follow him anywhere. At the same time he spoke of the Urals Trotskyist STOROZHILOV \[3\], who in Ufaley had either taken up petty trading or opened a small workshop of his own. He intended to take him from there to “his” construction project. STOROZHILOV also counted among his “faithful men.” In general, all the construction projects on which MRACHKOVSKY worked were simultaneously Trotskyist colonies organized by him. He related how he extracted a number of Trotskyists from exile and concentration camps through what he called local machinations. He boasted that he also had “his own Party secretary” at the construction site, who understood nothing and did whatever he was told. He complained that with the arrival of the OGPU plenipotentiary representative DERIBAS, “it became much harder to work.” To characterize ZINOVIEV's position, it must also be added that in conversation with me at Ilyinskoye in July 1932, he used the following comparison: “We are sitting in trenches, in dugouts. Just stick a finger above the trench and they will shoot the finger off; how can one stick one's head out?” After ZINOVIEV's expulsion, when I called on EVDOKIMOV at the former “Paris” hotel, EVDOKIMOV repeated approximately the same argument, recommending waiting “until better times.” In accordance with this instruction from ZINOVIEV, a meeting of Zinovievists at approximately the same time (with EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, GERTIK, and others participating), about which MAGYAR informed me, decided to reject A. KOSTINA's proposal to speak at Party meetings in defense of ZINOVIEV.

The active Zinovievists also included FLIOR \[4\], who was in Voronezh for a time; I find it difficult to establish even approximately the date of my meeting with him in the street in Moscow. It seems to have been in 1931. Speaking of Voronezh, he remarked: “We have our own colony there; Olga RAVICH is there too.”

I do not remember whether it was in 1932 or 1933, after his return from abroad, that MAGYAR spoke about MASLOW and RUTH FISCHER, saying that they were supposedly “loyal to the Party \[5\].” This mention may serve as an indication that he met with them.

Written in my own hand — G. SAFAROV.

INTERROGATED BY:

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE USSR NKVD — LYUSHKOV

HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE SSR — PETROVSKY.
