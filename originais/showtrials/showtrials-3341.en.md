City of Leningrad.

SPO of the UGB.

INTERROGATION RECORD

On December 19, 1934, I, RUTKOVSKY, conducted a supplementary interrogation of the accused, BAKAEV, Ivan Petrovich.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that until recently you were a member of the center of the counterrevolutionary anti-Party organization of former members of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc. What testimony can you give in this case?

ANSWER: I do not know of the existence of a center of the above-mentioned organization. From the Fifteenth Party Congress until recently I met EVDOKIMOV, GERTIK, FEDOROV, SHAROV, KUKLIN, GORSHENIN and PERIMOV. I met ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV until their exile from Moscow, that is, until the end of 1932. I saw KAMENEV once in 1933 after his return from exile. I met EVDOKIMOV, SHAROV, FEDOROV and KUKLIN mainly in the Kremlin dining hall, and we also visited one another's apartments. I also saw them at ZINOVIEV's apartment before his exile in 1932, at various times and in different groups.

I described in my previous testimony the most substantial political conversations held by ZINOVIEV at his apartment in which I participated. After the Fifteenth Congress, ZINOVIEV was interested in how local organizations were implementing the decisions of the Fifteenth Congress and how these organizations treated us, the former Zinovievists.

At that time I visited Moscow from Vyatka and subsequently from Nizhny Novgorod.

QUESTION: What do you know about negotiations between the former Zinovievists and the Rightists in 1928–1929?

ANSWER: I remember that there were some negotiations between KAMENEV and BUKHARIN.

QUESTION: How did you and the persons you listed above decide the question of a bloc with the Rightists?

ANSWER: The question of a bloc with the Rightists was not discussed in my presence. I remember that the question was put to ZINOVIEV in my presence and, I think, in the presence of EVDOKIMOV, who visited his apartment, of how he, ZINOVIEV, would regard an action by the Rightists if such a thing occurred. ZINOVIEV replied that the Rightists' idea deserved no support whatever. Either EVDOKIMOV or someone else replied that “we shall fight the Rightists with all our might.”

QUESTION: What do you know about negotiations between the former Zinovievists and the members of the Rightist-Leftist bloc, SHATSKIN, LOMINADZE and others, in 1930–1931?

ANSWER: I do not remember whether there were any such negotiations at all.

QUESTION: What do you know about negotiations between ZINOVIEV, EVDOKIMOV and others and SAFAROV concerning the submission of a collective letter to the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) about difficulties in the country and the lack of information about them within the Party in 1932?

ANSWER: I know of no such incident and do not think this could have happened, since, as far as I know, ZINOVIEV's relations with SAFAROV had been strained since the Fifteenth Party Congress.

QUESTION: What do you know about negotiations between the former Zinovievists and the Ryutinites and about acquaintance with the platform of RYUTIN and company?

ANSWER: I gave testimony on this question in the previous record. I do not know which former Zinovievists, apart from ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV, acquainted themselves with RYUTIN's platform.

QUESTION: What testimony can you give about gatherings of former Zinovievists disguised as family gatherings, anniversary celebrations and other meetings, at which Party policy was criticized and hostility toward the Party leadership was cultivated?

ANSWER: There were no special parties with anti-Party aims. I know nothing of hostile attitudes toward the Party leadership.

QUESTION: Do you plead guilty to having engaged throughout this period in counterrevolutionary, duplicitous, anti-Party activities?

ANSWER: No, I do not consider myself guilty of this. I plead guilty to having remained too long a prisoner of the force of inertia of factional struggle, which in a number of cases led to a dulling of Party vigilance; to having failed to break sufficiently resolutely with ZINOVIEV and a number of persons close to me through our past anti-Party work, which made it possible to suppose that I personally had certain special interests concerning questions of Party policy.

QUESTION: With which Zinovievists living in Leningrad were you in contact?

ANSWER: I have no regular contact with former Zinovievists living in Leningrad. During the period from the Fifteenth Party Congress to the end of 1934 I met Vladimir LEVIN, RUMYANTSEV and Konstantin SOLOVYOV. I do not remember meeting other persons.

Vladimir LEVIN: I met him in Moscow some two or three times. I last saw him several years ago in a hotel. I do not rule out the possibility of conversations with him on political subjects, but I do not now remember their content.

RUMYANTSEV: I met him several times earlier at NAUMOV's home. My last meeting with him was several years ago on the street in Moscow, when he was returning from leave. The meeting was fleeting, without conversation.

SOLOVYOV: I met him about twice on separate occasions. I last saw him in June–July 1934 in Leningrad. I visited him at work, at the trust, concerning speeding up the execution of an order. I asked how his work was going and how the Party organization treated him. His answer was positive.

QUESTION: What do you know about the existence in Leningrad of an underground counterrevolutionary organization of former members of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc?

ANSWER: I did not know or suspect the existence of such an organization.

QUESTION: What do you know about the existence of a terrorist organization of former Zinovievists in Leningrad?

ANSWER: I know nothing.

QUESTION: What testimony can you give concerning the terrorist act against Comrade KIROV committed by L. NIKOLAEV, a member of the counterrevolutionary organization of Zinovievists?

ANSWER: I think this is an act of hostile imperialist counterintelligence.

BAKAEV. —

INTERROGATED BY: RUTKOVSKY.

In addition, concerning the question of meetings of former Zinovievists at apartments, I testify:

Soon after the Fifteenth Party Congress there was a party at GERTIK's apartment on Arbat Square, at which individual comrades used anti-Party expressions, chiefly connected with the course of the pre-congress discussion, when opposition representatives were not admitted to certain Party meetings. —

I. BAKAEV.
