INTERROGATION RECORD

conducted by the Head of Section 1 of the SPO — Comrade LULOV

dated December 12, 1934

SOKOLOV, Georgy Vasilyevich (supplementary)

QUESTION: Describe in greater detail the political positions of the group of former Zinovievist oppositionists connected with you.

ANSWER: In my testimony of December 7 and 10 of this year, I stated that there still remained in Leningrad a group of former oppositionists, connected with me through occasional meetings, consisting of: 1) KHANIK Lev, 2) TOLMAZOV Andrey, 3) KOTOLYNOV Ivan, 4) NIKANOROV Vasily, 5) ANTONOV Nikolai, 6) VINOGRADOV Vasily, 7) RUMYANTSEV Vladimir, 8) ZVEZDOV Vasily, 9) NIKOLAEV Leonid.

In the same records I testified that the main features characterizing this group's nature were its continued resentment against the Party, its preservation of the old Zinovievist traditions, and its setting itself against the Party. Beginning in the autumn of this year, the group's members started strengthening their connections and establishing closer contact with one another. I testified about this in detail in the interrogation record of December 10, 1934.

QUESTION: What, then, were this group's political positions? What was the political platform that united them?

ANSWER: The group was essentially united on the basis of the old Zinovievist platform. This platform found expression in the following assessment of the situation in the country and the Party: 1) On the Party's policy in the countryside: the group proceeded from the view that the liquidation of kulak farms — a measure whose burden fell on substantial sections of the middle peasantry — the forcible imposition of collective farms, and the resulting lack of incentive to work among the overwhelming majority of the peasantry had led to the actual ruin of the countryside. All this caused acute discontent among the peasantry and resistance to the Party's measures, necessitating recourse to the introduction of political departments. The latter led to an extreme intensification of arbitrary administrative rule in the countryside. 2) On the country's industrialization, it was emphasized that the pace adopted was beyond its capacity, that the construction of industrial giants and new projects in general was being undertaken without regard to economic possibilities or expediency, and that this construction was proceeding at the expense of increased exploitation of the workers. As a result, we have the most intense discontent among the working class. 3) On internal Party questions, the group's members stated that the Thermidorian degeneration of the leadership had reached its full completion and that old Bolshevik cadres were being removed from the Party leadership. The exclusion from the leadership of LENIN's closest pupils — ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV — was noted with particular regret. All this aroused extreme bitterness toward the Party leadership among the group's members. This gave rise to the growth of their extremist sentiments, which had intensified particularly in recent times.

QUESTION: What influence did this group of people exert on NIKOLAEV Leonid?

ANSWER: I have already testified that NIKOLAEV Leonid associated with this group of former oppositionists and former Komsomol members, which had grown up in Leningrad under ZINOVIEV's ideological leadership, and drew nourishment from the group's political sentiments. The group's extremist sentiments against the Party leadership found their sharpest expression in NIKOLAEV's state of mind.

The record was taken down correctly from my words; I have read it and sign in confirmation —

SOKOLOV.

INTERROGATED BY — LULOV.
