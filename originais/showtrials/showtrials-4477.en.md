TESTIMONY

OF EVDOKIMOV Grigory Eremeevich

January 14, 1935.

On the question of Aleksandr Andrianovich KOROLEV's relationship to the underground counterrevolutionary organization of Zinovievists.

In 1925-1927 KOROLEV belonged to the Leningrad active core of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc. After that I did not meet him until 1929 or even 1930. At that time he was working somewhere in Central Asia. My ties with him resumed after he returned to Leningrad. It is now rather difficult for me to recall his sentiments at that time (1929-1930) precisely; in any case, they were the sentiments of someone who had not fully overcome his anti-Party views. During the subsequent period, 1930-1932, I met him rarely; despite his great enthusiasm for study (he was studying simultaneously at the Industrial Academy and the Electrotechnical Institute), his sentiments remained the same as in 1929-1930, that is, those of someone who had not fully overcome his anti-Party views. I began meeting him more frequently in 1933-1934. At that time he worked as director of the Dneprostroy aluminum combine and, when visiting Moscow on the combine's business, usually stayed with me. In the summer of 1934 he lived at my apartment, with short interruptions, for about a month and a half. At that time he had been removed from his job at the Dneprostroy aluminum combine and was negotiating with the People's Commissariat over a new appointment. During his work at the Dnieper aluminum combine he was wholly absorbed in that work, and our conversations were mostly devoted to that construction project. As for political questions, I did not conceal my views from him, but he usually said that his business was to build factories and manage them, rather than engage in high politics. But he did not object to my anti-Party assessments of the situation, though he said that he understood general political questions poorly. Among his sentiments during his last stay with me, his harsh, exaggerated characterizations of bureaucracy and careerism in the Soviet and Party apparatus deserve attention; he explained these by insufficient inner-Party democracy. He had no direct connection with the underground Zinovievist organization in Moscow, but he knew about our sentiments, meetings, and conversations, and met other members of the Moscow underground counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization at my apartment. All this compels me, despite the somewhat apolitical attitude I characterized above, to place him among the former Zinovievists who had not overcome their anti-Party sentiments. He also visited Leningrad during this period (1933-1934), but whether he met any members of the underground Zinovievist organization in Leningrad and whether he told me about it, I do not now remember. I know only for certain that in Leningrad he met another active participant in the former Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc, Nikolai SMIRNOV.

Written in my own hand — G. EVDOKIMOV.
