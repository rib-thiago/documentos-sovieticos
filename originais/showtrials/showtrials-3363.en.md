INTERROGATION RECORD

Of TARASOV, Ivan Ivanovich

Dated December 20, 1934.

Question: What can you add to your testimony of December 19 about the activities of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization?

Answer: As I have already testified, I happened to be in Leningrad in 1932, and during that visit I had meetings, together with KOTOLYNOV, with V. RUMYANTSEV and Ya. TSEITLIN, each separately.

I recall that at his apartment RUMYANTSEV, in connection with a discussion of the situation in the country and the Party, said the following: “There are difficulties in the country; these difficulties are so great that the Party leadership sees no way out; Comrade STALIN is pushing the country toward war, seeing in this the only means of escaping the difficult situation into which the country has been brought.” RUMYANTSEV went on: “STALIN considers it better to perish in struggle—in war with the bourgeoisie—than as a result of the failure of domestic policy.” In other words, RUMYANTSEV said then that Comrade STALIN was leading the proletarian revolution to ruin. As I recall, I expressed disagreement with this idea of RUMYANTSEV's, and KOTOLYNOV, who was present, supported me.

During this same visit, as I have already testified, we, KOTOLYNOV and I, saw Ya. TSEITLIN, who in conversation with us expressed literally the very same ideas that RUMYANTSEV had expressed to us about the country being led toward war.

I recall the following incident: during one of my visits to FAIVILOVICH I met Nikolai DMITRIEV at his home. In a conversation about the internal Party situation it was said that “STALIN is still somewhat afraid of ZINOVIEV.” I do not remember who said this or the circumstances of the conversation from which it followed. This conversation took place in 1932 or 1933.

Question: What do you know about the leadership of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist counterrevolutionary organization?

Answer: Among the leaders of the Leningrad counterrevolutionary organization I know V. RUMYANTSEV and also I. KOTOLYNOV. Throughout all the preceding years they stood at the center and headed the struggle against the Party and its leaders. They headed the duplicitous entry into the Party by the Leningrad Zinovievists (at that time I too was not in the leadership with them). Until recently, members of the organization of counterrevolutionary Zinovievism grouped around RUMYANTSEV and KOTOLYNOV.

During my conversation with RUMYANTSEV in 1932, about which I testified above, I formed the impression that what RUMYANTSEV said did not originate with him but was inspired by someone above him. I believe that he, RUMYANTSEV, was one of those leaders of the organization who until recently remained connected with the corresponding leaders.

In conclusion I want to say that I strove, particularly over the last year, to live entirely by the interests of the Party and the country. However, maintaining organizational connections with members of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization and participating in counterrevolutionary conversations against the Party leadership led to my being a criminal before the Party and the country.

I repeat: our counterrevolutionary conversations directed against the Party leaders cultivated terrorist sentiments, and counterrevolutionary Zinovievism, which in its struggle against the Party leadership developed to the point of employing fascist methods of struggle, is entirely and fully responsible for the murder of Comrade KIROV by NIKOLAEV, a member of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist counterrevolutionary organization. I personally did not know NIKOLAEV, but this does not relieve me either of responsibility for his crime. I ask that my sincere repentance for the crime be taken into account and that I be given the opportunity through honest labor to earn the right to bear the high title of Soviet citizen.

I. TARASOV.

INTERROGATED BY: HEAD OF THE 6TH SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB, KORKIN.
