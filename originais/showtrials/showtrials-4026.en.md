INTERROGATION RECORD

OF FAIVILOVICH Leonid Yakovlevich—

January 3, 1935.

QUESTION: The investigation knows that in 1928 a letter was sent to ZINOVIEV by the Tashkent group of Zinovievists. What do you know about the nature of this letter?

ANSWER: A letter was indeed sent; I do not remember its nature. I think I did not sign it.

QUESTION: PERIMOV A.V. testified that in the letter to ZINOVIEV the Tashkent group advocated drawing closer to the party, “but not dissolving into it” \[1\]. What can you testify about this?

ANSWER: The Tashkent group of Zinovievists, like most Zinovievists, entered the party without having fully overcome their disagreements with it. The Zinovievists set themselves the task of preserving an independent organization upon entering the party. Although I do not remember the content of the letter in question, the orientation “not to dissolve into the party” reflected the sentiments of most of us Zinovievists who were in Tashkent at that time.

QUESTION: Describe the nature of your connections with the Zinovievists about whom you testified previously.

ANSWER: Maintaining connections with individual Zinovievists about whom I testified and discussing their sentiments in fact constitutes the preservation and continuation of organizational connections among Zinovievists.

QUESTION: Did SEREDOKHIN inform you about his connections with Zinovievists and their political content?

ANSWER: SEREDOKHIN said that he was connected with TOLMAZOV A.I. and saw him often. To my question whether many old comrades (we meant former oppositionists) had returned to Leningrad, SEREDOKHIN replied: “Almost everyone has gathered, except you.” I asked about RUMYANTSEV; apart from saying that RUMYANTSEV worked in the District Soviet, SEREDOKHIN told me nothing. I had this conversation with SEREDOKHIN in December 1934 at my apartment.

QUESTION: Did you inform any Zinovievists about the situation in Kazakhstan and the North Caucasus, and exactly how?

ANSWER: Until 1932, I worked in Kazakhstan; in 1932, I was on a business trip to the SKK \[2\]. When meeting PERIMOV in Moscow and REINGOLD separately, I told them about the extremely grave food situation in those territories. I considered and said that GOLOSHCHYOKIN had stayed too long in Kazakhstan and was costing the country dearly. I may have said that the situation created could slow the pace of collectivization in those areas and that the process of economic recovery there would be lengthy. There were no anti-Soviet attacks against the party leadership in these conversations. I think PERIMOV said that the CC of the VKP(b) knew about the situation in Kazakhstan and that the blame lay not with GOLOSHCHYOKIN but with the CC. —

L. FAIVILOVICH.

INTERROGATED BY: KORKIN.

CERTIFIED CORRECT:

RGASPI, F. 671, Op. 1, D. 126, fols. 99–100.

\[1\] See the interrogation record of A. Perimov dated December 25, 1934.

\[2\] North Caucasus Territory.
