INTERROGATION RECORD

of NASONOV, Nikolai Mikhailovich,

January 22, 1935.

In answer to the investigation's questions about the practical counterrevolutionary activity of the persons named in my testimony of January 20, 1935, I testify:

On the basis of joint work in the Young Communist International and certain disagreements with the party, very close relations developed between myself and LOMINADZE, VARTANIAN, FOKIN, CHAPLIN, and others.

This group took shape during the period when the principal leading officials of the Young Communist International were opposing the Comintern leadership and to a certain extent suffered from vanguardism, when there were certain attempts to set the Young Communist International against the Comintern, that is, at the time when LOMINADZE and Lazar SHATSKIN headed the Young Communist International.

Opposition to party discipline and the party's guiding line continued to exist in our group until 1933. The leftist group included LOMINADZE, myself, N. M. NASONOV, VARTANIAN, CHAPLIN, FOKIN, LEVAN, MELIKSETOV, and Lazar SHATSKIN. The basis of the group, as we considered it, was a free attitude toward a number of party decisions and party discipline. LOMINADZE and SHATSKIN also had personal connections with other people, but the connection among the persons I listed above strengthened after LOMINADZE remained living in Moscow following the defeat of the Right-ultraleft bloc. The people who visited LOMINADZE were dissatisfied with party policy.

Our group (myself, NASONOV, VARTANIAN, MAZUT, CHAPLIN, MELIKSETOV, and FOKIN) considered that the Comintern was not developing or deepening the revolutionary movement growing in a number of countries, above all in Germany. The task of fighting for power was not being placed before the fraternal parties as an immediate issue; the Comintern was not responding promptly to the changing situation in a number of countries, as a result of which the principal parties in the Comintern lagged behind events. LOMINADZE held still further-left views on this question; he considered that Germany's immediate task in the struggle against fascism was the struggle to seize power.

In rural policy we considered the party line incorrect regarding agricultural contracting, since the contracting system created a situation in which collective farmers had no confidence in tomorrow; thus we considered that the collective farmer had no incentive to develop agriculture, that through the contracting system the party was creating and deepening additional difficulties in the countryside. On this question LOMINADZE considered that internal difficulties could be resolved only in the international arena. In 1930 dissatisfaction with the party line was most vividly expressed by Lazar SHATSKIN, who maintained contact with LOMINADZE until the last period and had great influence on him. Once SHATSKIN even remarked that he was so tired of all this faultfinding with him that it would be better to agree to expulsion from the party.

VARTANIAN was greatly dissatisfied with the leadership of the Red International of Labor Unions; he considered that it did not ensure implementation of the party line. I shared this assessment.

LOMINADZE was connected with HEINZ NEUMANN. HEINZ NEUMANN, who was connected with our group, had been dissatisfied with the Comintern since 1932, when it began sharply criticizing his leftist errors in Germany. Whereas previously, when he met LOMINADZE, he took a negative attitude toward his sentiments — for example, at his initiative “Rote Fahne” wrote, or the Berlin party activists adopted a resolution, demanding that the Right-ultraleft bloc be exiled to Solovki — after he began to be criticized and scolded in the Comintern for his errors, he became friendlier and grew closer to LOMINADZE.

On the German question LOMINADZE took the position that the fascists had to be forestalled and power seized by themselves. He considered that once the fascists came to power this would mean the defeat of the revolutionary movement in Germany. Thus leftist tactical directives were combined with a defeatist perspective.

The friendship with LEVAN was explained by the fact that LEVAN was dissatisfied with his removal from leading work in Transcaucasia. He was sharply hostile toward the leadership then in Transcaucasia.

On the basis of dissatisfaction with the party line, MAGYAR developed good relations with LOMINADZE.

Myself, NASONOV, VARTANIAN, and MAZUT were connected with KONETSKY, who had been removed from work in the Polish party for pronounced leftist errors.

MAZUT also expressed dissatisfaction with the state of affairs in the Comintern.

Recorded correctly from my words; the record has been read to me —

NASONOV.

Interrogated by:

HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO, GUGB, NKVD OF THE USSR — V. PETROVSKY.
