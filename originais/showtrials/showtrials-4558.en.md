INTERROGATION RECORD

Of SAFAROV G. I., January 23, 1935.

In addition, I consider it necessary to inform the investigation of the following: VARTANYAN belongs among LOMINADZE's closest like-minded associates and trusted persons. During his time in the leadership of the Young Communist International, VARTANYAN was, for the other Lominadze followers in that organization, the most authoritative figure, their organizer and spokesman for their common line. He always made speeches in their defense at meetings of the presidium and secretariat of the Executive Committee of the Communist International when criticism unfolded of the sectarian line of Lominadze's followers in the leadership of the Young Communist International. I believe that VARTANYAN went to work in the Red International of Labor Unions as deputy head of the Central European Secretariat by agreement with LOMINADZE, in order to inform him about German affairs, have contacts and acquaintances with people from that country, and influence affairs. VARTANYAN made strenuous efforts to insinuate himself into German homes and establish acquaintances. As emerges from conversations with FOKIN, in 1932–1933 VARTANYAN, together with LOMINADZE himself, determined the views of Lominadze's followers on the German question. The greatest anti-Party “activity” of Lominadze's followers on German affairs occurred during his time in the Red International of Labor Unions, which coincided with the period of the fascist offensive in Germany and Hitler's seizure of power. MAZUT was in the same leadership of the Young Communist International as VARTANYAN, NASONOV, and others. After this leadership was removed, he transferred to work on Eastern affairs in the Comintern. He was a constant participant in the meetings of Lominadze's followers (LOMINADZE, NASONOV, VARTANYAN, and others), but much more than the other Lominadze followers, he hid behind a double-dealing mask, trying to pass among the “uninitiated” as a one-hundred-percent Party man. My personal relations with him were too strained for him to “speak frankly,” but I remember hearing a favorable assessment of MAZUT from MAGYAR in mid-1932: “Even MAZUT — reserved man though he is — has started talking about difficulties, about wavering (the Party line is meant), about the critical situation in the leadership.” With MAGYAR, MAZUT gave himself freer rein.

FOKIN is also among LOMINADZE's close associates, connected with this entire group both through participation in the leadership of the Young Communist International and through political and personal closeness. In conversations with me in 1931, 1932, and 1933, he always emphasized how highly he rated LOMINADZE compared with the “present leaders,” as he put it. At the same time, during these years he always left me with the impression of a person deeply entangled both personally and politically. He drank heavily, and drinking friendships did much to keep him tied to Lominadze's followers; alongside this, he vainly tried to find his way onto the Party path (he was, among other things, secretary of the Red International of Labor Unions' cell). In 1932 and 1933, I heard him say: “If ‘critical sentiments’ are gaining the upper hand among our people, it is because we are far removed from major practical work.” Like other Lominadze followers, and perhaps more than the others, he avoided speaking about the affairs of his anti-Party group. For NASONOV, FOKIN, VARTANYAN, and MAZUT alike (despite the latter's failure to get along with MAGYAR on Eastern affairs), MAGYAR was an inexhaustible source of counterrevolutionary gossip and legends and a “mentor” on questions of international and domestic policy.

It is characteristic that in 1932 and the first half of 1933 both NASONOV and MAZUT took an intense interest in TROTSKY's counterrevolutionary writings. I remember them persistently asking me how to obtain Trotsky's book “The Permanent Revolution,” which was in the restricted section of the Comintern library, and whether there were articles by Trotsky on German questions in English (NASONOV knows German poorly; MAZUT knows only English). This was precisely when LOMINADZE, as I know from MAGYAR's words, was preaching reconciliation and an alliance with the Trotskyists.

Written in my own hand. —

G. SAFAROV.

Interrogated by:

HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE USSR NKVD — PETROVSKY.
