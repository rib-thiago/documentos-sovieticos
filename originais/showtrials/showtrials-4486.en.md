INTERROGATION RECORD

OF SAFAROV G. I. — January 14, 1935.

(supplementary)

In addition to my previous testimony, I consider it necessary to draw the investigation's attention to the following:

VORONSKY, an old and active Trotskyist, retained his hostile counterrevolutionary attitude toward the Party and Soviet power, as well as his anti-Party ties, until recently. In particular, he met Sergei ZORIN, VAGANYAN, VARDIN, and KAMENEV. He is especially close to Sergei ZORIN, on the basis of their equal bitterness against the Party leadership and their joint drinking bouts. In 1932 VARDIN spoke of encountering VORONSKY drunk by the “National.” He was coarsely abusing the Party's leader STALIN and the Central Committee, and VARDIN had difficulty persuading him to go home. GORBACHEV, in a conversation with me in Leningrad in 1934, warmly welcomed VORONSKY's “amnesty,” his appointment as director of the Institute of Arts, and the “possibility of close relations with people such as KAMENEV and VORONSKY.”

Sergei ZORIN belonged to the anti-Party grouping of VUJOVIC, LELEVICH, DOMBSKY, Zosya UNSHLIKHT, Regina BUDZINSKAYA, and others. He retained his hostility toward the Party and Soviet power, as well as his old ties and acquaintances. VARDIN spoke of this in a conversation with me in the autumn of 1932. In recent years he became especially close to VORONSKY.

KAPLINSKY is an active Trotskyist. In 1927 in Berlin, together with Pyotr PEREVERZEV, he “represented” Trotsky and the Trotskyist organization. A typical counterrevolutionary philistine who sought, for selfish reasons, to conceal his counterrevolutionary work from the Party and at the same time was already hinting at the possibility of rapprochement with the Mensheviks. Precisely for this reason I excluded him from participation in the “international conference” of the Trotskyist-Zinovievist counterrevolutionary bloc. In 1930 I encountered him briefly by the Second House of Soviets. He had then returned from exile and expressed indignation at my return to the Party, saying that “everything began with these betrayals.” By the word “everything” he meant the defeat of Trotskyism. The conversation did not continue because of a quarrel.

Written in my own hand —

G. SAFAROV.
