INTERROGATION RECORD

OF DMITRIEV, Nikolai Alexandrovich, dated 10/1-35.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that illegal gatherings of members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization took place in Moscow. What do you know about these gatherings, and did you participate in them?

ANSWER: I personally attended one gathering of participants in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization at KOZHURO's apartment in November–December 1932. I was invited to this meeting by GORSHENIN during one of my visits to Moscow from Saratov. Those who gathered then were GORSHENIN, KOZHURO, KOSTINA, myself, DMITRIEV Nikolai, and, I think, LUKYANOV.

GORSHENIN had just returned from a business trip to Eastern Siberia and informed those present of his impressions of the “Siberian countryside.” In his report GORSHENIN stated that, in his opinion, there was general peasant discontent in Siberia with Soviet policy and that the situation was so serious that a number of peasant uprisings in Siberia should be expected in the near future. As a way out of this situation GORSHENIN proposed abandoning the policy of collectivization and the liquidation of the kulaks as a class, that is, recommended the path of capitalist restoration. The general opinion of those present sided with GORSHENIN.

I must note that, being connected with GORSHENIN until the end of 1932, I gathered from conversations with him the opinion that a counterrevolutionary group of Zinovievists existed in Moscow which recruited new participants and directed their activity into a counterrevolutionary channel. The meeting of members of the counterrevolutionary organization at KOZHURO's apartment finally convinced me of this. Undoubtedly the Moscow counterrevolutionary organization was connected with the Leningrad one, and in particular one line of contact with Leningrad was ANISHEV, since in conversations with GORSHENIN, when he learned that I was going to work in Leningrad, he asked me whether I was connected with ANISHEV, who had already lived in Leningrad for several years.

QUESTION: What political conversations did you have with FAIVILOVICH when meeting him at his apartment in Moscow?

ANSWER: In conversations with FAIVILOVICH we both expressed the view that the former opposition leaders ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV needed to be brought into leading party work, supposing that with their arrival in the leadership and a changed attitude toward them, the position of all Zinovievists in the party would also change. In particular, speaking to FAIVILOVICH about myself, I indicated that by disposition and character I was the type of party worker, but the party kept me in economic work as a Zinovievist; I linked the possibility of my transfer to party work solely with ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's return to leading work. In conversations with FAIVILOVICH we also expressed doubts about a number of party measures. I remember that in this vein we discussed the party's incorrect solution of the meat problem, its failure to take measures to eliminate the existing shortage of manufactured goods, the excessive burden of taxes and procurements upon peasants in the countryside, and so forth, sliding in the resolution of these questions toward the Rightists' positions.

The testimony was recorded correctly from my words and read by me —

N. DMITRIEV.

INTERROGATED BY:

Officer of the 2nd Section of the SPO — GOLUBEV I. V.

Certified true: Khvatov
