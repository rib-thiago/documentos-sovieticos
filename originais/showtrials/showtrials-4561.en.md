INTERROGATION RECORD

OF SAFAROV G. I., dated January 24, 1935 (supplementary).

I consider it necessary to give the investigation the following additional information:

I met BARANOV Sergey at UFIMTSEV's apartment in mid-1932, when I went there to meet MRACHKOVSKY. He said that he worked at the People's Commissariat of Agriculture and had recently been in the Lower Volga Territory during the sowing campaign. He described the sowing campaign as a complete catastrophe: “Most peasants do not work at all and refuse to work. Only twelve- to fourteen-year-old boys and Komsomol members go into the fields. The peasant has no economic incentive — the harvest is not his anyway.” “What is the point of talking about kulak resistance,” BARANOV declared, “when all the poor and middle peasants without exception avoid sowing? Look what is happening: the Central Committee is ruining the countryside. Economic expediency is not taken into account at all.” “It would be different,” BARANOV said, referring to the fact that in 1929 both he and I worked in the Lower Volga Territory, “if collectivization and sowing were carried out as in 1929, without chasing after speed, on the basis of economic calculation. I fear for Soviet power — things have gone so far. The peasantry is seething with indignation.” When I asked him why he was taking it upon himself to champion the kulak, he replied: “I have worked in agriculture since 1928. I have studied the countryside. There has never been anything like it, and they will not let anyone open their mouth.”

From the words of SIMASHKO, who was present during the conversation, I understood that BARANOV was actively propagating his counterrevolutionary views. SIMASHKO said then and there: “BARANOV keeps rushing about as the peasants' protector and can talk about nothing else.”

Written in my own hand — G. SAFAROV.

Interrogated by —

Head of Section I of the SPO of the GUGB of the NKVD of the USSR — PETROVSKY.
