[Annotation at the top of the document: to Comrade Stalin. G. Ya., January 14, 1935.]

INTERROGATION RECORD

Of VARDIN–MGELADZE Illarion Vissarionovich, January 9, 1935.

VARDIN–MGELADZE Illarion Vissarionovich, born in 1890, native of Georgia, Lanchkhuti district, village of Aketi; resides in Moscow, Lubyansky Passage, 23, apartment 4; Georgian, USSR citizen. Writer, worked under contracts with publishing houses. Member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) since 1907, with an eleven-month interruption from December 1927 to November 1928; was expelled for active Trotskyist activity.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that you have not ceased the struggle against Soviet power and the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks)?

ANSWER: Yes, I acknowledge myself guilty of having, until recently, objectively, despite my statement of a break with the opposition, waged a counterrevolutionary struggle against Soviet power and the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), remaining in fact on counterrevolutionary anti-Party positions.

QUESTION: In what did your struggle consist, and along what lines did you conduct it?

ANSWER: This struggle of mine consisted in participation, together with the remnants that had become active of the counterrevolutionary anti-Party organizations of Trotskyists, Zinovievists, “Leftists,” and the like, and proceeded along the following paths: 1) counterrevolutionary criticism of the policy of Soviet power and the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), and cultivation of distrust toward the leadership of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) and its policy; 2) discrediting the leadership of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks); and 3) concealment and assistance to the activity of forces that had become active among the fragments of anti-Party counterrevolutionary organizations.

QUESTION: What specific forces do you mean when speaking of the activation of remnants of anti-Party organizations?

ANSWER: I know that supporters of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist group, the part of the former Trotskyist organization known to me, the so-called group of “Leftists,” and the “Workers' Opposition,” formally regarded as having broken with these counterrevolutionary organizations and groups, have in fact continued until recently to remain on counterrevolutionary positions of struggle against Soviet power and the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks). I mean to state that the activity of these double-dealers increased, especially in 1932, when readiness for a new attack on the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) and Soviet power was clear.

QUESTION: Which specific persons do you know as double-dealers and participants in the counterrevolutionary groupings you indicate?

ANSWER: 1) Among the Zinovievists, I mean: a) ZINOVIEV; b) KAMENEV; c) NAUMOV; d) MAGYAR; e) SAFAROV, and others.

2\) Among the Trotskyists: a) KAVTARADZE; b) KALANDADZE Fedor; c) ABIKH; d) GASVIANI, and others.

3\) From the group of “Leftists”: a) LOMINADZE; b) STEN, and others.

4\) From the “Workers' Opposition”: a) SHLYAPNIKOV; b) MEDVEDEV; and c) ROZEN.

QUESTION: What do you know about the specific counterrevolutionary activity of the persons you listed among the Zinovievists?

ANSWER: I consider it necessary to state before the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) and Soviet power that the formal withdrawal of participants from the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization, which began in 1928–1929, did not in fact prove to be a real, genuine withdrawal.

QUESTION: What grounds do you have for making this assertion?

ANSWER: The facts known to me that give me grounds to uphold my statement are as follows:

Fact 1: when I returned from exile to Moscow together with SAFAROV in June 1928, some time later I met KAMENEV. On one occasion, around July or August, TARKHANOV and I visited KAMENEV at his apartment, where we spoke about the internal Party situation and our further conduct.

The principal thing that struck me was that KAMENEV, who had withdrawn from the opposition before me and from whom I expected the first support and step, turned out to be dissatisfied with my withdrawal. Both TARKHANOV and I had the impression that KAMENEV was as if saying: “Why did you withdraw, why did you submit a statement? You would have been admitted after some time anyway.”

The next characteristic thing in the conversations between myself, TARKHANOV, and KAMENEV was that KAMENEV, summing up our conversation, said: “You go straight to STALIN.”

KAMENEV had in mind the nature of our statement submitted to the Central Committee, in which the question of leadership had been emphasized more clearly than in other statements of that period.

The fact that the question of leadership, the question of STALIN, was emphasized particularly clearly in our statement and in our conversation that evening displeased KAMENEV.

Subsequent events showed that the reason was that KAMENEV was then counting on certain anti-Party combinations, now with the Trotskyists, now with the Right.

Subsequently, I heard from KAMENEV or someone close to him the phrase: “BUKHARIN has already been devoured.” What followed showed that KAMENEV was counting on a bloc with the Right, as subsequently became apparent.

QUESTION: What do you know concerning the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization's desire to form a bloc with the Right?

ANSWER: Concerning the Zinovievists' desire to form a bloc with the Right, I subsequently learned the following: with SOKOLNIKOV's assistance, KAMENEV held conversations about a bloc with BUKHARIN.

Fact 2: when I returned from exile, both in Moscow and in Leningrad there was a fairly substantial group of former Zinovievists who had not submitted applications for admission to the Party.

In the very first days, I went to the Central Control Commission to speak with YAROSLAVSKY, since it was assumed that we would influence our supporters to help them withdraw from the opposition.

When I turned to concrete work in this direction, I encountered anti-Party sentiments: on the one hand, the influence of people whose position was tied to the position of KAMENEV and ZINOVIEV, people who opposed withdrawal; on the other, I encountered people who, under cover of counterrevolutionary “Left” phrases, resisted the desire to withdraw from the opposition.

With the knowledge of the Central Control Commission, I went to Leningrad to speak with the Zinovievists there.

QUESTION: Which participants in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization did you contact in Leningrad?

ANSWER: In Leningrad I found the following people: NATANSON Musya, who had already returned from official exile, and the leadership personnel remaining from the Zinovievist group: MANDELSHTAM, KATAI, KOTOLYNOV, RUMYANTSEV, CHARNY, and EZHOV, a worker at the Putilov plant. I also met a number of other activists whose surnames I cannot recall.

In Leningrad, as in Moscow, I encountered fairly determined resistance on the question of returning to the Party, resistance of two kinds: on the one hand, of the “Leftist” type, whose clearest representative was CHARNY; on the other, I encountered resistance of the Zinovievist type. Its most typical representatives were RUMYANTSEV and KOTOLYNOV, with KOTOLYNOV being more evasive and remaining silent, while RUMYANTSEV more actively resisted withdrawal from the opposition.

QUESTION: Did they remain on anti-Party counterrevolutionary positions?

ANSWER: The majority remained on counterrevolutionary positions; EZHOV and KATAI caused particularly much aggravation.

QUESTION: Besides Moscow and Leningrad Zinovievists, did you contact any participants in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization in other cities?

ANSWER: During the same period, my next journey was to Vologda for negotiations with VUYOVICH. With him, at DAVIDOVICH's apartment (Polifem), I likewise waged a desperate struggle for two days, persuading him to withdraw. I encountered resistance from VUYOVICH.

QUESTION: What other facts of counterrevolutionary activity by the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization do you know?

ANSWER: In 1929, certain regroupings of a counterrevolutionary anti-Party character were observed among the Zinovievists, amounting to a division among them depending on each person's attitude toward ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV.

Those with a positive attitude toward ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV and their anti-Party positions drew together separately from those with a negative attitude.

QUESTION: Did you personally meet with ZINOVIEV?

ANSWER: Before 1931, I did not meet with ZINOVIEV. Afterward, meetings were rare. In midsummer 1932, my meetings with ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV became more frequent.

QUESTION: Whom did you meet at ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's dacha?

ANSWER: When visiting ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV at the dacha near which I lived, at various times I met LUGOVOY (a former trade-union official), MAGYAR, and ROZEN there.

QUESTION: What can you testify about the political sentiments of these persons?

ANSWER: At ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's dacha, I encountered counterrevolutionary anti-Party sentiments expressed in statements by both ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV about the difficulties of the five-year plan and the like. Both held the opinion that something had to be changed and some new paths sought in the policy of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks).

KAMENEV said that the Party's current policy could lead to the decline of agriculture. In general, Party policy was criticized in a counterrevolutionary spirit and anti-Party opinions were expressed. The persons I listed who visited ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's dacha expressed no objections to the counterrevolutionary anti-Party opinions.

QUESTION: What do you know about the participation of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization in the RYUTIN–IVANOV and others case?

ANSWER: Besides the information published in the press and known to the Party, I know the following:

In that same year, 1932, shortly before the exposure of the counterrevolutionary organization of RYUTIN and others, I found SHLYAPNIKOV and MEDVEDEV at ZINOVIEV's apartment, holding counterrevolutionary anti-Party conversations with ZINOVIEV concerning assessments of Party policy.

I must confess that in 1932 I had an extensive conversation with SHLYAPNIKOV in which he set out his counterrevolutionary views to me. I characterized SHLYAPNIKOV's and MEDVEDEV's positions to ZINOVIEV in very sharp terms, and when afterward I saw SHLYAPNIKOV and MEDVEDEV at ZINOVIEV's apartment, it greatly surprised me.

After the counterrevolutionary organization of RYUTIN and others was exposed, I knew that the Zinovievists' participation in this bloc was being prepared there.

QUESTION: Did you conceal all this from the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks)?

ANSWER: Yes, I concealed all this.

QUESTION: What was ZINOVIEV's attitude toward SHLYAPNIKOV's and MEDVEDEV's counterrevolutionary positions, about which you informed ZINOVIEV after your conversation with SHLYAPNIKOV?

ANSWER: ZINOVIEV said nothing definite to me.

QUESTION: How did you react to ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's expulsion from the Party in connection with the Ryutin case?

ANSWER: I considered ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's expulsion from the Party correct.

QUESTION: The arrested participant in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization NAUMOV testifies that in conversation with him you considered ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's expulsion from the Party incorrect. What can you testify?

ANSWER: Yes, I acknowledge that in conversation with NAUMOV I behaved in an anti-Party manner: I regarded NAUMOV's loyalty on this question as hypocrisy and was indignant at his loyalty on this question, not at the fact of ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's expulsion from the Party.

QUESTION: What can you testify about MAGYAR's political activity?

ANSWER: I know MAGYAR as a Zinovievist who adhered to the group of the so-called “leaderless” (SAFAROV and others). During my time in exile, MAGYAR headed the remnants of this counterrevolutionary group in Moscow and carried out clandestine counterrevolutionary work against the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks). MAGYAR maintained contacts with the remnants of the clandestine counterrevolutionary “leaderless” group throughout the period when I, VARDIN, SAFAROV, and SARKIS were in exile.

QUESTION: In what did MAGYAR's clandestine activity during this period consist?

ANSWER: I do not know precisely about MAGYAR's clandestine counterrevolutionary activity during this period, but I know that he headed the remnants of this counterrevolutionary group, united them, and maintained contact with those in exile.

QUESTION: Which participants in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization adhered to the “leaderless” group during your, SAFAROV's, and SARKIS's time in exile?

ANSWER: Besides MAGYAR, another person who adhered to this group was ZILBERMAN. There were others as well, but they are unknown to me.

QUESTION: After your return from exile and in subsequent years, did MAGYAR's political views change?

ANSWER: After my return from exile, MAGYAR continued to preserve his anti-Party counterrevolutionary positions. MAGYAR expressed anti-Party counterrevolutionary views on questions concerning internal Party democracy and on the place of opposition participants in the Party, considering that they should be trusted and brought into active Party work, and so forth. MAGYAR maintained close contact with ZINOVIEV.

QUESTION: What was MAGYAR's role in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization?

ANSWER: Among the Zinovievists and, in particular, among the “leaderless,” MAGYAR was the person who supplied various kinds of secret internal Party political information. MAGYAR commented on this information in a counterrevolutionary, anti-Party spirit. This was chiefly what MAGYAR's counterrevolutionary work consisted in.

QUESTION: Where, from what sources, did MAGYAR obtain this political information?

ANSWER: MAGYAR had extensive connections among Party officials through his work in the Comintern and at the editorial office of “Pravda.” From whom personally MAGYAR obtained this information is unknown to me.

QUESTION: What do you know about the substance of the information supplied by MAGYAR?

ANSWER: MAGYAR's communications usually concerned questions of the disposition of Party forces (proposed transfers and appointments) and the Party's anticipated political plans and measures.

QUESTION: For what purposes was the information obtained by MAGYAR used?

ANSWER: The internal Party information clandestinely obtained by MAGYAR could only be used for counterrevolutionary purposes in the struggle against the Party.

QUESTION: What do you know about SAFAROV's clandestine work in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization and his sentiments after his formal break with the opposition?

ANSWER: SAFAROV preserved counterrevolutionary positions until recently. Until the last period of my association with him, in 1933, SAFAROV expressed his counterrevolutionary anti-Party views; in particular, he expressed dissatisfaction with the internal situation in the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), dissatisfaction that former oppositionists were insufficiently utilized, and the like.

In 1932, SAFAROV had counterrevolutionary sentiments of the Kamenev type toward Party policy on the five-year plan: that we were approaching some boundary at which it would be necessary to regroup somewhat in economic terms, reduce capital investment somewhat, and so forth.

QUESTION: What do you know about SAFAROV's viewpoint on the question of the alliance between town and countryside?

ANSWER: I know that on this question SAFAROV considered that the difficulties of the five-year plan created a threat of a break in the alliance between the proletariat and peasantry.

QUESTION: Was SAFAROV a supporter of stepping up clandestine counterrevolutionary work?

ANSWER: SAFAROV expressed his counterrevolutionary views frankly in his dealings with us. In 1932, SAFAROV told me that the “Leftists” were preparing a counterrevolutionary platform. In light of SAFAROV's counterrevolutionary sentiments, I also understood his report that the “Leftists” were preparing a clandestine anti-Party platform as SAFAROV's hint that we should resume active clandestine counterrevolutionary work.

QUESTION: What do you know about the clandestine activity of SHLYAPNIKOV and other representatives of the “Workers' Opposition” mentioned in your testimony?

ANSWER: Judging by the sentiments known to me of the leaders of the counterrevolutionary organization “Workers' Opposition,” headed by SHLYAPNIKOV, they did not cease their counterrevolutionary struggle against the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) and Soviet power.

QUESTION: Where and when did you meet with SHLYAPNIKOV, MEDVEDEV, and other representatives of the so-called “Workers' Opposition”?

ANSWER: After 1931, I met and conversed with SHLYAPNIKOV and MEDVEDEV several times, two or three, at the apartment of Party member ROZEN Mikhail Osipovich (resides at Vakhtangova Street, 15, apartment 3), with whom I met from 1931 until recently.

QUESTION: Who is ROZEN M. O.?

ANSWER: I know ROZEN as a double-dealer with counterrevolutionary, anti-Party sentiments, politically of a Shlyapnikov-type orientation.

QUESTION: Whom did you meet at ROZEN's apartment besides SHLYAPNIKOV and MEDVEDEV?

ANSWER: At ROZEN's apartment, besides SHLYAPNIKOV and MEDVEDEV, I also met several times IVANOV, a member of the Party Collegium of the Moscow Control Commission of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks); CHELYSHEV, a member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), who works in the Commission for Individual Amnesty Affairs under the All-Russian Central Executive Committee; and MILLER, a relative of ROZEN.

QUESTION: On what basis do you assert that the “Workers' Opposition” continued its counterrevolutionary struggle against the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) and Soviet power?

ANSWER: In conversations with me at ROZEN's apartment, SHLYAPNIKOV and MEDVEDEV defended their counterrevolutionary positions on all the main questions of their counterrevolutionary platform, condemned by the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) at the Tenth Congress. Once in 1932, as I have already noted above, I had an extensive conversation with SHLYAPNIKOV in which, in MEDVEDEV's presence, SHLYAPNIKOV set out his counterrevolutionary platform to me in detail (I informed ZINOVIEV of this conversation).

From the content of all these conversations, I concluded for myself with definite categorical certainty that both SHLYAPNIKOV and MEDVEDEV remained on their counterrevolutionary positions and had not ceased their struggle against the Party.

QUESTION: Specifically, in what did SHLYAPNIKOV's and MEDVEDEV's counterrevolutionary positions, expressed in conversations with you at meetings at ROZEN's, consist?

ANSWER: On the position of the working class in the Soviet Union, SHLYAPNIKOV and MEDVEDEV considered that too great a burden was being placed on the working class and that it was not in fact master of the state.

On the question of the Party, they spoke of the existence of internal Party repression, the need for broad democracy, and so forth.

On the character of our revolution, SHLYAPNIKOV set out a system of views from which it followed that we should not have striven for a socialist revolution, but should have stopped at a good democratic revolution.

QUESTION: What position did ROZEN take in these conversations of yours with SHLYAPNIKOV and MEDVEDEV?

ANSWER: ROZEN generally did not object to SHLYAPNIKOV's and MEDVEDEV's system of counterrevolutionary views.

QUESTION: What can you testify about the political positions of the IVANOV and CHELYSHEV you mentioned, who visited ROZEN?

ANSWER: I can say nothing definite about IVANOV's and CHELYSHEV's political positions; I know only that both are old friends of ROZEN; in particular, I know that ROZEN at one time sponsored IVANOV for membership in the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks). Both IVANOV and CHELYSHEV usually refrained from joining political conversations in my presence.

QUESTION: What do you know about the political activity of the “Leftists” you named, LOMINADZE, STEN, and others?

ANSWER: I know that the group of “Leftists” is a counterrevolutionary anti-Party grouping that continued the struggle against the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) and Soviet power.

As early as 1928–1929, during the consolidation then taking place among counterrevolutionary-minded Zinovievists, the “Leftists” displayed a desire to secure from the leadership of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) the involvement of ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV in active political activity.

I clearly remember that in a very brief meeting with LOMINADZE in 1929, I encountered such an anti-Party position on his part concerning the involvement of ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV in the leadership.

In 1930, Litfront was organized, headed by one of the “Leftists,” KOSTROV. As is known, this “Litfront” was exposed by the Party as an anti-Party “Leftist” grouping in literature. SHATSKIN apparently also adhered to this anti-Party group. I know from conversations with LOMINADZE that he sympathized with this “Litfront.”

In 1932, as I have already noted above, SAFAROV told me in secret that the “Leftists” were either preparing or had already prepared their counterrevolutionary platform. This greatly alarmed me, and I went to ask LOMINADZE about it. LOMINADZE declared to me with complete categorical firmness that this was slander, madness, and so on, and denied it.

LOMINADZE went to speak with SAFAROV about this. As subsequently became clear, MAGYAR had told SAFAROV about it. This whole affair caused a very sharp quarrel between myself, SAFAROV, and MAGYAR.

QUESTION: Why did you quarrel with SAFAROV and MAGYAR over the question of the “Leftists'” platform?

ANSWER: A quarrel subsequently occurred between myself and MAGYAR because MAGYAR considered that no one should have been told about this platform and that I should not have gone to LOMINADZE with it.

QUESTION: Was the counterrevolutionary platform about which SAFAROV and MAGYAR informed you actually written?

ANSWER: Apart from the testimony I have set out above, I know nothing.

QUESTION: In what did LOMINADZE's counterrevolutionary positions consist?

ANSWER: On LOMINADZE's sentiments, I can say on the basis of conversations with him that they were non-Party, the sentiments of a dissenter, of dissatisfaction and discontent. Everything amounted to the spirit and nature of the “Leftist” counterrevolutionary group.

QUESTION: What can you testify about STEN's political sentiments?

ANSWER: I know of his participation in the counterrevolutionary SYRTSOV–LOMINADZE bloc and his subsequent connections with RYUTIN's counterrevolutionary organization.

QUESTION: During STEN's arrest in the autumn of 1932, you were found at his apartment. For what purpose did you visit STEN, and what did you discuss with him?

ANSWER: I was going to see LOMINADZE, but he was not at home, and I knocked on the door of STEN, who lived next door in the same apartment. OGPU employees opened the door to me. STEN was not there; he came later. This was, incidentally, my first visit to STEN's apartment. But of course I had seen STEN before at LOMINADZE's apartment, and afterward STEN also came there.

Taken down correctly from my words, in confirmation of which I sign.

Il. VARDIN.

Interrogated by:

OPERATIONAL OFFICER OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB — BOGEN.
