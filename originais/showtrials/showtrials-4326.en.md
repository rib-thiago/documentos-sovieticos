TO THE INVESTIGATIVE COMMISSION

From Ivan Stepanovich GORSHENIN, arrested in the case of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization.

Since the investigation in the case of the underground counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization is nearing its end, I consider it necessary to make the following statement in addition to my testimony.

Repeatedly returning during the investigation to an analysis both of my own behavior and attitudes and of those Zinovievists with whom I was in close contact, I have come to the conviction that the ideological and political basis of the Zinovievist organization's existence and anti-Party activity after the Fifteenth Party Congress is disbelief in the possibility of building socialism in one country, which was not overcome in seven years despite the gigantic achievements of socialist construction.

In essence, our Zinovievist organization did not disarm fully, either ideologically or organizationally, and continued fighting the Party and Party leadership after the Fifteenth Congress from the old ideological positions inherited from the Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc.

In turn, disbelief in the possibility of building socialism in one country directly reflects the attitudes of classes resisting socialist construction and hostile to the proletariat and the proletarian dictatorship, in particular the most reactionary part of the petty bourgeoisie, which still has not lost hope of the failure of socialist construction in the USSR.

In general, the behavior of the Zinovievist organization and my own personally over the ten-year period (1925–1935) of its existence, with its panic-mongering, incessant vacillation, unprincipled criticism, sensational fabrications, double-dealing, and so forth, is the clearest indication of its petty-bourgeois nature.

Exposed and defeated at the Fifteenth Congress, finding not the slightest sympathy among the Party masses, and possessing no positive economic or political program that could be opposed to the Party's strictly and consistently implemented general line, the underground Zinovievist organization slid onto positions of negative criticism of the AUCP(b) Central Committee's fundamental decisions and the fomenting of hostility toward the present Party leadership.

Lack of principle facilitated the emergence of attempts to create equally unprincipled anti-Party and counterrevolutionary blocs (ZINOVIEV's connection with RYUTIN's group, negotiations with BUKHARIN, ties with STEN), directed against the present Party leadership generally and particularly against Comrade STALIN, whose irreconcilability toward all oppositions and groupings drove their members into an outright frenzy.

The cultivation within our organization of hostility toward the Party leadership and particularly Comrade STALIN, in the absence of any prospect of realizing the principal goal (the return of ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV to the leadership), could not fail to lead the most irreconcilable, most embittered, utterly class-degenerated elements, such as the Leningrad group that split off (RUMYANTSEV, KOTOLYNOV, LEVIN, and others), to turn to such purely fascist methods of struggle against the Party's leaders as individual terror.

The entire inglorious course and equally inglorious end of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization must serve all Party members as a lesson in the consequences of political errors in fighting the Party and Marxism-Leninism generally, and, in particular, of the incomplete disarmament of groups fighting the Party when they capitulate.

The criminality of the Zinovievist organization as a whole and my own in particular, since I was an active member, is further aggravated by the fact that after the Fifteenth Congress the Party placed full trust in us and provided every opportunity to break with our anti-Party past, yet we not only failed to use this trust to correct our errors but, on the contrary, constantly sought an opportunity to turn this trust against the Party, to use it in fighting the Party leadership.

I acknowledge my guilt for all the crimes I committed throughout the Zinovievist organization's existence and deeply repent my struggle against the Party and Party leadership.

I ask that, along with punishment, I once again be given an opportunity to make amends for my guilt through work in any sector of socialist construction and under any conditions.

GORSHENIN.

January 7, 1935.

Certified correct: A. Svetlova
