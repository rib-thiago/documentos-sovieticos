INTERROGATION RECORD

Of GESSEN, Sergei Mikhailovich —

Dated January 3, 1935.

QUESTION: Tell the investigation about your contacts with members of the Zinovievist organization in Leningrad after your attempt to establish a center there on ZINOVIEV’s instructions at the beginning of 1929?

ANSWER: During the period indicated (since January 1929), I visited Leningrad three times: in October 1929, when I stayed in Leningrad for about two or three weeks; in November 1931 (approximately from the 1st to the 7th); and in February 1934 (five days).

In October 1929, I had quite a large number of meetings with Leningrad Zinovievists, both individually and in various groups. Before traveling to Leningrad, I met ZINOVIEV once (he had just returned from vacation) and conversed with him about the political situation in the party and the country and about political prospects. ZINOVIEV gave me no organizational assignments on this occasion (as he had in January 1929), but merely recommended that I see those Zinovievists whom I knew best, while advising me not to expand the circle of meetings too widely. I remember meetings with the following persons: LEVIN, Vladimir, LEVIN, Mikhail, MANDELSHTAM, Sergei, Maria NATANSON, Vladimir RUMYANTSEV, Vasily LUKIN, Raisa VASILYEVA, Alexei SHURYGIN (now deceased), Foma NALIVAIKO, Alexander MOROZOV, Yakov TSEITLIN, Yefim KORSHUNOV, Vladimir SOBOL, Semyon BABAEV (now deceased), Pavel SURKOV, Semyon KAZITSKY, GAIDEROVA, and others.

With some of them I had political conversations, more or less frank depending on whom I was dealing with; with others (especially at “group” meetings), I sometimes avoided conversations about current political issues. For example, on the occasion of my arrival, NALIVAIKO and MOROZOV arranged a party attended by several other Zinovievists from the Narva district (I do not now remember exactly who was there), and were offended that I avoided discussing topical political questions in that circle. Likewise, at GAIDEROVA’s, where a politically diverse crowd gathered, there were no frank conversations on political subjects. The main subjects of my conversations with the other Zinovievists I met were information about the situation in the party and the country and an account of that situation from our point of view, with particular emphasis on the question of grain supply difficulties as confirmation that our views were correct, as well as questions I asked about attitudes among workers, in the Leningrad party organization, and among the wider circles of Zinovievists with whom these persons had contact.

I do not now recall any conversations of mine in Leningrad about any combinations with the Right. Conversations of this kind could not have taken place for the following reasons (in addition to those mentioned in previous testimony).

In January 1929, before my departure for Leningrad, ZINOVIEV, explaining why a bloc with the Right was impossible, told me: “For the very narrowest circle you may add that, if TROTSKY were with us now (that is, if we were stronger in the party), one could consider the possibility of a bloc with the Right; under present conditions, however, this is ruled out.”

On arriving in Leningrad, I conveyed this phrase, among other things, to EVDOKIMOV, but we decided not to discuss this subject with anyone else.

After returning to Moscow, I gave ZINOVIEV a detailed account of my trip to Leningrad and the meetings and conversations that took place there.

In November 1931, the number of meetings with Leningrad Zinovievists was considerably smaller, which is explained by the brevity of my stay in Leningrad and certain personal matters that took up a considerable part of my time.

I remember meetings with Vladimir LEVIN, Alexander BASHKIROV (we met at LEVIN’s, partly on personal matters), M. NATANSON, A. SHURYGIN, and EVDOKIMOV, who had come to Leningrad for the holidays. Conversations with these persons touched on current questions concerning the country’s political and economic situation, presented in the spirit of the Zinovievist group’s usual positions, which were hostile to the spirit of the party. I do not now recall whether I met ZINOVIEV before traveling to Leningrad, but I can say definitely that after the trip I saw both ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV and conveyed my Leningrad impressions to ZINOVIEV.

In February 1934, of the persons of interest to the investigation, I saw Vladimir LEVIN, Mikhail LEVIN, and MUSHTAKOV. I have previously testified about the meetings with LEVIN and MUSHTAKOV. I did not see Mikhail LEVIN alone; I met him in a circle of old acquaintances of mine, party members unconnected with the Zinovievist group. On this occasion I saw neither ZINOVIEV nor anyone else from the leading center of the Zinovievist organization.

QUESTION: What prompted your trip to Leningrad in February 1934?

ANSWER: The Gosplan of the RSFSR convened a conference in Leningrad on the Greater Dnieper problem, to which I was delegated by the Western Regional Planning Office, where I was then working.

QUESTION: The investigation has established that participants in the Zinovievist organization in Leningrad considered you a representative of the Moscow Zinovievist center. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: I do not know whether they regarded me as an official representative of the Zinovievist center, since this question was not raised, but in any event they considered me someone close to ZINOVIEV, EVDOKIMOV, and other members of the center, and regarded my statements not merely as a personal opinion but as an expression of the positions of the leading center.

QUESTION: What do you know about the existence of a terrorist Zinovievist organization in Leningrad?

ANSWER: I know nothing about the existence of a terrorist Zinovievist organization in Leningrad.

QUESTION: The investigation has established that the terrorist act against Comrade KIROV was committed by NIKOLAEV, L., a participant in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization in Leningrad. Do you acknowledge that the leading Zinovievist group and, in particular, you as a participant in that group bear responsibility for this murder?

ANSWER: On learning of Comrade KIROV’s murder, I did not doubt for a moment that it was the work of White Guards. Even now, knowing the results of the investigation, I cannot regard this act as anything other than White Guard activity. The objectives I pursued had nothing in common with this vile murder.

I fully acknowledge my responsibility for having been an active participant in the Zinovievist organization, which not only had failed to lay down its arms before the party but was also waiting for and preparing the conditions for a new attack on the party. I acknowledge that, had the Zinovievist organization disarmed ideologically and ceased to sustain distrust and hostility toward the party leadership among its supporters, the creation of a counterrevolutionary terrorist organization by people who emerged from its midst would have been impossible. Therefore, the Zinovievist organization shares moral and political responsibility for Comrade KIROV’s murder, and I personally share it as one of its participants.

GESSEN.

INTERROGATED BY: RUTKOVSKY.

Certified correct: V. Stoiko
