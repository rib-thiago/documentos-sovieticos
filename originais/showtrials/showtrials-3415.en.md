INTERROGATION RECORD

Of Grigory Eremeevich EVDOKIMOV

December 24, 1934.

QUESTION: The investigation has established that you maintained antiparty connections with ZINOVIEV until recently. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: Yes, I confirm it. In 1933–34 I met him less frequently than in previous years. For a time before the Seventeenth Party Congress and immediately afterward, ZINOVIEV's attitude was at its most loyal compared with earlier periods. Subsequently, however, his attitude again assumed an antiparty character.

Turning to the facts, I must point out that, in matters of leadership of the international workers' movement, ZINOVIEV and all of us remained until recently on the positions we held during the struggle against the party in 1925–27.

We considered that, in matters concerning the workers' movement in the West, the leadership took a position of “national narrowness,” that is, it interpreted the idea of the victory of socialism in one country in a way that led to underestimating the decisive importance of socialism's victory in the decisive countries of the West.

In our opinion, for the reasons stated above, insufficient attention was paid to questions of the international workers' movement, and at moments when great opportunities arose to intensify the revolutionary movement in a particular country, appropriate measures were not taken in that direction.

The difference between this assessment and our previous positions consisted only in the fact that we had been compelled to abandon criticism of the theoretical propositions developed by Comrade STALIN on this question, since those propositions offered no possibility of criticism on grounds of principle.

When discussing the situation of the workers' movement and the course of events in individual countries (Germany, France, Austria, Spain), and criticizing the measures taken by the leadership, we always proceeded from the positions described, while at the same time we ourselves could not advance any positive program, either as a whole or for particular cases.

Specifically, the question of the united front: ZINOVIEV, who usually informed us about the international workers' movement, formerly held the opinion that the Communist Parties of France and other countries themselves obstructed the establishment of a united front, by criticizing the Socialist parties in a petty way and alienating Social Democratic workers through the character of their criticism, while at the same time failing to advance with sufficient persistence slogans that could unite Social Democratic and Communist workers around demands common to the entire working class.

In my last conversation with ZINOVIEV, in November 1934, he criticized the work of creating a united front from a different angle, accusing the French Communist Party, and thereby the Comintern leadership, of moving toward a united front in France without making any demands on the French Socialist Party, thus handing the initiative in leading the united front to the Second International.

QUESTION: The investigation has established your connection with I. N. SMIRNOV and other Trotskyists in recent years. What can you testify on this matter?

ANSWER: In 1932 I. N. SMIRNOV several times invited me to visit his apartment. I accepted one such invitation, conveyed through Shura SAFONOVA.

I found SAFONOVA at SMIRNOV's. SMIRNOV asked whether I wanted to see MRACHKOVSKY and suggested going to him. I agreed, and we went to MRACHKOVSKY's railway carriage, where we met SAFAROV. When we arrived, VAGANYAN was not in MRACHKOVSKY's carriage; if he came, it was only toward the end of our visit. We had no conversations on political subjects. I explained the fact that SMIRNOV and MRACHKOVSKY did not touch on political subjects in our general conversation by SAFAROV's presence. After this I did not meet MRACHKOVSKY again, and I met SMIRNOV in surroundings (the Kremlin dining hall) that ruled out the possibility of antiparty conversations.

QUESTION: What additional testimony can you give about activities by yourself and others directed against the party?

ANSWER: Early in 1929, when I went to Leningrad for medical treatment with permission from the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), ZINOVIEV suggested that I try to establish connections, as he put it, with at least a few dozen workers who were party members employed in production.

I refused to carry out this assignment, stating that, first, it was an impracticable undertaking, since it would immediately become known and the severest reprisals would then have to be expected; and, second, it did not accord with our course of reintegration into the party.

EVDOKIMOV

INTERROGATED BY:

DEPUTY PEOPLE'S COMMISSAR FOR INTERNAL AFFAIRS OF THE USSR — (AGRANOV)

HEAD OF A DEPARTMENT OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD — (RUTKOVSKY)
