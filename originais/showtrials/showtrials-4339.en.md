INTERROGATION RECORD

Of Yakov Vasilievich SHAROV, January 8, 1935.

Question: In the previous interrogation record of January 5, 1935, you testified that you were a participant in the Zinovievist organization.

Do you acknowledge that this organization was counterrevolutionary?

Answer: Yes, I acknowledge it. Having gone underground after the Fifteenth Congress, opposing itself to the Party and Soviet power, the Zinovievist underground was a counterrevolutionary organization.

Question: What further testimony can you give about the Zinovievist organization's counterrevolutionary activity?

Answer: As I already testified in my previous statement of January 5, 1934, at the meeting in ZINOVIEV's apartment which took place soon after the Fifteenth Congress of the AUCP(b), it was decided to go underground in an organized manner, preserving the principal opposition cadres within the Party by submitting fraudulent, double-dealing statements expressing agreement with the Congress's decisions and renunciation of their opposition views.

Soon after this meeting I left for Ulyanovsk, where I worked until May–June 1928. EVDOKIMOV, with whom I had the closest ties, also arrived in Ulyanovsk after me.

Around August 1928 I returned to Moscow. My first action was to contact ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV.

By this time, that is, late 1928 and early 1929, a number of active participants in the Zinovievist organization began returning to Moscow. Eventually a fairly substantial Zinovievist organization took shape in Moscow, including ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, KHARITONOV, FEDOROV, NAUMOV, GERTIK, GORSHENIN, KOZHURO, KUKLIN, ZALUTSKY, KOSTINA, SAKHOV, BRAVO, and myself. There were a number of other participants whom I cannot now recall but about whom I shall try to inform the investigation in my further testimony.

I also knew of the existence of a number of underground Zinovievist organizations in the provinces. In Leningrad I knew of a Zinovievist organization led by the Leningrad center, which included Vladimir LEVIN, RUMYANTSEV, and MANDELSHTAM. Of the Leningrad organization's participants, I now remember SOSITSKY, KORSHUNOV, KOSTRITSKY, and Alexander ALEXANDROV.

Participants in our organization were located in Rostov-on-Don: GORDON and SOLOVIEV; in Smolensk, GESSEN; in Voronezh, Olga RAVICH; and in Kostroma, REM.

All participants in our organization, both those in Moscow and those in the provinces, were closely connected, meeting one another regularly. In Moscow the organization's most frequent gatherings took place at the apartments of ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, and EVDOKIMOV. All gatherings of organization participants at these “headquarters” took place under the guise of parties and family celebrations. Participants in the Zinovievist organization visiting from the provinces usually also called at these “headquarters.”

At almost all gatherings of our organization's participants, there was a lively exchange of political information accompanied by sharp criticism of a number of fundamental measures of the Party and Soviet government.

Essentially, our organization's political positions remained the same as before the Fifteenth Congress. During my meetings with ZINOVIEV, a number of counterrevolutionary positions were put forward, shared also by most participants in our organization.

Criticizing the Party's measures for agricultural collectivization, ZINOVIEV said that the Party began agricultural collectivization late, that the collectivization plan had not been thought through, and that as a result a “breaking of the alliance” between town and countryside was occurring.

Analyzing the question of the working class's material living conditions in the USSR, ZINOVIEV considered the Party's statement about improving material living conditions to be declarative and lacking a real foundation; workers' material living conditions were not improving but deteriorating, since real wages were continually falling.

The principal conclusion drawn by ZINOVIEV, and with him our entire organization, was that the situation of the working class and peasantry was continually worsening, that the threat of a “breaking of the alliance” between town and countryside was becoming increasingly real, and that the country faced colossal difficulties which should be used to fight the Party for leadership.

ZINOVIEV expressed his counterrevolutionary attitudes to me very sharply in the summer of 1932, when I visited him in Ilyinskoye. Analyzing the country's internal situation and prospects for the immediate future, ZINOVIEV declared: “Even the stones will soon speak, and how long shall we remain silent?”

Accusing the Party leadership of inept and incorrect leadership of the country, a number of participants in our organization, including ZINOVIEV, made malicious, slanderous attacks against the Party leadership, against STALIN.

The principal thing uniting all participants in our organization was disbelief in the Party leadership, which in our opinion was unable to lead the country out of the difficulties facing it (the country). The conclusion we drew was that the difficulties facing the country should be used to fight the Party to bring ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV into the leadership.

These counterrevolutionary attitudes of ours were to some extent encouraged by the circumstance that we considered ourselves “wronged,” unjustly removed from leading work.

Question: Who led the Zinovievist organization?

Answer: There existed in Moscow a center which led the organization's work. This center included ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, FEDOROV, KHARITONOV, KUKLIN, and myself, SHAROV.

Recorded correctly from my words and read to me — SHAROV.
