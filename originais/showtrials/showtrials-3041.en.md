INTERROGATION RECORD

OF NIKOLAEV L. V., 15 December 1934.

QUESTION: Since when have you known KOTOLYNOV Ivan, and under what circumstances did you become acquainted?

ANSWER: I have known KOTOLYNOV since 1922. At that time he was secretary of the Vyborg District Komsomol Committee, while I was the Committee's business manager.

I worked with him for about a year. Our relations became very good. In 1923 KOTOLYNOV was recalled to the VLKSM Regional Committee of Leningrad Region; I continued for some time in the District Committee in the same post under the district secretary KHANIK L.

While he worked in the Regional Committee I met him repeatedly. In 1924, 1925 and 1926 I did not see KOTOLYNOV; if I am not mistaken, he was in Moscow. After his return to Leningrad I did not meet him until 1928; from 1928 my meetings with KOTOLYNOV resumed.

Close relations with KOTOLYNOV began to develop when I started working in the City and Regional Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), in 1931.

QUESTION: How did your relations with KOTOLYNOV develop subsequently?

ANSWER: I knew that in 1934 KOTOLYNOV was studying at the Polytechnic Institute in Sosnovka. The Institute's location near my residence helped our meetings continue. I began meeting KOTOLYNOV in the Institute building. This was in mid-September, after my meeting with SHATSKY in August 1934.

KOTOLYNOV began speaking to me about the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization's work.

He referred to my August conversation with SHATSKY and suggested beginning work. I regarded KOTOLYNOV as a brave, determined man; that was how I had known him when he belonged to the opposition, when people spoke of his determination in fighting the party. He expressed dissatisfaction with the party leadership and STALIN in harsh terms. I answered in the same terms. From this conversation KOTOLYNOV could conclude that I was prepared for the most extreme actions, including carrying out a terrorist act.

At our next meeting, also in September in the Institute building, KOTOLYNOV spoke of terror.

KOTOLYNOV asked whether I was prepared to carry out a terrorist act against KIROV. I replied that I was prepared to undertake its direct execution.

QUESTION: How do you explain why KOTOLYNOV proposed that you specifically undertake KIROV's murder?

ANSWER: KOTOLYNOV knew my political sentiments and knew my character well.

QUESTION: Did KOTOLYNOV explain the purpose of committing a terrorist act against Comrade KIROV?

ANSWER: Yes. KOTOLYNOV said that KIROV's removal would weaken the existing party leadership and that the former opposition had particular scores to settle with KIROV because of the struggle he had organized against Leningrad oppositionists.

QUESTION: Did you discuss with KOTOLYNOV how and under what circumstances the terrorist act against Comrade KIROV was to be committed?

ANSWER: Yes. I told KOTOLYNOV that I had a NAGANT retained since 1918; indeed, he already knew that I had a revolver. It was decided that I would carry out the act with the revolver, alone. We examined possible variants: outside Smolny when KIROV arrived at or left the building; inside Smolny, particularly by entering KIROV's office under a suitable pretext; outside KIROV's apartment; at the railway station when KIROV left for or returned from Moscow. We also discussed an attack at a party meeting during KIROV's report.

It was decided that action would have to depend on circumstances, which would indicate which of the discussed variants should be adopted.

QUESTION: Was KOTOLYNOV to provide you with any assistance in directly carrying out the terrorist act?

ANSWER: I have already stated that KOTOLYNOV worked through the technical preparation and execution of the act with me. I forgot to mention that KOTOLYNOV directly recommended carrying it out outside the Smolny building, considering that this variant could be \[i\] the greatest success, although he did not reject the other variants.

KOTOLYNOV also gathered information about procedures at Smolny and told me that entering the building presented no difficulty. He put me in touch with SOKOLOV, who was to keep watch outside the Mariinsky Theater, where a ceremonial meeting was expected during the October anniversary holidays and KIROV was supposed to attend. This was connected with my and SOKOLOV's attempt to obtain tickets to the theater and attack KIROV there; nothing came of this undertaking, since we obtained no tickets.

This assistance from KOTOLYNOV and SOKOLOV did not relieve me of all the work of ascertaining KIROV's movements which I carried out while preparing the act, as I described in the interrogation record of 8 December and several others. Consequently I am the actual executor of the act from its preparation to the moment of KIROV's murder.

QUESTION: You prepared the terrorist act against Comrade KIROV beginning in September 1931, that is, over three months. What meetings and conversations did you have with KOTOLYNOV during this time, and what instructions and directives did he give you?

ANSWER: During that time I had two meetings with KOTOLYNOV: one in late October and another in early November, apparently on 4 November 1934. We avoided meeting more often because at that time I was preparing the terrorist act at full speed. At the late October meeting I asked KOTOLYNOV to give me one person to help. It was precisely then that he put me in touch with SOKOLOV. I also told him about the failure of the attempt on 15 October, when I was detained and taken to the People's Commissariat of Internal Affairs. KOTOLYNOV advised exceptional caution when observing KIROV.

In early November we agreed that I would carry out the terrorist act in the days preceding the November anniversary. It proved impossible during those days because I did not encounter KIROV. After the holidays I did not see KOTOLYNOV again. We were to meet in early December to determine the direction of my further preparations for KIROV's murder if I had not yet succeeded in carrying out the act; we were also to discuss organizing the terrorist act against STALIN and ascertain from each other what personnel and opportunities were available.

Directly connected with this is my work to involve KULISHER, YUSKIN and ERMOLAEVA in carrying out the act, about which I testified in several interrogation records.

QUESTION: Did you and KOTOLYNOV provide in the plan for KIROV's murder for your possible escape from the scene after the act?

ANSWER: We provided for as few casualties as possible; therefore I was to commit the act alone, which would give greater assurance of concealing the Zinovievist organization's involvement than if there were several accomplices. KOTOLYNOV and I realized that the executor of the terrorist act would have to perish.

In that case, the terrorist act against Comrade STALIN would be organized and carried out by the terrorists remaining at liberty.

QUESTION: Did KOTOLYNOV instruct you how to behave during investigation and trial in the event of arrest?

ANSWER: Yes; it was agreed that if arrested I must portray the terrorist act against KIROV as an individual act. In general I believed I would not remain alive, but at the last moment the thirst for life prevailed and I could not kill myself after shooting KIROV.

QUESTION: Did anyone edit for you the document found at your apartment entitled “ANSWER TO THE PARTY AND THE FATHERLAND,” which contains anti-party and counterrevolutionary statements?

ANSWER: Yes. I showed it to KOTOLYNOV, who read it carefully and edited it. He gave me several directions concerning the document in the spirit of the fundamental ideas making up the Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc's ideological baggage, and I used them.

QUESTION: Did KOTOLYNOV instruct you not to take a job because preparing the terrorist act against KIROV took a great deal of time?

ANSWER: I received no special instructions on this from KOTOLYNOV. I informed him that I had decided not to take a job while preparing the act, so as to have enough free time to carry out KIROV's murder. KOTOLYNOV approved my decision.

Correctly recorded from my words. The record has been read to me.

L. NIKOLAEV.
