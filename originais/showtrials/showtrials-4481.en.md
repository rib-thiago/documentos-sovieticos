INTERROGATION RECORD

OF PERIMOV, Alexei Viktorovich (additional).

Dated 14 January 1935.

QUESTION: Tell the investigation what you know about the existence of the center of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization until very recently.

ANSWER: I know that after the Fifteenth Party Congress, the connection that had previously arisen on the basis of the struggle against the party between the leaders of the Zinovievist organization — ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, ZALUTSKY, KHARITONOV, GESSEN, GERTIK, and others — was tactically not interrupted and continued to exist until very recently.

All the persons listed gathered in varying combinations at the homes of ZINOVIEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, GERTIK, and GORSHENIN and criticized the Central Committee's policy from the positions of the former Zinovievist faction, spoke hostilely of the party leadership, and in individual cases took decisions on tactical questions of the organization.

I personally never attended any meeting at which decisions on tactical or political questions were taken, but I heard, for example, from KOZHURO and others that after ZINOVIEV's expulsion from the party in 1929, the Zinovievists' tactics regarding speeches at meetings were decided in an organized manner at a meeting of Zinovievists.

QUESTION: What do you know about the counterrevolutionary activities of the Zinovievist organization until very recently?

<ANSWER:> In my previous testimony I already said that before 1930 I did not live in Moscow but visited occasionally; when I came, however, I established contacts with the Zinovievists through GERTIK's apartment, which was essentially a clandestine contact apartment. Thanks to this I always knew how the Zinovievist organization reacted to all current questions of the Central Committee's domestic and foreign policy. I received the principal information directly from ZINOVIEV himself, whom I visited whenever I came to Moscow. Later, after moving there permanently, I knew that contacts between Zinovievists in Moscow, Leningrad, and other cities, for example Rostov and Sverdlovsk, continued to be maintained as before and that Zinovievists in the provinces were informed about the center's sentiments. Concerning ZINOVIEV's personal connections with the provinces I can say that YELKOVICH visited him; I saw him at ZINOVIEV's apartment in 1931 or 1932. RAVICH from Voronezh visited him; TSVIBAK was at ZINOVIEV's dacha in 1934. According to TSVIBAK, he spoke with ZINOVIEV about publishing his article in “Bolshevik”. I know that before ZINOVIEV's exile to Kustanai, GESSEN visited him, coming from Smolensk.

Generally, I know that GERTIK maintained constant and frequent contacts and meetings with the Leningrad Zinovievists, and I think that through him ZINOVIEV could have and did have full information about sentiments in Leningrad.

From personal conversations with ZINOVIEV I gained the impression that ZINOVIEV was always very well informed about TROTSKY and his activities. He told me about TROTSKY's positions and the contents of his printed works. In the spring of 1934 I learned from ZINOVIEV that TROTSKY was trying to establish a Fourth International.

I know that in the past, until late 1929, BÉLA KUN visited ZINOVIEV. In the summer of 1929, while visiting ZINOVIEV, he spoke disapprovingly of KUUSINEN in my presence; in any event, BÉLA KUN subsequently ceased visiting ZINOVIEV, and according to my information all contact between them ceased.

QUESTION: Tell us about your activities in the underground Zinovievist organization and your role in it.

ANSWER: To my main testimony previously given to the investigation I can add the following: from the very beginning I was an active supporter of the Zinovievists' maneuver at the Fifteenth Congress. We submitted to the party not because we recognized our views as incorrect, but because we wanted to wait, retreat, and, after waiting for a more favorable political situation, again come out against the party leadership. The participants in the Zinovievist organization remained in these same irreconcilable positions toward the party leadership until very recently.

In late 1929 and early 1930 my personal mood was very sharply anti-party. I considered that collectivization was being conducted too rapidly, that it might play a fatal role in the alliance of the proletariat with the peasantry; I considered that the whole course of collectivization particularly revealed what in our Zinovievist language we called the “empiricism of the party leadership”. Therefore, on the question of the possibility, that is, the possibility in principle, of a bloc with the Rightists, I went further than other Zinovievists, for example GERTIK and GESSEN. During one of my visits to Moscow in late 1929 or early 1930, at GESSEN's, who was then living at the “Paris”, I met the Rightist MATVEEV, whom I had not previously known. I spoke at considerable length with MATVEEV about the Rightists' struggle against the Central Committee, sympathetically on my part. I do not remember the exact content of the conversation, but the next day GESSEN made a great scene over my judgments and, so to speak, corrected my line in the spirit of the general Zinovievist line.

My participation in the Zinovievist organization in 1930–1932 consisted in visiting GERTIK's, where the Zinovievists usually gathered, criticizing various measures together with them, and taking part in counterrevolutionary conversations. At GERTIK's I met BAKAEV, KOSTINA, BRAVO, KOZHURO, GORSHENIN, and KUKLIN. I visited EVDOKIMOV about twice and ZINOVIEV several times; I have already set out the content of my conversations with them.

During 1930–1932 (before the Ryutin affair) I went to Leningrad three times; as I have already said, I met a number of comrades, but I repeat once again that I made all these trips without any assignments from ZINOVIEV or any of the Zinovievists. I do not remember now, but it is possible that after my first trip to Leningrad in the autumn of 1930, when I saw several Zinovievists there, namely Mikhail LEVIN, KORSHUNOV, Timofei DMITRIEV, and MATORIN [1], I shared with some of the Zinovievists in Moscow my impressions of the political sentiments of the Leningrad Zinovievists I had seen. I clearly remember that I did not speak with ZINOVIEV on this subject.

Generally, in conversations with ZINOVIEV I sometimes briefly informed him about the sentiments of various comrades, but the discussion usually concerned Moscow people, since I met Zinovievists from other cities very rarely and insufficiently. For example, I spoke about PEKAR-ORLOV, FAIVILOVICH, and so on.

I know that during ZINOVIEV's stay in Kustanai, Stepan RADOMYSLSKY [2] received some literature for him from RADEK, but I do not know precisely what. I also know that at ZINOVIEV's request Stepan RADOMYSLSKY went to KRUPSKAYA to ask her to help alleviate ZINOVIEV's lot; but I know from Stepan that KRUPSKAYA flatly refused to help him in any way.

In late 1932 my mood, like that of many participants in the Zinovievist organization, was more aggressive than in the preceding period when our contacts had begun to weaken noticeably, that is, in 1930–1931. There was a feeling that it was time to become active, that is, to put into effect the maneuver made at the Fifteenth Congress.

After ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV left, these sentiments began to decline in me and, according to my observations, among other Zinovievists, at least those in Moscow. QUESTION: Do you acknowledge the counterrevolutionary work of the illegal Zinovievist organization?

ANSWER: I consider the very fact of double-dealing to be a fact that profoundly demoralizes the party. Moreover, the existence of a secret underground organization within the party is unquestionably a constant source of counterrevolutionary rumors and actions. Therefore I unquestionably acknowledge the activities of the Zinovievist organization as counterrevolutionary, and also consider my participation in it counterrevolutionary.

QUESTION: The terrorist act against Comrade KIROV in Leningrad was committed by a participant in the Zinovievist organization, L. NIKOLAEV. Do you acknowledge that the Zinovievist organization and you as a participant in it bear moral and political responsibility for this murder?

ANSWER: I did not suspect the existence of a terrorist group in Leningrad. I did not know NIKOLAEV personally and did not even know of his existence. When I learned of the attempt on KIROV, I was certain that it was a White Guard interventionist act. Now, insofar as I know the factual picture of KIROV's murder, I acknowledge that the Zinovievist organization as a whole, and I as a participant in it in particular, bear full moral and political responsibility for this counterrevolutionary act, since our seven years of double-dealing conduct and constant dissemination of anti-party and counterrevolutionary views and rumors inevitably had to poison the political atmosphere, and the instance of the attempt on Comrade KIROV is an indirect reflection of our activities.

PERIMOV.

INTERROGATED BY: RUTKOVSKY

Certified correct: Kazakova

[2] Here and below in the text, erroneously “Radomyshlsky”. Stefan Radomyslsky is G. Zinoviev's son by his second wife, Z. I. Lilina.
