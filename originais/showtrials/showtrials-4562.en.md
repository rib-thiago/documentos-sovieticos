INTERROGATION RECORD

OF MIRONOV, Vladimir Davydovich,

dated January 26, 1935.

QUESTION: At your interrogation of January 14, 1935, you testified that the active Trotskyist BARANOV, whom you met, could have concluded that you sympathized with him. Do you acknowledge that you were a clandestine Trotskyist and that your connection with BARANOV was based on your joint counterrevolutionary activity in the Trotskyist underground?

ANSWER: In 1927, while in Moscow, I met the active Trotskyists BARANOV S. and GRAUSBART A., who acquainted me with Trotskyist documents. At meetings with me, BARANOV and GRAUSBART criticized Party policy in a sharply hostile spirit. At the beginning of our meetings I argued with them, but after a short time I began sharing their Trotskyist views and joined the Trotskyists.

At that time I considered it necessary to declare my Trotskyist views openly to the Party. BARANOV and GRAUSBART recommended that I not do so, for tactical reasons; since I argued this question with them, GRAUSBART or BARANOV suggested that I go to BELOBORODOV to resolve it.

In October 1927 I went with GRAUSBART to BELOBORODOV's apartment. On meeting BELOBORODOV, I told him that I considered it necessary to inform the Party of my Trotskyist views. During the conversation BELOBORODOV asked where I worked, and I told him that I was at the disposal of the Party Central Committee. BELOBORODOV then proposed that I not inform the Party of my Trotskyist views, since he considered it necessary to preserve me in the Party as a Trotskyist. After this meeting I never met BELOBORODOV again.

After the Party congress I met UFIMTSEV, to whom I related my conversation with BELOBORODOV; UFIMTSEV approved BELOBORODOV's proposal.

Soon after the Fifteenth Congress I received an assignment to the North Caucasus. Before leaving for the North Caucasus, I agreed with BARANOV and GRAUSBART that they would maintain contact with me and inform me of the state of affairs.

On arriving in the North Caucasus, I was appointed a responsible instructor of the Territorial Committee. A month and a half or two months after my arrival, a man (whose surname I do not remember) telephoned me at the Party Territorial Committee, saying that he had come from Moscow and wished to see me. I arranged to meet him at Gosshveymashina; he gave me his surname then, but I cannot now recall it.

At the meeting the visitor from Moscow told me that BARANOV or GRAUSBART had instructed him to acquaint me with Trotskyist documents. There was a separate room in the Gosvsheymashina shop where I read the materials. After reading them, I asked whether he would remain permanently in Rostov or had come temporarily; he answered that he would be working permanently in Rostov at Gosshveymashina.

A month later he telephoned me again and we arranged a meeting. At this meeting he gave me new counterrevolutionary Trotskyist documents to read. Three months later I went to the shop myself, but did not find the man with whom I was connected through the Trotskyist underground, because he had left Rostov. I never met this man again.

QUESTION: The investigation knows that you remained connected until recently with BARANOV Sergey as one of the leaders of the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist underground. Tell the investigation about your connections and practical activity as a participant in this counterrevolutionary Trotskyist underground.

ANSWER: From 1928 until the day of my arrest I never met BARANOV anywhere and had no contact with him. I did not know that the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist underground continued to exist after 1928.

QUESTION: Your testimony that you broke off contact with BARANOV Sergey in 1928 does not correspond to reality.

KOSTINA A. P.'s testimony has established that until the beginning of 1933 you continued to maintain contact with the active Trotskyist Sergey BARANOV, particularly meeting him at the apartment of the active Trotskyist UFIMTSEV. The investigation demands a truthful answer about your participation in the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist underground until recently.

ANSWER: I deny KOSTINA's testimony; I did not meet BARANOV Sergey at UFIMTSEV's apartment.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that, as a Trotskyist concealed from the Party, you were connected with the leaders of the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist opposition, from whom you received instructions for further work. Tell the investigation exactly what instructions you received and what you did to carry them out.

ANSWER: Yes, I was indeed a clandestine Trotskyist within the Party in 1927–1928. The only directive I received from BELOBORODOV, BARANOV, and GRAUSBART was not to reveal myself in the Party as a Trotskyist.

QUESTION: You deny your connections with the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist underground after 1928. Yet your own testimony shows that you remained connected until recently with the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist-Zinovievist underground; in particular, you were connected with the active Trotskyists UFIMTSEV and SIMASHKO and representatives of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist underground — BAKAEV and KOSTINA. The investigation demands a truthful answer about your counterrevolutionary connections and your practical activity as a participant in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist underground until recently.

ANSWER: I was indeed connected with the counterrevolutionaries UFIMTSEV, SIMASHKO, BAKAEV, and KOSTINA. As I have already testified, I gave these persons tendentious information on questions that I encountered through my work in the Party Central Committee.

In addition, the Trotskyist GLUSKINA Anna, subsequently arrested for counterrevolutionary activity, once came to my apartment with UFIMTSEV and SIMASHKO.

I did not know that all these persons continued to participate in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist-Trotskyist underground, and I took no part in its work; nevertheless, I consider that my connections with these persons and the information I gave them show that I deceived the Party and was a double-dealer in its ranks.

Recorded correctly from my words. I have read the record —

MIRONOV.

INTERROGATED BY:

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR — LYUSHKOV

HEAD OF SECTION I OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR — PETROVSKY.
