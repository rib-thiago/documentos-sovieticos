INTERROGATION RECORD

OF YUSKIN, Ignaty Grigorievich

dated 8 December 1934.

Question: Relate the content of your conversations with Leonid Vasilievich NIKOLAEV.

Answer: I have met Leonid Vasilievich NIKOLAEV about nine times in all since 1931.

My last two meetings with NIKOLAEV were on the eve of the October celebrations, at my apartment.

In an aggrieved tone, NIKOLAEV complained that he was being sent to work in the countryside without regard for his poor health, that he was going from one Party and trade-union organisation to another and could not obtain a free voucher for treatment. In doing so, NIKOLAEV sharply expressed dissatisfaction with the Party leadership.

A few days later, NIKOLAEV again visited my apartment and again complained of his difficult situation. I treated NIKOLAEV sympathetically, encouraged him and told him: “It is certainly unfair; since you have worn out your health working in the countryside, they ought to give you the opportunity to recover.”

NIKOLAEV told me he intended to go to Moscow and asked me to help him by giving him money for the journey. I asked why he should go to Moscow over such a minor everyday matter; it would be better to approach S. M. KIROV directly. NIKOLAEV answered irritably that for an entire month he had been trying to get an audience with Comrade KIROV, who would not receive him. He added: “It is as though KIROV senses that I intend to kill him.”

Question: What did you answer NIKOLAEV when he expressed his intention of killing Comrade KIROV?

Answer: To NIKOLAEV's words about Comrade KIROV I said that he was saying things unworthy of a Communist Party member. Then I added that our Party does not teach individual terror and pointed out that he should know that it is not the individual who makes history.

Question: The investigation has information that, in response to NIKOLAEV's statement about murdering Comrade KIROV, you said: “Why KIROV? It is STALIN [who must be killed].” Do you confirm this?

Answer: I deny it; I did not say that.

Question: How do you explain that, knowing of NIKOLAEV's intention to kill Comrade KIROV, you told no one about it, either before or after Comrade KIROV's murder, or during the investigation?

Answer: Fearing that I would be considered an accomplice in Comrade KIROV's murder, and afraid of being held responsible, I did not report everything I knew about this case.

I have read the testimony; it has been correctly taken down from my words.

YUSKIN.

INTERROGATED BY:

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO, GUGB, NKVD OF THE USSR (LYUSHKOV)

OPERATIONAL OFFICER OF THE SPO, GUGB (FEDOROV)
