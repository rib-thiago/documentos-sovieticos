INTERROGATION RECORD

of ZELIKSON, Isaak Naumovich

January 17, 1935.

QUESTION: What do you know about terrorist sentiments among the participants in the Zinovievist organization?

ANSWER: During the period of the Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc's most acute factional struggle against the party, I was in Siberia. PEKAR-ORLOV, MYASNIKOV, KOTOLYNOV, CHARNY, KUKLIN, CHERNY, and DYAKOV were there with me. The latter was an instructor of the Siberian Territorial Committee of the AUCP(b) and was particularly hostile toward the party leadership.

Periodically we gathered in a small circle to read various factional documents and discuss the internal party situation.

At that time the factional center in Siberia included PEKAR-ORLOV, MYASNIKOV, and DYAKOV.

At one of our meetings, held in 1927, DYAKOV, speaking of the prospects for further struggle against the party, threw out this phrase: “The entire present party leadership must be physically destroyed.”

I do not remember anyone present at the meeting rebuffing this statement by DYAKOV.

QUESTION: What else do you wish to add to your testimony?

ANSWER: I consider it necessary to inform the investigation of the following circumstance known to me:

During my stay in Siberia I knew from PEKAR-ORLOV, who belonged to the Siberian Zinovievist factional center, that BRIKKE, an instructor of the territorial party committee (Siberian), was a concealed Zinovievist who skillfully disguised his affiliation with the opposition from the party. As a concealed Zinovievist, BRIKKE helped the opposition in every way, supplying our center with various secret party documents and providing information on organizational measures being planned by the AUCP(b) Central Committee against the opposition.

Once PEKAR-ORLOV told me that, while in Moscow, BRIKKE had had a lengthy conversation with Comrade STALIN concerning the political line pursued in Siberia by SYRTSOV. In doing so, BRIKKE slanderously characterized STALIN's assessment of SYRTSOV's conduct.

It must be acknowledged that the information BRIKKE passed to the Siberian center of the Zinovievists contributed considerably to strengthening our factional activity and was highly valued by our organization. At the end of 1927 or beginning of 1928, BRIKKE was recalled to Moscow by the AUCP(b) Central Committee and apparently subsequently worked in the Comintern sphere.

I know nothing of his subsequent ties with the Zinovievists.

A roughly similar role in the activity of the Zinovievist opposition at that time was played by VALDEN, SYRTSOV's secretary. I know for certain from PEKAR-ORLOV and DYAKOV that VALDEN supplied the Siberian center of the Zinovievists with information about meetings of the territorial committee bureau and provided collections of stenographic reports of AUCP(b) Central Committee plenums and other party documents for reading.

PEKAR-ORLOV should know in greater detail about the role of BRIKKE and VALDEN in the organization.

NUSINOV, an instructor of the territorial committee who subsequently participated in the activity of the SYRTSOV–LOMINADZE Right-ultraleft bloc, also knew of the existence of the Siberian territorial Zinovievist center.

Recorded correctly from my words and read by me.

ZELIKSON.

INTERROGATED BY:

ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE OO, GUGB, NKVD — GENDIN.

Certified true: A. Svetlova
