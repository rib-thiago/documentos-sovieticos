INTERROGATION RECORD

conducted on 15 December 1934 by the DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO, GUGB —

LYUSHKOV G. S.

MYASNIKOV, Nikolai Petrovich, born 1900, aged 34, from Syzran, Russian, father a merchant, citizen of the USSR; Leningrad, 4 Dzerzhinskogo Street, apartment 18. Worked in the Leningrad Soviet as deputy head of the Organization Department. Wife Anna Vasilyevna Krokhina, born 1904, housewife, party member since 1927; daughter Rema, aged 10; mother Fyokla Petrovna MYASNIKOVA, aged 63, dependent on her son N. P. MYASNIKOV, also rents out part of her small house; one brother, Aleksandr, former officer of the old army and then Kolchak's White army, died in 1922 after returning home from the concentration camp where he had been held after the White Guards' defeat; another brother, Anatoly, was a party member and served in GPU troops as a political instructor; he committed suicide in 1927 owing to dissatisfaction with his position. Father owned a house, flour and grain business, and horse; rented up to 15 desyatinas of municipal land which he cultivated using hired labor. Education — secondary school level. Party member since May 1917, expelled twice: 1) in May 1927 for factional work (Zinovievist opposition), reinstated in June 1927; expelled in November 1927 for the same reasons, reinstated in July 1928; political worker in various Red Army units until December 1926; in the regional planning organization in Novosibirsk until October 1927; in Leningrad without work from December 1927; head of the Moscow–Narva District Soviet's Organization Department from October 1928; in the Leningrad Soviet from October 1930; member of the latest convocation of the Leningrad City Soviet. Military registration category — command and political personnel, category 9. —

Question: Which Zinovievists do you know personally?

Answer: I personally know Levin Vladimir, chairman of Lenzhilsoyuz; RUMYANTSEV Vladimir — secretary of the Vyborg District Soviet; KORSHUNOV Efim, student at the Industrial Academy; GAIDEROVA \[1\], whose exact workplace I do not know; DMITRIEV Timofei, works at the canning trust in Rostov-on-Don; SOSITSKY Lev, director of the automobile repair plant in the Petrograd District; MANDELSHTAM Sergei, in some design and economic institution; KOTOLYNOV Ivan — I know he studies somewhere.

Question: Did you maintain contact with the Zinovievists you named?

Answer: I maintained personal contact with LEVIN V. and RUMYANTSEV, less frequently with KORSHUNOV and SOSITSKY.

MANDELSHTAM called on me at Smolny about a month ago. DMITRIEV also visited me during his trips to Moscow.

I saw KOTOLYNOV about three years ago, by chance at a demonstration. I last saw GAIDEROVA about three or four years ago.

Question: Which Zinovievists in Moscow do you know personally, and when and where did you meet?

Answer: I personally know EVDOKIMOV Grigory, KUKLIN Aleksandr and BAKAEV Ivan, who live in Moscow. I saw BAKAEV three or four years ago during an official trip to Moscow. I looked him up and visited his apartment. I saw KUKLIN in 1933 on the journey from Sochi to Moscow in 1934. KUKLIN was in Leningrad in May and called on me at work on the eve of the May Day celebrations. At his request I obtained a ticket to the reviewing stand through the Soviet's Organization Department head, Comrade ZELTSER, whom I told it was for KUKLIN. I saw EVDOKIMOV briefly several times in Leningrad in 1932, 1933 and 1934, mostly at Smolny. The last time, in summer 1934, while in Leningrad he called at my workplace, asked how I was living and then asked me to obtain gramophone records for him. Before leaving he came to my home for the records, stayed for a while and left for the station.

Question: What was the character of your conversations with EVDOKIMOV, KUKLIN and BAKAEV?

Answer: When meeting BAKAEV I asked about ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV and, in particular, their state of mind. I do not remember his answer. BAKAEV asked me about the Zinovievists BASHKIROV, ZELIKSON, PEKAR-ORLOV and LEVIN V. My conversations with KUKLIN on both occasions were private; we touched on no political or internal party questions. My meetings with EVDOKIMOV at the Soviet and at home were of exactly the same character.

Question: How can you characterize the political sentiments of V. LEVIN, RUMYANTSEV, KORSHUNOV and SOSITSKY?

Answer: On the basis of conversations on party and political subjects and personal observations, I must say that all the comrades named in the question are steadfast in party terms and firmly support the party's general line.

I must add that I am also personally connected with the former Zinovievist SHIRYAEV Dmitry, who frequently visits my home and has the same outlook as the comrades mentioned above. —

MYASNIKOV.

Interrogated by —

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO, GUGB: (LYUSHKOV)
