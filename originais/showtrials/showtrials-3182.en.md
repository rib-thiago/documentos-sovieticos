INTERROGATION RECORD

OF NIKOLAEV Leonid Vasilyevich

20/XII-34.

QUESTION: What further testimony can you give about your contacts with the consulates in the city of Leningrad?

ANSWER: I have decided to tell the whole truth about my contacts with the consulates in Leningrad.

My relations with the Latvian consul went much further than I testified during the investigation.

I had several meetings with the consul at which I told him that I belonged to an underground anti-Soviet organization, informed him of the nature of our organization, some of its members, and its relations with the AUCP(b).

The consul showed great interest in my statement and showered me with questions, seeking to ascertain what persons belonged to the organization; he was interested in establishing what the organization had in common with the former Leningrad opposition.

I asked the consul to put our group in touch with TROTSKY, observing (that the consul had it in his power to give us such assistance) that we would be very grateful to him.

I indicated that we were always ready to help the consul by giving an accurate account of what was happening within the Soviet Union; here I had in mind the conversation with SHATSKY and KOTOLYNOV about the need to interest the consulates in materials of an anti-Soviet character concerning the Soviet Union's internal situation, about which I wrote in my testimony of 14 December 1934.

I then asked the consul to give us financial assistance, indicating that we would return the money received from him as soon as our financial circumstances changed.

At the next meeting, the third or fourth, in the consulate building, the consul told me that he agreed to meet my requests and handed me five thousand rubles.

At the same time he said that he could establish contact with TROTSKY if I handed him some letter from the group to TROTSKY.

I informed KOTOLYNOV of my conversation with the consul, handing him four thousand five hundred rubles of the money received and keeping five hundred rubles for myself.

Delivery of the letter to the consul kept being postponed, as KOTOLYNOV delayed handing it to me for reasons unknown to me.

Once I went to see the consul on one of the agreed days. When I reached the consulate building, a car bearing registration No. 10168, with a red line on the license plate, stood at the entrance; as I understood it, it belonged to the military authorities.

I became afraid that I would be arrested, so I left quickly.

Then intensive work keeping watch on KIROV began, which took up a great deal of my time.

Recorded correctly from my words. I have read the record.

L. NIKOLAEV.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE EKO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR — MIRONOV

ASSISTANT TO THE HEAD OF THE EKO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR — DMITRIEV.
