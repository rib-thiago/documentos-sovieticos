INTERROGATION RECORD

OF TIMOFEYEV, Evgeny Timofeyevich

January 2, 1935.

TIMOFEYEV, Evgeny Timofeyevich, born in 1904, native of the village of Novgorodka, Velye volost, Opochka uezd, former Pskov province, from an employee family (father a Glavlit representative, died in 1933; mother a physician), Jewish, USSR citizen, residence: Leningrad, Slutskaya Street, building No. 5, apartment 22. Head of the Production Department of the Nevsky “Lenin” Machine-Building Plant. Married. Wife TARTAKOVSKAYA Yuliya Borisovna, 34 years old, engineer at the Nevsky “Lenin” Plant, currently under arrest, VKP(b) member, participant in the Zinovievist opposition. Daughter Tatyana—7 years old. Mother—TRAKHTENBERG Sofya Vulfovna, 55 years old, physician at a dispensary in Moscow. Res\<ides\> in Moscow, Tikhvinsky Lane, 12, apartment 21. Without property. Means of subsistence—a salary of 1,000 rubles a month. Education: higher specialized. Graduated from the Electromechan\<ical\> Fac\<ulty\> of the Len\<ingrad\> Polytechnic Institute in 1930. VKP(b) member since 1919. Expelled from the party on October 11, 1927, for factional work, and reinstated on September 11, 1928. Conducted factional work in Leningrad from 1925 to 1928. Was secretary of the Ekaterinoslav Provincial Committee of the KSMU in 1919. Currently, until his arrest, a member of the Volodarsky District Committee of the VKP(b). No convictions. Was investigated for economic offenses: 1) in 1930–1931, by the People's Investigator of the Volodarsky district for allowing defective production; 2) in 1931–1932, by the Regional Prosecutor for delaying a worker's proposal—both cases discontinued. Arrested in January 1928 by the OGPU Plenipotentiary Representation in the Leningrad Military District, held for sixteen hours and released without charges. Reserve military registration category—T-6, technical personnel on special registration at the plant. Did not serve with the Whites. Was in territory occupied by Denikin's White Army in Ekaterinoslav from May 5, 1919, to December 29, 1919.

QUESTION: When did you join the Zinovievist opposition, and what practical factional work did you perform?

ANSWER: At the end of 1925, I worked as a workshop organizer at the “Krasny Vyborzhets” fac\<tory\>. At the Twenty-Second Provincial Conference, I joined the opposition. KUKLIN drew me into factional work around February 1926. He introduced me to DMITRIYEV Timofei and GUSEV (from Molokosoyuz). DMITRIYEV T. was the district leader, and the three of us constituted the district group of three. By the end of 1926, DMITRIYEV was transferred to liaison with the region; I do not remember whether GUSEV withdrew or left, and I remained in charge of the Vyborg dist\<rict\> opposition organization. There were connections with several factories: “Krasny Vyborzhets” (KRASAVIN, KATTAI, BUSHUYEV, ROMANOV and others), “Krasny Mayak” (KOCHNOV), the powder works (VYSOTSKY, MIRSKY, GUSEV, KOVROV and others), “Krasnaya Nit” (BULYGINA), “Krasny Oktyabr” (SOSITSKY), and the optical factory (KRYLOV).

We distributed opposition literature, provided information and instructions, recruited new sympathizers, and thus conducted every kind of anti-party work. At the higher level, I was connected with the so-called Leningrad center, and in 1927 I was included in its membership. Personally, I frequently met KUKLIN, YAKOVLEV M., ROTSKAN, SAFAROV and BAKAYEV on opposition business. During visits by Muscovites to Leningrad, I was invited to meetings attended by ZINOVIEV, TROTSKY, SMILGA, RADEK and PREOBRAZHENSKY. During my trips to Moscow, I also saw and spoke with FYODOROV Gr\<igory\>, RAVICH, RAKOVSKY, GERTIK, KAMENEV and SHAROV. In addition, there was a connection in Leningrad with a group of Trotskyists, of whom I remember IVANOV Mikh., SUGAN, BOGOLEPOV, FRUMKIN and BRONSHTEIN (an old woman).

In October 1927, I was expelled from the party for factional work. Despite this, I actively continued opposition activity until the conclusion of the Fifteenth Party Congress (January 1928).

During the Fifteenth Congress, when ZINOVIEV and his closest circle submitted a statement that they were convinced of the erroneousness of their line, I was extremely indignant at this action, considering it double-dealing on their part on the one hand, and betrayal toward us (the more rank-and-file oppositionists) on the other. I considered it impossible to declare to the party that I was convinced we were wrong when in reality I did not think so. Soon the CC sent me to work in Semipalatinsk. In March 1928, I wrote a statement to the Central Control Commission indicating that I fully shared the general line but thought that it was following the path proposed by the opposition. The statement was unsatisfactory. In June 1928, I was able to write a satisfactory statement and express my disagreement with the opposition's platform for the Fifteenth Congress. I must acknowledge that some remnants of oppositional views still existed in me then, and it seemed to me that in 1928 the party was doing on the rural question what the opposition had proposed in 1925. I overcame these last remnants only around 1929. From Semipalatinsk, I corresponded with former Zinovievists of the Vyborg dist\<rict\>. Besides Leningrad, I maintained active correspondence with Sarkis (Minusinsk), SAFAROV (Achinsk) and VARDIN (Biysk).

In September 1928, I was reinstated in the party by the Central Control Commission and returned to Leningrad.

QUESTION: With which former Zinovievists did you preserve connections after 1928?

ANSWER: I had no connections, either political or organizational, with Zinovievists from 1928 onward. In Leningrad, I had several chance meetings with individual oppositionists: in 1928, with RUMYANTSEV, I think in KOTOLYNOV's presence; I do not remember whether at RUMYANTSEV's apartment or on October 25 Av\<enue\>; in 1929, with KATAI at my apartment; he asked my advice on whether to seek reinstatement in the party. He was very pessimistic and wanted to submit no further appeals. I recommended that he submit a statement to the CC; with KRYLOV—he was coming to see me, and we met on the stairs. There was an insignificant five-minute conversation. GUSEV, KOVROV and KOCHNOV each visited once, separately. The conversation concerned everyday and economic matters (at the plant). In 1931–1932, I met BEREZIN Ivan, who worked in the same workshop. In 1933, I met Gr\<igory\> FYODOROV in the NKTP waiting room. In that same year, 1933, I met MOGILNITSKY at the NKTP. In 1933–1934, I met ROTENBERG several times at Glavenergoprom. In 1934, I saw SOSITSKY at the city conference. I met LEVIN Vl. on the “Krasnaya Strela” on the way to Moscow. In January 1934, DMITRIYEV Timofei came from Rostov to our plant for tractor parts. I had two conversations with him in my office at the plant, exclusively about his business. In early 1933, I met FANTIKOV on the street; he merely asked whether I still lived in the same place. In 1934, FANTIKOV sent me a letter from a village in the Western Region, in which he wrote that, if I answered, he would write to me about abuses in the collective farm where he lived. I understood this letter from FANTIKOV as an appeal to his former ideological leader in the Zinovievist opposition, wishing to give me material for criticizing party policy in the countryside. Having understood this, I did not answer FANTIKOV's letter.

Throughout this time, I was aware of TARTAKOVSKAYA's sentiments and connections. I know that in November 1934, while in Moscow, TARTAKOVSKAYA met SHAROV, SHEPSHELEVA \[1\] and A. ILYINA, once at SHAROV's apartment. TARTAKOVSKAYA told me that SHAROV was dissatisfied with his position and resented the party for keeping him in disgrace. TARTAKOVSKAYA said that during this trip to Moscow she met EVDOKIMOV in MIKOYAN's waiting room.

The testimony has been recorded correctly from my words and read by me:

TIMOFEYEV.

INTERROGATED BY: GOLUBEV.
