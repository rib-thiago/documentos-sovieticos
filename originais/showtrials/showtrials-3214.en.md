INTERROGATION RECORD

of the accused ZVEZDOV Vasily Ivanovich

23 December 1934.

QUESTION: What details do you know concerning NIKOLAEV Leonid's recruitment into the ranks of the Zinovievist opposition and the struggle against the party?

ANSWER: The history of NIKOLAEV Leonid's recruitment into the Zinovievist opposition dates to his work in the apparatus of the Vyborg District Committee of the Komsomol in 1924, when opposition sentiments gradually began to take shape and the training of opposition cadres and their rapprochement with the future opposition leaders began: ZINOVIEV, ZALUTSKY, EVDOKIMOV, SAFAROV, and others, who then showed great interest in work among young people, particularly our Vyborg organization. These persons began to appear frequently at our Komsomol festivities and meetings and to give us substantial support and patronage in promoting Komsomol members from our district to leading work. In this way KOTOLYNOV Ivan (to work in the IKKIM and as a CC member), RUMYANTSEV (as a member of the Komsomol CC), TROFIMOV (as a member of the Komsomol CC), ANTONOV (as a member of the Provincial Committee), TSARKOV (as a member of the Komsomol CC), and TOLMAZOV Andrei (as a member of the Komsomol CC) were promoted.

The special patronage shown by ZINOVIEV, EVDOKIMOV, and others toward our Vyborg organization soon gave rise to those traditions of cliquishness, favoritism, and, I would say, the feeling of “Vyborg patriotism” that were so characteristic of us throughout the entire period of struggle against the party.

NIKOLAEV Leonid's recruitment into the core cadres of the Zinovievist opposition dates to this moment.

NIKOLAEV Leonid was then one of those closest to the leading workers of the Komsomol District Committee: KOTOLYNOV Ivan, TSARKOV Nikolai, VINOGRADOV Vasily, and NIKANOROV Vasily, who by early 1925 had turned the Vyborg organization into a base for rallying opposition forces and developing an offensive against the party leadership.

I myself was then the responsible organizer of the Komsomol collective at the Karl Marx factory and encountered NIKOLAEV only during my visits to the District Committee.

I became closer to NIKOLAEV in 1925 in Luga, through our joint work in the apparatus of the Komsomol Uyezd Committee, where I worked as head of the organizational department and NIKOLAEV as head of the general department. We lived in the same room.

By virtue of his official position, NIKOLAEV attended all meetings of the Uyezd Committee bureau, taking an active part in resolving the questions discussed.

The Zinovievist opposition's struggle against the party, which developed widely at this time, swept up all the workers of the Komsomol Uyezd Committee, who joined the opposition. NIKOLAEV Leonid, having determined his attitude toward the opposition even before arriving in Luga, and being closely connected with me personally, was constantly informed of all the opposition's informational materials, as well as the organizational and practical measures we planned.

The direct exponents of opposition views in Luga were myself, ZVEZDOV, and IVANOV Matvei. We carried out extensive organizational and propaganda work there, distributing printed materials which we in turn received in Leningrad from SEREDOKHIN Alexander, TSARKOV Nikolai, and RUMYANTSEV Vladimir, with whom we maintained organizational contact, meeting at clandestine apartments in Leningrad.

We carried out almost all the work of disseminating opposition views and printed opposition materials using the Uyezd Committee apparatus, which was entirely under our influence; NIKOLAEV, who consistently implemented our common line, gave us considerable assistance in this respect.

Altogether I was in Luga for one year and several months, that is, until March 1926. I left because I was removed from work and transferred to Pskov. NIKOLAEV remained there for several more months, until early 1927.

The fact that NIKOLAEV suffered no consequences whatsoever for belonging to the opposition, and was not even called before the Control Commission, is explained by our tactics of preserving our cadres within the AUCP(b). I have already testified in detail about these tactics during previous interrogations.

Within the Komsomol and the party we deliberately left concealed supporters of the opposition, who were not allowed to speak openly in favor of the opposition at meetings or in the press; they carried out our assignments to collect information about conditions in the party apparatuses and the control commissions' measures against oppositionists. Besides NIKOLAEV Leonid, such concealed Zinovievists included DRYAZGOV Grigory, UKKONEN, VASILIEV Konstantin, and others.

In 1928, NIKOLAEV worked at factory No. 7, maintaining close contact with ANTONOV Nikolai, SOKOLOV Georgy \[1\], VINOGRADOV Vasily, TSARKOV Nikolai, and MURAVIEV Mikhail. The leading role in the Zinovievist underground of Vyborg District at that time belonged to TSARKOV Nikolai.

During my last meeting with NIKOLAEV Leonid, in the spring of 1934, held at the premises of the “Association of Former Komsomol Comrades-in-Arms,” NIKOLAEV told me of his distress over his expulsion from the Communist Academy. He also shared his sentiments with me, which undoubtedly revealed his inner dissatisfaction with his personal situation. I testified in detail about this meeting with NIKOLAEV during previous interrogations.

At that time I already knew that NIKOLAEV was a member of our underground Zinovievist organization and that he was personally connected with KOTOLYNOV Ivan.

The record was written down correctly from my words; read by me.

ZVEZDOV

Interrogated by —

Deputy head of the SPO of the UGB — STROMIN

Certified true. — Potrokhova
