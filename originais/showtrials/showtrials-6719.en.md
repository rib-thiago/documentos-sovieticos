[Resolution by L. Kaganovich: To the PB members. L. Kaganovich]

[Signatures of Politburo members: Molotov, A. Mikoyan, A. Andreev, V. Chubar, Ya. Rudzutak]

October 23, 1935.

TO THE POLITBURO OF THE CENTRAL COMMITTEE OF THE ALL-UNION COMMUNIST PARTY (BOLSHEVIKS)

Comrade STALIN.

On returning from leave, I read Zinoviev's report concerning me.

The only truth in it is that I did not meet Zinoviev. The rest is confusion and unconscious or conscious slander, explained by the settling of old political scores.

It is untrue that I supposedly had a conversation with Kamenev in 1932. In reality I declined all Kamenev's invitations to visit him in 1932. My last conversation with Kamenev in which political questions were touched upon was actually in the first half of 1931.

It is an absolute fabrication that on that occasion I gave Kamenev a “general assessment of the situation,” and an “arch-Rightist” one at that. (What does that mean in Zinoviev's mouth?) On no even remotely substantial point of Party policy could I at that time have said anything differing from the Party's line and decisions, for the simple reason that I fully agreed with them. Zinoviev “remembered words” (whose?), which, however, he does not dare directly call mine, yet ambiguously tries to foist upon me.

On the contrary, I detected “Rightist” sentiments in Kamenev during that conversation. Considering that listening to him placed me in a false position, I decided to break off relations with him, which I did.

What confirms my actual attitude toward Party positions at that time? I point to my speech at the general meeting of the London Party organization exposing Preobrazhensky, who defended the principles of the Right-Leftist (Syrtsov) bloc, and to my struggle against Preobrazhensky's attempts at circle activity.

As early as 1927, Zinoviev declared me a “Rightist working for Stalin” (because of my speech at the Geneva Economic Conference). After I refused to sign the Trotskyist-Zinovievist opposition's platform and left it, Zinoviev declared: “We must declare Sok[olnikov] an extreme Rightist in order to prevent Stalin from using him as a working force.” I think Zinoviev's present “recollections” are colored by his old formula.

G. Sokolnikov.
