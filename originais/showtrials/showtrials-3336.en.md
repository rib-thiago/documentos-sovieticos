INTERROGATION RECORD

of TARASOV, Ivan Ivanovich. December 19, 1934.

QUESTION: What do you know about the membership of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization, and with whom did you personally maintain connections?

ANSWER: I do not know the organization's entire membership. I know the following members of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization, with whom I maintained constant (for example, KOTOLYNOV and FAIVILOVICH) or occasional contact.

I. I. KOTOLYNOV: I know him well and am more closely connected with him. KOTOLYNOV, together with RUMYANTSEV in Leningrad, directed the work of implementing among Zinovievist youth the instructions given by ZINOVIEV concerning a maneuvering return to the Party. Later I met KOTOLYNOV less frequently. I remember being here in Leningrad with KOTOLYNOV in 1932. We visited Ya. TSEITLIN and BOGOMOLNY. At the latter's apartment, among other questions, we discussed the possibility of bringing ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV into the Party leadership; I do not remember the details of this conversation.

In the summer of 1932 KOTOLYNOV and his wife were passing through Moscow and stayed one day. On that day KOTOLYNOV and I visited SAFAROV with our wives. We had no serious political conversations.

In 1933 I was on a business trip to Leningrad and stayed with KOTOLYNOV. This time we did not go to visit any of our old comrades. KOTOLYNOV and I discussed this; we then considered that our connections with participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc were political and that maintaining them openly might compromise us. In December 1933 KOTOLYNOV was in Moscow and stayed with me. We went together to SAFAROV's but did not find him at home. One evening KOTOLYNOV and I visited FAIVILOVICH. I have already conveyed the principal contents of our conversations at FAIVILOVICH's apartment. I can add that we also exchanged information about who had passed the Party purge and how; BOGDAN's suicide was discussed, and the matter was presented roughly as follows: BOGDAN had difficulties at work; the Party organization's insensitive attitude toward him was noted, allegedly constituting one of the reasons that drove him to suicide. FAIVILOVICH said that a number of former oppositionists had attended BOGDAN's funeral; we then condemned the very fact of participation in the funeral.

I did not see KOTOLYNOV in 1934.

L. Ya. FAIVILOVICH: he visited me once in 1932; we had no political conversations then. I visited FAIVILOVICH several times, most recently in early 1934. Of all these meetings, the most substantial conversations I remember were in December 1933; I have already testified about their contents.

I. P. BAKAEV: I visited him, I think, in 1932, asking him to help me find work that I could combine with study. He directed me to “Moges”; I failed to find work there and visited BAKAEV again, likewise without result. During my visit BAKAEV told me about his wife KOSTINA's speech at VIND in defense of ZINOVIEV in connection with the SLEPKOV case. He then said: “She should not have spoken; the woman did something foolish.” I do not remember other conversations connected with this.

KHARITONOV: I visited him once two or three years ago on a personal matter (I asked him to obtain a set of drafting instruments). He received me very reservedly and I had no conversations with him.

SAFRONOV: as I have already testified, I visited him in 1934 with KOTOLYNOV and did not see him again.

BOGOMOLNY: I know him through KOTOLYNOV. I visited him in 1932 with KOTOLYNOV and did not meet him again.

RUMYANTSEV: both during the open struggle against the Party and in the subsequent period of maneuvering entry into the Party he was one of the leaders of opposition youth (together with KOTOLYNOV and myself). Later I saw RUMYANTSEV on my visits to Leningrad in 1931 and 1932. I recall RUMYANTSEV saying that he had been assigned to deliver a report in connection with the SLEPKOV–ZINOVIEV case, that speaking against ZINOVIEV was very difficult for him, but that he would nevertheless give the report.

NAUMOV: I visited him several times at different periods; I think we had no conversations of a political character.

Ya. TSEITLIN: I met him several times at different periods. He is a sick, embittered man, but I had no serious political conversations with him, since owing to his illness he is very unstable.

I. TARASOV.

Interrogated by: HEAD OF THE 6TH SECTION OF THE SPO — KORKIN.
