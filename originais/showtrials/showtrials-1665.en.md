RECORD OF INTERROGATION 

 NIKOLAEV Leonid Vasilyevich 

 dated December 3, 1934 

 QUESTION: How did you get into Smolny on December 1?

 ANSWER: I entered using my Party membership card.

 QUESTION: When did you arrive at Smolny?

 ANSWER: At approximately \<at\> 13 h\<ours\> 30 m\<inutes\>, and I stayed there until 14 h\<ours\> 30 m\<inutes\>; then I left and returned at 16 h\<ours\> 30 m\<inutes\>.

 QUESTION: How did you spend that first hour inside the Smolny building?

 ANSWER: First I approached Comrade DENISOVA, an instructor of the Regional Party Committee, whose office is next to that of another employee, PLATONOVSKAYA. I have known DENISOVA personally since 1923. I asked her to give me a ticket for the meeting of Party activists, but she refused, explaining that there were no tickets available. Then, on the same third floor, I met an employee of the City Committee of the AUCP(b), SHITIK-SHNEIERSON (of the newspap\<er\> sector); I also asked her for a ticket and was told that she herself did not yet have one.

 Along the same corridor I then met LARIN, a responsible instructor of the City Committee, and asked LARIN for a ticket to the Party activists’ meeting, but again received a refusal. After that I met SMIRNOV, the head of the Regional Committee’s Party Personnel Section, and asked him for a ticket near his office. He replied that he, SMIRNOV, had nothing to do with distributing tickets. SMIRNOV directed me to Room 450 to obtain a ticket, but I did not go there because I knew I had no personal acquaintances in that room. Then I went to PETROSHEVICH, secretary of the agricultural group, entered his office on the left side of the main corridor, and spoke with him for about five minutes, first about general matters of no significance and then also asked him for a ticket. PETROSHEVICH told me that he currently had eight tickets and that, if one remained, he would give it to me. For this purpose PETROSHEVICH asked me to come back in the evening. Then I went downstairs, left the Smolny building, and walked for an hour along Tverskaya Street and Ochakovskaya Street, came out onto Sovetskaya Street, and returned to Smolny. Upon reaching the third floor, I went into the lavatory, relieved myself, and, on leaving, turned left. After taking two or three steps, I saw Sergei Mironovich KIROV approaching me along the right-hand wall of the corridor at a distance of fifteen to twenty paces. When I saw Sergei Mironovich KIROV, I first stopped and turned my back to him, so that when he passed me I was looking after him, at his back. After letting KIROV go ten to fifteen paces ahead of me, I noticed that there was nobody else anywhere near us for a considerable distance. I then followed KIROV, gradually catching up with him. When KIROV turned the corner to the left toward his office, whose location I knew well, that entire section of the corridor was empty. I ran up to within about five paces of him, drawing a Nagant revolver from my pocket as I ran, pointed the muzzle at KIROV’s head, and fired one shot into the back of his head. KIROV immediately fell face down.

 I turned back to prevent an attack on me from behind, cocked the hammer, and fired, intending to hit myself in the temple. As I was cocking the hammer, a man wearing a GPU uniform rushed out of the office opposite, and I hurried to shoot myself. I felt a blow to my head and fell down. When I regained consciousness and gradually began to come to my senses, I thought that I was about to die. Someone ran up to me, they began examining me, and I was carried into a room.

  

 Recorded correctly from my words and read back to me.

  

 L. NIKOLAEV

  

 INTERROGATED BY:

  

 Deputy Head of the Special Department (OO) of the Main Directorate of State Security (GUGB) – SOSNOVSKY
