INTERROGATION RECORD

Of RUSANOV, Ivan Mikhailovich

dated January 11, 1935.

RUSANOV, Ivan Mikhailovich, b\<orn\> in 1884, a nat\<ive\> of the town of Ranenburg, Voronezh reg\<ion\>; father leased orchards and engaged in fruit growing. Russian, cit\<izen\> of the USSR. Residence: Dzerzhinsky Street, building 4, apartment 39, Admin\<istration\> of the Leningrad br\<anch\> of the Agr\<icultural\> Bank. Married: wife BAKASHEVA Ksenia Matveevna, aged 35, member of the VKP(b) since 1916. Research worker at the Commun\<ist\> Academy in Leningrad. Daughter Evgenia—aged 12; sister Anna, married name MASTRYUKHOVA, housew\<ife\>, n\<on\>-p\<arty\>. Lives in Ranenburg. Own labor; mother has a small house in Ranenburg. Elementary education. Member of the VKP(b) since 1918. During the 1929 purge was reprimanded for insufficiently energetic struggle against alien elements. From May to October 1917 was associated with the S\<ocial\>-D\<emocratic\> Internationalists. In 1924–1925 was a member of the Leningrad Provincial Committee of the VKP(b); was transferred out of Leningrad in 1926 for participation in the Trotskyist-Zinovievist opposition. Returned to Leningrad in 1930 and was elected a member of the Regional Co\<mmittee\> of the VKP(b) and a member of the presidium of the Regional Executive Committee. No previous convictions. Removed from the military register because of age. Did not serve with the Whites.

QUESTION: When and under what circumstances did you last see ZINOVIEV?

ANSWER: I last saw ZINOVIEV in Moscow in December 1926 under the following circumstances: I was invited to a factional meeting held at ZINOVIEV's apartment. Those present included KAMENEV, ZINOVIEV, TROTSKY, KUKLIN; I do not now remember other persons. The meeting discussed tactical questions of the further factional struggle.

QUESTION: Which Zinovievists did you meet after withdrawing from the opposition?

ANSWER: I withdrew from the opposition in early 1927, submitting a declaration to that effect to the party organization of the People's Commissariat of Finance, where I then worked. Since then, living continuously in Moscow until the spring of 1930, I occasionally met KUKLIN during 1929–1930, visiting his apartment. Once at KUKLIN's I met NAUMOV, Ivan Kupriyanovich. I allow that I met other Zinovievists at KUKLIN's, but I cannot now recall precisely whom.

I met EVDOKIMOV several times at the “Paris” hotel, going there on official business. These meetings were fleeting; I never visited his room. Once I met BAKAEV; I do not remember the circumstances of that meeting.

QUESTION: What do you know about KUKLIN's political attitudes on the basis of your meetings with him?

ANSWER: During meetings with KUKLIN in 1929–1930 he expressed his disagreement with the party's collectivization policy, arguing that the deportation of the kulaks condemned considerable masses of the peasantry to poverty and a starving existence; that the government should have taken timely care of the economic and productive settlement of the deported kulaks; and that in general, in his opinion, the deportation of the kulaks from collectivization regions was unjustified.

After moving from Moscow to Leningrad I did not meet KUKLIN until I unexpectedly found myself together with him in August 1932 in Sochi, at the “Frunze” sanatorium. At that time KUKLIN had strong anti-party attitudes. In a bitterly hostile tone he argued that the collectivization policy was failing, that the collective farms were not producing the expected results, and that the kulaks torn from the land became a burden on the shoulders of the state, which gave them no opportunity to apply their labor in the country's economy. I cannot now recall the content of the other conversations with KUKLIN.

QUESTION: With which Zinovievists did you maintain relations after moving to Leningrad?

ANSWER: During 1931–1933 I encountered BOGRACHEV and LEVIN Vladimir through work in the Lensoviet. I never visited either of them at their apartments, nor did they visit me.

BOGRACHEV, LEVIN and I had no political conversations.

As far as I remember, I did not meet other Zinovievists in Leningrad.

In the summer of 1934, while in Sochi at the “Frunze” sanatorium, I encountered BAKAEV and KOSTINA there. During our meetings we did not touch on political questions.

QUESTION: With whom did you share KUKLIN's political attitudes?

ANSWER: I spoke to no one about KUKLIN's attitudes.

QUESTION: As a member of the VKP(b), knowing of KUKLIN's anti-party attitudes, did you inform the party bodies about this?

ANSWER: No, I did not.

QUESTION: Why did you not expose KUKLIN's double-dealing before the party?

ANSWER: I did not properly assess KUKLIN's statements, although I considered his attitudes bitterly hostile.

QUESTION: You are shown the poem entitled “Kresty,” counterrevolutionary in content, seized from you during the search. When and from whom did you receive this poem, and for what purposes did you keep it?

ANSWER: I know nothing about the origin of this poem. I allow that it belongs to my wife. Personally I see this poem for the first time. I clarify that I allow the possibility that my wife received the poem “Kresty” from someone.

Recorded correctly from my words and read by me.

I. RUSANOV.

INTERROGATED BY:

ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE OO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD — GENDIN

Certified correct: A. Svetlova
