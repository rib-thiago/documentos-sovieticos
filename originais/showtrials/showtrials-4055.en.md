INTERROGATION RECORD

Of SAKHOV, Boris Naumovich,

dated January 4, 1935.

QUESTION: Describe your meetings and the nature of your conversations with SAFAROV?

ANSWER: I had only one chance meeting with SAFAROV on Comintern Street, probably in 1930. I had no political conversations with him.

QUESTION: The investigation knows that, when meeting SAFAROV on Comintern Street, you told him that you often visited Leningrad, met members of the Zinovievist organization there, and exchanged information with them. You also said that you had helped the former oppositionist ABRAMSON extricate himself from some affair. Do you acknowledge this?

ANSWER: I told SAFAROV none of this. I did indeed have to examine ABRAMSON’s case, but this was later than my meeting with SAFAROV, that is, in 1932.

QUESTION: What assignments did you receive from members of the center in connection with your trips to Leningrad?

ANSWER: I never received any assignments from members of the center of the Zinovievist organization in connection with my trips to Leningrad.

QUESTION: Which members of the organization’s center did you inform about your trips to Leningrad and your contacts there, and how?

ANSWER: If I said anything to anyone about my trips to Leningrad and meetings with comrades there, these conversations were not political in character. KUKLIN asked me about SHEINBERG several times, but my answers about him were not in the nature of political information.

QUESTION: With which members of the organization did you discuss the decisions of the 17th Party Congress?

ANSWER: I did not speak with any members of the organization about the decisions of the 17th Congress in an anti-party spirit, and generally do not remember conversations with them on this subject.

QUESTION: Describe the nature of the information you gave EVDOKIMOV in connection with Iuda SHTERN’s case [1]?

ANSWER: I remember nothing about this question.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that LIBIN, a participant in the opposition from the Samara group, proposed in 1927 fighting “with weapons in hand” against the party leadership. What can you testify about this?

ANSWER: I do not remember LIBIN personally. When I was in Samara in 1927, either AZOVSKY or I. TARASOV did indeed tell me about LIBIN’s proposal to “take up arms,” or perhaps to “prepare an armed uprising.” We all reacted sharply to LIBIN’s proposal and to LIBIN himself. A few days later LIBIN was arrested; after his arrest, a Trotskyist, apparently SLOBODCHIKOV, approached me with a proposal to “protest against the arrest of the worker LIBIN.” I replied that, after what LIBIN had said, we could have nothing in common with him.

SAKHOV.

INTERROGATED BY: KORKIN.

Certified correct: V. Stoiko

RGASPI, fond 671, inventory 1, file 126, folios 214–216.

[1] Iuda Mironovich Shtern — participant in the 1932 assassination attempt against von Twardowski, counselor of the German embassy in Moscow.
