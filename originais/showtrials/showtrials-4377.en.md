INTERROGATION RECORD

Of BRAVO, Boris Lvovich, January 10, 1935

QUESTION: What was GORSHENIN's role in organizing the Zinovievist underground?

ANSWER: GORSHENIN played a prominent role in the organization's activity, being a member of its leadership. I know that meetings of the organization's active members took place from time to time at GORSHENIN's apartment on the Arbat, outwardly disguised as family parties of “close friends.”

In particular, I personally attended such meetings twice. The first time was, as far as I remember, in 1931, in connection with GESSEN Sergey's arrival in Moscow from Smolensk.

I received the invitation to this meeting through GERTIK. On arriving at GORSHENIN's, I found BAKAEV, KOSTINA, EVDOKIMOV, GESSEN, and GERTIK Dina there.

This meeting took the form of a discussion, in an anti-Party spirit, of current measures of the Party and government.

On another occasion, I attended a meeting at GORSHENIN's in October 1932, when GORSHENIN, EVDOKIMOV, BOGDAN, and I gathered there. As far as I remember, we discussed ZINOVIEV's position in connection with the Ryutin case and organizational measures concerning it.

As I have already testified, in 1933 I met GORSHENIN in the GORT dining hall on Nikolskaya, where he told me of his expulsion from the Party by the purge commission.

In an embittered tone, GORSHENIN conveyed to me his opinion that apparently all the purge commissions had a special directive from the Party leadership to expel former oppositionists from the Party.

QUESTION: At the interrogation on January 3, you testified that PERIMOV Aleksey belonged to the Zinovievist organization.

Clarify what you know about PERIMOV's activity as a participant in the organization.

ANSWER: My relations with PERIMOV resumed in 1929, after my return from exile to Moscow. During 1930, I frequently encountered PERIMOV at GERTIK's apartment at the Zinovievist gatherings held there. Being taciturn by nature, PERIMOV usually listened to the anti-Party, maliciously slanderous judgments of those assembled, expressing his personal bitterness toward the Party leadership, and exchanged information about the state of affairs in the organization with other participants in it.

I last saw PERIMOV in late autumn 1932, when he told me that, after ZINOVIEV's banishment from Moscow, he, PERIMOV, had moved into ZINOVIEV's apartment, from which he had been evicted.

Taken down correctly from my words and read by me.

BRAVO.

INTERROGATED BY:

ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE SPECIAL DEPARTMENT OF THE GUGB OF THE USSR NKVD — GENDIN
