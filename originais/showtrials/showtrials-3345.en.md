INTERROGATION RECORD

Of FADEEV, FYODOR FADEEVICH, DATED DECEMBER 19, 1934.

(Supplementary)

QUESTION: Clarify your previous testimony of December 18 concerning the persons belonging to the leading Moscow center.

ANSWER: To amend my previous testimony, changes of the following nature must be made: FAIVILOVICH, MINAEV and TARASOV do not belong to the center, but are merely figures close to, so to speak, associated with the center.

QUESTION: To what period does the beginning of the formation of the counterrevolutionary organization date?

ANSWER: The beginning of the formation of the counterrevolutionary organization to which I belonged should be dated approximately to 1931, that is, to Vladimir RUMYANTSEV's return from Magnitogorsk. Together with ALBANSKY I met him on Herzen Street, and he invited us to call at his apartment for a conversation. At first RUMYANTSEV described the progress of construction at Magnitogorsk, the work being performed by the former Zinovievist Pyotr TARSOV, and so forth. From my further conversation with RUMYANTSEV I concluded that he was pleased that the former Zinovievists scattered throughout the Soviet Union had begun gathering again and converging on Leningrad; he showed keen interest in asking me about the whereabouts of some of our mutual acquaintances, former Zinovievists. The trip to Moscow by TOLMAZOV and SEREDOKHIN should be dated to the same period, or possibly later; they informed me that in Moscow they had met KUKLIN and SAFAROV. Among other things, according to them, SAFAROV spoke very badly of ZINOVIEV.

QUESTION: Which members of the counterrevolutionary organization did you meet, and what was the nature of your conversations?

ANSWER: Together with ANTONOV and POPOV I visited SEVEROV's apartment, and I also called on him once alone. I visited TOLMAZOV most frequently and ANTONOV and RUMYANTSEV less often. Our conversations usually touched on questions of Party policy, and the assessments given were of an openly counterrevolutionary nature. During one conversation with SEVEROV I asked him about the whereabouts of GEDVABNY, with whom I had been in Kursk in 1926–1927. SEVEROV told me that GEDVABNY was not in Leningrad but in exile. I visited RUMYANTSEV twice with TOLMAZOV and also called on him about twice at his workplace in the Vyborg District Soviet. I met other members of the organization by chance on the street.

QUESTION: What did the practical work of the counterrevolutionary organization to which you belonged consist of?

ANSWER: The practical activities of our counterrevolutionary organization consisted of holding counterrevolutionary conversations when we met and gathered, criticizing Party policy, and pointing out that the present Party leadership was pursuing a policy disastrous for the country. All these conversations engendered sharply extremist sentiments among the Zinovievist youth, former Komsomol officials of Vyborg District, among whom KIROV's murderer NIKOLAEV grew up and was educated.

QUESTION: What testimony can you give concerning the organization and commission of the terrorist act against Comrade KIROV by NIKOLAEV, a member of your organization?

ANSWER: I consider, and am convinced of this, that our organization bears full political and moral responsibility for the murder of KIROV, committed by Leonid NIKOLAEV, who was connected with our counterrevolutionary organization.

Although I had no direct connection with NIKOLAEV, I do not absolve myself of responsibility for this terrorist act.

Recorded correctly and read by me, in confirmation of which I sign.

FADEEV

INTERROGATED BY: MAKAROV

Certified correct: M. Koroleva
