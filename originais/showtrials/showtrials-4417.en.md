INTERROGATION RECORD

Of LEIKIN, Elkon Georgievich

dated January 11, 1935.

LEIKIN Elkon Georgievich, b\<orn\> in 1902 (April 30), a nat\<ive\> of Minsk, res\<ides\> in Moscow, 48, Malye Kochki \[1\], building 7, apartment 181, Jewish, USSR citizenship. Passport issued by the 9th police precinct of Moscow. Research worker. I serve in the Committee for Higher Technical Education under the TsIK of the USSR. From the f\<ormer\> petty-burgher estate of Minsk. Father was a dentist. At present father is in Soviet employment in Minsk. Before the revolution studied; after the revolution also studied, until 1918. Divorced. Dependent daughter Lena, aged 9 y\<ears\>; brother Boris, aged 25 y\<ears\>, studies in Moscow at the Mosc\<ow\> Power Eng\<ineering\> Inst\<itute\>; sister Edya LEIKINA, aged 29 y\<ears\>, in party work (in Omsk) at Ikonnikovsky dairy state farm No. 157. Parents live in Minsk. Higher education, graduated from the Institute of Red Professors in 1927. Mem\<ber\> of the VKP(b) since 1918. Expelled from the party in 1928 for belonging to the Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc, reinstated in party membership in August 1928 (an interruption of six m\<onths\>). Never tried. I have no decorations. Private in the first-category reserve. Did not serve in the Red Army. Did not serve with the Whites. Worked on assignments from the culture and propaganda department of the CC of the VKP(b) and the industrial department of the MK of the VKP(b). Carried out various propaganda assignments in the army and navy.

QUESTION: Tell the investigation about your role in the Zinovievist opposition before the Fifteenth Congress of the VKP(b).

ANSWER: I joined the Leningrad Zinovievist opposition in 1925 while at the Institute of Red Professors. I belonged to a group of IKP students that included, besides myself, KAREV, KAPITONOV, GORSHENIN, SOLOVYOV, FURTICHEV, MALYSHEV, KARTASHEV, YUGOV, FEIGELSON, RIVLIN and LADOKHA.

I took an active part in the opposition's struggle and on ZINOVIEV's instructions carried out a number of important assignments, including drafting the economic section of the Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc's platform. Later, in 1927, while in Saratov, I belonged to the local opposition center, on whose behalf I spoke at numerous meetings.

As a member of the Saratov center I participated in developing the local opposition organization's line of conduct, outlined ways of struggling against the party, participated in carrying out that struggle and maintained direct contact with Moscow—personally with ZINOVIEV, whose directives I implemented.

QUESTION: The investigation possesses information that even after the Fifteenth Congress of the VKP(b) you retained organizational ties with the Zinovievist organization, which existed until recently. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: Yes, I do. I did indeed retain organizational ties even after the Fifteenth Party Congress, maintaining them until recently. I met more or less systematically with ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV (until their exile in 1932) and with GORSHENIN and KOZHURO until December 1934.

In 1934, while at GORSHENIN's apartment, I also met EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, KOSTINA and VUJOVICH.

QUESTION: The investigation has established that, when visiting GORSHENIN's apartment, you participated in discussing the policy and measures of the CC of the VKP(b). Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: Yes, I do. I did indeed participate in discussing and criticizing a number of CC decisions and measures concerning economic construction, the country's technical reconstruction, and international policy and Comintern activity.

QUESTION: The investigation also possesses information that you were a source of information for the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization on the above questions. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: Yes, I do. I did indeed inform ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV and GORSHENIN on questions of domestic and international policy of the CC of the VKP(b), in particular on the state of the USSR's national economy, and this information was simultaneously in the nature of consultation.

In addition I conveyed information to them about current news, the situation in the CC of the VKP(b) and the MK, and relations among individual leading officials of the CC and MK. This information of mine was anti-party and slanderous in character.

QUESTION: From what sources did you receive information about the situation in the CC and MK of the VKP(b)?

ANSWER: My sources were chiefly MAKAR, a member of the MK of the VKP(b); KRIVITSKY, a member of the editorial board of the newspaper “Izvestia”; PILMEISTER, a research worker at the Inst\<itute\> of Economics of the Communist Academy; ILYUKHOV, a research worker at the Inst\<itute\> for Economic Research of Gosplan of the USSR; and KHMELNITSKAYA, dep\<uty\> director of the Inst\<itute\> of World Economy.

All these persons, including MAKAR, confidentially conveyed information and rumors to me which were not to be made public, and which I in turn passed on to the member of the Moscow center of the Zinovievist organization, GORSHENIN Ivan Stepanovich.

MAKAR also informed me, very confidentially, of decisions taken by the bureau of the MK of the VKP(b), the state of individual branches of industry, and particular facts in the life of the Moscow party organization and relations among leading officials of the CC and MK of the VKP(b).

QUESTION: How often did you visit MAKAR and where did you meet him?

ANSWER: I met him mainly at his apartment, though I did not visit particularly often. My relations with MAKAR were based on family ties and personal friendship.

QUESTION: Did MAKAR know of your membership in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization and that you \<were\> using the information received from him to inform your like-minded associates?

ANSWER: MAKAR did not know that I had belonged to the organization until recently; nor did he know how I used this information.

As for my active struggle against the party before the Fifteenth Congress of the VKP(b), he knew about that very well.

QUESTION: Was MAKAR aware of your meetings with ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV and GORSHENIN?

ANSWER: MAKAR did not know of my meetings with ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV. As for my meetings with GORSHENIN, he knew about these and regarded them sharply negatively.

QUESTION: Did MAKAR know of your anti-party attitudes and disagreements with the decisions of the CC of the VKP(b)?

ANSWER: I never told him about this and, as it seems to me, gave him no reason to think so either.

The record has been taken down correctly from my words and read by me, in confirmation of which I sign.

E. LEIKIN.

INTERROGATED BY:

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO OF THE UGB — STROMIN.

Certified correct: A. Svetlova
