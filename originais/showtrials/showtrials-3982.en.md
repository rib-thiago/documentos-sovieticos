INTERROGATION RECORD

OF ANISHEV, Anatoly Isayevich

January 2, 1935.

In response to the questions put to me about the organizational structure and practical work of gathering the cadres of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization, I give the following supplementary testimony:

1\. By the time of the Fifteenth Congress, our organization was territorially scattered across several cities of the USSR, and in each city all the oppositionists there formed a cohesive colony of an émigré type. The backbone of such a colony consisted of persons who had come from Leningrad; the Trotskyists proper formed a separate group, but were organizationally closely connected with our groups; the surrounding circle consisted of the few persons whom we had worked on locally.

After the Fifteenth Congress, some regrouping took place. It consisted of the following:

a) some, mainly local people, gradually dispersed;

b) three groups emerged: Zinovievists, the “leaderless,” and Trotskyists proper. Connections among these groups, especially with the Trotskyists proper, weakened, but even here did not cease entirely;

c) transfers altered the territorial distribution. About a year later, returns to Moscow and Leningrad began.

All these transfers and movements did not lead to the disintegration of the counterrevolutionary organization itself, because wherever we were, we always and everywhere found one another and used every opportunity for contact both among ourselves and with the center.

For example, after being transferred following the Fifteenth Congress to the area of the Andijan district, I visited Moscow twice in 1928 and 1929. Each time I met GERTIK there, through whom my connections with the center had mainly passed even before the Fifteenth Congress; I traveled to Khodzhent to see YAROSLAV and repeatedly visited Tashkent, where I invariably met those who remained there (PERIMOV, FAIVILOVICH and other\<s\>); a Trotskyist woman from Samarkand (N. LIVSHITS), who in accordance with our directives had not revealed herself, visited me in Assake.

This had the character of “personal connections,” but was of course nothing other than preserving the counterrevolutionary organization. In the new places we visited, we immediately found ourselves “personally connected” with people we had never even met before.

Thus, in Khodzhent a group of three or four Trotskyists gathered around YAROSLAV (I do not remember their surnames), and during my visit there I met not only YAROSLAV but all members of the group.

In Tashkent, as early as 1930, DRONOVA and RITMAN, who had been transferred there, immediately sought me out; I had not known them before, but we formed a closely connected group.

As before the Fifteenth Congress, we always knew who was where, who had been transferred where, and who would be connected with whom in the new place.

For example, of the Central Asian group (of the period before the Fifteenth Congress), I shall list everyone who was there and indicate whom I met or knew about up to 1934.

PERIMOV—met in Moscow in 1933

FAIVILOVICH—ditto

FELITSINA—ditto

RITMAN—ditto

KOZHUROV \[1\]—ditto

TSVIBAK—ditto, in Leningrad

NATANSON—ditto

DMITRIYEV N.—ditto

LUKIN V.—ditto

NIKOLAS—ditto, in 1932

DRONOVA—ditto

TOIVO—ditto, in Moscow

GURALSKY—met in Moscow before his journey abroad.

Of the Trotskyists proper who were in Central Asia during those years, I met ALMAZOV, KAUFMAN and LIVSHITS in Moscow.

In short, I met almost everyone.

These were not chance meetings. This was the preservation of political and organizational connections; this was the continuation of counterrevolutionary work in a new form and under new conditions.

Nor was it merely the preservation of Central Asian connections: each of us at his new workplace was a member of the counterrevolutionary cell in his city; many of us preserved direct connections with the center. Thus the thread of meetings encompassed the entire organization, from ZINOVIEV, from the center, down to every individual member of the organization.

It should be added that in the period before the Fifteenth Congress we were all connected not only by workplace, and these connections likewise survived after the Fifteenth Congress. Between the Fourteenth and Fifteenth Congresses, for instance, I visited the following cities, everywhere contacting the Trotskyists there: Moscow, Leningrad, Saratov, Stalingrad, Rostov and Tiflis. A number of persons also visited Central Asia besides those working there (KHARITONOV, LILINA, SOKOLNIKOV and SMILGA came to establish contact). Thus, besides the old Leningrad connections, the range of connections expanded between the Fourteenth and Fifteenth Congresses.

After returning to Leningrad (1932), I immediately established connections with those I had known previously (NATANSON, RUMYANTSEV, TSVIBAK and other\<s\>) and through them with those I had not known before (KOTOLYNOV, Georgy FYODOROV, ALEKSANDROV, ANTONEVICH and so on).

Thus, here too all organizational connections of a definitely political, counterrevolutionary character were preserved.

The Leningrad colony was connected with the center and other cities both through our trips to Moscow (myself, NATANSON, KOTOLYNOV) and through visits by Muscovites to Leningrad (GERTIK) and the arrival of YELKOVICH.

All these chains transmitted and spread the counterrevolutionary sentiments and orientations of the center about which I testified earlier.

I note that the division into Zinovievists and the “leaderless” which arose after the Fifteenth Congress did not in any way prevent the preservation of organizational connections.

Connections were likewise preserved with those who had not been reinstated in the party, and also with the Trotskyists.

Written in my own hand—ANISHEV.

January 2, 1935.

INTERROGATED BY: HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE UGB—LULOV.

Certified correct: V. Stoiko
