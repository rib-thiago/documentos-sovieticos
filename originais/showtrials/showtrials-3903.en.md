[Annotation: to Comrade Kogan L.]

INTERROGATION RECORD

Conducted by the ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB — RUTKOVSKY, December 27, 1934.

KUKLIN, Aleksandr Sergeyevich.

(Supplementary).

Question: The investigation has established that you kept the archive of the Zinovievist organization. Do you confirm this?

Answer: Yes, I confirm it. The archive consisted of one folder of documents about twenty centimeters thick. I received this archive from Grigory FEDOROV in the summer of 1934 for safekeeping. I did not look into this archive and therefore do not know its contents. In December 1934, during the arrests of Zinovievists, I destroyed this archive. When handing the archive to me, FEDOROV said it was ZINOVIEV's personal archive. I replied to FEDOROV that I had nowhere to keep the archive and that in general I did not want to become involved in this matter. I do not remember how FEDOROV justified the need to hand Zinoviev's archive to me, but I remember agreeing with FEDOROV that I would take this archive temporarily, for a short period.

Question: What connection is there between the archive FEDOROV handed to you and the archive about which you inquired of LEVIN V. S. when you were in Leningrad in 1933?

Answer: I suppose there is no connection between these archives, and the archive I destroyed should not be part of the archive held by LEVIN.

Question: For what purpose did you keep at home the top-secret Party documents and the Trotskyists' platform discovered during the search of your premises?

Answer: I intended to destroy all these documents; the absence of a stove at home prevented me from doing so. The documents I kept relate to the distant past; when moving from Leningrad to Moscow, my wife mechanically packed them with the rest of the literature.

Question: The investigation has established that you were connected with the Zinovievist SAKHOV Boris. Describe SAKHOV's political sentiments.

Answer: I did indeed visit SAKHOV, and he visited me. I remember that once, I do not remember exactly when, we gathered at SAKHOV's with our wives: myself, EVDOKIMOV, NAUMOV, and ZALUTSKY, who came without his wife. I did not know that the persons listed above had been invited to SAKHOV's. I do not remember our conversations at SAKHOV's.

In 1934, on returning from the East Siberian Territory, SAKHOV told me that he had uncovered a whole series of abuses and embezzlements in the apparatuses of the territorial Party committee and territorial executive committee. He said these apparatuses took manufactured goods and passes to Vostokzoloto, engaging in self-provisioning, and used export furs to make fur coats for their wives, and so forth. My conversation with SAKHOV about affairs in Irkutsk arose, I believe, because I myself had previously been in Irkutsk in 1933 on flour-milling business, and the terribly neglected economy of the city had made a depressing impression on me.

Question: POZDEEVA's testimony of December 24 of this year establishes that you held anti-Party conversations with her in 1934. Do you confirm this?

Answer: I did not hold anti-Party conversations with POZDEEVA. —

A. KUKLIN.

Interrogated by: RUTKOVSKY.
