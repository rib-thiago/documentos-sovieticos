INTERROGATION RECORD

Conducted by RUTKOVSKY, December 25, 1934.

GESSEN, Sergei Mikhailovich (supplementary).

QUESTION: MUSHTAKOV's testimony of December 19 of this year establishes that, when meeting him at the resort in Sochi in 1932 and in Leningrad in the spring of 1934, you criticized the party leaders in extremely sharp and embittered tones.

ANSWER: I met MUSHTAKOV in the autumn of 1931 in Sochi, and also saw him in February 1934 in Leningrad, where I came for the Greater Dnieper conference.

I confirm that I held conversations with MUSHTAKOV in a spirit hostile to party policy and the party leadership. I cannot now recall the details and, in particular, do not remember what the episode of the “old Latvians” mentioned by MUSHTAKOV refers to.

QUESTION: What grounds did you have for such conversations with A. A. MUSHTAKOV? Did you consider him politically like-minded?

ANSWER: MUSHTAKOV had not been a supporter of ZINOVIEV since the autumn of 1926. In recent years he occupied a peculiar political position “of his own,” which allowed him, depending on his mood, to attack sharply now ZINOVIEV, now the party leadership. This position of his, together with my longstanding personal connection with him, explains why I considered it possible to hold the indicated conversations with him.

MUSHTAKOV's dissatisfaction with the party leadership centered on the internal party situation and various current party questions that I cannot now recall.

QUESTION: In the spring of 1934, while at MUSHTAKOV's, you telephoned Vladimir LEVIN and arranged to meet him. Describe your meeting with LEVIN.

ANSWER: I visited LEVIN's apartment once in February 1934 and stayed overnight.

The circumstances in which I found myself at LEVIN's ruled out any possibility of conversations with him on political subjects, since at that time he had many relatives and people unknown to me there.

QUESTION: What additional information can you give concerning the Zinovievist group's attitude toward the party leadership?

ANSWER: In accordance with the Zinovievist group's general political views and assessments that I described earlier, hostility toward the party leadership, and especially Comrade STALIN, prevailed in its midst.

Sharp expressions directed at Comrade STALIN and other party leaders were customary on the lips of ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV and other Zinovievists.

Alongside this, however, there were elements of ambivalence toward Comrade STALIN, insofar as his political figure had become inseparably merged with such undertakings as industrialization, collectivization and the proletarian dictatorship in the country. I remember, for example, two remarks by ZINOVIEV when assessing the Ryutin platform: “They hate STALIN more than the kulak,” and “In their hatred of STALIN they overlooked the proletarian dictatorship.” Such assessments, however, occurred chiefly in conversations of a more or less generalizing nature. The everyday tone of statements was hostile.

S. GESSEN

Interrogated by — RUTKOVSKY.
