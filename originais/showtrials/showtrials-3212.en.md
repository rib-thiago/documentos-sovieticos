INTERROGATION RECORD

OF NIKOLAEV, Leonid Vasilyevich,

23 December 1934.

QUESTION: The investigation asks you to state whether you conducted opposition work in 1924–1925 and the subsequent years.

ANSWER: In Vyborg District, in 1924–1925, the opposition had extensive connections. This applies to both the party and Komsomol organizations. Working in this district until 1931, I was in the very thick of the opposition struggle and actions.

I joined the opposition under the influence of active oppositionists such as KOTOLYNOV, SHATSKY, KHANIK, MURAVIEV, and others, who at that time held leading positions in Komsomol organizations.

They were then at the center of opposition work and were directly connected with the regional opposition leadership.

I remember party meetings of that time: KOTOLYNOV, I, and other comrades literally carried ZINOVIEV, EVDOKIMOV, and other opposition figures on our shoulders to the meetings.

The opposition regarded me as a like-minded person who could be trusted and could carry out individual assignments.

I spent the whole of 1925 in Luga. The leadership of the Komsomol Uyezd Committee was in oppositionist hands. The committee secretaries were Motya IVANOV and Nika KUZNETSOV, active opposition workers; the head of the Komsomol Uyezd Committee's organizational department was ZVEZDOV, a very active oppositionist. These were all solid opposition cadres who consistently fought against the party and exerted an exceptionally great influence upon me.

IVANOV, KUZNETSOV, ZVEZDOV, and others, while drawing me into the anti-party struggle, did not conceal the details of opposition work from me.

I remember IVANOV and KUZNETSOV saying that espionage was taking place in the Komsomol Regional Committee in Leningrad, conducted by supporters of the CC majority, and that this had to be resolutely fought.

During breaks between Uyezd Committee meetings and at the meetings themselves, we discussed how best to organize resistance to the supporters of the majority.

On instructions from the opposition leadership of the Uyezd Committee, ZVEZDOV frequently traveled to Leningrad to obtain information at the regional opposition center about the course of the struggle against the party and receive instructions for the further struggle. When ZVEZDOV came back with information, we all showered him with dozens of questions.

I lived for the interests of the inner-party struggle, took an active interest in its course, and, together with all the oppositionist comrades in the Uyezd Committee, keenly felt our setbacks; I read documents issued by the opposition.

TOLMAZOV, who was also an oppositionist, came to Luga many times to propagate opposition views. TOLMAZOV was then a member of the Komsomol CC.

In 1927, upon returning from Luga to Leningrad, I received a written invitation from Pavlusha TITOV, the new secretary of the Vyborg District Committee of the Komsomol, who was opposed to the opposition, to work in the District Committee. I refused, as I did not want to work with supporters of the majority. I preferred to work at a machine at factory No. 7, although both the material and general working conditions there were, of course, considerably worse than in the District Committee apparatus.

While studying in the factory's party education network, on one occasion during classes I advanced opposition positions, if I am not mistaken on questions of party policy in the national economy; I was immediately rebuffed and expected trouble from the party organization, but it passed without incident.

During this period I actively maintain contact with oppositionists ANTONOV, SOKOLOV, NIKANOROV, and VINOGRADOV, receiving information from them about the course of the opposition struggle.

I recall the following incident:

An oppositionist was discovered in the factory Komsomol organization; at the meeting they wanted to expel him. I spoke and argued that he should not be expelled.

Throughout this period I was never once held to party account for opposition work. I explain this by the fact that, out of solidarity, none of my comrades named me; apparently they considered it necessary to preserve me in the party.

Meanwhile, for a long time I feared being summoned to the party control commission and receiving a party penalty.

Until the opposition was crushed, I was closely connected with its active cadres. From 1928 my contacts with the opposition weakened. I continue to meet individual oppositionists and experience their influence.

From 1931 I turn sharply back into the opposition camp. My meetings with workers of the “Association of Former Komsomol Comrades-in-Arms” and my contact with Istmol played a major role here. At that time I met ZVEZDOV, POPOV, KHANIK, SHATSKY, KOTOLYNOV, and others, who continued to see me as a like-minded person. From their very first conversations with me, they immediately became convinced of my anti-party views; they knew my sharply negative attitude toward the removal of leading opposition figures from the party leadership; they knew that I disagreed with the policy of the existing party leadership; finally, they saw in me a person capable of decisive action.

The opposition document found in my possession during the search was received from ANTONOV in 1932. He gave it to me during one of our numerous conversations on opposition subjects. Handing it over, he said: “Take it; the document has not lost its significance, it will be useful; keep it.” I kept the document at home the entire time.

In August 1934 I rejoined the ranks of the Zinovievists as an old, proven participant in the previous struggle, capable of continuing it under new conditions.

I recall facts about my contacts with ANTONOV which I did not mention in previous interrogations. I knew from KOTOLYNOV and SOKOLOV that ANTONOV was a participant in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization. This caused me no surprise, as I knew ANTONOV as an old oppositionist.

On a day in the twenties of November of this year, at two in the afternoon, I was going to ANTONOV's apartment to meet him. I encountered him on Chugunnaya Street, and we began talking: I told him about the failure of my preparations for a terrorist act against KIROV, referring to the episode of 14 November, when I went to Moscow Station to meet KIROV, who at that time was already leaving the station. I then briefly informed him of my further work in preparing the terrorist act. —

Taken down in shorthand correctly from my words and read by me —

NIKOLAEV. —

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE EKO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR — MIRONOV.

ASSISTANT TO THE HEAD OF THE EKO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR — DMITRIEV.

CERTIFIED TRUE: M. Glazkov
