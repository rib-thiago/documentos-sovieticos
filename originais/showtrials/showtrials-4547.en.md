STATEMENT BY ELKOVICH.

TO THE DEPUTY PEOPLE'S COMMISSAR FOR INTERNAL AFFAIRS — Comrade AGRANOV.

Copy: TO THE ADMINISTRATIVE COMMISSION OF THE NKVD

Today I completed my testimony to the investigative authorities about the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization and my counterrevolutionary work as an active participant in this anti-Soviet organization, which had turned, as I formulated it in my testimony at the trial on January 16, 1935, into an internal White emigration. I formulated this definition for myself when, after my arrest, I honestly and thoroughly thought through the path of complete counterrevolutionary degeneration and transformation that I had traveled together with the Zinovievist organization. From my very first testimony upon my arrival in Leningrad, I was ruthless toward both myself and others in exposing the most vile counterrevolutionary essence of the Zinovievist organization. Mitigating nothing and concealing nothing, with complete truthfulness and sincerity, I told the investigative authorities everything I knew about the counterrevolutionary activity of the Zinovievist organization and my counterrevolutionary work as a member of this organization.

Both in my testimony to the investigative authorities and in my testimony at the trial, I did what I could to help eradicate completely, to tear out every root and rootlet of this anti-Soviet organization, which had descended to outright treason and betrayal. I have repented fully; I have burned away within myself everything, without remainder, that connected me with the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization. Neither in my testimony to the investigative authorities nor in my statement today have I made or do I make any attempt to diminish my guilt. For my most grievous crimes against Soviet power I must suffer severe punishment.

I wish only to assure you — and I think my entire conduct during the investigation and trial confirms this — that I have burned away everything within myself that connected me with the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization, that I have completely cast off the anti-Soviet scab of Zinovievism, that I have repented fully. This gives me strength and courage. I live in the hope that after some time I shall be able to prove this in practice.

That is why I ask for prolonged and harsh exile, but somewhere I could begin working immediately. I live in the hope that after several years I shall once again be able to earn the right to be called a Soviet citizen. Before I became a Zinovievist and counterrevolutionary, I was once a Bolshevik. And I live in the hope that perhaps someday I shall also earn the right to apply for admission as a sympathizer to the party of Lenin–Stalin.

ELKOVICH.

January 21, 1935.

Certified true: V. A.
