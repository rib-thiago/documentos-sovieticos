INTERROGATION RECORD

of the accused GORSHENIN I. S., dated January 12, 1935.

In answer to the investigation's question concerning the methods of struggle against the leadership of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) employed by our Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization, I report the following:

Our struggle against the existing Party leadership, conducted underground, assumed a systematic and purposeful character after the Fifteenth Congress of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks).

The entire process of reconstituting the Zinovievist organization, both initially and subsequently, invariably proceeded under the banner of preparing action against the leadership by creating appropriate conditions within the Party for ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's return to the Politburo.

All members of the Moscow Zinovievist center were firmly convinced that there were already sufficient forces within the Party and the Central Committee capable of radically changing the composition of the existing Party leadership and creating, as it was then called, a “Leninist Politburo.”

This conviction among the members of the center was especially strong during the first period of our organization's most active work, that is, before ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's exile in 1932.

On this basis, the center of the organization, under ZINOVIEV's leadership, pursued a policy of uniting within the Party all elements dissatisfied with the present leadership, concluding political agreements (blocs) with the “Rights” and “Lefts” (negotiations with BUKHARIN, ties with RYUTIN's group, ties with STEN's group), and preserving its forces within the Party by all available means.

At meetings of members of the organization's center, which took place, as I have already testified, at BAKAEV's apartment, my own, and GERTIK's, when discussing current decisions and measures of the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), it was openly said that if we were in power we would decide these questions differently.

As I have already testified at previous interrogations, systematic criticism of the Central Committee's principal decisions on international and domestic policy, and constant accusations against the Central Committee, especially STALIN, over various shortcomings and failures, created in all of us a particular feeling of hatred toward the Party's leaders.

With the same purpose of criticism and accusation, speeches and articles, chiefly STALIN's speeches and articles, were constantly examined. Theoretical and practical contradictions were artificially sought out in them and subjected to derisive mockery from our standpoint.

On the other hand, all articles and occasional speeches by ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV were publicized in every possible way, read, and studied, and assessments and responses to them were carefully collected. This happened, for example, with ZINOVIEV's speech at the session of the Communist Academy in 1934, when, as we acknowledged, he said something new against the background of supposedly colorless speeches by leading Comintern officials.

It must be said frankly that, for all our associates who came into contact with us, especially for members of our organization, we members of the center, especially ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV, were a nourishing source of various rumors and vile slander against the Party's leaders.

A contemptuous and hostile attitude toward STALIN was cultivated and sustained in our ranks.

I acknowledge that the hatred and bitterness we cultivated over such a long time could not fail to produce certain actions, and therefore the terrorist act committed against KIROV by a member of the Leningrad Zinovievist organization cannot be considered in isolation from our activities.

QUESTION: Describe the meetings of members of the center of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization held in 1934.

ANSWER: Meetings of the center took place without the full membership, at various times and with varying participants. The questions discussed at our meetings concerned assessments of current political events and the principal decisions of the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks).

During 1934 I participated in the following meetings of the organization's center:

in January — at SHAROV's apartment. Present were SHAROV, EVDOKIMOV, and myself, GORSHENIN. We discussed questions facing the forthcoming Seventeenth Congress;

in February — at EVDOKIMOV's apartment. Present were EVDOKIMOV, GESSEN (who had arrived from Smolensk), and myself. The congress's decisions were discussed, in particular the decision to liquidate the Central Control Commission–Workers' and Peasants' Inspectorate. On this question we were of the opinion that the existing Party leadership had adopted this decision out of a desire to free itself from the influence of the Central Control Commission–Workers' and Peasants' Inspectorate and the people working there, who behaved more or less independently;

in April — at my apartment. Participants were BAKAEV, KOSTINA, GESSEN, ELKOVICH, GERTIK, and myself, GORSHENIN. I do not now remember which questions were discussed.

in May — EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, SHAROV, KUKLIN, and GERTIK gathered at EVDOKIMOV's apartment. I personally was not present at this meeting and do not know the subject of the discussion;

in June — at GERTIK's apartment. Participants were BAKAEV, GERTIK, KUKLIN, myself, GORSHENIN, SHAROV, GERTSBERG, POZDEEVA, KOSTINA, KOZHURO, and SHEPSHELEVA. We did not discuss any political questions then, since, besides those listed above, relatives of GERTIK who did not belong to the organization were present;

in August — at my apartment. Besides myself, BAKAEV and GERTIK participated. At this meeting I gave information on the state of the Soviet regions in China and the situation on the Chinese Eastern Railway;

in August — at ZINOVIEV's dacha, present were ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, and myself. One question was discussed, connected with ZINOVIEV's position following his removal from the editorial staff of the journal “Bolshevik.”

I do not now remember any other meetings. Besides meetings at apartments, encounters took place in the Kremlin dining room, where BAKAEV, EVDOKIMOV, SHAROV, and KUKLIN regularly had lunch.

I personally did not go there and therefore can say nothing about the content of their conversations.

I have already informed the investigation at previous interrogations about the meetings of members of the organization's center held during 1931-1933.

The record was correctly written from my words, which I confirm by my signature. —

GORSHENIN.

INTERROGATED BY: —

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO OF THE UGB — STROMIN.
