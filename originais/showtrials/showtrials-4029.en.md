INTERROGATION RECORD

Of BRAVO, Boris Lvovich,

dated January 3, 1935.

QUESTION: With which other participants in the former Zinovievist opposition did you maintain relations, apart from the persons you named during previous interrogations?

ANSWER: Apart from the participants in the former Zinovievist opposition whom I have named thus far, I maintained relations with the following persons:

1. LUKIN, Vasily, whom I have known since 1926, when we worked together in Ukraine. I recall that during 1926–27 LUKIN sought a rapprochement with the “Workers’ Opposition” group in Kharkov, considering it necessary to establish contact and organizational rapprochement between the Zinovievists and the “Workers’ Opposition.”

LUKIN discussed this with me, LEVIN, Vladimir, and GORDON, Nikolai. We discussed LUKIN’s conduct, regarding him as a politically unsteady person inclined to ill-considered and hasty decisions and conclusions. I did not see LUKIN after 1927. In 1930, LUKIN unexpectedly appeared at my workplace at “Khlebotsentr,” asking me to give him TROTSKY’s book “My Life,” published abroad, to read. I replied that I was unable to give him the requested book by TROTSKY, and he left. Since then I have not seen LUKIN again.

2. PERIMOV, Alexei — I met him quite frequently in Moscow during 1930–32 at GERTIK’s apartment. PERIMOV visited my home several times, just as I visited his.

PERIMOV’s political attitudes at that time were no different from those of our entire group, about which I have already testified during previous interrogations: the same bitterness toward the party leadership, the spreading of malicious, slanderous rumors about the party leadership and especially STALIN, and exchanges of information about the situation of the Zinovievists, passed along from one person to another in a chain.

These rumors and gossip circulated in our midst as “small change,” and in them we found satisfaction for our sentiments.

Among the facts I forgot to report during previous interrogations, I should mention that at the end of 1933 GERTIK, who had returned to Moscow from exile, came to see me, informed me of the death of the former Zinovievist BOGDAN, and suggested that I attend his funeral. I replied that I would be unable to attend BOGDAN’s funeral, since I was occupied with work that day.

When, a few days later, I met EVDOKIMOV and told him about GERTIK’s visit, EVDOKIMOV said that BOGDAN had shot himself and that his suicide was a direct consequence of his persecution by the party leadership as a participant in the former Zinovievist opposition.

QUESTION: For what purposes did you give ZINOVIEV a copy of the memorandum on food supply difficulties in Ukraine in the autumn of 1932?

ANSWER: When ZINOVIEV asked me to give him a copy of that memorandum, he did not say for what purposes he needed it. However, it went without saying that he would use the memorandum to fight the party.

QUESTION: What do you know about the activities of the Zinovievist organization in Leningrad?

ANSWER: From meetings and conversations with LEVIN, Vladimir, and KORSHUNOV, I knew that their attitudes were the same as those of our Moscow group, brought together by EVDOKIMOV. From BASHKIROV’s words I knew that he personally maintained contact with RUMYANTSEV and LEVIN. BASHKIROV’s own attitudes seemed entirely loyal to me, and I gained the impression that he had completely withdrawn from the Zinovievists.

QUESTION: The testimony of the arrested leaders of the Zinovievist organization establishes that the organization’s leadership in Moscow used you to maintain contact with the organization’s “Leningrad center.” The investigation demands a truthful answer from you on this question?

ANSWER: I affirm that I was unaware of the existence of the “Leningrad center.” I never received any instructions from the leadership of the Moscow Zinovievist group to transmit to Leningrad.

I acknowledge that, when meeting KORSHUNOV and especially LEVIN, I exchanged information with them about the attitudes and situation of participants in the former Zinovievist opposition.

The answers to the questions have been recorded correctly from my words and read — BRAVO.

INTERROGATED BY:

ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE SPECIAL DEPARTMENT OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD — GENDIN.

Certified correct: Kazakova
