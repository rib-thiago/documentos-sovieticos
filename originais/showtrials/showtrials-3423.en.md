NKVD Directorate for Leningrad Region.

INTERROGATION RECORD

Of Nikolai Alexandrovich DMITRIEV

December 24, 1934.

DMITRIEV Nikolai Alexandrovich, born in 1898, native of the village of Golodavka, Raevskaya volost, Morshansk district, former Tambov province, from an employee family (father a volost clerk), Russian, citizen of the USSR, residing in Leningrad, Krestovsky Island, Konstantinovsky Lane, building 30, apartment 51, block 8. Head of the Leningrad office of “Soyuztranstroy.” Married. Wife: Zoya Mikhailovna DMITRIEVA, born in 1905, economist in the Workers' Supply Department of the “Krasnaya Zarya” factory, nonparty; children: Evgeny, 10, Izgolda, 8, Vadim, 3; mother: Paraskeva Vasilyevna DMITRIEVA, 60, supported by his sister Ekaterina. Brother: Evgeny Alexandrovich DMITRIEV, 31, head of the Economics Department at Stalin Communist University, member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), residing on the Petrograd Side, Grota Street, building 1/3. Sister: Ekaterina Alexandrovna LOBANOVA, 38, student at the Leningrad Institute of Soviet Construction, party member, 35 Krasnykh Zor Street, apartment 55. From 1925 to 1928, his mother owned a farmhouse and an allotment of 1½–2 desiatinas of land. Means of support: salary of 600 rubles per month. General education: elementary. Completed a two-class zemstvo school in 1912. Higher specialized education. Graduated from Stalin Communist University in 1924. Party member since 1917; expelled in January 1929 for belonging to the Zinovievist opposition. Readmitted in June–July 1929. Until 1926, member of the Bureau of the Moskovsko-Narvsky District Party Committee. In 1927, head of the Organizational Department of the Central Committee of the Uzbek SSR. Since 1927 in responsible economic work. Never convicted or prosecuted. K-10, political command personnel, registered with the Oktyabrsky District Military Commissariat. Did not serve with the Whites. Was not in territory occupied by the Whites.

QUESTION: What do you know about the existence of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization?

ANSWER: Following the defeat of the Zinovievist opposition at the Fifteenth Party Congress, its participants set themselves the immediate task of penetrating the party in order to preserve the cadres of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist opposition. Most characteristic in this regard was ANISHEV's speech to the group of Zinovievist oppositionists in Samarkand, of which I was a member. ANISHEV raised the point that upon returning to the party it was necessary to renounce factional work without renouncing opposition views, while continuing to examine questions of the country's political life in circles of oppositionists. As I knew, ANISHEV spoke on the basis of a directive from ZINOVIEV on this matter, which proposed entering the party while preserving not only opposition views but also the Zinovievists' organizational connections. The Zinovievists' penetration of the party for duplicitous purposes, their subsequent practical activities and the preservation of organizational political connections among Zinovievists led to the reestablishment of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization, which continues to exist to this day.

QUESTION: With which participants in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization were you connected?

ANSWER: From 1928 to 1929 I continued actively to struggle against the party, being connected with the Zinovievist group in Samarkand, which included, besides myself, ANISHEV, NIKOLAS and the Trotskyists LIVSHITS and KAUFMAN. The Samarkand group was connected with YAROSLAV's Tashkent group, while YAROSLAV in turn was connected with the opposition center in Moscow.

In 1930 I was sent to Saratov, where I was connected with SAFAROV, ZALUTSKY, ABRAMSON and AVDEEV. At the same time, I maintained contact with the Zinovievists in Moscow: FAIVILOVICH, PERIMOV and GORSHENIN.

In 1933–34, while in Leningrad, I was connected with Vasily LUKIN, TSEITLIN and KHANIK; I met the latter two by chance at Smolny, while I met LUKIN at home. During this last period, 1931–34, I met FAIVILOVICH several times in Moscow. I last visited FAIVILOVICH's apartment in October or November 1934.

QUESTION: What was the nature of the connections with Zinovievists that you have listed?

ANSWER: All these connections were in fact organizational and political in character.

At meetings we informed one another about preserved connections with former Zinovievists and gave political assessments of those connections. In particular, when meeting FAIVILOVICH, I told him that in Leningrad LUKIN and TSEITLIN were trying to establish contact with me, and described both as people whose party membership was insincere. When meeting me, TSEITLIN complained of being cut off from the fellows and sought support from me.

QUESTION: What can you testify concerning the terrorist act committed against Comrade KIROV by NIKOLAEV, a participant in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization?

ANSWER: As a Zinovievist, I accept political responsibility for the terrorist act committed against Comrade KIROV by NIKOLAEV, a participant in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization, because the atmosphere of vicious hatred toward the leaders of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) prevailing in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization educated NIKOLAEV in the spirit of terror.

The testimony has been correctly recorded from my words and read by me — DMITRIEV.

INTERROGATED BY:

Officer of the Second Section of the SPO — GOLUBEV.

CERTIFIED TRUE: Kuznetsova
