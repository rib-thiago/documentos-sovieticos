INTERROGATION RECORD

15 December 1934.

MYASNIKOV, Nikolai Petrovich — born 1900, member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) since May 1917, expelled twice for factional work, last reinstated in 1928; member of the Leningrad Soviet, deputy head of its Organization Department. His father owned a house and a trading business. Brother Aleksandr, a former officer in the tsarist and Kolchak armies, was held in a concentration camp and died in 1922; another brother, Anatoly, formerly a party member, served in GPU troops and committed suicide in 1927 owing to dissatisfaction with his position.

Question: What do you know about the existence of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization?

Answer: The ideological and political defeat of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc did not, however, eliminate the Zinovievist underground. After the “leaders,” ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV, submitted to the party Central Committee a statement renouncing their counterrevolutionary views and struggle against the party, they took every measure to explain the true meaning of this step to the remaining Zinovievist cadres. The tactics consisted in officially renouncing the struggle and views while actually preserving cadres from destruction and organizing them underground. In late summer 1928 a clandestine meeting took place at LEVIN V.'s apartment, attended by LEVIN, MOROZOV Aleksei — then working in the Moscow–Narva insurance fund —, me, MYASNIKOV, KORSHUNOV Efim, a student at the Industrial Academy, and somebody else whom I do not now remember. SHAROV Ya. appeared and conveyed on Zinoviev and Kamenev's behalf a directive to work on all those who had not yet submitted statements, so that they would submit statements renouncing their views in order to return to the party and preserve their forces for future struggle. I informed KOSTRITSKY, who had not attended, of this meeting. We began transmitting this instruction from the “leaders” to the Zinovievists connected with us. In particular, on V. LEVIN's instructions I personally instructed in this spirit MAKAROV Pyotr, then working at some small chemical factory in the Narva District, and MOROZOV A. I met him at MAKAROV's apartment, somewhere on one of the Krasnoarmeiskaya streets. LEVIN V. knows this address well. MAKAROV and MOROZOV were to pass on these instructions from Zinoviev and Kamenev to our people at Krasny Treugolnik and several Narva District enterprises. In connection with my statement requesting return to the party (after the “leaders'” statements), I was summoned to the Party Collegium of the Central Control Commission in Moscow. Before appearing at the Commission I went to GERTIK Artyom for instructions on how to behave. He gave instructions of the same kind as SHAROV: renounce everything, provided that I was reinstated in the party, to preserve myself for future struggle. Before the Party Collegium's meeting GERTIK promised to arrange a meeting with Zinoviev, but it did not take place. This fact also indicates the “leaders'” true intentions: in 1928, after Zinoviev and Kamenev submitted their statement, I visited Kamenev's apartment on Mokhovaya Street in Moscow, where, I believe, Olga Davydovna lives. During this meeting Kamenev, developing criticism of the party's economic policy (I was still outside the party), pointed to the need to gather forces, since “Stalin's policy will collapse anyway”; GORSHENIN was present. All this shows that the counterrevolutionary struggle against the party and its leadership did not cease and that a counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization had already taken shape in 1928.

Question: Who belonged to the leading center of the Leningrad counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization, and how was work distributed among its members?

Answer: LEVIN Vladimir, RUMYANTSEV Vladimir, KOTOLYNOV Ivan on the youth side, me — MYASNIKOV —, DMITRIEV Timofei (until his departure for Rostov). SOSITSKY performed leading work and undoubtedly also belonged to the center. I do not know precisely, since strict secrecy was observed, preventing us all from gathering together.

LEVIN was the Moscow center's representative and directed all the work. I do not know LEVIN's and RUMYANTSEV's contacts. KOTOLYNOV was responsible for work among youth. I gathered information on the sentiments of workers and of the Soviet and party apparatus, using my position as head of the Moscow–Narva District Organization Department and later of the Leningrad Soviet's Organization Department. I also maintained direct contact with Zinovievists: KOVALEVSKY — an old Putilov Works worker, with whom I was personally connected until 1929; contact then broke off, and in 1930 or 1931 LEVIN told me he had been arrested; KOROTKOV — an old worker of the Marti Works, now director of the Sirocco factory making fans. KOROTKOV informed me about workers' sentiments at Krasny Treugolnik, where his wife worked. In 1934 he came to see me at the Soviet. I know from LEVIN V. and SOSITSKY that organization members SAFRONOV and NALIVAIKO Foma also maintained enterprise contacts. As I testified above, members MOROZOV and MAKAROV also maintained such contacts. I must add that although KOROTKOV's former membership in the Zinovievist opposition was officially known, he was nevertheless appointed to one of the party purge commissions in the Volodarsky District in 1933.

Question: Who belonged to the Moscow center of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization?

Answer: I know that its Moscow center includes ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV and KUKLIN. Whether GERTIK A. belongs to the center I do not know precisely, but he plays a major role, especially in contact with the Leningrad counterrevolutionary organization.

Question: The investigation has information about contact systematically maintained by the Leningrad counterrevolutionary organization with the organization's center in Moscow. What can you testify on this question?

Answer: Direct contact was maintained between the Leningrad counterrevolutionary organization and the Moscow center. Essentially all contacts with the Moscow center were in the hands of LEVIN V., who was always informed of the sentiments and instructions of the “leaders,” Zinoviev and Kamenev. LEVIN V. traveled to Moscow very frequently; DMITRIEV Timofei traveled while in Leningrad; I traveled on LEVIN V.'s instructions; and, according to LEVIN, SOSITSKY traveled and supposedly met one of the “leaders.” GERTIK A., GESSEN, SHAROV and KUKLIN came to Leningrad. Whether EVDOKIMOV's last visit to Leningrad in 1934 was for transmitting directives I do not know. The leitmotif of all LEVIN's information was the instruction to gather cadres, strengthen organizational connections, sustain hostile sentiments against the party leadership and Comrade STALIN, and skillfully disguise one's views to remain in the party.

In 1929, when there were bread difficulties, LEVIN V. told me that Zinoviev and Kamenev considered the country's situation grave, that the party leadership had gone bankrupt, and that they could not be dispensed with. In the same year LEVIN conveyed, from the “leaders'” words, that a combination (bloc) with the Rights was not excluded and that contact had been made with them on this matter. When RYKOV came to Leningrad to report to the activists, LEVIN summoned me and assigned me to speak at the party activists' meeting criticizing the Trotskyists and Rights. He explained that such a speech would strengthen confidence in the Zinovievists and provide better camouflage for us. According to him, he gave a similar assignment to REM \[1\] and somebody else. After my speech LEVIN praised me. In 1929 LEVIN periodically summoned me to receive my information about the sentiments of the party organization and the district's workers (I worked in the Moscow–Narva District) and to transmit Moscow center instructions. Sometimes I visited his apartment, sometimes \[2\] DMITRIEV T., who lived at the Astoria. SLEPKOV, who worked at the university with GAIDEROVA, \[3\] attended these meetings. He usually remained silent, while GAIDEROVA displayed the sharpest dissatisfaction with party policy and leadership. In 1929, in connection with my journey to Moscow to conclude a socialist emulation agreement between the Moscow–Narva and Krasnaya Presnya District Soviets, LEVIN instructed me to visit Zinoviev, gave me telephone numbers and told me to inform him. Zinoviev lived on the Arbat; I do not remember the building exactly. Having informed him in detail about the sentiments of the party organization and workers and described our counterrevolutionary organization's cadres, I asked him for instructions. Zinoviev told me his spirits were low, since he was given no work or opportunities to deliver reports, and that he was writing “Marxism, Leninism and War.” He showed great interest in affairs within the leading core of the Regional Committee and Regional Executive Committee in Leningrad. On returning from Moscow I informed LEVIN, KORSHUNOV and somebody else, apparently DMITRIEV T., gathered for this purpose at LEVIN's apartment. Some time later I personally informed SOSITSKY of my meeting with Zinoviev.

Concerning visits to Leningrad by representatives of the counterrevolutionary organization's Moscow center: I know of two visits by KUKLIN, two by GERTIK and several by GESSEN.

KUKLIN first came at the end of 1929 to LEVIN V.'s old apartment on Nadezhdinskaya Street. Present during the conversations were me, LEVIN, KOSTRITSKY and somebody else whose surname I do not remember. KUKLIN then expressed dissatisfaction with the “leaders'” caution and favored decisive action. He considered that Zinoviev and Kamenev must demand from the Central Committee the right to speak publicly so that the masses would not forget them. The second time KUKLIN also stayed with LEVIN while passing through from Sestroretsk. I, KORSHUNOV, BASHKIROV, SOSITSKY and LEVIN V. gathered. Since KUKLIN was returning from a resort, he mostly listened to our information and only told us that we must continue accumulating forces; this was in autumn 1932.

I know of two visits by GERTIK to Leningrad in 1932. Both meetings took place at LEVIN V.'s apartment. The first was in spring 1932; DMITRIEV, BASHKIROV, LEVIN and I gathered; we also expected KOTOLYNOV, but he did not appear. GERTIK said that Zinoviev and Kamenev's determination to fight the party leadership was growing, and that to consolidate forces hostile to that leadership the “leaders” did not rule out supporting the Rights. Everyone present then gave information on sentiments; I spoke of sentiments in the Soviet apparatus and party circles.

Between the first and second meetings with GERTIK, LEVIN traveled to Moscow and visited Zinoviev's dacha. On returning he gathered me, KORSHUNOV and, I believe, BASHKIROV; somebody else was also there. He conveyed the same account of Zinoviev and Kamenev's sentiments as GERTIK. He also instructed us to register former oppositionists who had returned to the party, study their sentiments, and study and cautiously work on young people joining the party, since these people did not know the history of the Zinovievist opposition's struggle and learned it through official party materials.

The second meeting with GERTIK was at the end of summer. Present were LEVIN, RUMYANTSEV, KORSHUNOV, me and others whose surnames I do not remember. GERTIK said discontent was growing in the party and that even elements who had previously broken completely with Zinoviev and Kamenev were returning to them (Safarov); he cited STEN, who brought a platform \[4\] to Zinoviev and tried to negotiate a bloc with him. He informed us of Zinoviev and Kamenev's expulsion from the party, saying that the Central Committee had fabricated the case to settle accounts with them. He inquired about reactions to their expulsion and instructed us to vote for expulsion in the cells to avoid exposing ourselves.

During DIMITROV's trial LEVIN informed me of TORGLER's behavior in court and said that one ECCI official had turned out to be a Polish spy. He cited these examples to characterize Comintern cadres. In 1933 GESSEN visited. At LEVIN's apartment there were me, some woman and somebody else. GESSEN reported that Zinoviev and Kamenev had been exiled to different places, that their situation was poor and Zinoviev's grave, since he was very ill. LEVIN said that in such a condition Zinoviev would hardly recover and added: “STALIN will drive him to his death.” During every visit by Moscow center representatives instructions were given to sound out workers' sentiments, their attitude toward Zinoviev and Kamenev, whether they had forgotten them, and so on.

In one conversation LEVIN V. told me that we must appear more frequently before KIROV, CHUDOV and KODATSKY in order to gain promotion and occupy an appropriate position.

I know that during LASHEVICH's funeral in 1931, Zinoviev and Kamenev used their visit for an illegal conference with representatives of the Leningrad counterrevolutionary organization. Since I did not attend, I do not know its contents.

Question: Whom else, besides those named, do you know or suspect to be members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization?

Answer: Connected with me was SHIRYAEV Dmitry, an engineer at the Institute of Metals. He is married to Evdokimov's wife's sister, is connected with him and always informed me of his sentiments. I know from LEVIN V. that TARTAKOVSKAYA, an engineer at the Northern Shipyard in the Narva District, is connected with him and belongs to the organization; SHEINBERG, a Regional Finance Department official, is connected with LEVIN; in 1924 he conducted active factional work with me in Novosibirsk and was connected with LEVIN V.; LEVIN Moisei works in the Academy of Sciences at the Council of the Country's Productive Forces (SOPS) and is also connected with LEVIN V. LEVIN Mikhail, an employee of the State Publishing House and a great friend of GERTIK, had contacts with Trotskyists. In 1932 or 1933 LEVIN V. told me that LEVIN Mikhail was in Leningrad conducting work. The Zinovievist KASPERSKY, former secretary of the party committee at the Kazitsky factory, also has Trotskyist connections. I must inform the investigation that when DMITRIEV Timofei comes from Rostov he travels to Kolpino for contacts on the counterrevolutionary organization's business.

Question: The investigation has information that the numerous counterrevolutionary, anti-party documents found during the search of your premises constitute the counterrevolutionary organization's archive. What can you say?

Answer: I kept these documents intending, if necessary, to use them for the organization's purposes. I know that LEVIN V. hid its archive in a secure place in Razliv, near Sestroretsk; he told me this himself. I do not know the surname of the person keeping the documents, but that person is very close to Zinoviev.

Question: What can you testify concerning the terrorist act against Comrade KIROV committed by NIKOLAEV, a member of the Leningrad counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization, on the direct instructions of individual members of its center?

Answer: The malicious hatred of the party leaders, especially Comrade STALIN, cultivated daily in the organization educated individual members in the spirit of terror. It is no accident that the person killed was precisely Comrade KIROV, under whose direct leadership the Leningrad Zinovievist opposition had been defeated at the time. The counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization and each of its members individually bear political and moral responsibility for this murder.

Question: A Mauser and a Browning were found during the search. Where did you acquire these weapons and for what purpose did you keep them?

Answer: I acquired both revolvers in the Red Army, when I served there in command and political posts. I gave the Browning to my wife, a party member since 1927. There are permits to carry both revolvers, but both have expired. I kept the weapons for self-defense —

N. MYASNIKOV.

Interrogated by —

Deputy Head of the SPO, GUGB — LYUSHKOV.
