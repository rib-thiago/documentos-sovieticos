INTERROGATION RECORD

OF KHARITONOV, Moisei (Mikhail) Markovich

31 December 1934.

QUESTION: In your previous testimony you spoke of the Zinovievists' organized work in Leningrad. Explain in greater detail how the work was structured, who directed it and who maintained contact with whom.

ANSWER: As I have already testified, I was personally informed by ZINOVIEV about the Zinovievists' organized counterrevolutionary work. I myself had no contacts, did not converse with the Leningrad people and therefore do not know the details. One thing is certain: the task was preserving cadres and cultivating among them an ideology different from that cultivated by the party and its Central Committee. In practice this meant that systematic, malicious criticism of the leadership of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), inspired from above, particularly by ZINOVIEV personally and then his closest followers, cultivated among members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization a hostile attitude toward party leaders, especially Comrade STALIN. It is therefore no accident that the Leningrad counterrevolutionary organization, ZINOVIEV's principal base in his struggle against the party, slid into treason, betrayal of proletarian revolutionary interests and directly fascist methods of struggle against the party leaders.

I repeat that I do not know the organization's detailed work, but repeatedly heard positive assessments of the Leningrad people from ZINOVIEV in 1929, 1930 and probably 1931. I remember much talk in the first period after the Fifteenth Party Congress about attracting the youth, the so-called “leaderless” oppositionists. Later I heard ZINOVIEV's satisfaction that the youth had returned and were again working alongside the other Zinovievists. To this period, late 1929–early 1930, belong the conversations I related in my testimony of 24 December: “Our Leningrad youth not only managed to preserve themselves but are conducting active work and have district connections.” In the same period the question of ensuring representation in the direction of work was discussed; I remember ZINOVIEV mentioning in this connection two people who could and should ensure leadership of the counterrevolutionary organization's work: LEVIN Vladimir and RUMYANTSEV Vladimir.

In 1930, possibly 1931, ZINOVIEV shared his thoughts with me about Zinovievists returning to Leningrad from various parts of the Union. He considered this important for preserving cadres in concentrated form and continuing subversive work in the party.

As far as I knew, ZINOVIEV personally retained the main Leningrad contacts in his own hands, since Leningrad visitors to Moscow always called at his apartment. I heard from him that KOTOLYNOV visited in 1929–1930. Among the Muscovites, EVDOKIMOV and especially KUKLIN visited Leningrad most frequently. I say especially KUKLIN because I know ZINOVIEV more than once expressed dissatisfaction that EVDOKIMOV, during his Leningrad visits (this concerns 1930), was insufficiently active in meeting people.

I do not remember the exact time or circumstances, but approximately in 1929–1930, in a conversation about organizational work, somebody said contact must be maintained “along a chain,” preserving the appearance of personal acquaintance.

I cannot now recall other conversations about the Leningrad counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization's work; in particular, I cannot remember whether the organization's Leningrad center was discussed in my presence.

KHARITONOV.

HEAD OF THE 6TH SECTION OF THE SPO, GUGB — KORKIN.
