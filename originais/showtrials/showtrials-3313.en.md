INTERROGATION RECORD

On December 18, 1934, I, HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO,

LULOV, interrogated citizen SEVEROV, P. P.

SEVEROV, Pyotr Petrovich, born in 1906 in Leningrad; father a worker; Russian, a citizen of the USSR. 30 Tchaikovsky Street, apartment 4. Senior engineer of the KPP at the “Krasny Putilovets” factory. Married. Wife: Susanna Mikhailovna SEVEROVA. Daughter aged ten months. Self-educated. A member of the VKP(b) since 1923. In 1927 expelled from the Party by the Murmansk Control Commission for factional activity. Reinstated in 1928 by the Leningrad Regional Control Commission. Removed from the military register owing to illness.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that former participants in the Zinovievist opposition, having preserved their old organizational connections and worked practically to strengthen them, constitute a closely knit counterrevolutionary organization. What can you testify on this question?

ANSWER: Yes, I confirm this. After many of us were expelled from the Party and subsequently reinstated, over a number of years, beginning in 1928, we concentrated again in Leningrad, restored our old connections and undertook practical work to consolidate Zinovievist cadres, which led to the reestablishment of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization.

QUESTION: Name the members of your counterrevolutionary organization. From what elements was the organization formed?

ANSWER: Our organization was formed predominantly from the group of so-called “leaderless”, headed in its time by SAFAROV. Among those belonging to the organization I can name the following: myself, SEVEROV, KOTOLYNOV, G. POPOV, TOLMAZOV, ZVEZDOV, RUMYANTSEV, FADEEV and ANTONOV. I am closely connected with all these people in the affairs of the counterrevolutionary organization. I also know that some members of the organization belong to the association of former comrades-in-arms, which was used for counterrevolutionary purposes. I also maintained contact with NATANSON until her arrest.

QUESTION: What tasks did the organization set itself?

ANSWER: The main task consisted of struggling against the Party leadership, preserving ourselves at any cost within the Party as an independent force and safeguarding the old Zinovievist traditions. Ultimately the task was to replace the Party leadership. In practice this was expressed in cultivating sharply negative sentiments toward comrades STALIN, KAGANOVICH, KIROV and others.

QUESTION: How was your counterrevolutionary organization structured?

ANSWER: For reasons of secrecy we did not all gather together very often. Contact was maintained chiefly through a chain. Everything was done, however, to keep abreast of one another's sentiments. We gathered chiefly at my apartment (POPOV, FADEEV, ANTONOV), at GORSHENIN's and at meetings of the active old Komsomol members. Contact with the leading Zinovievist counterrevolutionary Center was maintained chiefly through RUMYANTSEV. I do not know who personally belongs to the Center, but I know of its existence. During our meetings and conversations we discussed topical questions of Party policy in various fields with a view to working out a common position on them.

I have read the record; it has been written correctly from my words.

SEVEROV.

INTERROGATED BY: HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO — LULOV
