SUPPLEMENTARY TESTIMONY

of REPNIKOV, Pyotr Mikhailovich — January 14, 1935.

QUESTION: During the interrogation of January 9, you testified that in 1928 ZINOVIEV personally instructed you to ascertain the composition of the participants in the Zinovievist group who were in Moscow and establish contact with them. Clarify what prompted this instruction.

ANSWER: As I have already testified, after ZINOVIEV returned from Kaluga in the summer of 1928, I visited his apartment. In our conversation ZINOVIEV told me that a struggle against the Right was coming to a head within the party. Therefore STALIN would supposedly be compelled to form a bloc with ZINOVIEV to fight the Right. In this connection ZINOVIEV instructed me to ascertain what opposition forces were present in Moscow, so that during negotiations with the Politburo he, ZINOVIEV, could say what resources he had for this struggle. I regarded ZINOVIEV's line in connection with the impending struggle against the Right as ZINOVIEV's desire to exploit the difficulties within the party to bring the opposition into the party leadership. In essence, this was a new plan to struggle for power, to overthrow the Stalin leadership. This step by ZINOVIEV was a naked, unprincipled striving to attain leadership at any cost whatsoever.

QUESTION: The investigation knows that you informed not only BLINKOV and BARANOV but also other persons of the existence of the underground Zinovievist group conducting a counterrevolutionary struggle against the party and Soviet power. The investigation asks you to name those persons.

ANSWER: I acknowledge that, apart from BLINKOV and BARANOV, about whom I already testified during the interrogation of November 9, I spoke of the existence of the underground Zinovievist group to Alexander ALEKSANDROV. This conversation with ALEKSANDROV took place before the Ryutin affair, that is, in the summer of 1932, in the office of ALEKSANDROV, who was then working at Lenoblsnab.

I do not remember exactly whether I spoke of this with Nikolai ANDREEV. I believe ANDREEV may have known of the existence of the Zinovievist underground from BLINKOV and BARANOV, with whom ANDREEV maintained contact.

QUESTION: The investigation knows that, among the participants in the Zinovievist group, you repeatedly discussed the party's policies and the measures of the Soviet government in a counterrevolutionary spirit. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: Yes, I confirm it. In particular, at the end of 1931 I visited BLINKOV's apartment, where, besides myself, BARANOV, BLINKOV, and Nikolai DMITRIEV were present. The latter had come to Moscow from Samara or Saratov, where he was in charge of the construction of some factory. In the conversation DMITRIEV spoke venomously about the party leadership and spoke of the personal dictatorship of STALIN, who had supposedly substituted himself for the Central Committee. Touching on the five-year plan, DMITRIEV asserted that the plan would not be fulfilled, since the relevant organizations in a number of regions were submitting inflated figures on the supposed fulfillment of the plan. Other questions were also touched upon in this conversation, but I do not remember how they were assessed.

QUESTION: The investigation knows that, while belonging to the underground Zinovievist group after the Fifteenth Party Congress, you conducted counterrevolutionary work until the day of your arrest. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: Yes, I confirm it. Having joined the Zinovievist opposition during the period of the Fourteenth Congress, an opposition that degenerated in the subsequent stages of its struggle against the party into an underground counterrevolutionary group, I did not sever my ties with it, remained a participant until the last days, and conducted counterrevolutionary work within its ranks against the party and the authorities. Specifically, this found expression in the fact that, while maintaining organizational ties with participants in the group, as early as 1931 I informed BLINKOV, BARANOV, and ALEKSANDROV that the Zinovievist underground was continuing its work against the party; I participated in counterrevolutionary conversations, in particular having a conversation with MANDELSHTAM in 1933 about defeatism; I maintained regular contact through a chain of connections with participants in the group — all this shows that I embarked on the path of outright betrayal of the party and Soviet power. This became possible because of the duplicity and double-dealing that persisted in me until the last days.

I have read the record, and it has been written down correctly from my words.

REPNIKOV.

INTERROGATED BY:

ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE OO, GUGB — BRICCI.

Certified true: A. Svetlova
