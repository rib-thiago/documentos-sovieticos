INTERROGATION RECORD

Of EVDOKIMOV, Grigory Eremeevich

December 19, 1934

QUESTION: With which former members of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc living in Leningrad did you maintain contact?

ANSWER: I had no regular contact with any of them. I visited Leningrad and in recent years met KOSTRITSKY, NALIVAIKO and MYASNIKOV; in Moscow I met NALIVAIKO, KOSTRITSKY and LEVIN, who came from Leningrad, and I think I met KOTOLYNOV.

KOSTRITSKY: I met him in 1933–1934 in Leningrad and Moscow approximately six to eight times. I did not discuss political subjects with him.

NALIVAIKO: He visited me in Leningrad once, without my invitation, I think in 1933. We discussed his studies; as far as I remember, I did not discuss politics with him. After that I saw him some three or four times in Moscow at the People's Commissariat of Supply, where he came on business. I had no political conversations with him.

MYASNIKOV: During 1933–1934 I met him twice and once—on the same day in Leningrad—in September 1934. I called at his workplace to ask him to obtain gramophone records. Then I called at his home that evening to collect the records. I discussed political subjects with him, but there was nothing anti-Party in our conversations.

KOTOLYNOV: I think I met him once by chance on the street in Moscow. My conversations with him were strictly personal. This meeting was, I think, in 1933.

LEVIN: During this same period, 1933–1934, I saw him once in Leningrad. The circumstances of the meeting precluded any conversation with him. In Moscow he visited me about twice in 1933. I do not remember my conversations with him.

QUESTION: What do you know about the existence in Leningrad of a counterrevolutionary anti-Party organization of Zinovievists?

ANSWER: In 1929, I do not remember exactly everyone present, but I remember KOTOLYNOV and GESSEN; with them was one other person or several people, who came to my apartment without my invitation. Someone among the visitors raised the question of establishing a center of the Zinovievist organization in Leningrad. I then had the impression that the discussion of a center among KOTOLYNOV, GESSEN and the others who came to see me had arisen earlier, before their visit. I said then: no centers. Those present insisted on their view and considered it necessary, as they put it, to establish “at least a little center” or chain connections. In our subsequent conversations none of them raised this question again.

QUESTION: What testimony can you give concerning the terrorist act against Comrade KIROV committed by L. NIKOLAEV, a member of the counterrevolutionary organization of Zinovievists?

ANSWER: I understand that if, in due time, ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, I or any other of us had declared to the young people with the utmost categorical firmness that we agreed entirely and completely with the line of the Party Central Committee, the emergence in Leningrad of an underground counterrevolutionary anti-Party organization among members of the former Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc would scarcely have been possible.

I therefore consider that we must bear moral and political responsibility for this terrorist act.

But I must say that this act contradicts all my political convictions and that, of course, I never thought of and could not have pursued in my activities any aim even remotely approaching this.

G. EVDOKIMOV

INTERROGATED BY: RUTKOVSKY.
