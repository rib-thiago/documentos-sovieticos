INTERROGATION RECORD

OF SAFAROV G. I., dated January 13, 1935.

(supplementary)

In addition to my testimony, I report the following to the investigation:

At a meeting in 1931 at the Comintern, in MAGYAR's presence, V. RUMYANTSEV insistently asked me: “Why don't you, Georgy Ivanovich, make peace with ZINOVIEV?” MAGYAR then told RUMYANTSEV: “Well, you know Georges's character.” RUMYANTSEV was clearly already closely connected with the Zinovievist organization then, and reported that he “had seen Uncle Sasha (KUKLIN) and Eremeich (EVDOKIMOV).”

In 1932, when I was at KOTOLYNOV's apartment in Leningrad, KOTOLYNOV said outright that “Volodka (RUMYANTSEV) has got in cahoots and is mixed up with LEVIN, who was the Zinovievist plenipotentiary in Leningrad.

At a meeting at my apartment in 1933, when KOTOLYNOV and TARASOV visited me with their wives, in response to my question about RUMYANTSEV, KOTOLYNOV said that he and S. MANDELSHTAM had definitively joined up with the Zinovievists.

I recall that after LOMINADZE visited me for an explanation, the following day or a day later MAGYAR informed me of LOMINADZE's impressions of this meeting. LOMINADZE told MAGYAR that he was fully satisfied with the meeting, that “one can go together with SAFAROV,” and immediately expounded to him his view of forming a counterrevolutionary bloc. First of all, in his conversation with

MAGYAR, LOMINADZE particularly emphasized that “we made a mistake by fighting TROTSKY and the Trotskyists, and now we are reaping the fruits. The balance of forces in the Party has changed.” LOMINADZE stressed that he considered this his grouping's fundamental mistake and hinted at the Zinovievists' “instability.” In this conversation LOMINADZE spoke “for joint action” by his grouping, the Trotskyists, and the Zinovievists, but against an agreement with the Rights.

I recall that during my meeting with ZINOVIEV at Ilyinskoye in July 1932, ZINOVIEV touched on the question of KOMAROV and UGLANOV in connection with his assessment of the conditions for forming a counterrevolutionary bloc of different groupings. He said: “Of course, we shall come to terms with the Lominadze supporters; we can probably come to terms with MRACHKOVSKY and SMIRNOV, and you should help with this. As for the Rights, we must take a look and sound them out.” After this the conversation turned to KOMAROV and UGLANOV.

In this connection I also recall that I. N. SMIRNOV and MRACHKOVSKY, in conversations with me, set out their view of forming a counterrevolutionary bloc of various anti-Party groupings. They favored participation in this bloc by their Trotskyist organization, the Zinovievist organization, and the Lominadze supporters. Bringing in the Rights aroused doubts in them. The question of “joint work” by their “kolkhoz” and the Zinovievist organization aroused no doubts in them. They also showed a very keen interest in LOMINADZE and his leftist “entourage,” asked about this grouping's sentiments and intentions, and expressed the conviction that the Lominadze supporters “will come with us.”

I recall that in the conversation at Ilyinskoye in September 1932 ZINOVIEV explained the delay in forming the counterrevolutionary bloc, or rather in formalizing it, by saying that “with this surveillance, it is difficult for all the responsible people to meet together. Such a meeting would greatly facilitate agreement and the elimination of all misunderstandings.”

In the same conversation ZINOVIEV said that the left Social Democrats — in Germany, Seydewitz's group — were gathering up workers who “with a correct course would come to us.” And considerable numbers of the left Social Democrats themselves would join the Communist Party “if the Stalinist regime did not dominate the parties.” ZINOVIEV recalled his success at the Independents' congress in Halle: “Those were different times. Nothing could stop the workers.”

Written in my own hand — SAFAROV.

INTERROGATED BY:

Head of the 1st Section of the SPO of the GUGB of the NKVD of the USSR — PETROVSKY.
