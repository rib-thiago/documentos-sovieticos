INTERROGATION RECORD

OF FAIVILOVICH, Leonid Yakovlevich, dated 14 December 1934.

FAIVILOVICH, Leonid Yakovlevich, born in 1900, from Leningrad workers, Jewish, Soviet citizen; resides in Moscow, Malaya Dmitrovka, Stary Pimenovsky Passage, 14, apartment 1-a. Deputy Head of the Main Cotton Directorate, NKZ of the USSR. Wife: TEREMYAKINA, Elizaveta Mikhailovna, instructor of the Zamoskvorechye District VKP(b) Committee; father: FAIVILOVICH, Ya. G., VKP(b) member, lives in Leningrad, works at the OAKh factory in Moscow District; home address: 10 Zvenigorodskaya Street, apartment 5. Without property. Education: three classes of town school, no specialised education; VKP(b) member since 1918. Expelled from the Party in 1928 for belonging to the Leningrad opposition. Registered at the Sokolniki District Military Commissariat, category 10; did not serve with the Whites.

QUESTION: With which participants in the Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc do you maintain contact?

ANSWER: I was transferred from work in Central Asia to Moscow in March 1932. During this time a number of persons who had participated in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc called at my home and sometimes at work.

I recall the following persons:

Leonid KHANIK called at my workplace in 1932–1933.  
Andrei TOLMAZOV visited my home in 1932.  
Ivan Ivanovich TARASOV visited my home several times, most recently in November 1934.  
Nikolai DMITRIEV visited several times at different dates, most recently in November 1934.  
Aleksandr SEREDOKHIN visited twice, in late November and early December 1934.  
Ivan Ivanovich KOTOLYNOV visited my home once in December 1933 and stayed overnight with I. I. TARASOV.  
Yakov TSEITLIN called a couple of times in summer 1934; he also visited me here in Leningrad during my business trip in October or November 1934.

I also had two chance meetings with EVDOKIMOV in Moscow, a month and a half ago at the Council of People's Commissars and on 18/VIII at the aviation festival. I think it was in 1932 that I met KHARITONOV in the street; he was then arriving from England, at the time of the rupture of trade relations with England.

QUESTION: The investigation knows that you are a member of the counter-revolutionary Zinovievist organisation. Do you plead guilty to this?

ANSWER: Yes, I was in fact drawn in and was a member of the organisation.

QUESTION: What do you know of the time and circumstances of the organisation's emergence?

ANSWER: I cannot say when the organisation emerged. From 1925 I worked in Central Asia and returned to Moscow only in March 1932. From then on participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc, mainly Leningraders, began calling on me systematically. Some of them, KOTOLYNOV and TARASOV, considered themselves wronged and were embittered against the Party leadership, chiefly Comrade STALIN. At these meetings hostile remarks against Comrade STALIN were made, thus fostering and cultivating counter-revolutionary sentiments against the Party leadership, in which I too participated.

We spoke of the need to bring ZINOVIEV and his supporters into the Party leadership. Such a conversation, for example, took place at my apartment a year ago with KOTOLYNOV and TARASOV.

INTERROGATED BY: Head of the 6th Section, GUGB — KORKIN.
