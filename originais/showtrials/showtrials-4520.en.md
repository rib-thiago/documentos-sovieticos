INTERROGATION RECORD

of ROTSKAN, Pyotr Eduardovich

January 18, 1935.

December 1932. I came to Moscow on assignment from the North Caucasus Territorial Committee of the AUCP(b); I met organization members KUKLIN and BAKAEV.

Regarding party policy, KUKLIN spoke in the same anti-party terms as on the previous occasion; he repeated his former argument about “overhead costs” and cited, as an example of the party leadership's incompetence, information he had about the Kuban: that half the Kuban had died of starvation,” that the party and government had failed to organize grain procurement. In conclusion KUKLIN pointed out that under a different leadership — meaning ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV — such a situation would have been ruled out.

BAKAEV expressed his opinion on the articles by ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV that had appeared in the newspapers criticizing their former opposition theories and views.

BAKAEV pointed out that these articles had appeared very opportunely in connection with the forthcoming party purge. Comparing ZINOVIEV's advice to me before I underwent the 1929 purge with this remark by BAKAEV makes clear precisely what purpose BAKAEV's remark served: the articles eased the Zinovievists' position during the screening and allowed them to continue double-dealing within the party.

During the same period I was in Leningrad; I met with the Zinovievist RUMYANTSEV. RUMYANTSEV's sentiments were decidedly anti-party.

In May or June 1933 I saw Nikolai GORDON in Rostov. He spoke in extremely harsh terms of the territorial party leadership and the overall party leadership; speaking of party policy, GORDON denied the achievements in socialist construction, pointing out that “it is unclear which there is more of in these achievements: the achievements themselves or the losses associated with them.”

As an example, GORDON pointed out that he had information about famine in the Kuban, and so on. GORDON repeated our usual assertion about the leadership's incompetence, that under a differently constituted leadership such facts could not have occurred. He told me of an alleged “hunger march” on Moscow by the weavers of Ivanovo-Voznesensk. GORDON spoke with incredible harshness about a number of Politburo members; GORDON's entire account breathed terrible malice against the party leadership.

In my opinion, two persons contributed in no small measure to shaping these sentiments and views in GORDON: KUKLIN and BELOBORODOV, a former Trotskyist who worked in Rostov in 1932–1933.

In September 1934, while on leave, I met RUMYANTSEV in Kislovodsk; his political sentiments had not changed in any way.

He told me he intended to visit ZINOVIEV, who was also resting in Kislovodsk at the time. I replied that I too intended to visit ZINOVIEV.

I visited ZINOVIEV. In answer to my question whether it was true that he had been removed from the editorial board of “Bolshevik,” ZINOVIEV confirmed this, stating that it had been done without grounds.

Regarding the events in Spain, ZINOVIEV said that in his opinion the revolution would be suppressed.

To what I have set out, I add the following fact: in the recent past I visited the apartment of GRIDASOV, head of the special sector of Glavenergo, an AUCP(b) member. I found there a person whose surname was either MIKHAILOV or AVAKUMOV.

According to GRIDASOV, this person had formerly been an active participant in the “Workers' Opposition” group. His signature appears on the Workers' Opposition document of the “22.”

I became convinced that both GRIDASOV and the person mentioned held sharply anti-party sentiments, and I formed the impression that GRIDASOV was clearly under this person's influence.

I informed BAKAEV of these impressions.

The record has been written correctly from my words and read by me.

ROTSKAN.

INTERROGATED BY:

ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE EKO, GUGB, NKVD OF THE USSR — DMITRIEV.

Certified true: A. Svetlova
