INTERROGATION RECORD

Of YAKOVLEV, Moisei Nakhimovich, dated January 5, 1935.

Question: In your testimony of January 4 of this year you say that participants in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization remained in the party for tactical maneuvering, intending to continue the struggle. Explain what fundamental tasks your organization set itself?

Answer: When, after the 15th Party Congress, we decided to enter the party, we considered any struggle outside the party inconceivable and believed it possible only on party ground.

While recognizing the decisions of the 15th Party Congress, in our assessment of the decisions of the 14th Congress we remained on the old counterrevolutionary Trotskyist positions, but we did not trust the party leadership, believing that it provided no guarantee against degeneration; we therefore considered that, on entering the party, we should not dissolve within it but, by concealing ourselves, preserve ourselves and continue the struggle against the party leadership.

The organization set as its fundamental task the struggle for the party leadership headed by Comrade STALIN.

One method of this struggle was cultivating hostility and hatred toward Comrade STALIN.

Question: Until what period did your counterrevolutionary organization exist?

Answer: The organization was created after the 15th Party Congress from a considerable part of the opposition activists of the Vasilyevsky Ostrov district; at its head were myself, YAKOVLEV, and SHEINBERG [1].

Our organization existed as an organized, active counterrevolutionary group until the beginning of 1929, when it was dissolved. Illegal meetings and regular gatherings ceased. But in fact, through chance meetings and conversations, it preserved itself in a curtailed form until the end of 1934.

Question: Describe your organization’s organizational structure and contacts?

Answer: The participants belonging to the organization constitute qualified, fully prepared activists of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization.

Our organization was not isolated; its contacts extended chiefly downward.

Upward contacts took the form of my two meetings with ZINOVIEV in 1928 and 1929. The organization’s downward contacts were maintained by its participants at individual enterprises and plants (SEMYONOV, “Skobar,” has contacts at the Cable Plant, and so forth).

Question: The investigation knows that KASPERSKY, a member of your organization, and SHEINBERG, who died in the autumn of this year, were closely connected with LEVIN, V. S.?

Answer: I did not know this. Recently I had not met SHEINBERG for about two years.

I met KASPERSKY twice in 1934; before that I had not seen him for two years. When we met, he said nothing to me about his contact with LEVIN.

I personally have not met LEVIN for about seven years; in the summer of 1934 I spoke with him by telephone concerning the search of the apartment.

Question: According to the testimony of SAKHOV, who was directly connected with ZINOVIEV, in his counterrevolutionary activities he had direct contact with SHEINBERG, a member of your organization. What do you know about this question?

Answer: I have not met SAKHOV for several years; the last time was about four years ago, or seven. SAKHOV is a close relative of SHEINBERG. I know nothing about his meetings with SHEINBERG except one occasion of which SHEINBERG told me: SAKHOV came to Leningrad on business and visited him. I do not remember well, but it is very possible that this was when I also saw SAKHOV.

I have known SAKHOV as a Zinovievist since the 14th Party Congress, when he worked in the Narva district; he was then away from Leningrad for a long time and reappeared in Leningrad during the 15th Party Congress.

I said nothing to SAKHOV about the existence of our Vasilyevsky Ostrov organization, but I allow, and it is probable, that he knew about it from SHEINBERG, since the latter was frequently visited by SEMYONOV (“Skobar”) and KASPERSKY. —

I have read the testimony; it has been recorded correctly from my words.

YAKOVLEV.

INTERROGATED BY

OPERATIONS OFFICER OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB — FEDOROV.

Certified correct: Churbanova
