TO THE DEPUTY PEOPLE'S COMMISSAR AND HEAD OF THE LENINGRAD NKVD ADMINISTRATION, Comrade AGRANOV.

In response to your proposal that I set out in writing additions and comments on my previous testimony, I report the following:

1. The conversation about ZINOVIEV's expulsion from the Party and KOSTINA's opinion on this question did indeed take place in my presence and with my participation twice: the first time at GORSHENIN's apartment, in the presence of KOZHURO [1], EVDOKIMOV, SHAROV, KOSTINA and myself; the conversation took place during a game of “kozyol” (a card game). When KOSTINA dug in, insisting that UGLANOV should be punished no less than ZINOVIEV, EVDOKIMOV declared: “Well, tomorrow we shall prove to you that the Central Control Commission's decision is correct in every respect.” Indeed, the following day EVDOKIMOV and SHAROV came to my apartment, and later GERTIK, and the conversation continued.

Thus the Central Control Commission's resolution on ZINOVIEV's case and KOSTINA's opinion on the matter were discussed at my apartment by a group of former Zinovievists in an entirely organized manner.

2. Besides the former Zinovievists working in Leningrad mentioned earlier, I met BRODSKY about three times (he works as director of the former Stary Lessner factory). The first meetings took place in Moscow, in the building of the People's Commissariat of Heavy Industry, where BRODSKY came on various business; I last saw him in the premises of the Leningrad Soviet secretariat, where I called for a railway ticket reservation during my last business trip to Leningrad. Political subjects were never touched on in conversations with BRODSKY.

In 1931, when I was in Leningrad for my brother's funeral, I met Pyotr REPNIKOV (in the Business Club dining hall). He was then studying, I think, at the Transport Academy; the conversation concerned his studies and REPNIKOV's work prospects as a future specialist; I remember REPNIKOV saying that local organizations were helping him with his studies in every way.

During my leave in 1934 I stayed at the same sanatorium as I. M. RUSSANOV, who works in the planning bodies of the Leningrad Executive Committee, and Sasha ALEKSANDROV, who worked in the system of the People's Commissariat of Supply; there were no political conversations with either comrade.

Several times in the Glavenergo building I met RUTENBERG or RUTENBERG (I do not remember the surname exactly), who works as director of the Leningrad Branch of the Thermal Engineering Institute (somewhere in Lesnoye), which belonged to the Glavenergo system. RUTENBERG asked me several times about ZINOVIEV: where he worked, what he was doing. The last time—it was, I think, in the spring of 1934—I answered RUTENBERG's question about ZINOVIEV: “I do not know, since I have not visited ZINOVIEV for a long time.” To RUTENBERG's question “Why?” I replied: “He is a great muddler; he muddled the theory of the ‘uneven development of capitalist society’ and the theory of ‘building socialism in one country’; he gets confused himself and confuses others; enough.”

3. Concerning various family evenings arranged to bring former Zinovievists together, I can say that such parties were indeed arranged. I attended some of them, for example at KAMENEV's and GERTIK's homes. The main thing at these parties was not anti-Party conversations (although these too sometimes took place), but meetings of former Zinovievists.

4. Concerning the platform of the Rightists I can add the following: first, after the examination of ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's case by the Central Control Commission in connection with the RYUTINITES' platform, I learned that KUKLIN too had read this platform.

Second, on calling at KAMENEV's once I asked him what was new, what Academia would publish in the near future. KAMENEV, after searching on my [2] desk, handed me some little book with a battered binding. The little book was approximately a quarter-sheet in size, untitled, handwritten but in printed lettering, with an introduction and divided into chapters. I asked KAMENEV what it was. KAMENEV answered approximately as follows: “Some malicious exercise by the Rightists.” To my question “May I read it?” KAMENEV replied: no, since he had to return it to ZINOVIEV the next day. About two days later ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV were summoned to the Central Control Commission over the Ryutin platform case. I think the document I saw at KAMENEV's was that platform. I think KAMENEV too had not read it, since he spoke of it indifferently.

5. Concerning the letter that ZINOVIEV and others intended to submit to the Central Committee in connection with certain difficulties in the country, I can say the following: around the summer of 1932 ZINOVIEV once asked me whether we ought not to write something like a collective letter to the Central Committee about the difficulties and the need to provide Party members with detailed information on this question. I replied that the causes of the difficulties were generally known to the Party rank and file, and that submitting such a letter might be understood as a factional attack. SAFAROV's participation in drafting such a letter is incomprehensible to me, since SAFAROV, in my observation, was on unfriendly terms with ZINOVIEV.

6. I cannot say definitely whether any negotiations were conducted by ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV with the “Rightist-Leftist” bloc. However, I know that in the summer of 1931, when I visited ZINOVIEV's dacha particularly often (my younger daughter was living there at the time), I frequently met STEN at ZINOVIEV's. To my question to ZINOVIEV, “What news does STEN tell you?” ZINOVIEV once replied: “We have various literary matters.”

7. On the question of a bloc with the Rightists I can say the following. In my presence KAMENEV told ZINOVIEV that he had not expected the meeting with BUKHARIN at all, or the latter's anguished complaints about the difficult situation in which he found himself. He came as though seeking sympathy, but did not say a word about the need to fight the present leadership. BUKHARIN abused STALIN personally with the choicest swearwords.

Some time later the conversation at ZINOVIEV's apartment about which I have already reported took place. In response to a question put to ZINOVIEV by one of those present about how he would regard an action by the Rightists, ZINOVIEV declared that the Rightists' ideas were not worth a brass farthing. And EVDOKIMOV remarked that we would fight the Rightists with all our might.

BAKAEV.

December 20, 1934.
