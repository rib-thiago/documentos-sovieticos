INTERROGATION RECORD

conducted by the HEAD of the 4th Section of the SPO of the UGB — KOGAN L.

17 December 1934.

TOLMAZOV Andrei Ilyich (supplementary)

QUESTION: What further information can you give about the activities of the counterrevolutionary organization?

ANSWER: Expanding on my testimony about our organization's links with the members of the center in Moscow, I can state the following: when we — SEREDOKHIN and I — were in Moscow in December 1931, we visited KUKLIN to obtain information about affairs at the center. KUKLIN informed us that ZINOVIEV had managed to publish the book “War and Marxism,” that this book should be widely publicized as one of the best books giving a genuinely Marxist-Leninist assessment of the treating \[1\] question. KUKLIN showed us the book. KUKLIN told us of the expulsion from the party of BAKAEV's wife, KOSTINA. I do not remember what he said about the reasons for the expulsion, but he described in great detail how he had spoken against KOSTINA's expulsion. KUKLIN went on to say that, in the cell where he had spoken against KOSTINA's expulsion, no one had contradicted him and he personally had not been subjected to any attacks. In KUKLIN's opinion, the cell's attitude toward the matter was due to his particular tactics, which consisted in his ability to gain the cell's confidence. When we asked how he did this, KUKLIN recounted giving his cell a lengthy address recalling his prerevolutionary struggle, that his address had been listened to very attentively, and that such addresses should be practiced because they enhanced the Zinovievists' standing in the eyes of the party membership. On the same trip, SEREDOKHIN and I visited SAFAROV, who was then working in the Comintern. He gave us fairly detailed information about the situation in the Comintern, particularly in Eastern policy. When we asked for information about affairs within the country and the party and about ZINOVIEV's situation, he told us nothing about the domestic political situation and spoke very sharply of ZINOVIEV as a person. We also visited EVDOKIMOV, but he was ill, so we did not touch upon political questions in our conversation. We asked only about people and their situation in the party and at their workplaces. EVDOKIMOV asked us about our personal circumstances and difficulties at work. He was particularly interested in RUMYANTSEV's situation. In Moscow I also met Lazar SHATSKIN \[2\], who worked at Tsentrosoyuz. At his home we found FEIGIN, then a member of the Central Control Commission, and therefore we had no conversations. I have already testified about my meetings with MINAEV. As to who came from the center to Leningrad, I have nothing to add to what I have already stated.

Recorded correctly from my words and read to me.

A. TOLMAZOV.

Interrogated by: — L. KOGAN.
