INTERROGATION RECORD

OF POPOV, Georgy Fedorovich — 15 December 1934.

1\. POPOV

2\. Georgy Fedorovich

3\. Born in 1902

4\. Native of Leningrad. Father worked as a watchman. Russian.

5\. Leningrad, Ozerki, Vyborg Highway, 10/3, apartment 1

6\. Worked as director of the Uritsky Tobacco Factory

7\. Wife: POPOVA, Genuvefa Vladislavovna, “Koopmetpribor” factory; daughter aged 1 year 2 months

8\. Without property

9\. Secondary education

10\. VKP(b) member since 1918; expelled in Kursk in 1927 for factional work, reinstated in 1928

11\. Has not been

12\. Exempted for illness.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that after the Party's ideological and political defeat of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc, its participants retained organisational ties and constituted a cohesive counter-revolutionary organisation. What can you testify about this?

ANSWER: Yes, I confirm it. The remaining participants in counter-revolutionary Zinovievism retained organisational ties with one another and in fact constitute a counter-revolutionary organisation.

I know that the organisation's members, educated by Zinovievism in a sharply hostile spirit towards Party leaders, especially Comrade STALIN, systematically continued at their meetings to conduct counter-revolutionary conversations aimed at discrediting the Party leaders.

QUESTION: Describe the tasks the counter-revolutionary Zinovievist organisation set itself.

ANSWER: As I have already testified, the organisation arose from Zinovievist cadres on the basis of struggle against the VKP(b) leadership. Its task follows from this and may be briefly formulated thus: to remove by any means the present VKP(b) leadership headed by Comrade STALIN and advance ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV and their supporters to Party leadership. In practice, as far as I know, this meant shaping sentiments against Comrades STALIN, KAGANOVICH, KIROV and others.

QUESTION: Whom do you know among the organisation's leadership?

ANSWER: I do not know precisely; I was not told. But I assume V. RUMYANTSEV is one of the leaders. I personally met him in 1933 and have not seen him since. I recall a fact that may interest the investigation: in 1928, on returning from Kursk, I was at RUMYANTSEV's apartment and he said in conversation that one must return to the Party. I understood this to mean returning not through complete disarmament but with double-dealing intentions. I was already a Party member then. I acknowledge my guilt in failing then to report these double-dealing intentions and RUMYANTSEV's organisational role. I concealed this from the Party. I personally tried to move away from him then, though I did not break completely, but subsequent meetings with other members of counter-revolutionary Zinovievism led me to participate again in the covert struggle against the Party leadership.

QUESTION: Which members of the counter-revolutionary Zinovievist organisation belonged to the Leningrad old Komsomol members' “Combat Veterans' Association”, and was the association not used for counter-revolutionary purposes?

ANSWER: The association included A. I. TOLMAZOV, N. SHATSKY, V. I. ZVEZDOV, V. RUMYANTSEV, N. ANTONOV, Fedor FADEEV and myself, G. POPOV.

Sincerely repenting of my crimes against the Party and Soviet power, and considering it necessary fully to expose the counter-revolutionary Zinovievism to which I belonged, I must state that all our work in the combat veterans' association was in fact a struggle against the Party line and leadership. Counter-revolutionary Zinovievism's work changed in recent years, becoming considerably more clandestine. The association was used primarily as a place to concentrate members of the Zinovievist organisation, where among other conversations a hostile attitude to Party leaders was revealed and formed.

In the “Combat Veterans' Association” and “Istomol”, the political significance of counter-revolutionary Zinovievism was blurred.

QUESTION: Which participants in the counter-revolutionary Zinovievist organisation did you meet, and what was the content of your conversations?

ANSWER: Besides the meeting already described above, I saw V. RUMYANTSEV twice in 1933.

The first meeting was accidental, and of what he said I remember only this: addressing me, he said, “If you need help finding work, come, we will find you a job.”

The second time I visited him at the District Soviet seeking help finding employment; he was drawing up a work plan, spoke reluctantly and gave no definite answer.

In late 1933 I met N. SHATSKY at a combat veterans' association meeting. From the meeting at 59 Moika we walked together to the tram stop. On the way SHATSKY expressed personal grievances and dissatisfaction with his position. At that time the theses for Comrade KAGANOVICH's report to the Seventeenth Congress had been published. SHATSKY expressed sharply negative sentiments about several questions addressed in them. He said “the Party leadership puts pressure on the Party”, that the theses actually meant “the victory of the apparatus over the Party”, and similar counter-revolutionary fabrications. He also spoke negatively about abolishing collective leadership.

I systematically met Pyotr SEVEROV once every three or four months; he visited me and I him. On one of my last visits, ANTONOV was also there at the same time. During our conversation SEVEROV said ZINOVIEV had been removed from the editorial board of “Bolshevik” magazine; when I asked why, SEVEROV and ANTONOV replied that the Central Committee had judged an article by ZINOVIEV opportunist. All of us, myself, ANTONOV and SEVEROV, assessed this as persecution of ZINOVIEV; we considered it further confirmation that the Party leaders gave him no opportunity to work in the Party.

At this meeting SEVEROV said he had visited V. RUMYANTSEV.

Apart from the evening at SEVEROV's, I met ANTONOV nowhere else.

In 1933 I met KHANIK and VINOGRADOV; in 1934 I saw TSARKOV, but had no substantial political conversations with them.

I have read the record; it has been correctly taken down from my words.

POPOV.

INTERROGATED BY:

Head of the 6th Section, SPO, GUGB — KORKIN.
