INTERROGATION RECORD

Of Grigory Fedorovich FEDOROV, dated January 6, 1935

(supplementary)

Question: In your interrogation record of December 30, 1934, you gave the investigation various information about the activity of the Zinovievist organization after the Fifteenth Congress.

What further testimony can you give about this organization's activity, its (the organization's) political views, and who led it (the organization)?

Answer: After the Fifteenth Congress, the Zinovievist organization did not cease its counterrevolutionary activity, but merely changed its working methods, adapting them to underground conditions. The principal task facing the Zinovievist organization at that time was to go underground in an organized manner; to restructure its underground working methods while preserving the opposition's principal cadres within the Party.

The organization's cadres were preserved by an instruction to submit fraudulent, double-dealing statements to the Party expressing agreement with and submission to the Congress's decisions.

Soon after the Fifteenth Congress a meeting of the Zinovievist organization's political center together with a number of active participants in that same organization took place at ZINOVIEV's apartment. This meeting worked out a number of measures pursuing one principal task: do not dissolve the organization; preserve contacts; do not cease work.

Of those present at this meeting, I remember ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, LASHEVICH, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, KHARITONOV, SHAROV, NAUMOV, GESSEN, KUKLIN, ZALUTSKY, and GERTIK. I do not now remember who else was there.

To ensure the organization's transition underground, the above-mentioned meeting at ZINOVIEV's apartment decided to carry out a screening of all the organization's members. As a result of the screening, all participants in the Zinovievist organization were divided into three categories.

The first category consisted of those members of the organization who had worked openly during the opposition period and who entered the Party on the organization's instructions without renouncing their old views.

The second category consisted of those members of the organization who had been concealed oppositionists during the opposition period and who, like the persons in the first category, had not renounced their old views.

The third category consisted of those members of the organization considered insufficiently reliable and unsuitable for work underground. Regarding some persons in this category, this was because it had not been precisely established whether they retained their old views and would go underground; regarding the others, because it was believed that these persons' qualities would not allow them to work underground.

It was decided to maintain the closest contact with the first category, disguising it as “comradely” meetings and “friendly” correspondence. With the second category, it was decided that contact should also be maintained, but more cautiously so as not to compromise them. Regarding the third category, it was decided to break all contact so as not to expose the organization's activity.

A number of our organization's political positions remained essentially the same as before the Fifteenth Congress.

On the possibility of building socialism in one country, our organization continued to consider the Fifteenth Congress of the AUCP(b)'s decision on this question incorrect. ZINOVIEV's constant assertion, including during the years of the Fifteenth Congress, amounted to saying that one could not speak of the possibility of building socialism in one country, since this contradicted the teachings of MARX–LENIN and led to “national narrow-mindedness”; the Party's adoption of the theory of the possibility of building socialism in one country obliged the Party to “grow into” the capitalist system, which in turn bound the Comintern's work hand and foot.

In the collectivization conducted by the Party, our organization saw nothing but shortcomings. The organization proceeded from the assumption that collectivization had failed, collective farms were breaking up everywhere, the peasantry was ruined, the area left unsown was continually increasing, the country was heading for famine, and the alliance between town and countryside was increasingly breaking down.

On industrialization, our organization likewise proceeded from the assumption that the industrialization plan would fail, that this plan was assured neither of material resources nor of raw materials, and that the plan would mainly be carried out through exploiting workers and impairing their material living conditions. The Central Committee of the AUCP(b) suffered from “gigantomania,” resulting in colossal costs in implementing the industrialization plan.

Our organization assessed the Party's decision to improve the working class's material living conditions as a declarative decision, a decision with no foundation whatever.

I do not now remember the numerical data used by ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV, but their conclusions on the working class's material living conditions were as follows:

a) The working class's material living conditions were not improving but deteriorating, since the relationship between rising prices for food and consumer goods and rising wages was such that real wages were falling;

b) Fulfillment of the plan for industrializing the national economy would require additional funds not provided for in the plan. The shortage of these funds would be covered by intensifying the exploitation of workers and worsening their material living conditions.

On the situation within the Party, our organization likewise proceeded from the old positions that there were “higher-ups” and “rank and file” in the Party, that self-criticism was being suppressed, and that all fundamental questions of Party life were decided by the Party leadership apart from the Party and even without the Central Committee.

This entire system of our organization's political views, which came from ZINOVIEV and the members of the organization's political center, reached every active participant in our organization, both in Moscow and in the provinces, thanks to the preservation of organizational ties. The entire thrust of this system of counterrevolutionary views was directed at discrediting the Party leadership and accusing it (the leadership) of inept and incorrect leadership of the country. This entire system of views set as its principal task bringing our organization's representatives, ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV, into the Party leadership, which in the organization's opinion was to weaken STALIN's leading role and ensure implementation of a number of our organization's political positions.

All gatherings of our organization's members, all meetings of its (the organization's) participants, were 99 percent devoted to analyzing the country's economic and political life in the spirit of the counterrevolutionary positions about which I testified above; I repeat, all this was accompanied by the most vile slander and the most vicious attacks on the Party's leaders.

In its struggle against the Party leadership, the organization disdained no means. The organization's members were breeding grounds for the most vile slander, “little rumors,” and gossip about the Party leadership.

The organization chiefly pinned its hopes on the Party still facing colossal difficulties (the worsening of workers' material living conditions, the failure of industrialization and collectivization, and consequently famine in the country), during which our organization would manage to unite around itself all the “discontented” within the Party for a joint struggle against the leadership of the AUCP(b).

Throughout the Zinovievist organization's activity, malice and hatred toward the Party leadership were cultivated in its ranks; the organization did not hesitate to resort to slander, lies, deception, and distortion of facts, that is, all the most vile means borrowed from the arsenal of fascism were employed. As a result, the Zinovievist organization brought up such scoundrels as NIKOLAEV and his closest accomplices, who slid onto the path of terror, the path of murdering KIROV. Therefore our entire Zinovievist organization, myself included, bears full political and moral responsibility for KIROV's murder.

On who led the Zinovievist organization, I report that there existed in Moscow a political center of the organization, historically formed from former participants in the Zinovievist center of the Fifteenth Congress period who retained their old positions and grouped around ZINOVIEV. The center was the headquarters directing the organization's work to weld together its cadres, maintain contact with the provinces, formulate its political line, and prepare forces for the battles with the Party leadership which, in the organization's opinion, still lay ahead.

The political center included ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, SHAROV, KUKLIN, GERTIK, KHARITONOV, NAUMOV, and myself. At the head of the center was ZINOVIEV, who directly led all the organization's work. ZINOVIEV's closest associates were KAMENEV and EVDOKIMOV.

Recorded correctly from my words and read by me.

FEDOROV

INTERROGATED BY:

SECTION HEAD, EKO, GUGB, NKVD OF THE USSR — CHERTOK

Certified correct: Churbanova
