INTERROGATION RECORD OF N. N. SHATSKY

Conducted on 7 December 1934 by LYUSHKOV, Deputy Head of the SPO, GUGB, NKVD of the USSR, and DMITRIEV, Assistant Head of the EKO, GUGB, NKVD of the USSR.

SHATSKY, Nikolai Nikolaevich, born in 1899 in the former Tula Province. Father: Nikita Vasilievich, railway worker, deceased; mother: Evdokia Semionovna, deceased; Russian, USSR citizen. Residence: Leningrad, Petrograd Side, 7/3 Pesochnaya Street, apartment 36. Standard-setting and planning engineer at the Electrotechnical Institute. Married; wife: Elena Ignatievna, 35; daughter: Lidia, 12; son: Oleg, 5. Owned land before the revolution; cannot state how much. Graduated from the Leningrad Mechanical Institute. Non-Party; VLKSM member from 1917 to 1927; VKP(b) member from 1923 to 1927, expelled in 1927 for belonging to the Trotskyist-Zinovievist opposition, manifested in distributing Trotskyist literature. Served in the Red Guard; in the Red Army from April 1919 to 1920; held various elected positions in Party, Komsomol and trade-union organisations from 1917 onwards. No convictions. Military registration category: private. Did not serve with the Whites.

Testimony on the substance of the case:

QUESTION: Where did you first meet NIKOLAEV, whose identity you recognised from the photograph shown to you?

ANSWER: I have known NIKOLAEV, whom I identified today, 7 December, from the photograph shown to me, since 1923. He was secretary of the Vyborg District VLKSM Committee — technical secretary. At the time I was secretary of the Komsomol organisation at the “Krasny Vyborzhets” factory and dealt with NIKOLAEV on Komsomol matters.

QUESTION: Did you meet NIKOLAEV during 1934?

ANSWER: Yes; once in approximately February–March 1934, and once in the summer, in July or August.

FIRST MEETING — I was in the building housing the Institute for Compiling the History of the Komsomol Movement, known as “Istmol”; I had come to the bureau of the Komsomol veterans' association, which was in the same building.

I happened to encounter NIKOLAEV, who worked at “Istmol”. He asked how I had got into the institute, where I worked and what I did. After my answers, he showed me where the bureau was, and we parted.

SECOND MEETING — it took place at the corner of Krasnykh Zor Street and Lev Tolstoy Square, near KIROV's apartment.

QUESTION: What did you discuss with NIKOLAEV at this second meeting?

ANSWER: I told him it was hard to be outside the Party, complained that I was cut off from the Party and had twice been dismissed from Soviet employment as a former oppositionist, adding: “The Soviet authorities no longer trust me.”

QUESTION: During your conversation with NIKOLAEV, did you see the car carrying Comrade CHUDOV, secretary of the VKP(b) Regional Committee for Leningrad Region?

ANSWER: Yes, the car drove past the tram stop where we were talking. NIKOLAEV nodded towards CHUDOV and asked: “Do you know him?” I looked towards CHUDOV.

QUESTION: When exactly were you dismissed as a former oppositionist?

ANSWER: In the spring of 1934 I was dismissed from the Ilyich factory, where I had taken a job as assistant mechanic. In June 1934 I was dismissed from my job as shop engineer at the “Progress” factory.

Correctly taken down from my words. I have read the record —

SHATSKY

INTERROGATED BY:

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO, NKVD OF THE USSR — LYUSHKOV

ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE EKO, GUGB, NKVD OF THE USSR — DMITRIEV

My words “near the house where Comrade KIROV lives” should be understood as indicating that this was near the house where responsible Party and Soviet officials live, including Comrade KIROV.

SHATSKY
