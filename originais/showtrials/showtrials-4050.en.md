INTERROGATION RECORD

Of SAKHOV, Boris Naumovich,

dated January 3, 1935.

Question: The investigation knows that, upon returning from the East Siberian Territory, where you served on a commission of the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), you informed members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization about the results of the commission’s work in a tendentious and distorted manner. What can you testify about this?

Answer: I told KUKLIN about the abuses uncovered by the Central Committee commission in the East Siberian Territory, giving an extremely negative assessment of that territory’s leading officials. I made no tendentious assessment, but merely described the actual situation,

uncovered by the commission. I told many comrades about this; possibly I also spoke to members of the organization among them. It is possible that I also spoke of disagreements on certain questions within the commission itself.

Question: EVDOKIMOV and GORSHENIN testify that you not only informed them tendentiously about the situation in the East Siberian Territory but also presented to them in a distorted manner the role of the central party and Soviet bodies. Do you acknowledge this?

Answer: I may have told EVDOKIMOV and GORSHENIN about the results of the commission’s work, but I deny informing them in a distorted manner.

Question: Did you approach EVDOKIMOV with a request to arrange a meeting with ZINOVIEV for you?

Answer: It must have been in 1929–30, perhaps also in 1931, that I told EVDOKIMOV several times that I wanted to see ZINOVIEV.

Question: For what purpose did you visit ZINOVIEV in 1930, and what questions did you discuss with him?

Answer: I wanted to learn personally about his attitudes. During this visit, the following questions were touched on in my conversation with ZINOVIEV:

1) I asked him whether he spoke anywhere. ZINOVIEV replied that he wanted to speak, but was not given assignments. He said that, where he had spoken, he had been

received very well.

2) About the Right and the Central Committee’s line. But I remember how this question arose, and I expressed my view that the party’s line on this question was correct. ZINOVIEV said: “Yes, it is correct, but we were talking about this long ago.”

3) About the Comintern. The day before I had read a counterrevolutionary, slanderous article by TROTSKY on the Comintern in the collection “From the Foreign Press.” I shared this with ZINOVIEV. I remember that he spoke of the Comintern’s insufficient activity. Throughout the conversation I sensed ZINOVIEV’s wariness; the conversation probably lasted about half an hour. Then KAMENEV came there too, and I left ZINOVIEV’s. This visit of mine to ZINOVIEV may have been at the end of 1929 rather than in 1930.

Question: Did you visit ZINOVIEV on any other occasion?

Answer: Once more, at the end of 1932. In the autumn of 1932, during one of my visits to EVDOKIMOV, I learned from him that ZINOVIEV was being summoned to the Central Control Commission and that he, EVDOKIMOV, was going to meet him. I went with EVDOKIMOV; BAKAEV met us at the Ilyinsky Gate. We waited there for ZINOVIEV for about an hour and a half, but, as became known later, ZINOVIEV did not appear because of a heart attack.

From BAKAEV and EVDOKIMOV’s conversation I learned that ZINOVIEV was accused of contact with some Rightist group. They considered that ZINOVIEV could not have had such contact and that this case was being instigated to finish ZINOVIEV politically.

Later I learned from colleagues at work (I do not remember whom) that a Rightist grouping had been uncovered and that ZINOVIEV was connected with it. I do not remember the month or day; in the morning, without telephoning beforehand, I went to ZINOVIEV’s apartment and found him at home. I told ZINOVIEV that, from the comrades’ words, I knew of his contact with the Rightist grouping and that his, ZINOVIEV’s, banishment was being considered. In turn, I asked ZINOVIEV to tell me what the matter was, but I could obtain no reasonably clear answer from him. ZINOVIEV merely said that “I shall have to leave, but I had no contact with this grouping.”

I told SHEINBERG about this visit of mine to ZINOVIEV on January 1, 1929 [1]; I do not remember whether I told anyone else.

Even before visiting ZINOVIEV, I had been at GORSHENIN’s apartment and had also told him about the Rightist grouping, ZINOVIEV’s contact with it, and the latter’s forthcoming banishment. GORSHENIN attached no significance to my report. I gained the impression that he already knew all this.

After that meeting in 1932, I never saw ZINOVIEV anywhere again.

Question: Do you acknowledge that in 1932 you visited ZINOVIEV to inform him of the forthcoming decisions in his case in connection with the discovery of Ryutin’s counterrevolutionary group and ZINOVIEV’s contact with it?

Answer: No, I do not. I went to ZINOVIEV in the hope of obtaining information from him about the substance of the case, his role in it, and his attitude toward the accusation the party had brought against ZINOVIEV.

Question: The investigation knows that during the 15th Party Congress you handed twenty copies of the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist-Zinovievist platform to a concealed oppositionist for illegal safekeeping. What can you testify about this?

Answer: I remember nothing about this question.

Question: KHARITONOV testifies that you personally told him about handing twenty copies of the platform “into reliable hands.” Do you deny this?

Answer: I do not remember the conversation with KHARITONOV. I recall that during the 15th Congress I personally burned several different documents in my apartment in Leningrad.

SAKHOV.

INTERROGATED BY: HEAD OF THE 6TH SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD — KORKIN.

Certified correct: Biryukov

RGASPI, fond 671, inventory 1, file 126, folios 210–213.

[1] Thus in the text; the events described, however, date to December 1932.
