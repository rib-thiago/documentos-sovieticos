INTERROGATION RECORD

Of ANISHEV, Anatoly Isaevich,

dated January 5, 1935.

QUESTION: What do you know about GERTIK, A. M.?

ANSWER: I know that GERTIK, A. M., has been a member of the Moscow center of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist opposition from the beginning of our struggle against the party until the most recent period. Organizational contacts converged chiefly on GERTIK. My factional trips along the Volga and to Tiflis during the period between the 14th and 15th Congresses were made on GERTIK’s instructions; he gave me the addresses of all the oppositionists with whom I was to make contact. At that time both I and other oppositionists arriving from the provinces also received illegal literature from him.

After the 15th Congress my contact with the center passed chiefly through GERTIK. At GERTIK’s apartment I met almost all members of the organization at various times. Concerning 1932 and 1933, until GERTIK’s first arrest, I know that he had direct contact on the counterrevolutionary organization’s business with ZINOVIEV, as well as the other members of the center, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, and KUKLIN, and also a considerable portion of the organization’s members, particularly in LENINGRAD (LEVIN, BASHKIROV, myself, NATANSON, TSVIBAK). At GERTIK’s above all one could always learn about almost any former oppositionist.

QUESTION: Tell the investigation facts relating to GERTIK’s activities in the Zinovievist organization?

ANSWER: I have already testified that GERTIK was the principal figure in the Zinovievist organization on whom all organizational threads converged, particularly those from the provinces. It was considered obligatory for each of us arriving from anywhere to visit GERTIK. GERTIK’s apartment, both on the Arbat and later by the Petrovsky Gate, was the site of constant oppositionist gatherings, constant meetings with one another. From there, through GERTIK, meetings with ZINOVIEV were also arranged.

Concerning recent years (1932 and 1933), I know the following facts: a gathering of members of the organization under the guise of a “party” at Gr. FEDOROV’s, attended by BAKAEV, KOSTINA, GERTIK, NATANSON, and myself (in 1932); the same at BAKAEV’s (in 1932); two trips by GERTIK to Leningrad to contact members of the Leningrad organization.

In all these conversations, as well as those at his apartment, GERTIK’s political attitudes and positions emerged as a position of irreconcilable bitterness against the party leadership and Comrade STALIN, as the position of a man infinitely devoted to ZINOVIEV and regarding all party policy as non-Bolshevik, since, from GERTIK’s viewpoint, there could be no correct policy under which ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, and others were not in the leadership. The question of leadership, of alleged “commanding” in the party, of party bureaucracy, of the promotion of “obedient and blind officials” and the suppression and pushing aside of “genuine Bolsheviks,” that is, ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, and others: these assessments and positions were central to all GERTIK’s statements. Counterrevolutionary bitterness against the party leadership, against STALIN and everyone with STALIN, always emerged in GERTIK, whatever he spoke about.

QUESTION: What can you testify about GERTIK’s work in Leningrad as a member of the center of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization?

ANSWER: I know of two trips by GERTIK to Leningrad. During these trips GERTIK, as a member of the Moscow center of the Zinovievist organization, contacted members of the organization living in Leningrad, learned about attitudes in Leningrad, and informed us about the situation in the country and the party.

I well remember the character of this “information” from GERTIK through a conversation he had with NATANSON and me at NATANSON’s apartment in 1932.

GERTIK told us about the famine in Ukraine and other difficulties in the countryside (I remember the North Caucasus). GERTIK described these difficulties in greatly exaggerated terms, obscuring the successes of collectivization and explaining them (the difficulties) by the crude maneuvers of the party leadership, supposedly carrying out collectivization by administrative methods and disorienting the entire party apparatus through its maneuvers. Asked whether we could set our own political program against the party line, GERTIK replied that we were now so isolated within the party that we could not at present offer a positive program, but that the issue now was not so much the program as who exercised party leadership and how; that until those old cadres now removed from leadership were brought into it, particularly ZINOVIEV into the Comintern, KAMENEV and others into leading work in economic policy, the difficulties would increase and could lead the country to ruin. GERTIK also spoke of the difficult situation in the Comintern because, according to him, in Western Communist Parties too, the party leadership and the Comintern leadership were removing the best cadres. Therefore, in GERTIK’s opinion (which NATANSON and I also supported), despite the rise of the revolutionary movement in the West, the Communist Parties had no real Bolshevik leaders and suffered a series of defeats.

I know that, apart from NATANSON and myself, GERTIK during his trips to Leningrad contacted the members of the organization BASHKIROV and LEVIN.

These counterrevolutionary positions of GERTIK, whom we knew as someone close to ZINOVIEV and as a member of the Moscow center, naturally had a definite significance for us all.

Written by me in my own hand — ANISHEV.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE UGB — LULOV.

Certified correct: A. Svetlova
