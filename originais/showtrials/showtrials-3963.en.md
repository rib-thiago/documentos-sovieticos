INTERROGATION RECORD

OF DMITRIYEV, Nikolai Aleksandrovich (supplementary).

January 1, 1935.

QUESTION: In your testimony of December 24, 1934, you stated that after the Fifteenth Party Congress most Zinovievists returned to the party's ranks for double-dealing purposes, continued counterrevolutionary activity and reestablished the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization. Clarify this testimony with regard to yourself personally.

ANSWER: Like most Zinovievists, I returned to the party in 1929 as a double-dealer. In my case, this double-dealing manifested itself, first, in the fact that I saw and personally helped ensure that former Zinovievists, while preserving their connections with one another, also preserved themselves as a group of people previously united by the struggle against the party over a long period before the Fifteenth Congress; second, by continuing organizational and political connections with former Zinovievists, I myself participated in preserving the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization, which gradually became more active in the struggle against the party; and third, I concealed all this from the party.

I likewise acknowledge that I was a member of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization until the very latest period, since the entire system of my connections with former Zinovievists and my political conversations with them aimed not only at preserving the Zinovievist cadres but also at directing the activity of these cadres toward counterrevolutionary activity.

QUESTION: What form did your practical participation in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization take?

ANSWER: My participation in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization consisted in preserving organizational and political connections with former participants in the Zinovievist opposition from 1930 until the day of my arrest; at meetings with them, I informed them and they informed me about other members of the organization and their sentiments; I also participated in discussing questions of the country's political life in a counterrevolutionary spirit.

My connections with members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization over the last four years can be described concretely as follows:

In 1930–1932, I was in Saratov and at that time had connections there with ABRAMSON, SAFAROV, ZALUTSKY, AVDEYEV and ROTENBERG. During these same years (1930–1932), when visiting Moscow, I maintained contact through home visits with FAIVILOVICH, PERIMOV and GORSHENIN; at the latter's home, I met ZILBERMAN, GESSEN and KOSTINA, NAUMOV, BRAVO and EVDOKIMOV—the latter I met by chance at the “Paris” hotel.

In January 1933, I came to Leningrad and established the following connections there during 1933–1934: 1) LUKIN Vasily, who visited me and whom I visited; we also met at the home of my brother DMITRIYEV Evgeny; 2) TSEITLIN—I met him once at the apartment of my brother Evgeny DMITRIYEV, once at FAIVILOVICH's home in Moscow, and once at Smolny; 3) LEVIN Mikhail—I met him twice at my brother DMITRIYEV Evgeny's apartment and once called at his apartment with my brother, but did not find him at home, so we called on MAVRIN, where RZHAVIN and a large group of others were playing cards; 4) ALBANSKY; 5) KHANIK; 6) ALEKSANDROV Aleksandr—I met these three at the “Combat Comrades' Association”; 7) SOBOL—I saw him at LUKIN's apartment; 8) MANDELSHTAM Sergei—I met him at my brother DMITRIYEV Evgeny's apartment; 9) REPNIKOV—I visited his apartment twice; 10) ANISHEV—I met him at Smolny; he invited me to visit, but I did not go.

During these two years (1933–1934), in Moscow I maintained contact only with FAIVILOVICH, whom I visited at his apartment during my trips to Moscow.

I must say that all the connections I have listed were connections among people who lived a life apart from the party, concealing their true political views and intentions from it. In conversations, these people expressed hostility toward the party leaders, Comrades STALIN, KAGANOVICH, KIROV and MOLOTOV. At the same time, the idea was put forward that the party was wrong to exclude the opposition leaders—ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV and other\<s\>—from the leadership. I remember the following conversations of counterrevolutionary content: if I am not mistaken, in 1931 I had a conversation with ZALUTSKY about questions of party policy in the collectivization and industrialization of the country. The thrust of this conversation was that the party made mistakes in practical work, creating financial strain in the country, severely undermining the ruble and thereby also leading other branches of the national economy into decline. At the same time, ZALUTSKY expressed dissatisfaction with his personal position and, as I understood him, was waiting for the party to come to him for advice and offer him a leading position in party work.

The meaning of conversations with GORSHENIN and NAUMOV came down to the assertion that although the party's general line was correct, it was not being implemented in practical work under the existing leadership, since the party CC acted too rigidly and inflexibly.

In a conversation with EVDOKIMOV dating from 1932, we expressed agreement with the Right on the peasant question. I remember EVDOKIMOV stating that difficulties in the countryside existed exclusively because “the peasant does not want to join the collective farm.” In conversation with me, GORSHENIN reinforced this same idea of EVDOKIMOV's by pointing to growing peasant dissatisfaction in the countryside with the party's agricultural policy. In our conversations, almost all of us complained of the party's distrust toward us and undeserved pressure on former Zinovievists. In this connection, I recall a conversation at GORSHENIN's home in KOSTINA's presence about her expulsion from the party. KOSTINA's expulsion was interpreted as improper, unfounded pressure on former Zinovievists, while KOSTINA herself was censured not for speaking in defense of ZINOVIEV, but for the very fact of an open, imprudent intervention lacking conspiratorial precautions, which could bring attacks upon other Zinovievists as well. TSEITLIN and LUKIN expressed resentment against the party for its supposedly senseless treatment of them.

The testimony has been recorded correctly from my words and read by me.

DMITRIYEV

INTERROGATED BY—GOLUBEV

I request the following addition to the record: from the end of 1932, upon arriving in Leningrad, I began withdrawing from my connections with former Zinovievists, finally breaking off connections in Moscow except with FAIVILOVICH and retaining those connections in Leningrad listed in the record; of the latter, I maintained constant relations only with LUKIN.

DMITRIYEV.

INTERROGATED BY—GOLUBEV.
