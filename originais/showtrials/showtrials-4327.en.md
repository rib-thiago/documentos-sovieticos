TO THE CENTRAL COMMITTEE OF THE AUCP(b), I. V. STALIN.

Glorious and wise Bolshevik leader Iosif Vissarionovich!

At precisely this moment, when I have fully realized the entire depth of my own fall, I felt an irresistible inner need to address this letter to you, so that at least now I might open my soul before the Party and say what made me a criminal before the Party and the country. And I address you specifically because you personally were the man who, ten years ago at the very beginning of my political error, tried to turn me onto the Bolshevik path. I was one of those young members of the Komsomol Central Committee toward whom you personally showed extraordinary concern, exceptional sensitivity, and comradely warmth, patiently explaining our error to each of us individually and teaching us Bolshevik wisdom. And now I distinctly remember how, at the AUCP(b) Central Committee, in such a personal conversation, you told me: “FAIVILOVICH and others with him are practicing circle cliquishness in the Komsomol Central Committee, doing anti-Party work. Circle cliquishness will lead to no good. You must break with it and work together with the Party.”

Soon after this conversation, I. D. KABAKOV himself came to my apartment and, literally for several hours in a friendly, comradely conversation, sought to convince me how ruinous ZINOVIEV's path was for the Party and called me onto the Bolshevik path.

BUT I did not let your direct instructions and advice penetrate to the depths of my Party consciousness, and instead of remaining a Bolshevik and fighting under the banner of LENIN–STALIN, I, among other young people, turned away from the Party path and followed the political adventurers ZINOVIEV, TROTSKY, and KAMENEV.

That is why today, when one involuntarily recalls that unforgettable conversation with you, one experiences with such agonizing sharpness, literally to the point of pain, the entire now-realized vileness of one's own position as a result of ZINOVIEV's treacherous path, which I followed for about ten years and which finally led me, together with the remnants of the former Leningrad opposition, into the swamp of counterrevolutionary Zinovievism, which politically inspired S. M. KIROV's murderer.

On what did ZINOVIEV educate the youth he drew into fighting the Party? Throughout all these ten years, the central axis of his ideas was the propagation of frenzied hatred toward the Party's glorious Bolshevik leadership and above all toward STALIN, an unremitting organizational struggle to overthrow that leadership.

This struggle took different forms in different periods. At first it cloaked itself in ideas and petty notions of “saving” the proletarian dictatorship and the world proletarian revolution from the “Thermidorians.” Under this flag, in 1927 the united Trotskyist-Zinovievist opposition took to the streets in an anti-Soviet demonstration against the Party leadership and Soviet power. And it was no accident that as early as 1927, even before the Fifteenth Party Congress, the skulls of individual oppositionists cracked, and fascist shoots appeared in those cracks.

“We must act with weapons in our hands!” proposed the Samara oppositionist LIBIN.

“It is time to put dynamite into action!” responded the Moscow oppositionist Komsomol member STREMYAKOV.

And when life dissipated the platforms, programs, and petty notions of the Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc like smoke, and it became harder to deceive the Party, the opposition widely employed the maneuvering tactic of double-dealing.

It was precisely by employing this tactic that I returned to the Party with others in 1928, when I, KOTOLYNOV, and RUMYANTSEV, on the one hand, visited Em. YAROSLAVSKY at the Central Control Commission to negotiate our return to the Party and, on the other, simultaneously sought advice on how best to write the statement to the Central Control Commission from such a political rogue and master of double-dealing as L. KAMENEV.

The Zinovievist anti-Party group, defeated at the Fifteenth Party Congress, returned to the Party to preserve its cadres in order to continue fighting the Party. Having returned, it preserved organizational ties among its members, thereby creating an underground counterrevolutionary organization which essentially had a single aim: to continue fighting the Party leadership and restore ZINOVIEV to the Party leadership. And in this criminal activity circle cliquishness rendered an indispensable service, becoming a universal form of double-dealing. Circle cliquishness made it possible to combine remaining in the Party ranks and using its trust with wild, unbridled counterrevolutionary slander against the Party's Bolshevik leadership, against the Party's leader and inspirer STALIN and his closest associates.

Under such circle cliquishness, a sort of tacit mutual cover became established: at meetings and conversations in “one's own milieu,” one could say anything whatever about the Party leadership, in every key and variation, from direct, malicious counterrevolutionary attacks to “minor” counterrevolutionary jokes, gossip, and whispering. And all this remained unpunished, everything stayed within the circle of “our own boys,” where one could sometimes argue or disagree with this or that “argument” of a conversational partner, but none of it reached the Party. Only after the shot on December 1 at Smolny did it reach the Party.

The ideology and practice of double-dealing and circle cliquishness became so fused with consciousness that a number of people from ZINOVIEV's former opposition sometimes conscientiously performed assignments in particular areas of work while at the same time, “as a matter of course,” weaving the counterrevolutionary web of a tacit conspiracy against the Party leadership. It seemed to me myself, especially during the last year of my life, that I had taken Party positions, toward which I was even impelled by sincere aspirations. But it only seemed so. In reality, by meeting people from the former Zinovievist opposition and participating in anti-Soviet conversations, I was a participant in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization, and I did not carry isolated glimmers of Party consciousness to the point of decisively and finally ending double-dealing and circle cliquishness and showing the Party Zinovievism's counterrevolutionary face.

And now, after ZINOVIEV and his followers for ten years instilled a consciousness of irreconcilable, militant struggle against the present Party leadership, after the slogan of replacing this leadership became the ABC of Zinovievism's counterrevolutionary arsenal, the alpha and omega of its entire program of fighting the Party, can one, after all this, be surprised that it was precisely within the shell of Zinovievist circle cliquishness that an underground terrorist organization was born and split off for the physical extermination of the Bolshevik Party's leaders? The banner of irreconcilable struggle against the Leninist-Stalinist leadership, above all to overthrow STALIN, unfurled by ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV at the Fourteenth Party Congress; the anti-Soviet demonstration of all opposition forces in 1927; the LIBINs and STREMYAKOVs; the treacherous tactic of double-dealing and circle cliquishness; the continual cultivation until the very latest time of counterrevolutionary slander and hostility toward the Party leadership; finally, the treacherous bullet against KIROV on December 1, 1934: all these are links in one unbroken chain.

The fascist thugs who fired at KIROV, at the Party's chest close to its heart, hoped to see ZINOVIEV and his associates in counterrevolutionary activity at the Party's head in place of their victim.

Yes, ZINOVIEV and Zinovievism, corrupting youth with impunity for ten years, nurtured with their own ferment the fascist murderers NIKOLAEV, KOTOLYNOV, RUMYANTSEV, and others, becoming the banner of terrorist interventionists who deprived the Party of the life of one of its finest leaders. And no one who fought alongside ZINOVIEV against the Party and its leadership headed by STALIN, hated by all the Party's enemies, who used the Party's trust to deceive and betray it, can escape this terrible, grievous truth.

I was among those who did ZINOVIEV's dirty counterrevolutionary work. Therefore I cannot and do not wish to evade political responsibility for S. M. KIROV's treacherous murder and must suffer severe punishment for it.

And now, however hard, however painful, I must admit that only now have I found the strength to break finally with the entire nightmarish past. Now I literally feel (alas, only now!) that simple Bolshevik truth which you, great and wise Iosif Vissarionovich, tried to instill in me ten years ago. Looking back on my ten-year course of political errors, of open and double-dealing struggle against the Party, which led me into Zinovievism's counterrevolutionary den, I understood that this ruinous path and such an end await anyone who, breaking away from the Party and the working class, persists in fighting the Party and its general leadership, who refuses to break with circle cliquishness and work together with the Party, who betrays the Party's interests to please his clique.

Now that I have recognized all my errors and crimes and told you all this fully, so as never to return to the old ruinous path, I have felt some inner relief, and from the cesspit of Zinovievism's counterrevolutionary remnants I again see the true Bolshevik sun and, illuminated by that sun, that very path onto which you called me ten years ago. And if then I proved utterly unworthy of the exceptional care you personally showed me to help me and other politically misguided Komsomol workers find the Bolshevik path, now I see the entire meaning of my existence solely in atoning for my guilt.

Whatever deserved punishment I may suffer for all my grave crimes against the Party and Soviet power, I can live and breathe only what the entire Party and country live and breathe. I shall live in the joyful hope that the Party and Soviet power will once again give me the opportunity to devote all my strength and energy wholly and selflessly to serving the great cause of LENIN–STALIN, to follow that sole path along which you, great helmsman of great victories, victoriously lead our country and the world communist revolution!

I. TARASOV.

Leningrad, January 7, 1935.

Certified correct: A. Svetlova
