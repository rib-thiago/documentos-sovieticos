INTERROGATION RECORD

Of TIMOFEI DMITRIEVICH DMITRIEV —

December 25, 1934.

Question: Tell the investigation about the Rostov group of Zinovievists.

Answer: I already testified at the previous interrogation that before my departure for Rostov in October 1930, Vladimir LEVIN in Leningrad and KUKLIN in Moscow told me that a group of active Zinovievists was in Rostov: Nikolai GORDON, PEKAR-ORLOV and N. GLEBOV-AVILOV. LEVIN and especially KUKLIN recommended that I contact this group of people.

Upon arriving in Rostov, immediately, that very day, I contacted GORDON, who then worked in the Territorial Party Committee. Some time later I contacted Petr Eduardovich ROTSKAN, who worked in Rostov in the territorial land administration. I had known ROTSKAN since our joint factional work in Leningrad. I then met PERKAR-ORLOV. Later, in the spring of 1933, former oppositionist Pavel KUSHNIKOV arrived in Rostov; he works as deputy head of Rostov port, and I established contact with him too.

We were all in close communication. We often gathered at GORDON's apartment. I do not know how closely GLEBOV-AVILOV communicated with the persons listed. Personally, I saw him once at GORDON's apartment in 1931.

Until the very latest period, I, DMITRIEV, ROTSKAN and GORDON gathered regularly at GORDON's apartment. At these meetings we touched upon party policy in various fields. Most often our conversations revolved around the position of ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV and others; our viewpoint on this question consisted in the unconditional necessity of bringing ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV into the party leadership. We approved in every way of ZINOVIEV's work in “Bolshevik,” his contributions to “Pravda” and the Marx-Lenin Institute, regarding it as one means of popularizing him, as one legal link to seize upon in order to accomplish the task of bringing him (ZINOVIEV) into the leadership.

Question: Was there a connection between the Rostov counterrevolutionary Zinovievist group and the Moscow center?

Answer: Yes, such a connection existed. It operated in both directions: through trips by Rostov group participants to Moscow and a visit to Rostov by Moscow center member KUKLIN. I do not remember exactly, in 1932, I believe, Nikolai GORDON traveled to Moscow and met ZINOVIEV. On returning to Rostov, GORDON informed us about his meeting with ZINOVIEV. He said that they still would not give him (ZINOVIEV) real work appropriate to his standing. The conclusion following from GORDON's information was the same as before: continue working to strengthen connections among Zinovievist cadres and between them and ZINOVIEV personally; the task of bringing ZINOVIEV and others into the party leadership remained unchanged.

In 1931 Moscow center member KUKLIN came to Rostov. He stayed with GORDON. I was summoned by telephone. I cannot reproduce the substance of his information in detail, but remember precisely that the conversation concerned ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV, the practical tasks arising from the nature of our relations with them (along the lines described above), and the need to maintain contact.

In July 1934 I traveled to Moscow and met KUKLIN. My purpose in visiting KUKLIN was to learn the mood of the leading Zinovievist circle, where like-minded people were located, to report on people's situation in Rostov, and so forth. In addition, I informed him that early in the year (1934) I had been in Leningrad and contacted Vladimir LEVIN and other members of the Zinovievist organization there. KUKLIN approved this.

Finally, I last met KUKIN in October 1934 in Sochi, where I had come from Rostov. The conversation chiefly revolved around ZINOVIEV's removal from the editorial board of “Bolshevik.” I recalled that in 1931 Boris SAKHOV, who was close to KUKLIN, visited Rostov. He then worked in the prosecutor's office. The conversation, in which GORDON, SAKHOV and I participated, took place at GORDON's apartment. The substance of SAKHOV's information was analogous to the information from KUKLIN and GORDON that I set out above.

Question: In your previous testimony you said that, having been a member of the center of the Leningrad counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization until 1930, you maintained contact with it until 1934. What did this connection consist of?

Answer: While in Rostov, I corresponded with MYASNIKOV and LEVIN. The strengthening of my connection with members of the Leningrad organization's center dates to 1934. This strengthening was dictated by organizational and political interests. During 1934 I came to Leningrad three times: in January, July and November; on each visit I contacted center members Vladimir LEVIN and MYASNIKOV.

Question: What was the nature of your conversations with LEVIN and MYASNIKOV?

Answer: Mutual information about the moods of like-minded people in Leningrad and Rostov, connections with Moscow, the situation of ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV and other center members, prospects and tasks. I informed both of the directives arising from my conversation with Moscow center member KUKLIN, consisting of the need to preserve cadres, not disperse, not forget ZINOVIEV, assist his popularization in every available form, maintain contact with him, remind him of our existence, accumulate strength and gain the trust of party leaders. LEVIN spoke of certain achievements in this field, but complained that difficulties constantly arose in gaining party leaders' trust (in our organization's interests): access was not granted everywhere, there was no complete trust, and so forth.

Question: Did you participate in the meeting of members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization at LEVIN's apartment in July 1934? Who was present? What questions were discussed?

Answer: Yes, I participated. This meeting coincided with my July 1934 visit to Leningrad. Present were myself, DMITRIEV, Vladimir LEVIN, BASHKIROV, Efim KORSHUNOV and Aleksandr ZILBERMAN. There too I reported on the directives arising from my conversation with KUKLIN, whose substance is set out in the answer to the previous question. On LEVIN's initiative we discussed ZINOVIEV's statement to the Party Central Committee. We approved this step by ZINOVIEV, regarding it as a correct maneuver toward accomplishing the task of bringing ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV and others into the leadership. We considered that ZINOVIEV's readmission to the party would facilitate accomplishing this task.

Correctly recorded from my words, read by me —

DMITRIEV.
