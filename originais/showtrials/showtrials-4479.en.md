INTERROGATION RECORD

OF YELKOVICH, Yakov Rafailovich, dated 14 January 1935

QUESTION: The investigation possesses information that the murder of Comrade KIROV, committed by NIKOLAEV, a member of the Zinovievist organization, resulted from the systematic incitement of hatred toward the leaders of Soviet power and the party by the leadership and members of the Zinovievist organization. What can you testify on this matter?

ANSWER: The principal content of the activities of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization, both its leading Moscow center and the members of the organization associated with that center who worked locally, including my activities as a member of the organization, throughout all the years of the organization's existence and up to 1934, consisted in cultivating and inciting hostility and hatred toward the leaders of the party and Soviet power among the organization's members; the murder of Comrade KIROV was precisely the result of this incitement of hatred toward the party's leaders. The discussion of almost every current issue in the country's life and the party's policy, which was practiced in our organization throughout these years, was accompanied by vicious attacks on the party's leaders and personally on STALIN, by seizing upon and unrestrainedly exaggerating the difficulties the country was experiencing. Our organization pinned its hopes on these difficulties. Every conversation I had with members of the center (on whose composition I have already testified), beginning with ZINOVIEV, during my visits to Moscow, every instruction I received from them, was always imbued with a spirit of hatred toward the party leadership and above all Comrade STALIN. During my repeated meetings with ZINOVIEV throughout 1929–1934, he repeatedly emphasized the “empirical character” of the party leadership and the “additional costs” this caused. ZINOVIEV spoke of the leadership's lack of Leninist foresight (always meaning Comrade STALIN first and foremost). These and similar counterrevolutionary attacks always implied that the sole bearer of Marxist-Leninist foresight was our organization, headed by ZINOVIEV. The counterrevolutionary slander concerning the empirical character of the party leadership and the policy vacillations allegedly caused by it circulated widely within the Moscow center throughout these years, being essentially a repetition of the most vile, poisonous and slanderous Trotskyist-Zinovievist thesis concerning “swaying”.

The statements of the members of the organization's center ZINOVIEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, and also GORSHENIN (beginning in 1930), on questions of collectivization were built entirely on this thesis and invariably accompanied by slanderous accusations against Comrade STALIN. It was in this spirit that I received instructions during my visits to Moscow on organizational business in 1930 from center members ZINOVIEV and EVDOKIMOV and from GORSHENIN. I conveyed these same instructions during my visit to Leningrad that same year to members of the Leningrad organization Timofei DMITRIEV, Vladimir LEVIN (the latter were members of the Leningrad center), GAIDEROVA, BASHKIROV, and others.

During my visits to Moscow in 1930, 1931, and 1932 I also received a number of instructions on agricultural questions from center member EVDOKIMOV. He made a special practice of selecting agricultural materials tendentiously. He analyzed reports, selected and supported his counterrevolutionary conclusions concerning the state of agriculture and the party's policy in this field with manipulated figures. The essence of these conclusions and instructions I received from EVDOKIMOV amounted to slanderous

assertions concerning the continuing, uninterrupted decline of agriculture, the collective farmers' lack of interest in the success of collectivization, statements that soon nothing would remain of livestock farming, and so on. EVDOKIMOV accompanied these conclusions with malicious attacks on the party leadership and personally on Comrade STALIN, with slander concerning the “empiricism of Stalin's leadership”, which, as he put it, cost the country and the party very dearly. I too shared these capitulationist and restorationist instructions of EVDOKIMOV and other center members, and through me they were passed on to other members of the organization. Among the members of our organization, the reliance on difficulties and the associated slander against the party leadership and Comrade STALIN became particularly pronounced in 1932.

We exaggerated the scale of the poor harvests in Ukraine and the North Caucasus dozens of times over and presented them as the collapse of the collective-farm system. In conversations I had during my visit to Moscow in 1932 with center members EVDOKIMOV and BAKAEV, and also with GERTIK and GORSHENIN, one repeatedly heard: “This is what Stalin's leadership has brought the country to”. Collectivization issues were discussed in the same spirit at one of the meetings of the organization's members at BAKAEV's apartment in 1932. Present were organization members BAKAEV, KOSTINA, GORSHENIN, ANISHEV, KOZHURO, myself, NATANSON, and NAUMOV. I think organization member OLKHOVSKY, who had come from the city of Gorky and was connected with BAKAEV on organizational business, was present at this meeting. I do not remember the individual speeches at this meeting, but the meaning of all the propositions we formulated there amounted to a monstrous exaggeration of the scale of the poor harvest in particular regions, to counterrevolutionary assertions that the cause of collectivization had been thrown far back, that collectivization had brought the country to a dead end, and that the crude empiricism of the party leadership had led to this. I well remember the capitulationist and restorationist, distinctly anti-Soviet character of our statements at this meeting, imbued with unrestrained hostility and hatred toward the party leadership and personally Comrade STALIN. The defeatist positions of our organization's members also emerged most clearly in connection with the discussion of this set of questions. It was precisely in 1932 that GORSHENIN and GERTIK widely circulated among the organization's members the counterrevolutionary proposition that, because of famine

in a number of regions and the collective farmers' lack of personal interest, in the event of war they “would not fight”. I in turn conveyed these propositions to members of the Leningrad organization ANISHEV and NATANSON during my trip to Leningrad in December 1932.

I stated that, given the situation that had developed on the collective farms, the collective farmer would not go to fight. The starting point of this vile slander against the party and Soviet power was the old Trotskyist thesis concerning CLEMENCEAU.

The exceptional lack of principle and malice of the members of the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization toward the party and its leadership are vividly characterized by the position we adopted in 1933 on the question of grain deliveries. When the party and government published the decision on grain deliveries, members of our organization began to discredit this decision. At first we conducted this barrage under the slogan “the collective farmer will not believe it anyway”, and later (also in connection with the resolution on strengthening individual collective farmers' livestock farming) under the claim that proprietary and private-capitalist tendencies would revive on the collective farms. I received such an instruction from GORSHENIN in 1933.

We, the members of the Zinovievist organization, made wide use of a measure such as the 1933 party purge to intensify the slanderous struggle against the party leadership. We portrayed the purge as a measure specially organized by the party leadership, by Comrade STALIN, to settle scores with all those deemed undesirable and above all with all Zinovievists. The interrogations associated with the purge were also discussed at a meeting of organization members at FEDOROV's apartment in late 1933. Present were organization members G. FEDOROV, KUKLIN, GESSEN, myself, and GORSHENIN. In particular, the purge was discussed there in connection with the expulsion of GORSHENIN and FEDOROV from the party. At this meeting every expulsion of a Zinovievist was regarded as an act of reprisal by the party leadership against the Zinovievists and received as a blow against the organization as a whole. This discussion too was saturated with counterrevolutionary slander against Comrade STALIN. On my next visit to Moscow, in late October 1934, I met organization members GORSHENIN, BAKAEV, KOZHURO, and KOSTINA. Again there was talk of reprisals over ZINOVIEV's removal from “Bolshevik” and attacks on the party leadership in this connection. Finally, my last meeting with ZINOVIEV in November 1934 (about which I have already testified), during which he personally spoke of his removal from “Bolshevik” as an act of reprisal by Comrade STALIN.

The facts I have cited show clearly enough that in all our counterrevolutionary, anti-Soviet work we, the members of the Zinovievist organization and above all our Moscow center, systematically and persistently cultivated hostility and incited hatred against the leaders of the party and state and above all against Comrade STALIN. This poisoned atmosphere of virulent counterrevolutionary hostility and incitement of hatred toward the party leadership regularly spread to Leningrad as well, through systematic two-way contact between the Moscow center and the Leningrad organization. In particular, GESSEN, GERTIK, KHARITONOV, myself, KOZHURO, and others traveled to Leningrad and contacted organization members. V. LEVIN, ANISHEV, NATANSON, BASHKIROV, and others traveled to Moscow and met members of the center. The direct consequence of this counterrevolutionary work and harassment of the party's leaders by us, members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization, was the murder of Comrade KIROV by NIKOLAEV, a member of the Zinovievist organization.

Recorded correctly from my words, read by me —

YELKOVICH.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE UGB — LULOV.
