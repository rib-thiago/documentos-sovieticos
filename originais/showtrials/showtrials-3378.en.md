RECORD

Of the supplementary interrogation of the accused I. S. GORSHENIN.

December 21, 1934.

QUESTION: In your testimony of December 19 of this year you indicated that political and moral responsibility for the terrorist act against Comrade KIROV committed by Leonid NIKOLAEV falls on the Moscow center of the Zinovievists and chiefly on ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV personally. Tell us what you know about the existence of this center, its activities and its individual membership.

ANSWER: Concerning the Moscow Zinovievist center, I know that although it was formally dissolved and liquidated after the Fifteenth Party Congress, in reality, judging by the meetings and relationships, it actually continued to exist until recently.

According to my information, until very recently the center included the following persons: Grigory Evseevich ZINOVIEV, Lev Borisovich KAMENEV, Grigory Eremeevich EVDOKIMOV, Aleksandr Sergeevich KUKLIN, Ivan Petrovich BAKAEV and Yakov Vasilyevich SHAROV. Compared with the old Moscow Zinovievist political center that existed before the Fifteenth Congress, the present membership differs only in not including Pyotr ZALUTSKY, Mikhail KHARITONOV and Grigory FEDOROV.

I personally did not belong to the political center in recent times and believe that Artem Moiseevich GERTIK too was not a member.

QUESTION: What connection did you personally have with the Moscow center? Name all the persons known to you who were connected with this center.

ANSWER: I personally was close to individual members of the center, particularly BAKAEV, EVDOKIMOV and the center's leader, G. E. ZINOVIEV. Besides myself, A. M. GERTIK, Anna Porfiryevna KOSTINA, Anna Evgenyevna KOZHURO (my wife), Sergei Mikhailovich GESSEN, Boris BRAVO, Regina Lvovna BUDZINSKAYA, Elkon LEIKIN and Ilya LUKYANOV were also closely connected with the center. All the persons listed are active Zinovievists.

QUESTION: What do you know about the political directives of the Moscow center and its attitude toward the leadership of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks)?

ANSWER: Until recently the Moscow political center of the counterrevolutionary organization of Zinovievists maintained a critical attitude toward the decisions of the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) and an unfriendly attitude toward the Party leadership. I know this from statements by individual members of the center and chiefly from its leader G. E. ZINOVIEV, whom I met at his apartment on Kaloshinsky Lane [1].

I can cite the following facts:

a) Around August 1932, after returning from a trip through Western Siberia with a brigade of the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), I had a lengthy conversation with ZINOVIEV, during which he inquired about my impressions of this trip. He told me then that the policy of industrialization and collectivization was being carried out at enormous unproductive cost to the country, which was enduring unbearable privations that could have been avoided under different leadership. He, ZINOVIEV, said literally: “Yes, STALIN will cost the country dearly.”

b) Essentially the same conclusion was drawn by individual members of the center (BAKAEV, EVDOKIMOV, KUKLIN, SHAROV), including ZINOVIEV, concerning the results of the first Five-Year Plan: enormous unproductive construction costs; achievements in agriculture were not justified in view of the crisis in livestock farming; construction of enormous combines was carried out without consideration and sensible preliminary study; the cultural and living conditions of workers in the new industrial centers were ignored.

c) Concerning the decision of the Seventeenth Party Conference to raise workers' consumption levels two- or threefold by 1937, the opinion was expressed that such a decision was unrealistic and declaratory in character. EVDOKIMOV, KUKLIN and BAKAEV spoke to me about this.

d) The decision to abolish the Central Control Commission and the Workers' and Peasants' Inspectorate was characterized by ZINOVIEV and other members of the center as Comrade STALIN's desire to remove from influence within the Party apparatus groups of old Bolsheviks who might have independent opinions on particular internal Party questions.

On questions of international policy and Comintern activities, the Moscow Zinovievist center adhered to the following directives:

a) The fascist coup in Germany and Hitler's accession to power were explained by the incorrect policy of the Comintern and the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks). There was an opinion that the Comintern's slogans before the fascist coup were abstract and did not reach the masses, that the united-front tactic, which could have prevented the coup, had not been applied. The conclusion was drawn that Comintern policy had facilitated Hitler's accession to power. All this was told to me personally by G. E. ZINOVIEV at the end of 1933.

b) The Vienna uprising (the action by the Schutzbund members), in the opinion of ZINOVIEV and other members of our center, was likewise not used by the Comintern to strengthen the Communist Party of Austria.

It was pointed out that Comintern policy in this case too had proved incorrect and inflexible. The slogans, once again, came very late, and the Communists' action was unorganized.

c) Concerning the revolution in Spain, the opinion existed that in this case too the Comintern had played a passive role. Various facts were cited to prove that the revolutionary situation in Spain was not being used, that the Spanish Communist Party was not being strengthened and that disorganization reigned everywhere.

As for the attitude of our organization's center toward the present Party leadership, it can be characterized by the foregoing and, in addition, by constant corresponding statements that included attacks on Comrade STALIN and other members of the Central Committee Politburo. There were many such instances, and it would now be difficult for me to list them with exact places and dates. I particularly remember conversations with ZINOVIEV at the time of TROTSKY's expulsion from the Union and later during discussion of the results of the first Five-Year Plan, when ZINOVIEV argued that the Central Committee took insufficient account of working-class interests, while making personal attacks on Comrade STALIN.

QUESTION: At which illegal meetings of the Zinovievist center in Moscow did you participate?

ANSWER: Apart from individual gatherings of Zinovievists at my apartment and other apartments, attended by individual members of the Moscow center—I. P. BAKAEV, G. E. EVDOKIMOV, less frequently A. S. KUKLIN and Ya. V. SHAROV—about which I testified in my previous testimony of December 17 and 19, I also participated in a meeting of the center where all the center's members except ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV were present, discussing the situation created by the Central Committee resolution expelling ZINOVIEV from the Party over the RYUTIN case. This question was discussed in October 1932 in terms of the conduct of the organization's members at Party meetings at their workplaces, where this Central Committee resolution was to be discussed.

QUESTION: What can you add to your previous testimony concerning assignments you received from ZINOVIEV?

ANSWER: In 1929 I received an assignment to organize publication of a collection dedicated to LASHEVICH's memory (collect articles, photographs, documents and other information). I completed half of this assignment, since it soon became clear that the collection could not be published. I handed the articles and photographs of LASHEVICH I had collected over to ZINOVIEV. At the same time I received an assignment, together with LASHEVICH's wife, to organize a collection of money to purchase a bust of LASHEVICH for installation in the LASHEVICH corner at the Moscow House of the Red Army. After SLEPKOV's arrest in the RYUTIN case I was summoned to ZINOVIEV, who asked me to find STEN and inform him that ZINOVIEV wanted to meet him. I carried out this assignment.

While ZINOVIEV was in exile in Kustanai, at his personal request I collected newspapers and foreign magazines for him, which his son Stepan Grigoryevich RADOMYSLSKY sent him by ordinary post.

After ZINOVIEV's removal from work on the editorial staff of Bolshevik, when visiting him at his apartment I informed him about plans for developing the USSR's national economy in 1935. I obtained this information at my workplace, the Gosplan of the RSFSR.

I sign to confirm the correctness of my testimony; I have read the record.

GORSHENIN.

INTERROGATED BY: DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO OF THE UGB, STROMIN. —
