INTERROGATION RECORD

OF MANDELSHTAM, Sergei Osipovich (supplementary).

18 December 1934.

Question: In the record of your interrogation of 17 December, you testified to the existence in Leningrad of a counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization of which you are a member. Explain how this organization took shape.

Answer: The counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization essentially never ceased to exist from 1928 through 1934. Depending on the period and circumstances, the organizational forms of its activities and its tactics changed, but its essence, consisting in struggle against the party leadership, remained unchanged. After a number of former Zinovievists (including myself) returned to Leningrad and a number of leaders to Moscow, from 1928 onward we undertook practical work to consolidate the Zinovievist cadres, as a result of which the organization was restored. This organizational restoration was undertaken to preserve ourselves within the party as an independent political force which, in the event of any internal complications in the party, could act in an organized manner. It was toward such internal complications that we directed our course. The formation of our organization's Leningrad center dates to the same period; it consisted of myself, MANDELSHTAM, RUMYANTSEV Vladimir, LEVIN Vladimir, and SOSITSKY, and still exists in that same form. Further connections were built on the “chain” principle, that is, through the mutual attraction of those closest to one another. The work consisted in receiving and transmitting information, maintaining contact with the Moscow leaders, and developing uniform courses of action. Contact with our organization's Moscow center, expressed in practice in journeys by members of the Leningrad center to Moscow to exchange information and receive directives, continued until the most recent period. Personally, these contacts were maintained by myself, MANDELSHTAM, with EVDOKIMOV; by LEVIN with ZINOVIEV; and by RUMYANTSEV with EVDOKIMOV, SHAROV, and others. The directive received by the members of the organization, to which we adhered throughout, dates to the same period. As a means of smuggling through their directives, members of the organization were to involve themselves in the practical work of the party, Soviet, and economic apparatus.

Question: What tasks did your counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization set itself? What directives did it formulate?

Answer: I have already testified that the task consisted in struggle against the party leadership and in bringing ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV into the leadership. In practice, this meant cultivating hostility toward the leadership, particularly Comrade STALIN, and discrediting party policy in our organization's interests. I will give several examples:

1\. We favored supporting the party in its struggle against the Rightists, while simultaneously instructing members of the organization to emphasize that this had been borrowed from the opposition platform;

2\. The same applied to the slogan of self-criticism and a number of other questions;

3\. We characterized party policy on the theoretical front as pointless and vulgar. In a conversation between myself and ELKOVICH in 1931 concerning party policy on the theoretical front, it was emphasized that the very term “Menshevizing idealism” was a bogeyman. In that same conversation we formulated hostile attacks against Comrade STALIN in connection with his definition of theory's lag behind practice.

4\. In a conversation with EVDOKIMOV in his Moscow apartment in 1932, we discussed Comrade STALIN's six conditions in sharply negative terms. During that same conversation EVDOKIMOV expressed a number of anti-party, counterrevolutionary views on collectivization and party policy in the countryside generally.

5\. Throughout 1932–1934, RUMYANTSEV and I repeatedly discussed various current questions of party policy in a counterrevolutionary spirit and, in this connection, the question of the attitude toward Comrade STALIN. We expressed the view that the leadership made very abrupt transitions and turns in its policy, which cost the country and the party very dearly. During these same conversations, the question of returning ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV to the leadership invariably arose.

6\. At a gathering in SOSITSKY's apartment (after SHURYGIN's funeral) in the autumn of 1932, attended by myself, MANDELSHTAM, RUMYANTSEV, SOSITSKY, and LEVIN Vl., the latter made a statement with which we all agreed: the task was to continue working toward bringing ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV into the leadership.

7\. In 1932 \[1\], LEVIN Vladimir and I discussed the tactics the organization should follow in connection with ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's expulsion from the party over the Ryutin case. LEVIN remarked that this circumstance might damage the preservation of the Zinovievist cadres, in that they would be obliged to vote for expulsion at meetings. We concluded that, from the standpoint of preserving our cadres within the party, we should vote for expulsion.

Question: In your testimony of 17-XII-34, you named the following members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization existing in Leningrad: yourself, MANDELSHTAM, RUMYANTSEV, LEVIN Vladimir, MYASNIKOV, SOSITSKY, and KOTOLYNOV. Who else is a member of this organization?

Answer: In addition to those listed, the members of the organization include TOLMAZOV Andrei, KHANIK Lev, POPOV Georgy, and SEVEROV Pyotr. Contact with these people on organizational matters was maintained chiefly through RUMYANTSEV.

Question: How was your counterrevolutionary organization structured organizationally?

Answer: I have already testified about the organization's composition; about its Moscow center, contact with which was maintained through myself, LEVIN, and RUMYANTSEV; about a number of members of the organization, contact with whom was maintained through RUMYANTSEV; and about contacts being maintained on the “chain” principle. When members of the organization moved to other cities, they established contact with members of the organization there and continued their work. This was the case when RUMYANTSEV and I were assigned to Magnitostroy in 1930; by traveling to Sverdlovsk, we established contact with ELKOVICH, a member of the organization. Passing through Moscow on my way from the Urals, I contacted EVDOKIMOV. After returning to Leningrad in 1932, I renewed organizational contact with the other members of the organization. In addition, while in the Urals, I contacted ZORIN, who had come from Moscow. In 1932, RUMYANTSEV and I contacted TARASOV Pyotr, who had come to Leningrad. During my business trip to the Urals in 1934, I contacted ELKOVICH. All these meetings and contacts arose from the interest in strengthening links among members of the organization.

Question: What can you testify about the murder of Comrade KIROV committed by NIKOLAEV, a member of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization?

Answer: I can testify that our counterrevolutionary organization of Zinovievists bears political responsibility for the murder of Comrade KIROV, having educated NIKOLAEV in a spirit of hostility and hatred toward the party leadership.

Recorded correctly from my words; read by me.

S. MANDELSHTAM.

Interrogated by — LULOV.
