INTERROGATION RECORD

OF KUKLIN Aleksandr Sergeyevich—

January 3, 1935.

Question: Do you acknowledge your guilt in having entered the party after the Fifteenth Party Congress for double-dealing purposes?

Answer: I acknowledge my guilt in this. The testimony I gave the investigation on December 18 and 20, 1934, confirms my guilt.

Question: The investigation has established that you are a member of the Moscow center of the Zinovievist organization. Do you confirm this?

Answer: The political center of the Zinovievist organization, of which I was a member in the past, ceased active work after the Fifteenth Party Congress, but connections among us—myself, BAKAYEV, EVDOKIMOV, SHAROV, FYODOROV, GESSEN and GORSHENIN—existed until recently. The connections among center members retained a political character, as I testified earlier; therefore, one may consider that the center, which worked actively in the past, recently existed in potential form.

Question: GORSHENIN's and GESSEN's testimony has established that you were connected with members of the Zinovievist organization in Leningrad as a member of the Moscow center. Do you confirm this?

Answer: No, I do not.

Question: GORSHENIN's testimony of December 19, 1934, has established that at the end of 1928 you went to Leningrad on ZINOVIEV's instructions to negotiate with the leaders of the Leningrad Zinovievist organization, in particular Vladimir RUMYANTSEV. Do you confirm this?

Answer: I do not. I was not in Leningrad in 1928.

Question: List your visits to Leningrad after the Fifteenth Party Congress.

Answer: After the Fifteenth Congress, that is, from the beginning of 1928, I was in Leningrad three times: once in 1931, once in 1933 and once in 1934.

Question: MYASNIKOV N.P.'s testimony of December 15, 1934, has established that at LEVIN V.S.'s apartment at the end of 1929, in the presence of MYASNIKOV, LEVIN, KOSTRITSKY and other\<s\>, you expressed dissatisfaction with ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's caution and insisted on decisive action. Do you confirm this?

Answer: I was not in Leningrad in 1929. For other years, I remember neither such a combination of Zinovievists assembled in Leningrad nor similar conversations.

Question: The same testimony of MYASNIKOV has established that in 1932, at LEVIN's apartment, in the presence of MYASNIKOV, BASHKIROV, SOSITSKY and LEVIN, after hearing the information of those assembled, you gave the orientation to continue accumulating forces. Do you confirm this?

Answer: I confirm it, but this was not in 1932, but in 1933; I do not remember the content of the conversation.

Question: On December 18, 1934, you testified that in 1933 you asked LEVIN V.S. about the Zinovievist organization's archive. Did you ask about the archive on this occasion while at LEVIN's home?

Answer: Yes, I asked LEVIN V.S. about the archive on this occasion, while alone with him before MYASNIKOV and other\<s\> arrived.

Question: Who summoned MYASNIKOV, BASHKIROV and SOSITSKY to LEVIN's apartment?

Answer: Evidently LEVIN did. I did not ask him to do so.

Question: Which Zinovievists living in Leningrad did you meet and speak with in Moscow in recent years?

Answer: In Moscow, TOLMAZOV and SEREDOKHIN visited me in 1933. I told them about the results of my purge in 1929. I said that my purge had gone well because I had worked well.

Question: TOLMAZOV's testimony of December 17, 1934, establishes that you told SEREDOKHIN that the party organization's attitude toward you resulted from your particular double-dealing tactic of conduct. Do you confirm this?

Answer: No, I did not say there was a special tactic; I said that we had a good cell bureau secretary and, in general, a good set of people had come together, so we worked harmoniously.

Question: The same testimony established that you recommended that TOLMAZOV and SEREDOKHIN deliver lengthy reports of reminiscences about prerevolutionary work at the cell, to strengthen the Zinovievists' authority among the party membership.

Answer: I do not remember; I may have said so. I remember speaking of my own experience: many young party members unfamiliar with the prerevolutionary struggle listen attentively and thereby learn the history of the revolutionary struggle.

Question: Whom else besides SEREDOKHIN and TOLMAZOV did you meet in Moscow in 1933–1934?

Answer: I think Yakov TSEITLIN visited me in 1933 with someone else (I do not now remember exactly who).

Question: Describe your political conversations with TSEITLIN in 1933.

Answer: As far as I remember, TSEITLIN asked me about the details of ZINOVIEV's exile. I told him of ZINOVIEV's connection with STEN, that I had read RYUTIN's platform, and that ZINOVIEV's connection with the Ryutinists had been established by the Central Control Commission's investigation.

Question: Why was TSEITLIN interested in the details of ZINOVIEV's exile?

Answer: I do not know.

Question: SHIRYAYEV's testimony has established that you met him in 1934 at EVDOKIMOV's apartment. Do you confirm this?

Answer: Yes, I do.

Question: Describe your political conversations with SHIRYAYEV D.N.

Answer: I do not remember. I saw SHIRYAYEV at EVDOKIMOV's housewarming, where, besides Zinovievists known to me, there were also people I did not know.

KUKLIN.

Interrogated by: RUTKOVSKY.

Certified correct: Biryukov
