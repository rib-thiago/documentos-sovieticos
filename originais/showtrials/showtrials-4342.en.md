INTERROGATION RECORD

Of BRAVO Boris Lvovich

January 8, 1935.

QUESTION: At the interrogation of December 19, 1934, you testified that you belonged to the “Moscow group of Zinovievists.”

The investigation demands a truthful answer from you as to what this group represented organizationally.

ANSWER: The Moscow group to which I belonged in fact constituted an organization of the Zinovievist underground, continuing the struggle against the Party by the methods I indicated at previous interrogations.

Underlying our anti-Party counterrevolutionary activity were common dissatisfaction with the Party leadership and furious malice against STALIN, which the organization cultivated among participants in the former Trotskyist-Zinovievist opposition.

QUESTION: What do you know about the membership of the leadership of the Zinovievist organization in Moscow?

ANSWER: Leadership of the Zinovievist organization in Moscow was exercised by EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, GERTIK, GORSHENIN, KUKLIN, and SHAROV.

Upward, this leadership was connected with ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV through EVDOKIMOV and KUKLIN, and until 1932 through GERTIK as well.

Discussion of particular questions was usually held at EVDOKIMOV's and GERTIK's apartments, sometimes at GORSHENIN's.

QUESTION: List the participants in the Zinovievist underground in Moscow known to you who were connected with the organization's leadership.

ANSWER: Personally, I was most closely connected with EVDOKIMOV. Of other participants in the organization, I know GERTSBERG, NAUMOV, FEDOROV Grigory, KOSTINA, PERIMOV, SAKHOV, Dina GERTIK (until her death), KOZHURO Anna, GORSHENIN's wife, and BOGDANOV (now deceased).

QUESTION: What do you know about the Zinovievist organization's contacts in the provinces?

ANSWER: As far as I know, the closest contact in the organization existed between Moscow and Leningrad. The principal connections were concentrated in the hands of GERTIK, who frequently traveled to Leningrad. In turn, LEVIN Vladimir and KORSHUNOV came from Leningrad to Moscow and always met with GERTIK.

As I have already testified, EVDOKIMOV personally visited Leningrad quite frequently, but I do not know whom he met there.

Of participants in the Zinovievist underground who shared the organization's views, the following persons are known to me:

1\. GESSEN Sergey, who worked in Smolensk. Until 1932 I met him at EVDOKIMOV's, GERTIK's, and GORSHENIN's. I last saw GESSEN in the summer of 1934 while on an official trip to Smolensk.

2\. TSVIBAK, whose whereabouts I do not know. I know about his views and sentiments from a meeting with him in late 1929 in Moscow at GERTIK's apartment, when he, TSVIBAK, was returning from Central Asia. I did not meet him subsequently.

3\. ELKOVICH Yakov, who worked in Sverdlovsk. I met him periodically until 1932 at GORSHENIN's and GERTIK's apartments. ELKOVICH expressed his dissatisfaction with the Party leadership, which had placed him, as he put it, in “senatorial work” at the editorial office of the “Urals Encyclopedia.”

I recall that in 1930, while on an official trip to Sverdlovsk, I met ELKOVICH there and exchanged views with him on the situation of the Zinovievists.

4\. DMITRIEV, who worked in economic administration in Saratov. He informed me that the Zinovievist ABRAMSON was also in Saratov and proposed seeing him. Together with DMITRIEV, we visited ABRAMSON, whom I previously knew as an active Zinovievist from the period of the sharpest factional struggle against the Party in Leningrad.

Later I saw DMITRIEV in 1931, when he called at my home while on an official trip to Moscow. I also met him at GERTIK's apartment.

QUESTION: What was SAKHOV's role in the Zinovievist organization?

ANSWER: I frequently met SAKHOV at EVDOKIMOV's and KUKLIN's, less often at GERTIK's. Anti-Party views were openly expressed in his presence. KUKLIN and EVDOKIMOV considered SAKHOV “one of our own.” I recall that SAKHOV informed EVDOKIMOV and KUKLIN about his official activity in the Procuracy, in particular relating some major trial he was conducting at one of the enterprises.

On one occasion, after SAKHOV's prolonged absence, I met him either in late 1933 or early 1934 in the GORT \[i\] dining hall on Nikolskaya Street. He told me that he had recently returned from an official trip to inspect corrective-labor camps, where he had gone as part of a commission, exactly which one I do not remember.

SAKHOV did not express his personal views and sentiments in my presence.

Taken down correctly from my words and read by me.

BRAVO.

INTERROGATED BY:

ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE SPECIAL DEPARTMENT OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD — GENDIN

Certified true: A. Svetlova
