INTERROGATION RECORD

OF MINAYEV, S.P., January 2, 1935.

QUESTION: What additional testimony can you give about your meetings with members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization?

ANSWER: In 1930, I worked for the radio newspaper attached to the People's Commissariat of Communications; on an assignment from the newspaper, I went on a business trip to Nizhny Novgorod. There I sought out BATASHEV, formerly a very active member of the Zinovievist opposition. From my conversation with BATASHEV, I established that a Zinovievist group existed in Nizhny Novgorod consisting of BAKAYEV, KOSTINA, BATASHEV and Nyurka VOLPE, who was BATASHEV's wife. I had political conversations with both BAKAYEV and BATASHEV; both held anti-party and anti-Soviet sentiments; I cannot now remember exactly which questions we touched on when discussing political (questions) subjects.

As for the group's work, I did not manage to find out what it did, since as soon as the conversation turned to this, BAKAYEV and BATASHEV immediately became reserved; it became clear to me that they were concealing their work from me.

BAKAYEV, and then BATASHEV, cautiously asked me which members of the former Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc I remained in contact with, which of them were in Moscow, what their sentiments were and where each worked.

In 1932, I visited EVDOKIMOV at his invitation. I immediately became convinced of EVDOKIMOV's anti-party sentiments. EVDOKIMOV began asking which participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc I maintained connections with. I clearly saw that EVDOKIMOV was interested in the disposition of opposition cadres at that moment; he particularly tried to ascertain the attitude of party bodies toward us former oppositionists and whether any persons connected with me were dissatisfied on that account; I told him everything I knew.

Before I left, EVDOKIMOV asked me to keep in touch with him and visit him.

In 1932 and subsequent years, I met NAUMOV Ivan. I discussed political questions with him, on which he spoke in anti-party terms; I recall a conversation with NAUMOV about the possibility of ZINOVIEV and other\<s\> returning to the party leadership.

I had meetings with members of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization—BULAKH and ROZOVSKY—in 1933 and 1934. In conversations about questions of internal party policy, each of us spoke of one party measure or another in anti-party terms.

Before undergoing the party purge, I specifically went to BULAKH for advice on how to behave during it. The nature of this meeting was that we agreed behind the party's back on my conduct. BULAKH had already undergone the purge by that time and had not disclosed his group political connections with former members of the bloc or his participation in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist organization.

Naturally, during the purge I too tried carefully to skirt everything that pointed to my connections with the organization.

I recall a meeting with FAIVILOVICH, a member of the organization. He had just returned from Central Asia and said there was famine everywhere, that at almost every station one could encounter the bodies of people who had starved to death.

Conversations with other Zinovievists whom I met in recent years were of the same character; I mean meetings with SEREDOKHIN and FYODOROV.

Recorded correctly from my words. I have read the record.

S. MINAYEV.

INTERROGATED BY:

ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE EKO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR—DMITRIYEV

Certified correct: V. Stoiko
