INTERROGATION RECORD

dated December 17, 1934.

BOGOMOLNY, Yakov Isakovich, born in 1894 in the town of Dashevo, former Kiev province; his father was an intermediary (a broker in the town); Jewish, a Soviet citizen, residing in Leningrad, 23/59 Kronverkskaya Street, apartment 68; deputy dean of the LGU Faculty of History. Wife: ROKHLINA, Fanya Aronovna, teacher, residing at 10 Kolokolnaya Street, apartment 7; a child aged 1 year 9 months lives with the mother, F. A. ROKHLINA. First wife: BOGOMOLNAYA, Sofya Iosifovna, lives in the same apartment as me; my daughter Irina BOGOMOLNAYA, aged 16, also lives there. Father: Isak Iosifovich BOGOMOLNY, aged 69, lives in Leningrad, Vasilievsky Island, corner of the 8th Line and Sredny Avenue, no. 43, apartment 50. Does not work anywhere; lives on funds received from his children. My father had a house in the town; personally I have never owned anything. Higher education; a member of the VKP(b) since 1920. In 1927 I was expelled from the VKP(b) for opposition; in 1928 I was reinstated. In 1917 I belonged to the Poale Zion organization, first in the town of Dashkovo and later in Omsk. I effectively left this organization upon joining the RKP(b) in 1920. Voluntarily joined the RKKA in 1920. Never tried; military registration: K-9; did not serve with the Whites. Lived in White-held territory in the cities of Biysk, Barnaul and Novosibirsk.

QUESTION: When and where did you obtain the “Nagant” seized from you upon arrest?

ANSWER: In 1921, while serving as a political worker in the RKKA, I received this “Nagant” there. I was demobilized from the army in 1924, but since no one asked me about the “Nagant”, it remained with me, and I have kept it to this day. I have a permit to possess the “Nagant”.

QUESTION: For what purpose did you keep your counterrevolutionary appeals of 1927 seized during the search (to the Third Congress of the KP(b) of Turkmenistan and to the Central Control Commission)?

ANSWER: I consider keeping these documents inadmissible and plead guilty to this. I must say that I simply attached no importance to keeping these documents.

QUESTION: Do you acknowledge that the return to the Party of former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc was double-dealing?

ANSWER: Yes, I do. Among the bloc's participants, the instruction was widespread to seek reinstatement in the Party not as a result of complete disarmament before the Party, but to deceive it. I remember that at the time, in 1928, people said: “We must write declarations in such a form and in such numbers as will suffice for the Central Control Commission”, proceeding not from convictions but from the need to secure reinstatement in the Party at any cost, in order to preserve Zinovievist cadres in Leningrad and prevent their organizational destruction.

I recall that at the time remarks such as these were widespread among us: “The further course of construction will yet confirm the forecasts made by the ‘leaders’ of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc.”

The most active role in organizing all this and in preserving Zinovievist cadres was played by V. RUMYANTSEV, S. MANDELSHTAM, M. NATANSON and also I. I. KOTOLYNOV. I, BOGOMOLNY, was likewise under the sway of these sentiments before my reinstatement in the Party.

BOGOMOLNY.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE 6TH SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB — KORKIN.
