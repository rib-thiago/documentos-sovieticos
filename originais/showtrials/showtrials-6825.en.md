## INTERROGATION RECORD

OF LIVYANT Zinovy Borisovich, dated 10 January 1935.

LIVYANT Zinovy Borisovich, born in 1901, native of Vitebsk, Byelorussian SSR. Last residence Samara, previously Moscow.

Passport issued by the Samara Militia Administration.

Son of a merchant. Jewish by nationality. Worked in Samara as director of the Planning Institute.

Member of the AUCP(b) since 1919.

1\. QUESTION: Did you conduct counterrevolutionary, anti-party work?

ANSWER: From 1930 I did not share the party line, and on the basis of common anti-party, counterrevolutionary positions I established contacts with persons constituting a counterrevolutionary, anti-party grouping.

2\. QUESTION: With whom personally did you establish contacts?

ANSWER: In counterrevolutionary, anti-party work I established contacts with the following persons: STEN Jan (now works in Moscow, on the encyclopedia's editorial staff), KUSHNER Boris Anisimovich (recently worked in Magnitogorsk in a writers' brigade), REZNIK Vladimir (recently was in Saratov, where he works I do not know), GAZGANOV (recently works in Smolensk), CHERNYA Iosif (now works in Magnitogorsk), INDYUKOV, TSEITLIN Efim (works in Moscow), GOLDMAN Evgeny (recently works in Gorky), PLOTKIN David (in economic work in Moscow), LEIKIN (recently appointed head of a political department), POPOV Ivan Nikolaevich (works in Moscow as chairman of Moskustpromsoyuz), LOMINADZE Beso (recently worked in Magnitogorsk), SHATSKIN Lazar (where he works recently I do not know).

3\. QUESTION: What was the counterrevolutionary, anti-party grouping you named, and what role did each of these persons play?

ANSWER: Until 1933 the group of persons I listed above constituted a grouping in which STEN Jan played the leading and organizing role, while its cadres were myself, LIVYANT, SHATSKIN, LOMINADZE, KUSHNER, REZNIK, GAZGANOV, CHERNYA, and INDYUKOV.

4\. QUESTION: Where did the persons you named gather, and what was the character of these gatherings?

ANSWER: The meeting places for discussing questions and our attitude to party policy and the group's further activities were my apartment and STEN's apartment, which may be called, especially STEN's apartment, a peculiar political club where, besides discussing political questions, all sorts of political insinuations and gossip also arose.

At STEN's apartment, besides those listed above, I met other persons unknown to me personally.

5\. QUESTION: Who else knew of your anti-party sentiments?

ANSWER: I spoke of my attitude to the party line in the form of doubts and vacillations also to nonparticipants in the “political club.” For example, Comrade KAMINSKY Grigory Naumovich, People's Commissar for Health, and DYACHKOV, former secretary of the Moscow Regional Executive Committee, knew about my doubts and vacillations. They visited me, but in their presence my like-minded associates did not converse on political subjects, whereas I expressed my doubts.

6\. QUESTION: The investigation knows that you gave many of those persons information from secret materials. Where did you obtain them?

ANSWER: Through KAMINSKY and DYACHKOV I used secret materials of the CC of the AUCP(b), such as plenum records, Politburo records, and others. Through KAMINSKY and DYACHKOV I also learned political news from within the CC, CC decisions, and received information on unpublished questions.

I shared this information received from KAMINSKY and DYACHKOV with my like-minded associates.

Naturally, I did not speak openly about my double-dealing in the party to KAMINSKY, still less to DYACHKOV.

7\. QUESTION: Describe the platform of the counterrevolutionary, anti-party group in which you participated.

ANSWER: We believed: 1) that an incorrect regime existed in the AUCP(b) and Comintern, and on this basis, despite the absence of a clearly elaborated platform uniting counterrevolutionary leftist elements (LOMINADZE, SHATSKIN) and counterrevolutionary rightist elements (TSEITLIN, GOLDMAN), we all coexisted; 2) we considered untenable the thesis advanced by Comrade STALIN at the Sixteenth Party Congress concerning the USSR's entry into the period of socialism; 3) that the party, following its chosen rates and methods of collectivization and industrialization, would be unable to achieve victories in economic construction; 4) we had a negative attitude toward the party press, which, in our opinion, glossed over difficulties; 5) we considered it necessary, to change the situation, to bring ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, and BUKHARIN into the leadership of the AUCP(b).

8\. QUESTION: Describe the tactics you practiced while holding anti-party, counterrevolutionary positions.

ANSWER: Naturally we had no written tactics. We practiced double-dealing tactics, concealing everything from the party, as a method of preserving ourselves within the AUCP(b) in order to struggle against the party's line and policy.

9\. QUESTION: You testified that the group you named conducted illegal work until 1933. What work did the group and you personally conduct after 1933?

ANSWER: In early 1933, under the influence of the results of implementing the first five-year plan and practical work in Samara, changes occurred in my sentiments, and I began withdrawing from my anti-party positions. In connection with this, contacts with my former like-minded associates ceased. With some of them — REZNIK, PLOTKIN, CHERNYA — only personal relations remained, and I had no political conversations of the former character with them.

Since I withdrew from the group, the activities and sentiments of the remaining group members have been unknown to me since 1933.

Recently I consider that I conducted no anti-party work; I nevertheless acknowledge my guilt in all the crimes against the party committed by me and set out above.

I have read what was written; it was recorded correctly from my words.

LIVYANT.
