RECORD

of supplementary interrogation of the accused V. I. ZVEZDOV.

12 December 1934.

QUESTION: In your testimony of 11 December this year you report the existence in Moscow of a counter-revolutionary organisation formed on the basis of the former Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc. Specify this organisation's structure, its ties with other cities, particularly Leningrad, and what you know of its practical activity.

ANSWER: Clarifying my previous testimony about the existence and activity of our organisation's Moscow centre, I state the following:

The Moscow Centre took organisational shape during 1933. It included the former leaders of the Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc — ZINOVIEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, GERTIK, SHAROV, KHARITONOV and GORSHENIN. Regarding the Moscow Centre's ties with other cities, I know only of contact with our Leningrad organisation, which acted exclusively on directives received from Moscow. I do not know what other practical activity the centre engaged in besides organisational and ideological leadership of our Leningrad organisation; I did not ask about it, and no one here told us about it.

QUESTION: What was the organisational structure of your Leningrad organisation?

ANSWER: At the head of the organisation stands the so-called local (Leningrad) centre. This centre in turn is connected with representatives of individual districts of Leningrad, such as Petrograd, Volodarsky, Vasilievsky and others, as well as with the leaders of groups forming smaller associations of our organisation's members. This particular structure was adopted to facilitate contact between (individual) members and, principally, because of the need to maintain secrecy.

QUESTION: In your testimony of 11/XII this year you speak of a special tactic employed by your organisation to retain its people within the Party and Komsomol, that is, cadres of former participants in the Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc. State precisely what this tactic consists of.

ANSWER: The essence of the instructions on this matter was never under any circumstances to tell anyone (in the control commission of Party organisations) about opposition participants who, for one reason or another, had not been exposed. If anyone was summoned to a Party organisation's Control Commission or other bodies to testify about opposition activists, they were instructed not to give frank testimony and, above all, not to name new names and connections. In the latter case, they were advised to speak only of persons who had already received a Party penalty.

QUESTION: From whom specifically did these instructions originate, and who told you about them?

ANSWER: They originated from the organisation's Moscow Centre. I learned of them from TSARKOV, a member of the Leningrad Centre, who conveyed them to me for implementation as a directive.

QUESTION: What instructions guided your organisation in attracting (recruiting) like-minded people?

ANSWER: In this respect there were very clear instructions. We had instructions from the Centre first to restore and intensify to the utmost the old ties of the former Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc and examine individually who might be capable of activity in the organisation. Direct contact was also established with Trotskyists, who were likewise drawn into the organisation (Nikolai BARDIN and Nikolai KORSHUNOV). In general, our organisation, pursuing the aims of changing the Party's course and its present leadership, considered it possible and necessary to unite and lead all active forces hostile to the Party, not only former participants in anti-Party groups but also non-Party people. (For example, BOGOSLOVSKY, an Industrial Institute student, a professor's son, non-Party.)

QUESTION: What do you know about the terrorist act being prepared against Comrade KIROV? Why was this act committed by your organisation's member Leonid NIKOLAEV, and on whose instructions did he act?

ANSWER: I did not know that this terrorist act was being prepared; nor do I know whether in this instance NIKOLAEV was carrying out the will of our entire organisation or acted on his own initiative.

However, I must say frankly that NIKOLAEV's action is the logical consequence of all our political views and instructions. We were all resolute opponents of the policy and measures pursued by the present VKP(b) leaders, considering them chiefly responsible for the difficulties now being experienced by the working class and peasantry.

All our hopes that the VKP(b) Central Committee Politburo itself might change the composition of the top leadership are not being fulfilled; we were indeed in favour of removing Comrade STALIN and Comrades MOLOTOV, KAGANOVICH and KIROV from leadership, regarding them at the same time as chiefly responsible for the defeat of ZINOVIEV's policy.

In this light, NIKOLAEV's action naturally appears entirely logical: he took a decisive step towards achieving the aims we had set ourselves.

The record has been correctly written from my words and read by me.

ZVEZDOV

INTERROGATED BY —

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO, UGV — STROMIN
