INTERROGATION RECORD

of BRAVO, Boris Lvovich,

dated December 19, 1934.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that after your reinstatement in the ranks of the VKP(b) you joined an anti-Party counterrevolutionary group of former Zinovievists and actively struggled against the Party until recently. What can you testify about this?

ANSWER: I acknowledge that after my reinstatement in the Party, that is, from 1929, I engaged in anti-Party activities while remaining a double-dealer toward the Party. On returning from exile to Moscow in mid-1929 and temporarily staying at Dina GERTIK's apartment, I restored relations through her and expanded my acquaintance within the circle of former active figures of the Trotskyist-Zinovievist opposition grouped around EVDOKIMOV. This group included BAKAEV, GORSHENIN, SHAROV, GERTIK, KUKLIN and myself, BRAVO.

Meeting at EVDOKIMOV's apartment, the other figures of the former ZINOVIEV–TROTSKY opposition belonging to this group exchanged opinions on various facts of political life, VKP(b) Central Committee decisions and its organizational measures. The judgments we expressed on particular questions differed from and contradicted Party decisions.

For example, after the decision to expel ZINOVIEV from the Party in 1932 was published in the press, I, EVDOKIMOV and, according to him, KOSTINA considered this decision incorrect as to the punitive measure and the classification of ZINOVIEV's crime before the Party.

When, at the end of 1932, “Pravda” refused to publish an announcement of Dina GERTIK's death in its pages, we were indignant, considering that the Party's central organ thereby displayed undeserved and excessive suspicion toward former opposition figures.

After KOSTINA was expelled from the Party for speaking in defense of ZINOVIEV at a meeting, EVDOKIMOV, informing me about it, indicated that he considered KOSTINA's behavior careless and more than imprudent, and that BAKAEV shared this view. Discussing the incident involving KOSTINA with EVDOKIMOV, we considered that, although her speech at the meeting demonstrated her political straightforwardness, she should nevertheless have remained silent, knowing beforehand that this speech would immediately be followed by her expulsion from the Party's ranks.

KUKLIN, a member of our group, repeatedly declared that he preferred retirement to being, as he put it, “in the position of a hired farmhand”, considering that the present Party leadership was “sidelining” him as a participant in the former Zinovievist opposition, refusing to provide work corresponding to his abilities.

EVDOKIMOV expressed the same viewpoint when meeting me in 1932.

In mid-1933, GORSHENIN, then working in Gosplan, once told me when we met that he had been expelled from the Party by the purge commission. He stated that, in his opinion, the Party Central Committee had issued a directive to expel all former oppositionists from the Party's ranks during the purge.

Meeting EVDOKIMOV afterward, I conveyed the contents of my conversation with GORSHENIN, to which EVDOKIMOV replied that no conclusions could yet be drawn from GORSHENIN's expulsion from the Party, and that only as the purge continued could it be determined how correct the supposition was that a Central Committee directive existed to expel former oppositionists from the Party.

QUESTION: When and under what circumstances did you last meet ZINOVIEV?

ANSWER: Sometime in late August 1932, EVDOKIMOV suggested that I go with him on a day off to ZINOVIEV's dacha in Ilyinskoye. There we found ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, KUKLIN and another person unknown to me. EVDOKIMOV spoke at length with ZINOVIEV about something. When we later gathered around the table, ZINOVIEV, knowing of my work in the Procurement Committee attached to the Council of Labor and Defense, asked me how grain procurement was proceeding. I pointed out emerging procurement difficulties in the North Caucasus, particularly the Kuban.

On the basis of my information, ZINOVIEV indicated that a general deterioration of the country's food situation should be expected.

QUESTION: What did the counterrevolutionary anti-Party group of former Zinovievists, membership in which you have acknowledged, represent organizationally?

ANSWER: The group gathered around EVDOKIMOV had no organizational bond.

The people grouped around EVDOKIMOV were united by personal friendship and their past joint struggle against the Party, as well as dissatisfaction that the Party distrusted us and did not employ us in work corresponding to our abilities.

Recorded correctly from my words and read by me.

BRAVO.

INTERROGATED BY: ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE OO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD — GENDIN.
