INTERROGATION RECORD

conducted on December 18, 1934, by the section head of the GUGB of the NKVD — RUTKOVSKY.

KUKLIN, Aleksandr Sergeevich, born in 1876, a native of Gorky; father a worker. Resides in Moscow, Plyushchikha, 4-a 6th Rostovsky Lane, apartment 1. Wife: Elena Grigoryevna KUKLINA, aged 39; child in his care: Boris, aged 9; Galina GOLESHCHENKO, aged 16. Without property. Elementary education. A member of the VKP(b) since 1908. A break from the Fifteenth Party Congress until June 1928. Social and revolutionary work from 1903: Sormovo, Petersburg, then Sormovo and Petersburg again. Never tried.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that until recently you belonged to the Center of an organization of former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc. What can you testify concerning this case?

ANSWER: I do not know of a Center's existence, but I visited ZINOVIEV alone and in the presence of others: EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, SHAROV, FEDOROV and others. In conversations with them we criticized Party policy and the Party leadership, and also decided the tactics of our anti-Party activities.

I remember that as early as 1929 ZINOVIEV told me that BUKHARIN had visited him and proposed that ZINOVIEV enter a bloc with the Rightists. We then decided not to enter a bloc with the Rightists.

We assessed the Party's decision on collectivization as approximately two years late. This opinion was held by ZINOVIEV, myself and SHAROV. We also considered that self-criticism among us was conducted from the top down and amounted only to talk. I personally thought that ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV had been needlessly removed from major work.

ZINOVIEV told me that disagreements existed among Politburo members. ZINOVIEV also told me that comrade STALIN had heart disease and would not live long. In 1932 ZINOVIEV gave me the Ryutinists' platform to read at his apartment. I did not finish reading it and told ZINOVIEV that it was a Rightist document hostile to the Party and that one should not become involved in this affair. ZINOVIEV said nothing in reply.

QUESTION: What do you know about meetings of former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc?

\<ANSWER:\> In 1929 we gathered at the so-called seminars organized by ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV. There were two seminars: a political one headed by ZINOVIEV, and an economic one headed by KAMENEV. I attended the economic seminar about twice and the political seminar once. I do not know the hidden political meaning of organizing these seminars, but the very character of the sessions at these \<seminars\> was anti-Party, since Party policy was criticized there. We also often gathered at Artyom GERTIK's apartment until his wife Dina GERTIK's death, that is, until the end of 1932. We also gathered at GORSHENIN's apartment: GERTIK, BAKAEV, KOSTINA and myself. I once saw GESSEN at GORSHENIN's.

QUESTION: Describe your connections with former Zinovievists in Leningrad.

ANSWER: When visiting Leningrad between 1930 and 1934 I met RUMYANTSEV, Vladimir LEVIN, I. M. RUSANOV and MYASNIKOV; I met the latter once.

I met RUMYANTSEV most often. On one of my visits to Leningrad in 1933 I asked LEVIN about the old archive of the Leningrad Zinovievists. I had no definite purpose concerning this archive. I asked whether it was intact or had been destroyed, telling LEVIN that the archive should contain documents about the Zinovievists' objections to a bloc with the Trotskyists. LEVIN told me that he had handed the archive to EMELYANOV. I did not ask EMELYANOV about the archive.

I do not remember the time exactly, but I had a conversation with RUMYANTSEV at LEBEDEV's apartment. I remember that SOLOVYOV was at LEBEDEV's apartment at 10 Tchaikovsky Street. I admit the possibility that I conveyed to RUMYANTSEV KAMENEV's and ZINOVIEV's inclinations to continue the struggle against the Party leadership and ZALUTSKY's rapprochement with them, but I cannot recall this now.

I have read the record. Recorded correctly from my words.

A. KUKLIN

Interrogated by: RUTKOVSKY.
