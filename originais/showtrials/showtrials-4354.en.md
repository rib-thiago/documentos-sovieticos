INTERROGATION RECORD

Of REPNIKOV, Pyotr Mikhailovich

January 9, 1935.

REPNIKOV, Pyotr Mikhailovich, born in 1894, native of the former Arkhangelsk province, Onega uyezd, Priluki volost, village of Fektelma; of peasant origin, Russian, USSR citizen, resides in Leningrad, Podolskaya Street, house 43, apartment 16. Deputy director of the Institute of Design and Surveying for Water Transport. Wife: STARKIS Aleksandra Ivanovna, heads the instructional section of a Soviet Party school; daughters Margarita and Lyudmila; mother Taisiya Ivanovna, dependent on me; brothers: Pavel works at Eksportles; Konstantin and Grigory work at a sawmill. Without property. Education: graduated from the Academy of Water Transport in Leningrad in 1933. Member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) since 1912. In January 1926, received a reprimand from the Central Control Commission for participation in the opposition. In 1928, received a severe reprimand with a warning from the Zamoskvorechye District Control Commission for drunkenness abroad. In 1933, received a severe reprimand from the Leningrad Control Commission for failure to display Bolshevik persistence in fulfilling the Lenvodput trust's plan for 1932. In 1916, was tried under Article 102 on the charge of belonging to the Party. I am registered as political personnel in category 8. Did not serve with the Whites.

QUESTION: The investigation knows of your membership in the Zinovievist organization. What can you testify on this matter?

ANSWER: Since I was an active oppositionist during the Fourteenth Party Congress, I acknowledge that after the Fifteenth Congress I joined the Zinovievist grouping and maintained contacts with individual participants until November 1934. In February 1928, I submitted an individual statement to the Central Control Commission concerning my submission to the decisions of the Fifteenth Party Congress. The submission of this statement had a double-dealing purpose, since immediately after submitting it I continued to struggle against the Party and its leadership.

QUESTION: What assignments of the Zinovievist grouping did you carry out after the Fifteenth Party Congress?

ANSWER: In January 1928, I called at the apartment of ZINOVIEV, who then lived in Moscow in the Arbat area (I do not remember the street name or house number). At ZINOVIEV's apartment I found LASHEVICH, KUKLIN, EVDOKIMOV, and, I believe, ZORIN. In conversation, in the presence of the persons listed, ZINOVIEV instructed me to establish contact with the Leningrad group of “leaderless” Leftists, naming NALIVAIKO, EZHOV, RUMYANTSEV, SHURYGIN Aleksey (deceased), LUKIN, and SAFRONOV — I do not remember the other surnames — and convince them of the need to submit formally to the congress decisions, since outside the Party one could not participate in the country's political life. This instruction from ZINOVIEV aimed to preserve his cadres in the Party's ranks. On arriving in Leningrad, I spoke, concerning the assignment ZINOVIEV had given me, with NALIVAIKO, LUKIN, EZHOV, RUMYANTSEV Vladimir, SAKHOV, SAFRONOV, and, I believe, MANDELSHTAM.

I spoke with all the persons listed both privately and at group meetings.

Except for SHURYGIN Aleksey, none of the persons I named submitted a statement to the Central Committee.

Soon after ZINOVIEV's return from Kaluga, in the summer of 1928, I went to his apartment to receive instructions on further work. During this visit, ZINOVIEV instructed me to find out which Leningrad participants in our grouping were in Moscow and establish contact with them. In addition, I was to sound out the sentiments of the former secretary of Leningrad's Central district, TOIVO, and establish his attitude toward ZINOVIEV.

In carrying out this assignment, I met with TOIVO several times at the home of one of his acquaintances (I do not remember the surname), and also at GERTIK's apartment, and established his agreement with ZINOVIEV's views, which I reported to ZINOVIEV (I do not remember whether personally or through one of the grouping's participants). I also established contact with ZILBERMAN and another comrade who had arrived from Turkestan with TOIVO, whom I already knew from Leningrad (I do not now remember the surname).

I did not establish contact with the other Leningraders, since they personally visited GERTIK's headquarters and also called on ZINOVIEV. In the autumn of 1928, one of the then leaders of the Leningrad grouping, KOSTRITSKY, came from Leningrad to Moscow.

At KOSTRITSKY's request, I visited ZINOVIEV with him; ZINOVIEV then lived at a dacha along the Kazan railway line.

In my presence, KOSTRITSKY received political instructions from ZINOVIEV and directions to draw the “leaderless” group closer to himself at any cost. From conversations at GERTIK's apartment, I know that after ZINOVIEV's conversation with KOSTRITSKY, representatives of the “leaderless,” in particular NALIVAIKO, came from Leningrad and held negotiations with ZINOVIEV.

QUESTION: Whom did you meet at GERTIK's apartment?

ANSWER: At GERTIK's apartment during 1928 and early 1929, I repeatedly met BAKAEV, KUKLIN, FEDOROV Grigory, GESSEN Sergey, EVDOKIMOV, KHARITONOV, DMITRIEV Nikolay, GORSHENIN, ZILBERMAN, BARANOV Mikhey, SARKIS, NALIVAIKO, RUMYANTSEV V., SHAROV, KOSTINA Anna, NAUMOV Ivan, LEPESHINSKAYA Aleksandra, and others whose surnames I do not remember. I must say that almost all participants in the Zinovievist grouping visited GERTIK's apartment as a headquarters. GERTIK's apartment was a connecting link between ZINOVIEV, who was in Kaluga, and the grouping's participants. At GERTIK's apartment, all those arriving from the provinces were given instructions and ZINOVIEV's contact address in Kaluga. I personally did not visit GERTIK's apartment after the spring of 1929.

QUESTION: What conversations were held in your presence at GERTIK's apartment?

ANSWER: The conversations held at GERTIK's apartment were openly anti-Soviet in character. Measures of the Party and government were frequently discussed and criticized.

Jokes and gossip of a counterrevolutionary kind were spread, aimed at discrediting members of the government and the Party leadership, especially STALIN. Among those visiting GERTIK's apartment, a pamphlet in verse entitled “Repentance” was read, whose author was GESSEN Sergey. This pamphlet was dedicated to ZINOVIEV, but was directed mainly against the Party leadership, in particular STALIN.

QUESTION: Which participants in the Zinovievist grouping did you meet and maintain contact with until the day of your arrest?

ANSWER: I met and maintained contact with the following persons, participants in the Zinovievist grouping: ANDREEV Nikolay, director of the carburetor factory in Leningrad. Meetings took place both at my apartment and at ANDREEV's. I last saw ANDREEV in the second half of May 1934 at Smolny. ANDREEV told me that he met participants in the grouping, BLINKOV and BARANOV.

In 1931, while on an official trip to Moscow, I called at BLINKOV Boris's apartment, where I met a mutual acquaintance, BARANOV Mikhey. During the same visit, though this may not be precise, I called at NAUMOV's apartment. I maintained contact with BLINKOV, BARANOV, and NAUMOV through 1934 inclusive.

In 1932, during my stay in Moscow, I stayed at SAKHOV Boris's apartment. I also met SAKHOV twice in 1933 in Leningrad: the first time at the Sestroretsk resort, and the second on the day of his departure from Leningrad. This meeting took place by prior arrangement at the apartment of SHEINBERG (I do not know his first name or patronymic), also a participant in the Zinovievist grouping and, in addition, a relative of SAKHOV.

In 1932, I had chance encounters at Smolny with LEVIN Vladimir, ABRAMSON, MYASNIKOV, and ALEKSANDROV Aleksandr. I met these same persons at Smolny in 1933–1934.

In late 1933, MANDELSHTAM Sergey called at my apartment. At approximately the same time, LEOSHKO (I do not remember his first name) visited me at my apartment; the latter also called on me in 1934. I met EVDOKIMOV once in 1933 in the Smolny dining hall. In April 1934, also at Smolny, I met KUKLIN. In the spring and autumn of 1934, MANDELSHTAM again called at my apartment.

QUESTION: The investigation knows that after 1929, when meeting participants in the Zinovievist grouping, you spoke about the work being carried out by the Zinovievists against the Party and Soviet power?

ANSWER: I acknowledge that in November or December 1931 I called at NAUMOV's apartment. In conversation, NAUMOV and his wife LEPESHINSKAYA informed me that ZINOVIEV's group, represented by EVDOKIMOV, GESSEN Sergey, and others, continued to struggle against the Party and Soviet power. They had supposedly learned this from direct conversations with GESSEN.

In all probability, I told BLINKOV and BARANOV about this, since I always shared with them matters reflecting the mood of the Zinovievist grouping.

QUESTION: What conversations of an anti-Soviet character did you hold when meeting participants in the Zinovievist grouping?

ANSWER: In the summer of 1932, I was in Moscow and called at SAKHOV's apartment. In conversation, SAKHOV told me about a number of materials in the Procuracy, in particular about peasant dissatisfaction owing to improper actions by local authorities, that the Procuracy was not sufficiently monitoring excesses, and that consequently the removal of certain Procuracy officials was impending.

In the autumn of 1933, MANDELSHTAM Sergey called at my apartment. In a conversation on political topics, the question of strained relations with Japan was touched upon. MANDELSHTAM argued to me that, despite the army's technical equipment and the general growth of the national economy as a whole, a war with Japan would be lost, since the peasantry, dissatisfied with the policy of Soviet power, would not fight.

I do not now remember the conversations with other persons.

The record has been read by me and taken down correctly from my words.

P. REPNIKOV.

INTERROGATED BY:

ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPECIAL DEPARTMENT OF THE GUGB — BRICCI.

Certified true: Kazakova
