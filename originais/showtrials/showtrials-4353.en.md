INTERROGATION RECORD

Of SEMYONOV Semyon Georgiyevich, January 9, 1935.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that you returned to the Party in 1928 not as a result of complete disarmament, but for a maneuvering purpose. Do you acknowledge this?

ANSWER: No, I do not acknowledge this.

QUESTION: The leader of the Vasilyevsky Ostrov Zinovievists, YAKOVLEV M., testifies that you entered the ranks of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) for a double-dealing purpose. Do you continue to deny this?

ANSWER: Yes, I deny this.

QUESTION: The investigation knows of your participation in clandestine Zinovievist gatherings after the Fifteenth Party Congress. Do you acknowledge this?

ANSWER: I attended one clandestine meeting at YAKOVLEV's apartment in early 1928, about which I have already testified. Apart from that, I attended no meetings.

QUESTION: The accused YAKOVLEV, KASPERSKY, and others name you as a member of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization. Do you acknowledge this?

ANSWER: No, I do not; I am not a member of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization.

However, I consider it necessary to inform the investigation that, according to my suppositions, a counterrevolutionary underground Zinovievist group possibly exists in the Vasilyevsky Ostrov district and includes Moisey YAKOVLEV, a convinced Zinovievist, SHEINBERG (died in 1934), Ivan SEMYONOV (Skobar), and PANKO. The last two are close to YAKOVLEV. The basis for my suppositions is a conversation PANKO had with me during a chance encounter in the summer of 1934, in which he, PANKO, reproached me for not maintaining contact with him. He then said: “Senya, you have completely forgotten us.”

QUESTION: You testified to the investigation about participating in one or two clandestine meetings at YAKOVLEV's after the Fifteenth Congress; about an anti-Soviet conversation with the Trotskyist CHEKAN in 1932, at a meeting specially arranged by him; about the counterrevolutionary, slanderous conversations of the oppositionist PERSHINA; about PANKO's conversation with you; and about a meeting of Zinovievist activists of the Vasilyevsky Ostrov district in 1934. Do you acknowledge that these facts show a continuation of the struggle against the Party on your part?

ANSWER: All this is certainly not accidental; the upbringing of Zinovievism and mutual shielding had their full effect on me. These facts show that I had not completely overcome the poison of Zinovievism, but at that time I did not understand that all this harmed the Party — the country.

SEMYONOV.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE 6TH SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE USSR NKVD — KORKIN.

Certified true: Kazakova
