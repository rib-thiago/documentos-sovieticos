CONTINUATION OF THE INTERROGATION RECORD

Of the accused LYUBIN Ivan Vladimirovich

dated January 11, 1935.

LYUBIN Ivan Vladimirovich, born in 1892, n\<on\>-p\<arty\>, belonged to the VKP(b) from 1923 to 1927, was expelled for participation in the Zinovievist opposition and reinstated in 1928. Left the party voluntarily in 1929. Ass\<istant\> to the duty officer responsible for receiving and dispatching mail at the Samara Post Office.

Question: List all the members of the f\<ormer\> Trotskyist-Zinovievist opposition known to you, apart from the persons you have already named in your testimony of January 11, 1935.

Answer: Apart from the persons I listed, I knew several more Trotskyists in Samara, but I find it difficult to recall their names now; however, I know that KURAEV should know them, since he met them more frequently than I did.

I recall that a certain MILOSHCHENKO used to visit TARASOV at the hotel, where TARASOV received him under conspiratorial conditions, one to one.

Question: With which members of the f\<ormer\> opposition did you meet after the Fifteenth Party Congress?

Answer: At the time of the Fifteenth Party Congress I was under arrest and held in the Samara GPU; after my release (in early January 1928) I continued meeting KURAEV, LEMER and NIKISHIN. I saw AZOVSKY, LESHKEVICH and GESSEN once or twice.

Question: What did you discuss when meeting AZOVSKY, GESSEN and others?

Answer: After my release I did not speak with AZOVSKY, GESSEN or LESHKEVICH at all, since our meetings were accidental. I met AZOVSKY at the entrance to the Control Commission, greeted him but spoke with him about nothing. I saw GESSEN several times simply on the street but likewise did not talk with him about anything. Nor did I talk with LESHKEVICH. Until recently I met KURAEV, LEMER and NIKISHIN quite frequently, since we are neighbors in the apartment.

Question: The investigation has established that at one meeting with participants in the former opposition you proposed “raising an armed uprising” against Soviet power and beginning an “armed struggle” against the leaders of the CC of the VKP(b). Do you confirm this?

Answer: Yes, I confirm this; I did indeed propose raising an armed uprising and beginning an armed struggle against the leaders of the VKP(b).

These proposals of mine were a direct result of the atmosphere of hatred and intense hostility toward the leadership of the VKP(b) that prevailed among the participants in the f\<ormer\> opposition with whom I was then meeting.

When meeting me, GESSEN, TARASOV and AZOVSKY systematically spoke of STALIN with hostility, using the harshest abuse, and argued that only ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV were the true leaders of the party and Soviet power. Fully sharing these views, I proposed waging an armed struggle against the leaders of the VKP(b).

Recorded correctly from my words and read. LYUBIN.

Question: When and where did you speak about this?

Answer: I put forward my proposal on the need to take the path of decisive action, beginning the use of armed methods of struggle against the leaders of the VKP(b) and Soviet power, at an illegal gathering at LESHKEVICH's, at the address I indicated earlier, approximately in December 1927; at the same time I also spoke of the need for armed struggle, as what I considered the most radical action, in personal conversations with TARASOV and, I believe, others.

Question: Who was present at this gathering when you put forward the proposal for “armed struggle”?

Answer: Those present at this gathering were AZOVSKY, LEMER, KURAEV, NIKISHIN, myself, LESHKEVICH, KOVALENKO, who had arrived from Moscow, a woman, SLOBODCHIKOV, and two or one workers from the Samara Tube Factory.

Incidentally, afterward KOVALENKO approached me and asked me to write down the address of the hotel where he was staying in Samara, and asked me to call on him. —

The foregoing has been recorded correctly from my words and read. —

LYUBIN.

INTERROGATED BY:

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE OO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR — SOSNOVSKY

OFFICER OF SECTION I OF THE OO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR — PETERS.
