DRAFT

INDICTMENT

in the case of ZINOVIEV G. E., KAMENEV L. B., FEDOROV G. F., PERIMOV A. V., BAKAEV I. P., EVDOKIMOV G. E., KUKLIN A. S., SHAROV Ya. V., GERTIK A. M., TARASOV I. I., FAIVILOVICH L. Ya., GERTSBERG A. V., GESSEN S. M., SAKHOV B. N., GORSHENIN I. S., BASHKIROV A. F., TSARKOV N. A., BRAVO B. L., ANISHEV A. I., accused of crimes

provided for in Articles 17, 58-8, and 58-11 of the Criminal Code of the RSFSR.

The findings of the preliminary and judicial investigations in the case of the underground counterrevolutionary terrorist group that, under the leadership of the so-called “Leningrad center,” prepared and carried out in Leningrad on December 1, 1934, the murder of Comrade S. M. KIROV, member of the Presidium of the Central Executive Committee of the USSR, member of the Politburo and secretary of the Central Committee and Leningrad Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), established that at the same time the so-called “Moscow center” was conducting its underground counterrevolutionary activities in Moscow. It was composed of leaders of the former anti-Soviet Zinovievist group, which, despite its capitulation, had not ceased its underground work directed toward achieving counterrevolutionary aims in the spirit of the so-called Zinovievist-Trotskyist platform.

The preliminary investigation conducted in this connection fully confirmed these findings, establishing that the so-called “Moscow center” included ZINOVIEV G. E., KAMENEV L. B., FEDOROV, GORSHENIN, BAKAEV I. P., EVDOKIMOV G. E., KUKLIN A. S., SHAROV Ya. V., and GERTIK A. M., who united around themselves a number of the most active members of the former anti-Soviet Zinovievist grouping and established systematic ties with members of the Leningrad group convicted by the Military Collegium of the Supreme Court of the USSR on December 28-29, 1934, in the case indicated above.

The preliminary investigation established that the so-called “Moscow center” did not confine itself merely to maintaining ties with the Leningrad underground group of former Zinovievists and individual associates in other cities, but played the role, as the accused FEDOROV expressed it, of a political center systematically directing for a number of years the underground counterrevolutionary activities of both the Moscow and Leningrad groups.

Despite the denials of the principal leaders of the “Moscow center,” the accused ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV, the investigation considers this circumstance fully established not only by the testimony of persons convicted in the “Leningrad center” case, in particular LEVIN, who admitted that he had been the “Moscow center's” representative, RUMYANTSEV, MYASNIKOV, and others, but also by a number of members of the “Moscow center” themselves: BAKAEV, GORSHENIN, FEDOROV, SHAROV, and members of the Moscow underground counterrevolutionary group of former Zinovievists charged in the present case — BRAVO, ANISHEV, GESSEN, and others.

One of the principal tasks of this underground counterrevolutionary group, headed by the “Moscow center,” was to preserve its “cadres,” accumulate forces, and strengthen among members of the group a feeling of bitterness and open hatred toward the leaders of the Party and Soviet power.

The accused FEDOROV, characterizing the activities of the underground counterrevolutionary Zinovievist group, confirmed that —

“the principal task then facing the Zinovievist organization was to go underground in an organized manner, restructure the methods of its underground work, and preserve the principal opposition cadres within the Party. The cadres were preserved by issuing a directive to submit deceptive, double-dealing statements to the Party expressing agreement with and submission to the congress's decisions…”

(Testimony of January 6, case file folio……)

These double-dealing, treacherous methods of counterrevolutionary activity constitute a characteristic feature of the methods of struggle adopted and widely practiced by participants in the underground counterrevolutionary Zinovievist group, who stopped at no means to achieve their aims.

In his testimony of January 4 of this year, speaking of how “the Moscow Zinovievist counterrevolutionary center crystallized, its voice listened to by participants in the Zinovievist groups,” the accused BAKAEV testified:

“…Our center had no positive program that it could oppose to the Party's Central Committee. There was only malicious, hostile criticism of the Party's most important measures; this criticism was a match for the White Guard degenerates of ‘Latest News.’

Herein lies the tragedy of our position: in our struggle against the Party, in our malice toward it, we slid straight into the White Guard swamp — the counterrevolutionary swamp. Lenin was a thousand times right when he said that the logic of struggle leads every opposition to direct, undisguised counterrevolution. Such is the result that must be acknowledged…”

(case file folio……)

Essentially the same was confirmed by the accused SAKHOV, who testified:

“…The tasks of the counterrevolutionary organization were: a) to preserve the Zinovievist cadres and b) to continue their political education in a spirit different from that in which the Party and its Central Committee educate their members. All this was subordinated to a single aim — advancing ZINOVIEV to the leadership of the Party and the Comintern…

Through systematic attacks of a counterrevolutionary character, a hostile attitude toward the Party leadership, especially STALIN and his closest comrades-in-arms, was cultivated in the organization…”

(case file folio……)

The same task was also served by the information widely circulated among members of this underground group about various events in domestic and foreign policy, interpreted in an openly counterrevolutionary manner, with deliberate distortion of reality.

The same accused BAKAEV says in this connection that —

“the information was always counterrevolutionary in substance; it covered all questions of domestic and foreign policy…

Thus the threads of political ties gradually extended downward from ZINOVIEV's apartment, through our apartments to the Moscow comrades, directly to Leningrad and other places; the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist underground was created and grew stronger…”

(case file folio……)

This counterrevolutionary “information” was disseminated through a number of participants in the group closest to ZINOVIEV and EVDOKIMOV, who used their official positions for criminal purposes.

The investigation established that it was precisely in this manner that the members of the “Moscow center” — KUKLIN, GERTIK, EVDOKIMOV, and SHAROV — acted, systematically traveling to Leningrad and maintaining counterrevolutionary ties with members of the “Leningrad center.”

In its struggle against Soviet power this underground counterrevolutionary group, as the accused FEDOROV testified, disdained no means:

“…The members of the organization were breeding grounds for the vilest slander, ‘little rumors,’ and gossip about the Party leadership…

…Throughout the entire activity of the Zinovievist organization, malice and hatred toward the Party leadership were cultivated in its ranks, and the organization did not shrink from setting slander, lies, and deception in motion or distorting facts; that is, all the vilest means borrowed from the arsenal of fascism were employed…”

(case file folio……)

The participant in this group SAFAROV G. I., whose case has been separated and referred for further investigation, also speaks of the counterrevolutionary degeneration of the underground Zinovievist group.

SAFAROV testified that —

“we assiduously stewed in our own juice, intensifying our decay more and more and drawing into the quagmire all those who, like us, were flung by the magnificent force of the proletarian onslaught against all the filth of the past into the little counterrevolutionary afterworld of internal emigration…”

“All thoughts and ideas amounted to giving ideological form to the bourgeois restorationist reaction against socialism…

Politically, in the anti-Party underground, all the counterrevolutionary, anti-Party groupings merged into one solid reactionary mass. We became condensers of the counterrevolutionary malice and hatred of the remnants of the exploiting classes…”

(case file folio……)

It was no accident that in this soil, poisoned by the venom of the Zinovievist underground, truly fascist methods of struggle and openly terrorist sentiments grew and strengthened, leading to the vile murder of Comrade S. M. KIROV.

A whole series of accused persons, both those charged in the present case and those whose cases have been separated and referred for further investigation, speak of the inevitability of sentiments of this kind, logically prepared by the entire character and content of the Zinovievists' underground activities.

These include the accused TSARKOV, who directly links NIKOLAEV's shot with “extremist sentiments,” as he expressed it (case file folio……), that grew on the basis of Zinovievist counterrevolutionary directives, and the accused BASHKIROV, who stated —

“that NIKOLAEV's shot was the consequence of his (NIKOLAEV's) membership and upbringing in the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist-Zinovievist organization,” and that “the entire Trotskyist-Zinovievist counterrevolutionary organization bears political responsibility for this act…”

(case file folio……)

The accused GORSHENIN, in a statement submitted to the investigation, directly acknowledged that —

“the cultivation within our organization of a hostile attitude toward the Party leadership, and especially Comrade STALIN, in the absence of any prospect of achieving the principal aim (returning ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV to the leadership), could not fail to lead the most irreconcilable, most embittered, absolutely class-degenerate elements, such as the Leningrad group that had branched off (RUMYANTSEV, KOTOLYNOV, LEVIN, and others), to turn to such purely fascist methods of struggle against the Party's leaders as individual terror…”

(case file folio……)

The investigation has not established facts that would provide grounds for directly charging the members of the “Moscow center,” in particular its leaders ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV, with having consented or given any instructions for organizing the terrorist act directed against Comrade KIROV. But the entire situation and the entire character of the activities of the underground counterrevolutionary “Moscow center” prove that they knew of the terrorist sentiments of members of this group and inflamed those sentiments.

The members of the “Moscow center,” and foremost the accused ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV, must therefore bear not only moral and political responsibility but also responsibility under Soviet law for the consequences of their underground counterrevolutionary activities, which pushed their Leningrad group onto the path of terrorist actions.

The accused themselves cannot fail to accept this responsibility, at least in moral and political terms.

Thus, speaking of his responsibility for the atrocity committed on December 1, 1934, the accused ZINOVIEV testified:

“…The objective course of events is such that with bowed head I must say: the anti-Party struggle, which assumed particularly acute forms in Leningrad in previous years, could not fail to contribute to the degeneration of these scoundrels. This vile murder cast such an ominous light upon the entire preceding anti-Party struggle that I acknowledge: the Party is entirely right in what it says on the question of the political responsibility of the former anti-Party ‘Zinovievist’ group for the murder committed…”

(case file folio……)

Those charged in the present case testified:

1. BAKAEV I. P. — that he had indeed been a participant in the underground counterrevolutionary group formed from former Zinovievists, and that this group had indeed been headed by the “Moscow center,” consisting of ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, FEDOROV, GORSHENIN, KUKLIN, SHAROV, and himself, BAKAEV; that the “counterrevolutionary group of Leningrad Zinovievists included a fairly large number of old oppositionists tested in the previous struggle,” grouped around RUMYANTSEV, MANDELSHTAM, SOSITSKY, and MYASNIKOV, convicted by the Military Collegium of the Supreme Court of the SSR in the case of the so-called “Leningrad center,” who had direct ties with ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, KUKLIN, GERTIK, and himself, BAKAEV. According to BAKAEV's admission, the underground counterrevolutionary work of participants in the former anti-Soviet Zinovievist group continued from 1928 through 1934.

2. GORSHENIN I. S. — that he too was a participant in the group indicated above and was closely connected with members of the Moscow Zinovievist center. GORSHENIN acknowledged that the Zinovievists' underground counterrevolutionary work continued throughout the entire decade of 1925-1935 under the direct leadership of ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV. In incriminating other participants in this group, GORSHENIN particularly singled out the role of SHAROV, directly connected with ZINOVIEV, and GERTIK, who until December 1934 was a member of the underground counterrevolutionary Zinovievist group and personally connected with its head, G. E. ZINOVIEV.

3. FEDOROV G. F. — that he too was a participant in the said underground counterrevolutionary group and that its political center included the persons named in the indictment.

4. EVDOKIMOV G. E. — acknowledged that he was a member of the “Moscow center.”

5. KUKLIN A. S. — also acknowledged that he was a member of the “Moscow center” and that he maintained ties with members of the Leningrad group: LEVIN, MYASNIKOV, SOSITSKY, and TOLMAZOV.

6. BRAVO B. L. — acknowledged himself to be a participant in the group indicated above and testified that throughout the subsequent period, up to his arrest, he had, on the basis of counterrevolutionary activities, maintained the closest ties with GERTIK, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, KUKLIN, GORSHENIN, SHAROV, ZINOVIEV, and KAMENEV.

7. ANISHEV A. I. — acknowledged himself to be a participant in the Leningrad group and testified that, of the members of the “Moscow center” known to him personally, he had been connected for a number of years, until October 1933, on the affairs of the counterrevolutionary organization, with EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, KUKLIN, and GERTIK.

8. TSARKOV N. A. — acknowledged himself to be a participant in the group indicated above and gave the testimony cited in the indictment.

9.. SAKHOV B. N. — acknowledged that he had been an active participant in this group until 1932 and that until recently he maintained ties with EVDOKIMOV, KUKLIN, and other active members of this underground counterrevolutionary group.

10. GESSEN S. M. — acknowledged that, sharing this group's counterrevolutionary views, throughout the period after the Fifteenth Congress he maintained, on the basis of counterrevolutionary activities, ties with a number of active participants in the underground counterrevolutionary Zinovievist group, in particular ZINOVIEV, EVDOKIMOV, SHAROV, BAKAEV, GORSHENIN, and others.

11. BASHKIROV A. F. [1] — acknowledged that he had returned to the Party with double-dealing aims on ZINOVIEV's direct instructions, conveyed to him by one of the participants in the group. He acknowledged that he was directly connected with members of the “Leningrad center,” LEVIN and SOSITSKY.

12. TARASOV I. I. — also acknowledged that he had returned to the Party with double-dealing intentions in accordance with the directive given by ZINOVIEV in his letter to RUMYANTSEV of June 30, 1928, and had since actively participated in this group's underground counterrevolutionary activities. He confirmed that he too was connected with members of the “Leningrad center,” RUMYANTSEV and KOTOLYNOV.

13. GERTSBERG A. V. — acknowledged that he was a member of the underground counterrevolutionary Zinovievist group and cited a number of facts concerning this group's underground counterrevolutionary activities up to 1934.

14. FAIVILOVICH L. Ya. — confirmed his participation in the said underground counterrevolutionary Zinovievist group.

15. PERIMOV A. V. — acknowledged that throughout the period up to 1933-1934 he maintained ties with active leaders of the underground counterrevolutionary Zinovievist group: GERTIK, GORSHENIN, BAKAEV, and ZINOVIEV.

16. GERTIK A. M. — confirmed that until recently (December 1934) he was an active participant in the underground counterrevolutionary Zinovievist group and was closely connected with members of the “Moscow center.”

During the confrontation with GORSHENIN he confirmed that illegal meetings and gatherings took place in his apartment, as in the apartments of other members of this group.

17. SHAROV Ya. V. [2] — pleaded guilty to being a member of the “Moscow center” together with ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, FEDOROV, and KUKLIN. SHAROV confirmed that during 1931-1932 he received a number of counterrevolutionary directives from ZINOVIEV.

18. KAMENEV L. B. — pleaded not guilty to the charge brought against him, explaining that he had not finally broken his ties with ZINOVIEV solely because of a number of everyday circumstances. He acknowledged that, having returned to the Party, he had not fought actively and energetically enough against the decay that resulted from the struggle against the Party and “on whose soil a gang of bandits from the dregs of the former anti-Party organization could arise and carry out its crime.”

He is incriminated by the testimony of the accused named above: BAKAEV, GORSHENIN, FEDOROV, BRAVO, SHAROV, and others.

19. ZINOVIEV G. E. — pleaded not guilty to the charge brought against him, stating that “after 1929 the organization no longer existed,” and that the participants in the group who remained in Leningrad could initially regard him, ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, and others as “a certain moral center.”

ZINOVIEV also acknowledged that “the former Leningrad leadership continued to meet” after 1932 as well, and that he, ZINOVIEV, experienced vacillations and dissatisfaction with the Party's policy in 1932.

He is incriminated by the testimony of the accused named above.

ON THE BASIS OF THE FOREGOING, THE FOLLOWING ARE ACCUSED:

1. ZINOVIEV Grigory Evseevich, born in 1883, employee, expelled from the ranks of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) in 1932 and reinstated in 1933 in connection with a statement of complete solidarity with the Party's policy;

4. EVDOKIMOV Grigory Eremeevich, born in 1884, employee, expelled from the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), but reinstated on the same grounds;

5. GERTIK Artem Moiseevich, born in 1879, employee, expelled from the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) in 1927, but reinstated on the same grounds;

7. SHAROV Yakov Vasilievich, born in 1884, employee, expelled from the ranks of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) in 1927, but reinstated in 1928 on the same grounds;

8. BRAVO Boris Lvovich, born in 1900, employee, expelled from the ranks of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) in 1927 for factional activities, but reinstated in 1929 on the same grounds;

9. GESSEN Sergei Mikhailovich, born in 1898, employee, expelled from the ranks of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) in 1927 for factional activities, but reinstated on the same grounds;

10. SAKHOV Boris Naumovich, born in 1900, employee, former member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), has a severe reprimand with a warning for factional activities;

11. TARASOV Ivan Ivanovich, born in 1902, student, expelled from the ranks of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) in 1927 for factional activities, but reinstated in 1929 in connection with his statement of complete solidarity with the policy of the Party and Soviet power;

12. FAIVILOVICH Leonid Yakovlevich, born in 1900, employee, expelled from the ranks of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) in 1928 for factional activities, but reinstated in 1929 on the same grounds;

13. GERTSBERG Aleksandr Vladimirovich, born in 1892, employee, former member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks);

14. GORSHENIN Ivan Stepanovich, born in 1894, employee, former member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks);

15. ANISHEV Anatoly Isaevich, born in 1899, employee, former member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), expelled in 1927 for factional activities, but reinstated in 1928 in connection with his statement of complete solidarity with the policy of the Party and Soviet power;

16. TSARKOV Nikolai Alekseevich, born in 1903, employee, former member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), expelled from the Party for factional activities, but reinstated in 1929 on the same grounds;

17. BASHKIROV Aleksandr Fabianovich, born in 1903, student at the Electrotechnical Institute, former member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), expelled for factional activities in 1927, but reinstated in 1928 on the same grounds;

18. PERIMOV Alexei Viktorovich, born in 1897, employee, expelled from the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) in 1928, but reinstated the same year on the same grounds;

19. FEDOROV Grigory Fedorovich, born in 1891, employee, expelled from the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) in 1927 for factional activities, but reinstated in 1928 on the same grounds — all former members of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) —

of the fact that the majority of them were participants in the underground counterrevolutionary group formed in Moscow and Leningrad from former participants in the anti-Soviet Zinovievist group and took an active part in the underground counterrevolutionary activities of these groups, which led to the murder on December 1, 1934, of Comrade S. M. KIROV, member of the Presidium of the Central Executive Committee of the USSR, member of the Politburo of the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), and secretary of the Central Committee and Leningrad Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks).

Furthermore, ZINOVIEV G. E., KAMENEV L. B., EVDOKIMOV G. E., BAKAEV I. P., GERTIK A. M., KUKLIN A. S., SHAROV Ya. V., FEDOROV, and GORSHENIN —

of the fact that, belonging to the underground counterrevolutionary group indicated above, they were members of this group's “Moscow center,” directing the activities of both the Moscow and Leningrad underground counterrevolutionary groups —

that is, of crimes provided for in Articles 17, 58-8, and 58-11 of the Criminal Code of the RSFSR.

In consequence of the foregoing and in accordance with the resolutions of the Central Executive Committee of the USSR of July 10 [3] and December 1, 1934, the above-named persons are subject to trial by the Military Collegium of the Supreme Court of the Union of SSRs.

The indictment was drawn up in Leningrad on “ ” January 1935.
