INTERROGATION RECORD

Of the accused SEREDOKHIN, A. K.

January 5, 1935.

QUESTION: In your previous testimony you stated that until your arrest you were a member of the underground Zinovievist organization. Describe in greater detail all the circumstances connected with your joining and working in this organization.

ANSWER: As I have previously testified, I rejoined the organization in 1932. My joining was preceded by a lengthy and detailed conversation with my friend TOLMAZOV, Andrei Ilyich, who then told me in detail about the ongoing process of restoring old contacts with former participants in the Zinovievist opposition capable of also continuing the struggle begun. [1]

We spoke about how all of us former Zinovievists were specially registered, how we were not trusted, how the party apparatus and special bodies (the GPU was meant) watched us, and how all this would not end until the regime established in the party changed fundamentally and ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV were returned to party leadership.

Discussing with TOLMAZOV the ways and methods of returning ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV to party leadership, TOLMAZOV informed me that there was a directive to restore old organizational contacts, form a single organization, and conduct a consistent struggle within the party to achieve the goals set by our organization.

During this conversation one clearly sensed that the organization was already active and that some leading center existed, giving certain instructions and directing the activities of the entire organization.

During my subsequent meetings with TOLMAZOV, which took place approximately at the beginning of 1933, TOLMAZOV told me directly that a local Zinovievist center headed the Leningrad Zinovievists, in turn connected with an all-Union center located in Moscow. During this conversation he, TOLMAZOV, also told me about the membership of the Leningrad center and, in particular, about the leading role of RUMYANTSEV, Vladimir, who, among other things, kept a register of all former Zinovievists returning to Leningrad who could be used by our organization in further struggle; he also told me that RUMYANTSEV, in keeping such a register, systematically informed ZINOVIEV himself about every Zinovievist newly appearing in Leningrad.

Having thus consented to join the organization, I subsequently began to observe for myself all former Zinovievists I met, assessing each one’s merits from the standpoint of the possibility of drawing them into the organization.

After each such meeting I ascertained each person’s residence and living conditions, then entered him in my notebook, registered him, so to speak. I conveyed my impressions of each to TOLMAZOV.

While in Moscow on frequent business trips, I tried each time to meet one of the former Zinovievists and clarify for myself the prevailing attitudes in Moscow and what forms the struggle was taking.

During my stays in Moscow I met the following persons: EVDOKIMOV, Grigory Yeremeyevich, KUKLIN, Alexander Sergeyevich, SAFAROV, Georgy Ivanovich, FAIVILOVICH, Leonid Yakovlevich, TARKHANOV, Oskar, and SAKHOV, Boris. To one degree or another I informed each of them about the Zinovievists living in Leningrad and the attitudes prevailing there.

I visited KUKLIN, EVDOKIMOV, and SAKHOV in 1933, together with TOLMAZOV.

QUESTION: Do you consider your organization’s activities counterrevolutionary?

ANSWER: Yes, unquestionably. Our organization, while fighting against the policy of the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), also cultivated feelings of hatred and bitterness toward the party leadership, especially toward Comrades STALIN, KAGANOVICH, and KIROV. The organization’s methods of activity — spreading slanderous rumors, distorted presentation of the country’s situation, disbelief in the possibility of building socialism in one country, a negative attitude toward the decisions of the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) on fundamental questions of international and domestic policy — has created [2] a system of views characteristic of fascism.

QUESTION: Did you personally know NIKOLAEV, Leonid, a participant in your organization, and how do you assess the terrorist act he committed against Comrade KIROV?

ANSWER: I did not know NIKOLAEV, Leonid, personally. As for my assessment of this action by our organization, I am sincerely outraged by this most vile crime.

At the same time I must acknowledge that the terrorist act committed by NIKOLAEV, a member of our organization, is the logical consequence of all the activities of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization.

QUESTION: What did you know about preparations for this terrorist act?

ANSWER: I knew absolutely nothing about the fact of preparations for the terrorist act.

The record has been written correctly from my words and read by me, in confirmation of which I sign.

A. SEREDOKHIN.

INTERROGATED BY:

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO OF THE UGB — STROMIN.

Certified correct: A. Svetlova
