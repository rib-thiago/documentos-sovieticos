INTERROGATION RECORD

OF MYASNIKOV, Nikolai Petrovich

dated December 16, 1934.

I consider it necessary to bring to the investigation's attention facts concerning the activity of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization which I omitted when giving testimony on December 15 of this year:

1\) Apart from the counterrevolutionary organization's center, there were special organizers in the districts of the city of Leningrad, designated as early as 1928. I knew some of them from that time. Thus, in the Volodarsky district I knew one of the organizers whose surname was either FEDOROV or FEDOTOV. I once met him at LEVIN V.'s home; after FEDOROV (FEDOTOV) had left, LEVIN praised him as a good organizer. His description: medium height, dark-haired, broad-shouldered, of sturdy build, apparently clean-shaven. At that time FEDOROV (FEDOTOV) worked at either the “Lenin” or the “Bolshevik” factory.

In the Vasileostrovsky district I knew as organizers Moisey, whose surname I believe was LEVIN (I testified about him on December 15), KASPERSKY, and SHEINBERG; in the Narva district, MAKAROV and MOROZOV. The former covered the area from the “Red Triangle” factory to the Narva Gates, and the latter from the “Red Triangle” factory to the Moscow Gates.

2\) In the summer of 1934 DMITRIEV Timofey, a member of the counterrevolutionary organization, spent the night at my apartment. He displayed extreme dissatisfaction with Party policy and bitterness against the leadership of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks). Expressing regret that KAMENEV and ZINOVIEV had been removed from the leadership, DMITRIEV said: “Will ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV never come to power?” His embittered feelings were of the most acute kind.

3\) After the October holidays of 1934, in my office at Smolny, LEVIN V., in the presence of VIGONT — head of the political department of an MTS in the Chelyabinsk region — related that NIKOLAEV, the director of a state farm in the West Siberian Territory, had been dismissed from his post, expelled from the Party, and put on trial for failing to meet the grain delivery plan. LEVIN said that NIKOLAEV was extremely embittered, ready for anything, and essentially harbored terrorist sentiments. NIKOLAEV, formerly secretary of the Party collective of the former OGPU Plenipotentiary Representation in Leningrad, had at one time associated with the Zinovievist opposition. I know him as an impetuous and determined man. From LEVIN's knowledge of him, I assume that he has remained connected with NIKOLAEV until recently.

QUESTION: Is the NIKOLAEV you named related to Comrade KIROV's murderer, NIKOLAEV Leonid?

ANSWER: I do not know.

MYASNIKOV.

INTERROGATED BY —

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB — LYUSHKOV
