TO THE CENTRAL COMMITTEE OF THE ALL-UNION COMMUNIST PARTY (BOLSHEVIKS)

TO IOSIF VISSARIONOVICH STALIN.

Great Iosif Vissarionovich!

I have deprived myself of the right to address the Central Committee as a Bolshevik… I address it AS A CRIMINAL WHO WANTS TO PUT AN END TO HIS crime and asks for the party’s help. I have given my testimony to the investigation; here I must explain above all the path to crime.

It is vile and shameful that I had to be confronted with the socialist motherland, with responsibility for the murder of a great Bolshevik, before I resolved to tell of my crimes and the crimes of other counterrevolutionaries with party cards. Even bourgeois revolutionaries knew that “he who makes a revolution only halfway digs his own grave.” One could not return to the party of world proletarian revolution and the building of socialism, after years of the most bitter anti-party and anti-Soviet struggle, without cleansing oneself ENTIRELY AND WITHOUT REMAINDER of the attitudes, ideas, entire structure of thought, political contacts, and mutual protection of the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist opposition. It was criminal to join the ranks of the army of victorious socialism with divided feelings and thoughts, with the doubts of a deserter and traitor, which at that time (in 1928) were driven underground to soothe the pitiful remnants of party conscience. Subsequently nothing could come of this except the lowest and most perfidious double-dealing, except the vilest amalgam of an outwardly demonstrative “party” appearance with a counterrevolutionary essence. Active and leading participation in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist-Trotskyist conspiracy against the party, its Leninist Central Committee, and its leader of genius transformed me from someone once committed to the party, possessing the strength and courage to think fundamental political questions through and resolve them to the end, into a “half-party,” that is, anti-party monster and degenerate. Returning to the party in 1928, I thought: “Subjectively I have finished with the idea of a second party and the creation of dual power in the country, with the Trotskyist, Menshevik-White Guard theory denying the possibility of building socialism in one country. Therefore I have burned my bridges sincerely and honestly.” In reality, the strength of my subjective repulsion from TROTSKY and his “faithful” far outweighed the straightforward desire to return to the party for honest work within it. In reality, the counterrevolutionary idea of overthrowing the party leadership had not been abandoned; the defeat and collapse of the counterrevolutionary opposition bloc merely pushed this idea temporarily into the background, its vitality drawn from malicious hostility toward the Leninist-Stalinist Central Committee. In reality, even when I supposed that at precisely that moment I had come closest to the party, I rejoiced that the party had not forced me to expose the network of the opposition’s counterrevolutionary organization. I preserved my loyalty to that Cain-like mutual protection of conspirators against the party and socialism which proved to be the casing for the bullet that struck KIROV. It seemed to me that I was returning sincerely and wholeheartedly to the party with which I had been connected throughout my life since the age of fourteen. In reality I mistook the bitterness of a hangover after counterrevolutionary intoxication for a restored “party spirit.” I mistook my bitter resentment against the bankrupt leaders of anti-party and anti-Soviet struggle, particularly TROTSKY and ZINOVIEV, for “party spirit.” The anti-party, anti-Soviet struggle tore, tore people away from the party, from the working class, made these people hostile, and one could not overcome by purely intellectual means this separation, this alienation, this hostility already ingrained in the brain toward the proletariat building socialism, toward its party. In Saratov in 1928–29 I began to feel the first signs that I was returning to life, that is, to the party, wrenching myself out of the vile underground of superfluous people who had spent themselves entirely in struggle against the party. The party placed excessive trust in me by transferring me to work in the Communist International, and there, without having undergone sufficient necessary retempering in grassroots, everyday hard work, I began sliding downhill again, at first imperceptibly to myself and then faster and faster, until in 1932 I found myself at the very heart of a new counterrevolutionary conspiracy against the party as its active organizer.

The great party of LENIN–STALIN created and nurtured a mighty, unprecedented world of socialism. In these years it advanced farther than all humanity had ever advanced in an entire century. Factories, machines, fields, people, tens of millions of people, everything became new, was remelted in the Leninist-Stalinist crucible into an invincible alloy of socialism. And we, insignificant and base conspirators, alien and hostile to these world-historic victories and the people who created them, the farther socialist construction rose, the more the strain of Bolshevik muscles increased, the more we “weakened in spirit,” decomposed alive, and offered one another the cadaveric poison and stench of this counterrevolutionary decay of ours as a new revelation, as a new platform for “saving the party.” Just as in 1917 the country and revolution had to be saved from “saviors of the motherland and revolution” of the Dan-Chernov-Savinkov variety, so in these years the party had to be rid of “saviors” of the ZINOVIEV, EVDOKIMOV, SAFAROV, MRACHKOVSKY, SMILGA, and similar types.

Most participants in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc “returned” to the party as a piece of rusty iron penetrates beneath the skin to infect the organism. The subverters of this counterrevolutionary underground, camouflaged by party cards and a smokescreen of every sort of “loyal” declaration, degenerated so far that they could not count on even the remotest possibility of finding any common language with party proletarians, with the non-party working masses. But precisely for this reason we stewed intensely in our own juice, increasing our decay more and more and drawing into the mire everyone who, like ourselves, was cast by the magnificent force of the proletarian assault on all the filth of the past into the counterrevolutionary afterworld of internal emigration. The “struggle for communism, for the party, for the proletarian revolution,” by which we swore and vowed, became entirely, for us superfluous people of the counterrevolutionary underground, a struggle against the Leninist of genius, the leader of steel, against the Central Committee, for the advent to power of the leaders of counterrevolutionary renegacy. The disintegration of personality, moral decay in everyday life, and loss of all self-control went hand in hand with political degeneration. In the circle of degenerate former Leningrad Komsomol members, from whose midst the vile fascist murderer emerged, counterrevolutionary conspiratorial activity was criminally and invariably intertwined with reckless drunkenness and debauchery. I bear full responsibility for these degenerates, who had been under my influence since 1934. This degenerate milieu, which became an agency of the bloody interventionists, was constantly nourished by the counterrevolutionary rumors, gossip, and information of the Zinovievist center, which for years had conditioned it in a spirit of reckless, bestial-fascist hatred toward the party’s leaders. The cult of “oppressed innocents,” of “LENIN’s disciples hounded by STALIN,” bound together the motley anti-party rabble that united for drinking bouts, games of dominoes, savoring slanderous gossip about “fights in the Central Committee,” discussing the current situation in an internal-emigrant spirit, and eventually came to serve directly the instigators of counterrevolutionary war against the USSR, the fascist executioners.

ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV have been known to the party for more than a decade as the embodiment of unrestrained anti-proletarian promiscuity combined with truly exceptional political cowardice, men who always seized the immortal LENIN by the elbow when it was a matter of striking a mortal blow against the proletariat’s enemies, always hurled themselves like rabid curs at the great STALIN only to burst immediately into tears of ink-written repentance and then begin slandering him and the Central Committee once again; they proved to be the head of that putrid abscess that sprang up on the party’s body. LENIN said that gossip was a weapon of the counterrevolutionary bourgeoisie. Zinovievist-Trotskyist gossip-provocation was the travel pass for NIKOLAEV and KOTOLYNOV.

The Zinovievist organization, schooled by such a master of counterrevolutionary provocation and gossip as TROTSKY, an organization founded by outstanding chameleons and collectors of “little rumors” such as MAGYAR and by serious organizers of their dissemination, from BAKAEV to GERTIK, is above all an organization based on counterrevolutionary gossip, slander, and provocation. LENIN considered gossip an instrument of the counterrevolutionary bourgeoisie, understanding by this that in this way the overthrown class attempts to influence politics. All counterrevolutionary groupings and organizations, from the Ryutinites to the Zinovievists, Trotskyists, and Lominadzeites, borrowed this poisoned weapon from the bourgeoisie for the needs of bourgeois restorationist policy. However, the Zinovievist organization broke every record in this respect. This is explained by the fact that precisely this method of underground influence on political life proved as suitable as could be for people of the Zinovievist cast and type, speculators of the counterrevolutionary underground who, since the 15th Congress, had invariably avoided bearing responsibility for their actions.

Among the Zinovievists gossip became the axis of every counterrevolutionary construction and plan. The degenerates based their calculations on slander; slander became their hashish, their opium, without which they could not live a single day. This hashish and opium gratified the bankrupts’ megalomania, forever thirsting for incense to be burned to their petty vanity as “unrecognized saviors.” In the trade in counterrevolutionary, slanderous narcotics, all the anti-party groupings drew closer, united, and jointly wove the fabric of counterrevolutionary conspiracy, from rabid Trotskyists of the SMIRNOV–MRACHKOVSKY type and Zinovievists to STEN and LOMINADZE, who tried not to bring their fingers too close to the fire while receiving warmth from the counterrevolutionary bonfire they had built.

Bankrupts, conspirators, and double-dealers of every stripe bargained with one another over how much to reduce the pace of building socialism, by what percentage to thwart the expansion of socialist industry and construction of the collective-farm system. None of us had any program other than one of braking and moving backward. During the period of two five-year plans, not a single counterrevolutionary grouping advanced, or could advance, one positively formulated, independent idea of its own about the paths of building socialism. All thoughts and ideas were reduced to giving ideological form to bourgeois restorationist reaction against socialism. Precisely such an astounding political lack of ideas in counterrevolutionary cliques was bound inevitably to produce a generalization of all reactionary tendencies against socialism in the idea of struggle against the Stalinist leadership. The colossal world-historic victories of this leadership opened an ever wider gulf between us and socialist reality, which became more alien and incomprehensible to us the more it grew and materialized. We remained inwardly alien and hostile to the central idea of the epoch, the idea of building socialism, brilliantly formulated by STALIN, and we turned all our reactionary malice as superfluous people of the counterrevolutionary underground against the organizers of the great construction. Politically, in the anti-party underground, all the counterrevolutionary anti-party groupings merged into a single solid reactionary mass. We became condensers of the counterrevolutionary malice and hatred of the remnants of the exploiting classes and a transmission belt for the bandits of imperialism, and we must bear the full horror of political responsibility for KIROV’s murder, for he was killed not by the bullet of a hireling of foreign fascists sent from abroad but by a bullet cast in our underground of abomination and desolation.

Reactionary lack of ideas, inspired by the sole desire to drag the country backward, fed day after day the intense bitterness against the leadership, whose failures were prophesied everywhere; and bitterness against the leadership swelled and swelled into degenerate, bourgeois restorationist hatred toward the proletariat building socialism.

Monstrous, incredible, unprecedented in history is this degree of degradation of people who owe their entire political development and the happiness of participating in the struggle of worlds to the proletariat! But the fact remains a fact. We reached such a loss of revolutionary identity that in 1932 we attempted to throw down as a challenge to the Leninist-Stalinist Central Committee a platform glued and stitched together from Zinovievist-reptilian calculations that “the Central Committee will burst at the first serious internal or international scrape”; from speculations on the all-destroying force of “inflation”; from the ZINOVIEV–SMILGA thesis of the petty-bourgeois degeneration and corruption of the proletariat and proletarian youth of the country of victorious socialism; from Mrachkovsky’s complicity with the special settlers; from Safarov’s panic before an invented “rupture of the alliance” with the peasantry; from Magyar-Lominadze “fears” of the revival of classes through the party-Soviet leadership; from “declarations” about the bankruptcy of the Third International. If such joint action did not take place in 1932, this is explained by the fact that the corpse worms, from a sense of self-preservation, did not dare crawl out onto the surface, crawl as far as the Central Committee’s reception room. They preferred to continue acting by the method of “bacteriological warfare,” hoping that one way or another, as a result of poisoning the party atmosphere with the bacilli of counterrevolutionary slander and provocation, something would break out somewhere at a point of particularly high tension. After the especially frequent and lively meetings of 1932, when the counterrevolutionary conspirators against the party of LENIN–STALIN expected to credit to their own account certain transient difficulties in the transition from the first to the second five-year plan, all underground circles, frightened by the arrests and crushing of the Ryutinites, returned to their customary way of life, to underground sapping, to creeping counterrevolution, to poisoning wells.

“The proletariat has become peasant-like; the victories of socialism lead it toward degeneration”: the anti-party subverters not only made this old thesis of the father of the revisionist prostitution of socialism, Eduard Bernstein, their own, but also used it to justify the continuation of deep tunneling beneath the foundations of Bolshevik party spirit and the Soviet system. The degenerates, in revenge for their own bankruptcy and decay, declared the proletariat degenerate!

The atmosphere of internal emigration and the policy of war through little rumors, gossip, and provocations nurtured and sustained the scoundrel NIKOLAEV and his closest accomplices. Over the years people had already grown accustomed to the idea that years equal to centuries separated them from the proletariat and set them against it. This counterrevolutionary alienation from the proletariat and its vanguard, this fascist degeneration, crystallized into a system of political views wholly coinciding with TROTSKY’s White Guard positions: “The proletariat has degenerated; on the shoulders of the degenerated proletariat, the privileged bureaucracy struggles against the peasantry, leading the country to ruin.” What TROTSKY did and continues to do abroad, setting the vile dogs of capital and instigators of war against the USSR, was duplicated within the USSR by the Zinovievist organization, Mrachkovsky and Smirnov, I. N.’s “our collective farm,” the Ryutinites, and, at a certain ostensible distance from them, the Lominadzeites.

In the anti-party counterrevolutionary underground there arose, grew, and hardened the Socialist-Revolutionary-fascist ideology of an “enterprising minority, suffering and fighting alone because the proletariat has lost its class consciousness and organization.” This ideology was simultaneously one that nourished terrorist sentiments and the search for a “way out” in counterrevolutionary banditry.

Participants in the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc bear on their faces the mark of a curse, the mark of Cain, the mark of the murderer NIKOLAEV. From counterrevolutionary gossip, slanders, provocations, meetings with would-be leaders, underground negotiations, and Zinovievist and chameleon-like Magyar methods of bacteriological warfare was cast the bullet of the murderer who, in his monstrous baseness, surpassed the fascist murderers of Karl Liebknecht and Rosa Luxemburg.

I answer for all this. I am a grave criminal before the party and Soviet power, a degenerate who trampled his own Bolshevik past into the mud. I ask that the harshest punishment be imposed upon me. And at the same time I ask you, great leader, I ask the Central Committee to give me the opportunity to atone for my grave crime, so that much later, having demonstrated through my work, all my actions, and all my conduct my willingness and ability to serve the cause of the party of LENIN–STALIN, I may return to its ranks as an ordinary fighter. Apart from the hope of returning to the party, there is and can be no life for me. —

G. SAFAROV.
