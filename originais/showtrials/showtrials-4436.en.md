TESTIMONY

OF EVDOKIMOV, Grigory Eremeevich,

dated January 12, 1935.

In addition to my previous testimony, I consider it necessary to add the following:

on the question of the underground counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization in Leningrad.

I knew that in Leningrad there was a group of people united by shared anti-Party sentiments, who maintained the counterrevolutionary positions of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc of 1925-1927.

I knew this both from other members of the center and from my personal meetings with some of them in Leningrad. In 1928, during LASHEVICH's funeral, which was organized with the intention of demonstrating sympathy for the Zinovievists, ZINOVIEV was at my apartment, and after the funeral a group of Zinovievists came there. Of the members of the Moscow center, those present were ZINOVIEV, BAKAEV, and myself (I do not remember who else from Moscow was there); of those from Leningrad, there were RUMYANTSEV, NALIVAIKO, and KOSTRITSKY — I do not remember the others (BAKAEV has a better memory for people; he and ZINOVIEV can add the others). This meeting developed into conversations about preserving the cadres in Leningrad and ties with the Moscow Zinovievists.

I spoke about my meetings with Zinovievists in Leningrad in 1929 and later in my previous testimony. It is only necessary to add that until 1931 my son-in-law, TARASOV Pyotr, also a Zinovievist, lived in Leningrad and at that time remained on the former counterrevolutionary positions of 1925-1927. During one of my visits to Leningrad in 1930-1931, I met RUMYANTSEV at his home. In addition, another relative of mine, SHIRYAEV Dmitry, also a former Zinovievist, lived in Leningrad until recently. I met him in both Moscow and Leningrad. I did not conceal my views from him, but he did not argue with me when I expressed them, while he himself did not speak on general political questions. Of those from Leningrad, LEVIN, MANDELSHTAM, MIRONOV, and REM visited me in Moscow up to 1933. REM also visited me in July or August 1934; during this last visit we had no conversations of a political nature. But all these meetings, regardless of whether there were political anti-Party conversations, were meetings of people united by a common anti-Party political past who had not renounced that anti-Party past or the counterrevolutionary positions of 1925-1927. I cannot, however, say the latter of my meetings with NALIVAIKO and KOSTRITSKY in 1933-1934 and with REM in 1934. During these meetings I not only did not discuss political subjects with them, but did not know their sentiments either.

Furthermore, I knew of the existence of an underground group of Zinovievists in Leningrad also from conversations about the meetings of other members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist-Kamenevist center with people from Leningrad: ZINOVIEV, GERTIK, BAKAEV. And when the Moscow center adopted decisions such as, for example, how to behave at meetings concerning ZINOVIEV's expulsion from the Party for his ties with RYUTIN and company, the decision was adopted not only for Moscow but also for Leningrad; this means that ⟨it was possible⟩ to count on its being put into effect in Leningrad in a more or less organized manner.

Personally, since the spring of 1933, I have not met any of the Leningrad Zinovievists apart from those I mentioned in my previous testimony.

Written in my own hand — EVDOKIMOV.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE EKO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD — MIRONOV.

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB — LYUSHKOV.

ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB — RUTKOVSKY.

Certified correct: Kazakova
