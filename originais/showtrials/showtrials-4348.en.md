INTERROGATION RECORD

Of BRAVO Boris Lvovich

January 9, 1935.

In response to the investigation's proposal that I summarize everything known to me about the activity of the organization of the Zinovievist underground and my personal role in it, I can report the following:

I submitted a statement withdrawing from the opposition in 1929, that is, sixteen months after the overwhelming mass of Zinovievists declared their renunciation of opposition struggle. I did this only when, having painfully undergone a reassessment of my views, I could, as it seemed to me, sincerely and honestly say that in the struggle against the opposition the Party had been right and that the views of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist opposition were counterrevolutionary through and through. Returning to Moscow in the summer of 1929, I sought out NAUMOV Ivan Kupriyanovich, who, as it seemed to me, had withdrawn from the opposition as sincerely as I, without intending to maneuver. At his home I met GERTIK Dina and, having no accommodation, accepted the offer to stay temporarily with her.

Unexpectedly, I found myself again almost exclusively in the circle of former figures and participants in the Zinovievist opposition. GERTIK's apartment was visited not only by most of the former Zinovievists living in Moscow — BAKAEV, KOSTINA, GORSHENIN, KOZHURO, SHAROV, NAUMOV, KUKLIN, EVDOKIMOV, PERIMOV, SAKHOV — but also by former oppositionists arriving in Moscow from the provinces — DMITRIEV, GESSEN, LEVIN, KORSHUNOV, TSVIBAK, ZILBERMAN, and others. Here I renewed acquaintance with a number of former like-minded associates and made new ones. For two and a half to three years, GERTIK's apartment, on Arbat Square and later in Kolobovsky Lane, was the headquarters of figures and participants in the Zinovievist organization.

Personal friendships formed back in Leningrad and over two years of struggle against the Party played no small role in this attraction of former Zinovievists toward one another and their concentration around several persons (GERTIK, EVDOKIMOV, GORSHENIN). But the chief and fundamental thing bringing the listed persons together and cementing their connection was their joint struggle against the Party in the past and furious malice against the Party leadership, chiefly Comrade STALIN, in the present. Already defeated ideologically by life itself, feeling no ideological basis beneath it, the group of former opposition figures sought satisfaction in savoring various rumors and gossip about supposedly emerging or existing disagreements in the leadership, in inventing supposedly existing or impending economic difficulties, in whose presence we imagined it possible to bring ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV into the Party leadership, and in endless mutual complaints about the Party leadership's distrustful attitude toward us.

No decision of Party organizations concerning any former Zinovievist was interpreted otherwise than as “fault-finding,” a “desire to disadvantage,” or “squeeze.” Responsibility for this kind of attitude toward former participants in the Zinovievist opposition was placed personally on Comrade STALIN, all sorts of slanderous fabrications being adduced as proof. In our malicious slander against Comrade Stalin, we went so far that, for example, GORSHENIN's expulsion from the Party during the 1933 purge was regarded by some participants in the organization as the result of a directive supposedly given by Comrade STALIN to the Purge Commission to expel all former Zinovievists.

Moving until mid-1932 almost exclusively in this circle, where an atmosphere poisoned by slander and malice against the Party leadership prevailed, I too succumbed to the sentiments dominant there, became infected by them, and became their transmitter among the former oppositionists I met.

The circle of former oppositionists grouped around EVDOKIMOV, GERTIK, and BAKAEV had already in 1930–1931 effectively turned into a counterrevolutionary organization whose activity consisted in malicious slander against the leadership of the Party and Soviet government, systematic undermining of their authority, and disintegration of Party unity. Slanderous fabrications passed as if along a chain from one member of the organization to another and were transmitted through acquaintances, former oppositionists, to other cities. Thus malicious fabrications against the central organ “Pravda,” over its failure to publish a notice of Dina GERTIK's death, were spread and transmitted to Leningrad. Thus slanderous inventions about persecution by the Party leadership of former opposition figures KAMENEV, ZINOVIEV, EVDOKIMOV, and others were invented and spread.

Personal connections of participants in the organization with former participants in the Zinovievist opposition living in other cities were widely used to disseminate all sorts of counterrevolutionary fabrications, rumors, and gossip. Personally, on my visits to Leningrad I met with BASHKIROV and LEVIN Vl., sometimes with KORSHUNOV, and conversed with them about the position of former opposition figures, reflecting in these conversations the sentiments prevailing in the organization headed by EVDOKIMOV. EVDOKIMOV and GERTIK regularly traveled to Leningrad. In turn, LEVIN and KORSHUNOV, arriving from Leningrad, met with EVDOKIMOV, GERTIK, and myself. Contact was maintained with former oppositionists living in other cities as well. Personally, until 1932, I met with DMITRIEV, then living in Saratov, and ELKOVICH Yakov of Sverdlovsk.

Individual participants in the organization, EVDOKIMOV and KUKLIN, maintained contact with ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV, informing them about the underground's sentiments. Personally, in August 1932, I happened to be present at a meeting between EVDOKIMOV and ZINOVIEV. Arriving with EVDOKIMOV at ZINOVIEV's dacha in Ilyinskoye, we found KUKLIN and FEDOROV Grigory there. At this meeting, I informed ZINOVIEV about grain procurement prospects, difficulties already arising in procurement in the Kuban, and food difficulties in one district of the Odessa region, which I knew about from a copy of a memorandum given to me for the journal by the inspector of the All-Union Society “Zagotzerno,” OBELOV (died in 1934). Later, at ZINOVIEV's request, I sent him a copy of this memorandum through EVDOKIMOVA Ksenia Vasilyevna, understanding that ZINOVIEV would use it for anti-Party purposes.

The following fact also testifies to the systematic contact of members of the organization with ZINOVIEV: in the summer of 1932, I read an article by Radek in “Izvestiya” on problems in the Far East, expressing the idea of the possibility of joint action with America against Japan should the latter attack the Soviet Union. In conversation with EVDOKIMOV about this article, I asked his opinion on the possibility of joint action by the Soviet Union with an imperialist state in a defensive war of the USSR. Having confirmed the possibility, under certain conditions, of joint action with America, EVDOKIMOV returned to this question several days later and said that he had discussed it with ZINOVIEV, whose viewpoint coincided with his own, EVDOKIMOV's, opinion.

This same meeting with ZINOVIEV showed me how deep and at the same time unprincipled was the malice of former opposition figures toward Comrade Stalin. When someone present related that Comrade YAROSLAVSKY had been removed from the editorial staff of the central organ “Pravda” on Comrade STALIN's proposal, ZINOVIEV made a maliciously slanderous remark about STALIN that met with no objection from anyone.

On a number of other occasions, EVDOKIMOV, noting some successes of socialist construction, remarked: “That is good, but it would be even better if STALIN were not General Secretary.”

I acknowledge myself guilty of: 1) having taken the path of double-dealing soon after returning to the Party, and subsequently, in 1930, joining the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization, in whose work I participated by spreading slanderous fabrications about the leadership of the Party and Soviet government, which undermined their authority; 2) having committed a crime by using my official position to pass a document to ZINOVIEV, knowing he would use it for anti-Party struggle; 3) having, together with other participants in the organization, helped incite malice and hatred toward the Party leaders among those former oppositionists I met.

The testimony was written in my own hand — BRAVO.

Interrogated by:

Assistant head of the Special Department of the GUGB of the NKVD — GENDIN.
