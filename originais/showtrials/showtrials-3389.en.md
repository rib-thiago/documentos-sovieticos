INTERROGATION RECORD

Of KOSTINA, Anna Porfiryevna. —

Dated December 22, 1934.

QUESTION: With which participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc did you maintain contact?

ANSWER: I maintain contact with former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc: EVDOKIMOV, GORSHENIN, KOZHURO [1], SHAROV, GERTIK, OLKHOVSKY and Pyotr ZALUTSKY; the latter visited me and maintained contact only with my family. I met ZINOVIEV until his expulsion from the Party in 1932. Occasionally I met ELKOVICH, GESSEN and BOCHAROV; I also met Nikolai DMITRIEV at GORSHENIN's home.

QUESTION: Tell us what you know about meetings in Moscow of former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc and the nature of these meetings.

ANSWER: At meetings at Artem GERTIK's apartment before his wife's death in 1932, at GORSHENIN's apartment and at mine, the persons I listed were present at various times. Political questions too were touched on in conversations during these meetings.

QUESTION: What questions did you discuss at your meetings?

ANSWER: After the Fifteenth Party Congress, recognizing the harmfulness of factional struggle under the dictatorship of the proletariat, most participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc entered the Party without abandoning their views. Many participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc—myself, BAKAEV, OLKHOVSKY, BOCHAROV, RUDY and GOROKHOV—considered that disagreements on the fundamental questions had diminished, since we considered the congress's main decisions correct.

We disagreed with the criticism of the opposition in the decisions of the Fifteenth Party Congress. After readmission to the Party, in 1928–1930, we criticized many Party decisions on economic policy and policy in the countryside. In doing so we expressed bitterness toward Comrade STALIN. In 1928 or 1929, after ZINOVIEV's return from Kaluga, I came from Vyatka to Moscow and called on ZINOVIEV. During the conversation the question of the Party's attitude toward participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc was raised. ZINOVIEV told me that at present there could be no question of leading Party work, that we must gradually integrate into the Party, that some comrades did not understand that this was a lengthy process, that struggle against the Rightists within the Party was inevitable and that then we would prove useful. In the summer of 1931, during my visit to ZINOVIEV at his dacha at Ilyinskoye, he raised the question of the situation in the countryside. ZINOVIEV said that coercive collectivization was being carried out in the countryside, that not only local bodies but the Party leadership were to blame for excesses, that the excesses could not have escaped the center's notice, that the Party leadership was not informing the Party about the situation. There ZINOVIEV also concluded that coercive collectivization was leading to the ruin of the countryside.

On the question of the Comintern's leadership of fraternal Communist parties, ZINOVIEV said that as the revolutionary movement grew, it received neither help nor leadership. From this ZINOVIEV concluded that the Comintern's work and leadership were weak. I know that in 1932 SAFAROV visited ZINOVIEV to “pour out his heart”; I do not know what SAFAROV discussed with ZINOVIEV. In 1930 ZINOVIEV conducted negotiations with the Rightists; I, EVDOKIMOV and BAKAEV knew about this; whether the other participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc knew, I do not know. At the end of 1932, when ZINOVIEV was expelled from the Party in connection with the Ryutin case, I knew I would have to speak at the cell meeting; I did not understand why ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV were assigned to the Ryutin group while UGLANOV, STEN and others were placed in a separate group with the right to appeal after one year. I told BAKAEV that I intended to express at the cell meeting my disagreement with the Central Committee plenum's decision to expel ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV from the Party without the right of appeal. BAKAEV tried to dissuade me from speaking, and when he could not convince me he insisted that we discuss the question with EVDOKIMOV, KUKLIN, SHAROV and GORSHENIN. That same day we gathered at my apartment, and after an exchange of views all those present tried to persuade me not to speak, since my speech would be childish and useless and its consequences for me very severe.

The gathering concerning my speech at the cell had the character of a meeting. In the autumn of 1933 I, BAKAEV, KOZHURO and EVDOKIMOV met at GORSHENIN's apartment. During the conversation the question of the grain and fodder balance was raised. GORSHENIN and EVDOKIMOV told us that the country's grain resources were in a difficult situation, that it would be hard to correct the situation, that major excesses had occurred in Ukraine during grain procurement. It was also said that if there were a war, given the unsettled sentiments in the countryside, complications could arise within the country; KOZHURO and I objected to everyone present, since we considered that they were exaggerating the difficulties. The question of the Central Committee and Council of People's Commissars' decision on grain deliveries was also raised, and we concluded that the decision had been taken late; had it been adopted in spring, it would have encouraged more stable sentiments in the countryside. From our readmission to the Party until recently, I, KOSTINA, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, GORSHENIN, SHAROV, KUKLIN and Artem GERTIK considered that the Party was isolating us and did not trust us, that our position in the Party was difficult. At meetings we particularly said that the position in the Moscow organization was the most difficult, because KAGANOVICH treated all former oppositionists with greater hostility than in other organizations.

Recorded correctly from my words and read to me. —

KOSTINA.

INTERROGATED BY: HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB, PETROVSKY.
