INTERROGATION RECORD

Of KHARITONOV Moisey (Mikhail) Markovich

December 29, 1934.

Question: Was the question of the attitude toward the collectivization of agriculture discussed at Zinovievist meetings?

Answer: As I have already testified, on this question I had repeated conversations only personally with ZINOVIEV in 1929–1930. Calling the methods of collectivization “Asiatic,” ZINOVIEV said then that “people are driven into collective farms by violent measures; the peasantry, protesting against this violence, slaughters livestock, and agriculture in general is being brought to ruin.” In one such conversation, ZINOVIEV cited figures about the supposedly difficult situation in Kazakhstan and related that allegedly there were cases of emigration by the Kazakh population in connection with this.

Assessing the situation in the country in the darkest colors, in an openly counterrevolutionary spirit, ZINOVIEV accused the Party leadership, and especially Comrade STALIN, of incorrectly directing the Party and the entire life of the country. In one conversation, apparently in 1931, ZINOVIEV expressed his sentiments, among other things, as follows: “How much longer will we old Bolsheviks remain silent?” I do not remember this statement of his being discussed, or him developing it and proposing any practical measures; I convey this episode to the investigation as it has remained in my memory.

I consider it necessary to inform the investigation of the following fact, which shows that ZINOVIEV held counterrevolutionary views not only on questions of the country's internal situation, but also on the international situation, Comintern work, and the role of the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) in this. I believe it was in 1931 or early 1932, after the publication of Hitler's book “My Struggle,” that ZINOVIEV wrote an article about fascism. He showed me this article, which was directive in character. In a conversation about why he was not sending it for publication, ZINOVIEV expressed the following thoughts: “The Party leadership cannot fail to see that fascism is moving toward power, but does nothing against it.

Developing his thought about the work of the Comintern, ZINOVIEV said: “For the sake of the idea of building socialism in one country, the interests of the world proletarian revolution and the work of the Comintern have been relegated to the background.”

I recall that in 1929, when KAMENEV's notes on negotiations with the Right (BUKHARIN and TOMSKY) became known to the Party, BOGDAN telephoned me and asked me to come and see him. At his apartment, BOGDAN told me that ZINOVIEV had instructed him to investigate how the document could have disappeared from KAMENEV's desk and that ZINOVIEV had asked me, KHARITONOV, and GESSEN to help BOGDAN sort out this matter. I gave BOGDAN no help in this matter and did not return to the question again.

Of my meetings with ZINOVIEV, I recalled the following: in 1929, BOGDAN telephoned me and said that ZINOVIEV was asking me to visit him at Tsentrosoyuz. I went there to see ZINOVIEV, but now I have absolutely no recollection of the matter about which he had summoned me. —

M. KHARITONOV.

INTERROGATED BY THE HEAD OF THE 6TH SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE USSR NKVD, KORKIN.

Certified true: Churbanova
