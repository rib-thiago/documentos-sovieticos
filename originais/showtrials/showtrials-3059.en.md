INTERROGATION RECORD

OF LEVIN Vladimir Solomonovich — 15 December 1934.

QUESTION: Tell us about your counterrevolutionary struggle directed against the party.

ANSWER: The former Zinovievists' statement, submitted after the Fifteenth Party Congress on the basis of the declaration of the 23 concerning complete organizational and ideological disarmament, in fact amounted only to curtailing factional work, ceasing large-scale publication of printed documents, and narrowing the grouping's membership; essentially, however, the cadres, including myself, continued organizational connections and the struggle against the party.

After the Congress the group divided into several currents of supporters of the declaration of the 23, who left themselves room for struggle against the party. After joining the party this group in Leningrad was headed by me, SHURYGIN Aleksei (died in 1933), and KOSTRITSKY Ivan.

The second current, the so-called Lefts, considered the declaration of the 23 shamefully capitulationist and refused to join the party on the Congress's terms; this group included all the former Zinovievist Komsomol officials headed in Leningrad by RUMYANTSEV Vladimir. In Moscow SAFAROV led this group, and after their exile one of its leaders was ZILBERMAN Aleksandr, who came to Leningrad on the group's business.

Besides Komsomol officials, this group in Leningrad included Lev SOSITSKY and Sergei MANDELSHTAM; owing to disagreements over the tactics of entering the party I knew nothing of this group's work at the time. The group adhering to the 23 existed in a semi-organized form until 1930, until the struggle against the Rights in the party began. Its principal work consisted in propagating the idea of all former opposition supporters joining the party, on the premise that only inside the party was struggle possible, as well as correcting its supposedly mistaken policy and bringing ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV into its leadership. The latter said: “We must crawl into the party and its leadership through the General Council supporters.” This was a catchphrase of KAMENEV's. Even the Zinovievists' technical equipment — hectographs, shapirographs and duplicators — was retained until 1930 partly by me, and mostly by SHURYGIN; only in 1930 was it all destroyed, burned and smashed. At that time I worked in Luga near Leningrad, and my meetings with my like-minded associates SHURYGIN, MYASNIKOV, KORSHUNOV, and sometimes KOSTRITSKY and PCHELKIN took place during my frequent trips to Leningrad, once a week. In 1930–1932, as the party struggle against the Rights intensified, our group's work increased considerably; reports reached us of negotiations between KAMENEV and BUKHARIN for a joint struggle against the party leadership. Our Leningrad group considered it more correct to offer the party active help against the Rights. The Muscovites who visited then — Artyom GERTIK and Sergei GESSEN — convinced us that no tactics were possible except those outlined by ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV. The Leningrad group did not pursue its disputes with its leadership and tacitly agreed with ZINOVIEV–KAMENEV's actions; individual members, including me, nevertheless participated in the party's struggle against the Rights, since I considered the Right danger then the principal one, and my dissatisfaction with the state of affairs in the party and its leadership a much smaller danger than a Right victory.

Hence also a certain falling-out with my associates. At the same time all the former Lefts gradually submitted statements, some together with SMILGA, others with SAFAROV and other leaders of these groups. A considerable number gathered in Leningrad; they kept apart from our group, apparently distrusting it. Nevertheless, individual meetings and conversations about our conduct in the party did occur, either at my apartment or RUMYANTSEV Vladimir's. During 1928–1931 I maintained no contact with Moscow, since I did not visit it; contact was maintained by occasional visits from the Muscovites GERTIK Artyom and GESSEN Sergei, or by trips of individuals from Leningrad — RUMYANTSEV Vladimir and MANDELSHTAM Sergei; I do not remember whether MYASNIKOV traveled to Moscow; there they met Artyom GERTIK. At the end of 1931 I made business trips to Moscow in connection with my work at LSPO; during 1931–1932 I visited Artyom GERTIK, GORSHENIN, and EVDOKIMOV at his hotel; Ivan BAKAEV visited me at my hotel once and Boris BRAVO twice. From the hotel we went to GORSHENIN's. Furthermore, in July or August 1932, during one of my Moscow trips, GERTIK invited me to accompany him to ZINOVIEV's dacha in Ilyinskoye; on arrival, over the table ZINOVIEV questioned me about work in Leningrad. I told him that by then the party organization's leadership had assembled a new body of activists, some of whom did not even know Leningrad's former leadership. I also told him individual facts about workers' lives, especially supply questions, the field in which I then worked and was informed: I informed ZINOVIEV that workers' supplies were worsening, rations shrinking, and the free market beyond workers' means.

ZINOVIEV briefly informed me of STEN's \[1\] visit; without speaking directly, he set out the position of the platform STEN had brought him, calling it an unprincipled and weak document. \[2\] Discussing individuals who could characterize the mood among party activists, ZINOVIEV also remarked that “even the furious SAFAROV is now seeking a rapprochement,” apparently hinting at the possibility of uniting all ZINOVIEV's supporters. After visiting ZINOVIEV I informed RUMYANTSEV Vladimir, SOSITSKY Lev and MYASNIKOV. Afterward I visited Moscow three more times. Once I visited GERTIK; BAKAEV Ivan, KOSTINA Anna, GERTIK's deceased wife and, I believe, Olga RAVICH were present. This was after ZINOVIEV's expulsion from the party for connections with the Ryutin–Slepkov group.

At this meeting it was said that former supporters of the Zinovievist opposition must speak at meetings in favor of ZINOVIEV's expulsion, since otherwise nobody would be able to remain in the party, and that the Ryutin group's platform was unacceptable.

By that time the more or less structured connections that had existed in Leningrad before 1931 no longer existed, and all conversations about the situation inside the party took place during chance meetings. This concerns supporters of the 23; whether the Left group had organized connections I do not know; in any event, I informed RUMYANTSEV Vladimir of this meeting at GERTIK's.

I last visited GERTIK on 27 November 1932 while passing through on my way to Sochi, but did not discuss anything with him. In Sochi I learned from Sergei GESSEN of ZINOVIEV's exile, GERTIK's arrest and his wife's death. In Sochi I met ZALUTSKY Pyotr, GESSEN and Olga RAVICH; with the latter I did not discuss ZINOVIEV's exile.

ZALUTSKY condemned ZINOVIEV in conversation. GESSEN, on the one hand, condemned ZINOVIEV and, on the other, disapproved of the Central Committee's decision to expel him; I inclined toward ZALUTSKY's view. In 1932 GERTIK visited my apartment once or twice. On one occasion BASHKIROV and MYASNIKOV were with me; during GERTIK's second visit PERIMOV, BASHKIROV and MYASNIKOV were present; whether TSVIBAK and KORSHUNOV were there I do not now remember. At the meeting GERTIK informed us of ZINOVIEV's sentiments, which amounted to this: discontent was growing in the party, and even those who had previously broken with ZINOVIEV were now trying to restore connections for a struggle against the party (SAFAROV); in confirmation he also cited STEN, who had visited ZINOVIEV to establish contact — connections for the struggle against the party. GERTIK questioned us about the Zinovievists in Leningrad, and those present informed him about them. I remember the names KOSTRITSKY, MANDELSHTAM, SOSITSKY, RUMYANTSEV V. and KOTOLYNOV Ivan being mentioned; I do not remember the others named to GERTIK. That same year KUKLIN Aleksandr Sergeyevich visited my apartment; MYASNIKOV, BASHKIROV and, I believe, KORSHUNOV were present. After SHURYGIN's funeral in Leningrad, SOSITSKY invited me to his home; MANDELSHTAM and RUMYANTSEV were also invited; I do not remember whether MYASNIKOV was there, but I remember we shook off PCHELKIN on the way. At SOSITSKY's I informed those present of my meetings with ZINOVIEV and Sergei GESSEN.

Correctly recorded from my words and read to me.

LEVIN.

HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO, GUGB — PETROVSKY
