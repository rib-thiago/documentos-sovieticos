INTERROGATION RECORD

conducted by the section head of the SPO of the GUGB of the NKVD — RUTKOVSKY

on December 17, 1934.

KAMENEV, Lev Borisovich, born in 1883, a native of Moscow; his father was an engineer; Moscow, 3 Karmanitsky Lane, apartment 5; director of Pushkin House, IRLI. Wife: Tatyana Ivanovna GLEBOVA, aged 39; children: sons Aleksandr Lvovich KAMENEV, aged 27, chief engineer of the GUGTsF; Yuri, aged 12, a student; Vladimir, aged 5. Owned no real estate. Incomplete higher education. A member of the VKP(b) since 1901. A break from November 1932 to April 1933. Revolutionary work from 1901 in Moscow, Tiflis and then abroad in emigration. From 1904 again in Moscow, subsequently arrested. Was arrested in Leningrad in 1909 and exiled. Again abroad until 1914. In that same year, 1914, returned to Leningrad, was arrested and exiled to the Turukhansk territory. Released by the February Revolution.

Question: The investigation has information that you headed a counterrevolutionary anti-Party organization composed of members of the former Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc. What can you testify concerning this case?

Answer: I deny not only having led any counterrevolutionary anti-Party organization, but also belonging to one, as well as being aware of the existence of such organizations. From the time I left for Minusinsk (November 1932), I maintained no connections with any former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc and saw none of them except for a few chance encounters. Of these I recall meeting EVDOKIMOV at the dacha last year or this year, without discussing political matters with him. I once saw ZALUTSKY on a tram but did not speak with him.

After I returned from Minusinsk, in April 1933 someone conveyed to me (I do not remember exactly who) that EVDOKIMOV and BAKAEV would like to see me. I declined this proposal. On my next-to-last visit to Leningrad, approximately at the beginning of summer 1934, I happened to meet Moisei YAKOVLEV in the Academy of Sciences dining room; he asked to speak with me privately after lunch about Academy matters. I refused, remembering that YAKOVLEV had previously belonged to an anti-Party organization. I also refused ZINOVIEV's proposal that I intercede for Artyom GERTIK, who was in exile after my return from Minusinsk.

During this period I saw Olga RAVICH a couple of times when she came from Voronezh to Moscow on business. I had no conversations on political subjects with her.

At my workplace several people who had previously belonged to anti-Party organizations — VAGANYAN \[1\] and LELEVICH — approached me asking for work. In both cases I asked comrade STETSKY's permission.

This conduct on my part is explained by my entirely conscious and firm decision to break with former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc and earn the Party's trust.

Question: You have informed the investigation of facts about your conduct and sentiments in 1933–1934. Were you a participant in the organization about which the investigation is asking before 1933?

Answer: In 1932 I was already seeking to withdraw not only from political but also from personal connections with former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc, and I can confidently say that I did not attend meetings that decided any political questions. I formed the impression that, at that time, former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc considered it necessary to obtain information on all current questions of Party policy from ZINOVIEV rather than from me.

Question: Have you maintained political and personal connections with ZINOVIEV up to the present?

Answer: From the time I was held responsible over the Ryutin platform — although I had no connection with any participant in RYUTIN's organization, having received the platform from ZINOVIEV to read — my relations with ZINOVIEV cooled considerably. However, a number of everyday circumstances (a shared dacha) prevented me from finally severing my connection with him. I consider it necessary to note that, while living in the same dacha in the summer of 1934, we led entirely separate lives and rarely met. Different people visited us and we spent our time separately. EVDOKIMOV and, I think, KUKLIN, who visited him at the dacha, were his guests, not mine. Nevertheless, finding this situation unacceptable for myself, at the first opportunity I began building a dacha for myself on another railway line.

Even during our joint struggle against the Party I never considered ZINOVIEV capable of leading the Party, and recent years have confirmed my conviction that he possesses none of the qualities of a leader.

Question: What do you know personally, or from what others have told you, about the existence in Leningrad of a counterrevolutionary organization composed of former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc?

Answer: I know nothing about the existence of such an organization.

The record has been read. Recorded correctly from my words.

L. B. KAMENEV

Interrogated by: RUTKOVSKY
