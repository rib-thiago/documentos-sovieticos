INTERROGATION RECORD

OF DRYAZGOV Grigory Mikhailovich,

January 2, 1935.

Question: On December 22, you testified about the Zinovievists' double-dealing return to the party. In testifying about RUMYANTSEV's and KOTOLYNOV's group, you stated directly that “they entered the party in accordance with Zinoviev's orientation in order to preserve the Zinovievist cadres from possible destruction.” Tell us, for what purpose were these cadres preserved?

Answer: The conversations I cited in my testimony took place at T. DMITRIYEV's apartment shortly after the Fifteenth Congress. A.S. KUKLIN was also at that meeting. I then left the meeting, stating that I could not deceive the party. I heard no further conversations on this question. I assume that preserving the Zinovievist cadres was necessary for the struggle against the party.

Question: Do you acknowledge that by maintaining connections with Zinovievists and being present at anti-party conversations (such as LUKIN's conversation about which you testified), you thereby participated in carrying out ZINOVIEV's directives for preserving the Zinovievist cadres?

Answer: I had only professional or personal comradely relations with former Zinovievists. I had no political connections with them. I am guilty only of failing to report immediately the anti-party sentiments expressed by LUKIN on the eve of our departure for the provinces in 1930. Later, in Kuznetsk, when LUKIN left there without permission, I and TARASOV P. told the Party Committee secretary, Comrade KULAKOV, about LUKIN's sentiments.

Question: Do you know ZVEZDOV?

Answer: Yes. I have not seen him for a long time.

Question: TOLMAZOV, KHANIK and ZVEZDOV name you as a member of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization. Do you continue to deny this?

Answer: I deny it; I did not belong to the counterrevolutionary organization.

Question: The investigation knows that when compiling books on the youth movement you deliberately distorted the VKP(b)'s role in the youth movement. Do you confirm this?

Answer: No, I do not. I acknowledge that my books “On the Road to the Komsomol,” published in 1925, and “Notes of a Komsomol Member,” published in 1930, do indeed contain a number of distortions and anti-party propositions, but they were committed because of my insufficient education at the time. I gave my books to more knowledgeable comrades for preliminary editing.

DRYAZGOV.

INTERROGATED BY KORKIN.

Certified correct: Churbanova
