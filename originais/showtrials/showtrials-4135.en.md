INTERROGATION RECORD

On January 6, 1935, I, GENDIN, assistant head of the OO, GUGB, NKVD, interrogated on this date as a witness

ANIKIN, Dmitry Alexandrovich, born in 1896, native of the village of Plakhino, former Ryazan Province, Mikhailov District, Plakhino Volost, member of the AUCP(b) since 1919, head of the political department of the Udokh MTS, Porkhov District, Leningrad Region, married, residing in Leningrad at 30 Dzerzhinsky Street, apartment 15,

who answered the questions put to him as follows:

Question: How long have you known BOGRACHEV, and what do you know about his anti-Party, counterrevolutionary activity?

Answer: I have known Solomon Naumovich BOGRACHEV since late 1926, when I was appointed secretary of the Party collective at the Volodarsky garment factory. At that time BOGRACHEV was head of the garment industry association “Leningradodezhda” and was attached, through the Party, to the collective at the Volodarsky factory. We therefore had to be in constant contact through Party work.

As a supporter of the Trotskyist-Zinovievist opposition bloc, BOGRACHEV conducted active organizational work to weld together opposition cadres in the garment industry. He personally rarely spoke at open meetings, but was very actively engaged in individually influencing particular Party members in an opposition spirit.

When I categorically demanded that BOGRACHEV make an open and clear statement at a meeting about his political views, he could think of nothing better than to detach himself from our collective and transfer permanently to the collective of the “Leningradodezhda” association.

From conversations with a number of comrades and personally with BOGRACHEV, I know that he was in the closest contact with ZINOVIEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, GERTIK, and other opposition leaders.

As an active oppositionist, BOGRACHEV skillfully placed “his own” people in the garment industry.

For example, he appointed AGRANOVSKY, a member of the AUCP(b), director of the Volodarsky factory; although the latter did not officially join the opposition, in practice he was a most typical double-dealer, a concealed enemy of the Party, BOGRACHEV's closest like-minded associate and collaborator in fighting the Party.

The following fact is highly characteristic for determining AGRANOVSKY's true political character:

during the anniversary session of the USSR CEC in Leningrad in 1927, I happened to enter AGRANOVSKY's office, where I found his deputy, KUBLANOV, also a member of the AUCP(b) who had not formally joined the opposition. They were discussing the fact that TROTSKY, who had arrived in Leningrad for the anniversary session, was organizing some meeting to which both had been invited. Asked by me whether they intended to attend this meeting, AGRANOVSKY and KUBLANOV answered affirmatively. I expressed bewilderment that both, being supporters of the opposition, nevertheless intended to go to a meeting organized by Trotskyists, and declared that in that case I would go to this meeting with them.

AGRANOVSKY categorically opposed my intention, declaring that in that case the meeting would not take place. During the ensuing argument, AGRANOVSKY and KUBLANOV declared that they would not go to the Trotskyists' meeting.

The next day it emerged that despite the assurance AGRANOVSKY and KUBLANOV had given me, as secretary of the collective, that they would not go to the Trotskyists' meeting, both had attended the meeting organized by TROTSKY.

I raised the question of AGRANOVSKY's and KUBLANOV's conduct at the bureau of the city district Party committee, to which both were summoned for an examination of their conduct.

At the district committee bureau, AGRANOVSKY and KUBLANOV behaved like true double-dealers: they confirmed that they had attended the Trotskyists' meeting, but, pleading forgetfulness, refused to name the place where it had taken place and did not name the person who had conveyed the invitation to them.

Somewhat later I learned that AGRANOVSKY and KUBLANOV had been taken to the Trotskyists' meeting by the superintendent of the Volodarsky factory, TOMSON, a concealed Trotskyist whose relationship to the opposition we learned of only as a result of a statement he himself submitted to the factory's Party organization.

Until the very latest time, BOGRACHEV has remained an irreconcilable Zinovievist who has not ceased fighting the Party but merely changed the tactics and methods of struggle, adapting himself to the new conditions.

BOGRACHEV's attitudes were most clearly expressed in the following incident:

Once, in late December 1930 or early January 1931, I found BOGRACHEV in the office of the director of the Volodarsky factory. Present were BOGRACHEV; AGRANOVSKY, by then already head of “Leningradodezhda”; KUBLANOV, director of the Volodarsky factory; and SHEININ, head of that factory's administrative and economic department. The conversation revolved around the removal of KAMENEV and ZINOVIEV from the Party leadership.

BOGRACHEV declared that the Party was making an enormous mistake in cutting the opposition leaders off from the leadership, since both KAMENEV and ZINOVIEV were supposedly the “best Leninists,” who had built our Party from its very inception and led it to victory. “In place of these people the Party is bringing into the leadership persons of little authority and unprepared for the further struggle.” In doing so, BOGRACHEV spoke slanderously and insultingly about Comrades KALININ and VOROSHILOV.

To my explanations of KAMENEV's and ZINOVIEV's true political character, BOGRACHEV replied literally as follows: “Today we have no differences with the Party, since the present Party leadership has fully adopted the opposition's platform on the pace of industrialization and the crushing of the kulaks.”

AGRANOVSKY and KUBLANOV tried to soften somewhat the impression made by BOGRACHEV's open statement, without in any way opposing BOGRACHEV, but merely clothing his expressions in a somewhat vague form.

Question: What do you know about BOGRACHEV's, AGRANOVSKY's, and KUBLANOV's relationship with the Zinovievist organization recently?

Answer: From KUBLANOV's words I know that over the past few years EVDOKIMOV and SHAROV visited Leningrad from Moscow, constantly meeting BOGRACHEV, AGRANOVSKY, and KUBLANOV.

Question: What do you know about the circumstances of BOGRACHEV's formal withdrawal from the former Zinovievist opposition?

Answer: Above all, it always surprised me that BOGRACHEV, despite being an active Zinovievist, had received absolutely no penalties for his factional and anti-Party activity. I tried several times to establish where and when BOGRACHEV had declared his withdrawal from the opposition.

From individual conversations one could judge that BOGRACHEV had made a formal statement of withdrawal from the opposition to Comrade KOMAROV. I do not know how far this corresponds to reality.

I know that BOGRACHEV always enjoyed strong support from KOMAROV.

I assert once again that BOGRACHEV remains to this day as convinced a Zinovievist as I knew him to be before.

Recorded correctly from my words and read by me.

ANIKIN.

INTERROGATED BY:

ASSISTANT HEAD, OO, GUGB, NKVD — GENDIN.

Certified correct: Churbanova
