INTERROGATION RECORD

of FADEEV, Fedor Fadeevich,

dated December 18, 1934.

QUESTION: What does the Zinovievist underground in Leningrad currently represent?

ANSWER: The Zinovievist underground has survived in Leningrad to the present. All Zinovievists living in Leningrad maintain contact with one another and constitute a formally organized counterrevolutionary organization.

QUESTION: Who personally belonged to the Zinovievists' counterrevolutionary organization, and who actually directed its leadership?

ANSWER: The counterrevolutionary organization consisted chiefly of old Zinovievist Komsomol members, namely: Vladimir RUMYANTSEV, KOTOLYNOV, SHATSKY, ANTONOV, ZVEZDOV, myself (FADEEV), TOLMAZOV, POTASHNIKOV, VINOGRADOV, SEVEROV and Georgy POPOV. Leadership of the organization was exercised by RUMYANTSEV, who was closest to the leaders of the Zinovievist opposition, EVDOKIMOV and others.

QUESTION: Describe the organizational forms of the counterrevolutionary organization to which you belonged.

ANSWER: The counterrevolutionary organization to which I belonged has its Center in Moscow, which directs all the organization's work. The leading Center includes EVDOKIMOV, MINAEV, Ivan TARASOV, BAKAEV and Lyonya FAIVILOVICH. I personally met none of the Center's members. Connections between those belonging to the Leningrad organization were arranged so that, for reasons of secrecy, we could all gather only very rarely. Thus I could meet TOLMAZOV, ANTONOV and, less frequently, RUMYANTSEV and other members of the organization. Contact with the Center went chiefly through RUMYANTSEV and TOLMAZOV, who frequently traveled to Moscow. The “Association of Former Comrades-in-Arms”, created at the Leningrad Istmol, was used as one of the legal opportunities for our counterrevolutionary activities. The “Association of Former Comrades-in-Arms” chiefly included active participants in the Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc who were associated with our counterrevolutionary organization, and this circumstance led to the formation at Istmol of a group of our counterrevolutionary organization, headed by KHANIK.

QUESTION: Where did you obtain the revolver found during the search, for which there was no permit?

ANSWER: This revolver was out of order and had remained with me since the Civil War. I repeatedly intended to surrender it but feared being held responsible, since I had another revolver for which there was a permit.

Recorded correctly and read by me, in confirmation of which I sign.

FADEEV.

INTERROGATED BY:

Operational officer of the 1st Section of the SPO — MAKAROV.
