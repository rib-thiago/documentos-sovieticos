RECORD OF SUPPLEMENTARY INTERROGATION

conducted by the Deputy Head of the SPO, UGB — Comrade STROMIN,

in the case against V. I. ZVEZDOV,

dated 11 December 1934.

QUESTION: What do you know about the organisational formation of your former Zinovievist group of young people from Vyborg District?

ANSWER: The process of forming our group began long ago. At first, with mutual support, all former participants sought to get to Leningrad, where their connections, gradually being restored, continued to exist unchanged as before. We all knew how and where each person worked and how their personal and Party affairs were being arranged.

Our tactic then was to gather our forces, necessarily (be reinstated) enter the Party, but at the same time, as far as possible, avoid conspicuous public and Party work under various pretexts. It was important somehow to remain in the shadows, awaiting better times.

When we met, we discussed the situation that had arisen and spoke of the atmosphere of constant distrust surrounding us, created by local Party organisations.

Our group's organisational formation occurred gradually, with Vladimir RUMYANTSEV and Ivan KOTOLYNOV acting as the principal organisers; they in turn were connected with the organisation's Moscow centre, about whose composition I have already testified at my previous interrogation.

Our group did not adhere to strict organisational forms but adapted to local conditions, using every legal opportunity for this purpose.

At the same time, however, to facilitate contact between members, there were individual quasi-subgroups at the Industrial Institute, in the Leningrad Komsomol “Combat Veterans' Association”, and elsewhere.

Our meetings (of group members) took place in various places and were almost never regular. We rarely met in apartments, and even then every meeting was disguised by family pretexts. I personally attended two such meetings, at my apartment and at Pyotr SEVEROV's apartment. On both occasions those present were VINOGRADOV, ANTONOV, FADEEV, NADEL, myself — ZVEZDOV — and SEVEROV. There were also gatherings at the home of MIRONOV, director of a bread factory (Moscow District), who, incidentally, was one of the most active leaders of the Zinovievist opposition in Leningrad.

Our group of so-called young people always enjoyed a measure of moral and material support from other Party members who had formerly adhered to the opposition. Among others, BRODSKY, director of the “Dvigatel” factory, supported us, employing ANTONOV and ALADEINIKOV at his factory. BRODSKY had in his day been a prominent Zinovievist and instructor of the Leningrad Provincial VKP(b) Committee.

In terms of our group's practical activity, the most significant occasion was the attempt to use the VLKSM's fifteenth anniversary, when a number of commissions were set up to conduct the celebrations. These commissions, both at the VLKSM Regional Committee and the district committees, gave our group's members broad opportunities to enhance the role and importance of former Komsomol members who had participated in the opposition.

In connection with the anniversary, many of us managed to be considerably active, namely to write and publish a number of articles and collections on VLKSM history which in substance blurred the entire activity and anti-Party essence of the Zinovievist opposition. In addition, we managed to bring the Komsomol “Combat Veterans' Association” under our influence, with members of our group — Lev KHANIK, Egor POPOV, ZHESTYANNIKOV and Raisa VASILIEVA — joining its board.

QUESTION: What was the nature of your group's members' meetings, and what specifically did you discuss?

ANSWER: Our meetings were organisational in character. We exchanged views on current political questions and informed one another about events, transfers or changes in the situation of individual oppositionists, and the lives of ZINOVIEV and other leaders of our movement, who, according to our information, were suffering all manner of hardships and persecution. We also discussed questions of the general policy pursued by the VKP(b) Central Committee in agriculture and industry. We believed that the abolition of collective leadership and of the Central Control Commission contradicted the principles put forward by LENIN in his theses on building a classless society, and therefore criticised these Party measures.

We also discussed the difficulties experienced by the Soviet Union's population and explained them by saying that there were no longer any major figures in the Party leadership. In general, all our statements criticised the Party's measures and policy, as well as the current Party leadership.

QUESTION: What do you know of your group's ties with the organisation's Moscow Centre?

ANSWER: There was contact with the Moscow Centre, maintained by our group's leaders, Vladimir RUMYANTSEV and Ivan KOTOLYNOV, who were connected with ZINOVIEV and BAKAEV.

In addition, representatives of the centre came from Moscow to Leningrad at various times, bringing information about our leadership's mood and its latest instructions. I also received all this information from ANTONOV.

QUESTION: Who specifically belonged to your group?

ANSWER: Our group's members were: Vladimir RUMYANTSEV, Ivan KOTOLYNOV, Lev KHANIK, Nikolai TSARKOV, Vasily VINOGRADOV, Mikhail NADEL, Nikolai ANTONOV, Egor POPOV, Mikhail MURAVIEV, Grigory POTASHNIKOV, Konstantin KOZIN, Fedor PAIO, Fedor FADEEV, Andrei TOLMAZOV, myself — V. I. ZVEZDOV — and others whose surnames I do not remember.

QUESTION: What was Leonid NIKOLAEV's relationship to your group?

ANSWER: He was connected with us for a long time, beginning in 1924. For a period he worked under KOTOLYNOV's immediate direction, when the latter was secretary of the Vyborg District VLKSM Committee. NIKOLAEV was brought up by us, nourished on our group's ideological sap, and undoubtedly absorbed the whole experience of our factional struggle against the Party and its leadership.

It should also be said of NIKOLAEV that he belongs to the category of withdrawn people who are nevertheless ready for decisive action.

I last saw NIKOLAEV in the spring of 1934. As I have already testified, we met at the House of Party Activists, where he told me of his difficulties arising from his exclusion from the Communist Academy, which had personally hurt him deeply.

NIKOLAEV was then very agitated, and one sensed that he was seeking an outlet for his sentiments hostile to the Party.

The interrogation record has been correctly written from my words and read by me, in confirmation of which I sign.

ZVEZDOV.

INTERROGATED BY:

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO — STROMIN.
