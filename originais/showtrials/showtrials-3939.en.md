INTERROGATION RECORD

conducted by the Head of the 4th Section of the SPO, KOGAN L.,

31 December 1934.

POZDEEVA Maria Vasilyevna.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that while in Germany you were connected with local groups in the German Communist Party fighting against the Comintern line. What can you testify about this?

ANSWER: Before I left for abroad in November 1927, SAFAROV assigned me to establish contact in Berlin with the Wedding opposition \[1\], intending to use it as our mouthpiece in the event of the Zinovievists' failure here. This put me in a difficult position, since I knew nothing of the international situation, particularly Germany's. The bloc with the Trotskyists still existed then, and they worked vigorously abroad. In Berlin I met Pyotr PEREVERZEV, a Trotskyist working in the Trade Representation. He conducted extensive work among anti-party groups in the German Communist Party.

The confusion among Russian Zinovievists before the Fifteenth Congress, disagreements and subsequent division into groups and little groups inclined me personally more toward SAFAROV, NAUMOV, SARKIS and VUJOVIC, who were with neither ZINOVIEV nor TROTSKY. SAFAROV personally inclined more toward the Trotskyists. In this impasse I found myself abroad, not knowing where to go, quarreling with the Trotskyists (PEREVERZEV) and with the Zinovievist, my husband GERTSBERG. Under these conditions PEREVERZEV introduced me to two workers from the Wedding opposition: Max BADDING and Peter (whose surname I do not know). Peter, as I subsequently learned, had participated in the 1923 uprising, been sentenced to fortress imprisonment, but escaped and was hiding in Berlin. BADDING was a metalworker. I informed them about events in the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) at the time. I explained why one could not follow TROTSKY and why our group had split from ZINOVIEV, but with whom to go and what to do next I did not know myself and could not tell them. PEREVERZEV was furious with me, often mentioning SAFAROV and saying he favored following the Trotskyists (where he obtained this information I do not know). I impressed them, the Wedding people, as a bewildered person. That was essentially the case.

PEREVERZEV was quickly removed from Berlin and my contacts broke off.

After the Fifteenth Congress, left alone, I sought the path to take and decided the only path was back to the party. The White Guards' guffaws over the split within the party helped me in this. I saw opposition groups in the German party forming the Leninbund, another party fighting against the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) and the Comintern. The Fifteenth Congress and Central Committee decisions and post-Congress Pravda editorials, however, built a broad bridge back into the party. I then wrote to SAFAROV in Aktyubinsk that a new party, the Leninbund, had formed in Germany, and that party decisions on the peasant question, self-criticism and other matters were a bridge into the party.

SAFAROV's first replies to these sentiments of mine said: “Madam, you are surrendering your positions quickly.” Their tone then softened, and finally SAFAROV submitted an application to the party, apparently in mid-1928.

I wrote one or two letters about my feeling that we must return to the party to SARKIS and his wife BORYAN in Minusinsk, when I learned from SAFAROV's letters that they were there. Incidentally, when SARKIS and BORYAN submitted applications, which I think was in April 1927, SAFAROV “worked over” SARKIS. Evidently SAFAROV was not yet “ripe” for returning to the party, for ideological disarmament.

I wrote from Germany to VUJOVIC and his wife BUDZINSKAYA in Arkhangelsk, who had sent my husband and me a letter requesting German newspapers. I wrote that the opposition here had organized a second party, the Leninbund, with which we could not go along.

I later encountered BADDING and Peter again in Wedding at a Rotfrontbund meeting. Afterward they visited us; my husband GERTSBERG and I constantly discussed returning to the party, and they returned. I know Peter subsequently worked in the Red Metalworkers' Union in Reinickendorf, while BADDING has worked within the Comintern sphere since 1929 or 1930.

Twice, together with Comrade EMEL \[2\] (who worked in the German Communist Party Central Committee), I met MASLOW and Ruth FISCHER (EMEL had worked with them when they led the Central Committee). From EMEL's conversation with them I understood that they drifted about as private individuals; FISCHER worked somewhere in social welfare. They joined neither the Leninbund nor the party. Both impressed me as people with little inclination to return to the party.

MASLOW told EMEL he had visited the Soviet embassy to exchange his passport. I was surprised he had a Soviet passport and asked why he did not go to the Soviet Union, where he would be given work. He answered that if they left Germany, they would not be allowed to return. This meeting was after 1929. The second was in Potsdam. From EMEL's conversation with us I then understood that they intended to submit applications to the Central Committee and that REMMELE had promised to speak to THÄLMANN about it.

M. POZDEEVA.

INTERROGATED BY — KOGAN L.

Certified correct: A. Svetlova

RGASPI, fond 671, inventory 1, file 126, folios 160–163.

\[1\] The Left opposition in the German Communist Party, named after a district of Berlin.

\[2\] Also known as Moisei Ilyich Lurie, one of the defendants at the Moscow public trial in August 1936.
