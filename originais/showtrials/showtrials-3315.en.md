INTERROGATION RECORD

of FAIVILOVICH, Leonid Yakovlevich — December 18, 1934.

QUESTION: Who wrote the letter seized from you, signed “Isak” (the letter is presented), to what period does it belong, and what can you say about its contents, particularly such passages as: “Grig. spoke at two factories and two institutes. He was welcomed and seen off very warmly... They telephone him and invite him from all sides... From all this nonsense only one thing is clear — some alarm at the workers' sympathies revealed during Gr.'s speech... They are applying countersteam.”

ANSWER: The author of the letter is Isak Isaevich REINGOLD, currently working as head of the Central Cotton Administration of the USSR NKZ. I think this letter dates to the middle of 1929; I cannot give a more precise date.

This letter shows that both I and I. I. REINGOLD had not overcome our disagreements with the Party upon joining it, and consequently indicates an insincere entry into the Party. The part of the letter speaking of workers' sympathies in connection with ZINOVIEV's speeches and of “the Central Committee's alarm” over this slanders the Central Committee and the Party leaders, and incorrectly reflects the workers' mood. I showed this letter to no one, but did not bring it to the attention of the Party authorities. I do not know for what purpose REINGOLD conveyed all this political information to me. Sending me this letter constitutes the maintenance of an organizational connection between REINGOLD and myself.

FAIVILOVICH.

INTERROGATED BY: head of the 6th Section of the SPO of the GUGB — KORKIN.

DEAR LYOSHA!

The most interesting thing recently is the publication of the stenographic record of the meeting of the Politburo and the Central Control Commission Presidium, setting out the entire “trial session” of the Rightists.

It seems to be the stormiest meeting of all those held in recent years. The exceptionally oppressive atmosphere prevailing at the meeting is striking. It is impossible to convey everything that happened there. But you must read the stenographic record at Is. Abr.'s. Grigory spoke at two factories and two institutes. He was welcomed and seen off very warmly. At one factory he was elected a member of the Moscow Soviet, and at the other to the meeting's honorary presidium (together with the entire PB membership).

They telephone him and invite him from all sides.

L. V. is allowed nowhere. It is perfectly obvious that this is being done in connection with his diary.

The other day KRINITSKY gave a report on the results of the Provincial Party Conference. He “worked over” Grigory a little. He quoted a speech by one of the conference delegates saying that Grigory sees only industrialization and collectivization in the countryside, but fails to notice individual farming; I do not know which article by Grigory this was taken from; apparently from an unpublished article by Grigory (“On the Kulak Front”). Krinitsky called this “semi-Trotskyism”. In answering written questions he said the following:

1\. Why is Grigory being “pickled” at “Tsentrosoyuz”? He is not being “pickled”, because he is not in the pickling department but in the cultural department (an elephant's jokes).

2\. Did I like Grigory Zinoviev's report at your Institute?

You should know better.

3\. Why is Zinoviev not being promoted? He muddled a great deal; he can sit a while longer.

4\. Will he be elected to the Central Committee? At the Seventeenth Congress, perhaps. We have no lifelong oppositionists.

Not at this congress. But anyway, we shall see.

From all this nonsense only one thing is clear — some alarm at the workers' sympathies revealed during Grigory's speech.

Down with countersteam.

Of course Krinitsky is too small a figure for his words to be given any decisive significance. The congress is still far off, and much will change.

Such are the important political news items.

To this one must add that, in my impression, at the forthcoming plenum the boss will attempt to keep the Rightists in the PB. Of course this is only a stage, since these people's fate is decided.

I intend to come to you soon. A telegram has arrived from Zel., to which Gleb has not yet replied. He intends to talk with me. He will probably keep me here until the end of the five-year plan, that is, until the beginning of April.

There is a fair amount of trouble here with the local sectional scoundrels. The backlog of business is considerable too. After all, before my arrival not a single STO resolution was being implemented here.

The congress of planning bodies is proceeding rather colorlessly; there are no debates of principle, just rubbish.

I bear a grudge against August over Davis. He acted in a provincial manner and does not understand that the main task is to bring Davis into the analysis of the large schemes and projects in Gosplan's portfolio, not to have him deal with every little detail and scatter his attention and energies.

At the meeting of the DEPUTIES and the CHAIRMAN of the KK, I secured the creation of the State Design Institute and, in principle, the State Hydrotechnical Institute. The idea of creating unified water management is finding great sympathy.

Sooner or later things are heading toward an NKTU for water management. I shall soon send ALEKSANDROV to you to agree on major projects to be transferred to the State Design Institute (Giprovod).

Please do not display localism. Otherwise we shall wreck the five-year plan. Just think that, according to a report from EKSTESS, of the twenty-four projects submitted to him, only three have been approved.

What has happened at your end with Ravat-Khadzha?

Send someone here quickly with a report. Everything agreed with August has been carried through by us and approved by the STO.

The other day we pushed through the appropriations for Chirchik-Angren.

You are doing well with small-scale construction in Kirghizia and Turkmenistan. What is being done in Uzbekistan and Tajikistan is unclear to me.

Please send the material.

I shake your hand and kiss you and Liza.

Yours, Isaak.

Warm greetings to Tur and Artyom.

I hope we shall see each other soon.

Greetings from Zhenechka.
