STATEMENT BY G. E. ZINOVIEV

## STATEMENT

I see from the newspapers that abroad, forces hostile to the Soviet Union are using the trial against myself and my former associates to attack the Soviet government and the AUCP(b).

Part of the bourgeois press, the fascist press, some Social Democratic leaders, Mr. Trotsky — all of them come out in my “defense,” in “defense” of the other defendants tried in our case. This is in the order of things. The very fact of my being “defended” by the camp mentioned is already a fully deserved additional disgrace for me. That the enemies of the USSR cannot fail to use such an occasion to try once again to slander Soviet power — that too is in the order of things.

I not only consider it my duty to state what I shall say below, but would positively experience great relief if I could hope that this statement would reach the workers of those countries where the aforementioned slanderers operate.

And what I wish to state is the following:

Every word of the verdict of the Military Collegium of the USSR Supreme Court in our case is confirmed by dozens of verified facts. The writer of these lines, as the principal accused in this case, and equally, as I saw, all the other accused — without a single exception — fully admitted their guilt in court and considered the sentence passed entirely and absolutely deserved. I sincerely repented before the court of the working class. I shall submissively bear the punishment imposed on me by the proletarian court and shall console myself with the hope that, since the proletarian court spared my life, it therefore admits the possibility of my reform, the possibility that someday I might still render some service to the Soviet motherland and the working class. My repentance is utterly complete and boundless. The murder of S. M. KIROV brought such an outcome to a decade of anti-party struggle that had grown into counterrevolutionary struggle that one could not fail to be horrified by this outcome. The martyr's blood of the heroic fighter and builder of socialism S. M. KIROV has bound the entire Soviet Union still more closely together, and a handful of renegades who had descended into counterrevolution, on the very brink of the abyss, understood their full disgrace. In particular I, as one of the accused, as the principal accused, could not fail to acknowledge the full criminality of my actions, could not fail to acknowledge the great truth of the Party, its Central Committee, and its brilliant leader STALIN.

In a few words, the decade of my anti-party struggle amounted to the following: first, “merely” forming a faction on an anti-Leninist platform; then a bloc with counterrevolutionary Trotskyism; then the anti-party, in fact counterrevolutionary, demonstration of November 7, 1927; then verbal “submission” to the party at the Fifteenth Congress and deception of it in practice, double-dealing; then ever greater alienation from the working class, the people, socialist construction; then an actual slide into the swamp of counterrevolution and everything that brought myself and others to the dock. One thing drew another after it. This chain was inevitable. A more shameful path for people once connected with the proletarian revolution is impossible even to imagine. This is not accidental but follows a pattern: the bourgeoisie, some Social Democrats, and Mr. Trotsky must now attempt to “show solidarity” with us. This, I repeat, is the disgrace we have deserved. The working class of the entire world will draw only one conclusion from this solidarity: it will see in it one further proof that the former “Zinovievist” group had landed in the swamp of counterrevolution.

I further see from the newspapers that the same “defenders” — in particular the General Committee of the Paris District Union of reformist trade unions — champion the executed White Guard terrorists and shout about the shooting of “more than 100 workers(?!), neither whose complicity in the assassination attempt nor whose connection with the White Guards has been established.” This intervention exposes these “defenders'” intentions still more fully. It is a campaign of lies and slander consciously conducted by the bourgeoisie. This campaign by contemptible slanderers will receive its proper assessment from the workers.

If the workers of other countries wish to pay any attention to what has happened to me, they must draw only one conclusion: the proletarian court condemned me correctly and impartially. Lenin's thesis on the inevitability of the opposition's transition into the camp of counterrevolution, on its transformation into a center attracting all counterrevolutionary forces — this thesis has been confirmed by my example with a clarity never before seen. The bourgeoisie's attacks on the Soviet Union in connection with the trial of the “Zinovievists” merely testify once again to the unattainable moral height on which the Soviet Union stands.

For my part I can say only one thing: on my knees I implore the working class for forgiveness. As long as I live, I shall experience the torments of repentance. My only consolation will be that, despite first my mistakes and then my crimes, the cause of the world proletariat, the cause of Lenin–Stalin, marches proudly and irresistibly onward and onward.

G. Zinoviev

January 21, 1935.
