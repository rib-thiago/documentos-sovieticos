INTERROGATION RECORD

OF KULISHER R. M.

15 December 1934.

QUESTION: What were your relations with Leonid NIKOLAEV over the past year?

ANSWER: From the end of 1933 Leonid NIKOLAEV's personal attitude toward me became closer than before; he began speaking to me more often about political and personal subjects, and my influence over him increased. I saw in this his desire to find an opportunity and basis for sharing with me the questions troubling him.

QUESTION: On that basis how did you assess Leonid NIKOLAEV's political sentiments and views, and what do you know about them from him?

ANSWER: Leonid NIKOLAEV expressed his political views in several conversations with me, and they generally characterized a gradual, rapid departure from the party and transition into the camp of the party's enemies, noticeably beginning for me in June 1934.

The first significant conversation in this respect occurred in mid-June concerning Leonid NIKOLAEV's planned assignment to transport work outside the major centers. Although unemployed then, he declared with obvious irritation that he would not go to transport work because he was worn out by work in the provinces. When I asked how he could refuse to go if mobilized, he answered in a way that made clear to me that, unlike his former views, personal considerations now prevailed over party interests. He expressed exactly the same sentiments to me repeatedly afterward.

The next point revealing the political crystallization of the anti-party sentiments I had noticed in Leonid NIKOLAEV was his assessment of events in Germany, expressed on 18 June.

Leonid NIKOLAEV declared that intervention against the Soviet Union should be expected shortly; the content and character of his statement were such that he would have been pleased if intervention actually occurred.

At the end of September or beginning of October, concerning the need to go to Moscow, as he said, to obtain a sanatorium voucher, Leonid NIKOLAEV expressed extreme irritation and bitterness. From the conversation I then had with him and the hatred accompanying his statements, it was apparent that he was ready and capable of actively fighting the party.

QUESTION: What intentions and methods of struggle against the party did Leonid NIKOLAEV explain to you in this and other political conversations?

ANSWER: Leonid NIKOLAEV did not have conversations with me setting out practical means of fighting the party.

QUESTION: How can you explain to the investigation why Leonid NIKOLAEV expressed to you the political views and intentions about which you testified above?

ANSWER: By his desire to find sympathy for his sentiments in me. Several of his remarks were intended to probe and ascertain my sentiments; moreover, he had grounds to count on my sympathy because he had witnessed some remarks of a Trotskyist character made by me in 1930, which he witnessed once.

QUESTION: You testify that, despite his statements hostile to the party, Leonid NIKOLAEV did not discuss practical measures of struggle against it with you. Say when you had a conversation with him about individual political terror.

ANSWER: Such a conversation took place between us at the end of 1933, but we discussed individual political terror as a means of political struggle purely theoretically. While selecting in my presence questions on which he was to prepare a presentation at the Institute of Red Professors, Leonid singled out terror (LENIN's article), although this question had no independent place of any significance in the syllabus and was not listed as a separate topic.

QUESTION: What do you know of Leonid NIKOLAEV's membership in a counterrevolutionary organization setting itself the task of terror against party leaders?

ANSWER: Leonid NIKOLAEV told me nothing about this, and I know nothing.

QUESTION: Did you know that Leonid NIKOLAEV's brother Pyotr NIKOLAEV deserted his Red Army unit in May and November of this year and is hiding with relatives?

ANSWER: Yes, I knew. My wife told me.

QUESTION: How can you explain that, in answering the investigation's previous written and oral questions on this last point, you told an untruth and asserted that you did not know about Pyotr NIKOLAEV's desertion from the army?

ANSWER: I did not remember the facts of Pyotr NIKOLAEV's desertion sufficiently well.

QUESTION: The investigation in your case considers your testimony about Leonid NIKOLAEV's intentions, known to you, to wage an active struggle against the party insincere and incomplete. Do you acknowledge that he told you of his decision to commit a terrorist act against Comrade STALIN, received your approval, and that you agreed to assist him in preparing an attempt on Comrade STALIN's life in Moscow?

ANSWER: No, I do not acknowledge this.

QUESTION: What can you add to your testimony?

ANSWER: At present I do not recall significant circumstances that I could add to my testimony.

Correctly recorded from my words and read by me, in confirmation of which I sign.

KULISHER

INTERROGATED BY:

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE OO, GUGB, NKVD OF THE USSR — SOSNOVSKY

ASSISTANT TO THE HEAD OF THE OO, GUGB, NKVD OF THE USSR — GENDIN
