INTERROGATION RECORD

Of Moisei (Mikhail) Markovich KHARITONOV

(supplementary)

December 24, 1934.

QUESTION: Whom else among the former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc did you meet, and what was the nature of these meetings?

ANSWER: In the autumn of 1933 or the summer of 1934, I had a chance meeting with SAFAROV at Otdykh station. Our conversation was brief and took place at SAFAROV's dacha. I told SAFAROV that I intended to go to work in the Comintern in the future; he replied that it was not worth doing, that the work was uninteresting. SAFAROV also said that organizational leadership was in poor shape and that there were no personnel apart from PYATNITSKY. He asked me about the Communist Party of England; I briefly told him what I knew, and the conversation ended there.

In the summer of 1933, when I was on leave in Moscow, Yakov ELKOVICH, who had arrived from Sverdlovsk at that time, visited me. At his suggestion, we went to GORSHENIN's apartment. We spent the evening at GORSHENIN's; ELKOVICH stayed overnight. We had no political conversations.

I consider it necessary to supplement my previous testimony with the following:

Around August 1928, immediately after our return from the provinces to Moscow following our readmission to the party, there was a meeting at ZINOVIEV's Moscow apartment attended by about ten people. ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, LASHEVICH and others were there. As far as I remember, no conversation of any seriousness developed on this occasion.

At the end of 1930 or the beginning of 1931, after one of my business trips, I visited ZINOVIEV; as always, he asked about the situation in the countryside. The conversation concerned collectivization and the liquidation of the kulaks. ZINOVIEV summed it up roughly as follows: “It is right that collectivization is being carried out,” but he considered the pace and methods wrong, saying: “These are Asiatic methods. This policy of the leaders of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) costs the country far too much.” In this conversation, as in all others, ZINOVIEV named Comrade STALIN as the culprit for everything he considered negative. ZINOVIEV's hostility toward Comrade STALIN thrust itself to the fore in every conversation. I remember that, in 1930, I believe, ZINOVIEV reacted positively to a decision of the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) (I do not remember on what question), but ten minutes later, having turned the conversation to Comrade STALIN, he spoke of him in the harshest terms.

KHARITONOV

INTERROGATED BY: HEAD OF THE SIXTH SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB — KORKIN.
