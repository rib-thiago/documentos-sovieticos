INTERROGATION RECORD

Dated January 5, 1935, conducted by the head of the 4th Section of the SPO, KOGAN, L.

BEREZIN, Alfred Mikhailovich, born in 1905 in Leningrad, father a tailor, Jewish, citizen of the USSR, residing at 25 Radishchev Street, apartment 6, director of Lenobltorg, married; wife: Lida Stanislavovna DUDA, member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), works in the “Gastronom” association as business manager; son by former wife, aged six, lives with his mother in Murmansk. Father: Mikhail Osipovich, aged 63, 14 Vereiskaya Street, apartment 3 (tailor); mother: Maria Borisovna, housewife, lives with father. Brothers: Boris, non-party, aged 37, cooperative agent, lives with father; Alexander, born in 1909, non-party, storekeeper, lives with father. Sister: Praskovya Mikhailovna LEVINA, wife of the executed LEVIN, V. S. Without property, incomplete secondary education, three-class municipal school; member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) since 1926; received a party penalty for failing to inform party bodies of his receipt of an anti-party letter from ISAEV. Removed on appeal in 1931. In 1927 a member of the bureau of the Central District Komsomol Committee. No criminal convictions; I am registered in the Smolninsky district, KP-5. Did not serve with the Whites.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that you belonged to the Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc. What can you testify in this connection?

ANSWER: In 1925–26 and 1927 I worked in Komsomol work in the Central district. In 1925–26, as secretary of the Komsomol cell at Novtresttorg (in Leningrad), I did not belong to the opposition. The district committee promoted me to a large cell at Sevpechat, and I replaced the oppositionist cell secretary SLOBOZHANINOV. In this same cell I fought the Zinovievists throughout 1926 and dispersed the cell bureau. All the work proceeded under the direction of the Komsomol District Committee secretary, ZIMA. Then I was cell secretary at the “KUZhD” factory and at the Institute of Labor. This was in 1927. There I also conducted work with the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc. On the eve of the 15th Party Congress, after ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV were expelled from the party, I began to doubt the correctness of this expulsion. There was no intervention by oppositionists in the emergence of these vacillations of mine. It seemed strange to me that the expulsion had occurred on the eve of the party congress rather than at the congress itself. The district committee assigned me to deliver a report at the cell meeting about the expulsion of ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV from the party. Since the reasons for the expulsion were unclear to me and I doubted its advisability, I approached the district committee secretary and explained my doubts. The district committee secretary, Comrade ZIMA, gave me all the materials of the Central Committee plenum. I read them but still did not immediately agree. A few days later my case came before the district committee bureau. I was removed from the district committee and from the work of cell secretary. During these same days MARTINKOVSKY worked in the district committee, gathering supporters of the Trotskyist-Zinovievist bloc in the district. On learning of my vacillations, he came to me, then called in RUMYANTSEV too, and both worked on me in favor of the need for a political statement at the District Committee plenum in support of the Zinovievists and proposed that I sign the platform. I categorically refused.

RUMYANTSEV and MARTINKOVSKY proposed to introduce me to EVDOKIMOV and invited me to an illegal factional meeting. I declined the introduction to EVDOKIMOV but took the meeting’s address and immediately informed the district committee secretary, ZIMA, of my conversations with RUMYANTSEV. As a result of my report the district committee dispersed the factional meeting. A few days later, while the 15th Party Congress was still in session, I spoke at the cell meeting of the “Proletary” cooperative, declaring my errors and the vacillations I had experienced. I conducted no factional struggle, read no anti-party documents, and all my vacillations lasted only a few days. I did not see LEVIN, V., during these days, and there was no outside influence on me.

QUESTION: With which Zinovievists did you maintain contacts until the most recent period?

ANSWER: I had no contacts with Zinovievists apart from LEVIN, Vl., who was my sister’s husband. I sometimes called at LEVIN’s apartment. In most cases I met BASHKIROV there, who was closest to LEVIN and a former director of the “Volodarsky” factory. He held no counterrevolutionary conversations in my presence. From LEVIN’s words I knew that he was connected with BOGRACHEV from the Leningrad Soviet, GAIDEROVA, and ALEKSANDROV, Alexander. I never personally saw either BOGRACHEV or GAIDEROVA.

QUESTION: What do you know about ISAEV, German, and what are your relations with him?

ANSWER: I became acquainted with him when he was in the Central District Komsomol Committee. In 1926–27 he fought the opposition, although he had previously belonged to the Trotskyists. I met him through working together in the “Proletary” cooperative in 1928. In 1930 he was sent to the Saratov region for the collectivization campaign. Around the same time I was sent to Samara through mobilization. I worked in cooperatives. Once I received a letter from ISAEV describing collectivization methods. The letter was written with carbon paper, and one could see it had been written in several copies. It took the form of literary-satirical notes and was essentially anti-party. He asked me to forward it after reading to an acquaintance of his in Novgorod, a member of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks), KHOLMOVSKAYA. I did so. KHOLMOVSKAYA forwarded this letter to the party organization. During the examination of this case by the Leningrad Control Commission it emerged that he had issued a whole series of such letters. ISAEV was expelled from the party for this in 1931, and all of us recipients and readers of these letters were given party penalties. Subsequently my reprimand was removed at my request. ISAEV was later readmitted to the party by the Central Control Commission. My meetings with him afterward were chance ones. ISAEV gave me the impression of a double-dealer, an insincere person hostile to the party. I did not have sufficient factual material. It was an impression formed over several years. At the beginning of 1933, once in conversation with a member of the Regional Control Commission, D. A. LAZURKINA, I expressed my opinion of ISAEV to her. At her request I also wrote her a statement about it. Some time later I inquired of her about the results of my statement; she replied that it had been forwarded to the party organization at his workplace. During the party purge of 1933 ISAEV was expelled from the party. Possibly my statement played some role in this.

In my statement addressed to LAZURKINA I indicated that ISAEV had gathered groups of former Zinovievists and their relatives around him at “Krasnaya Zvezda.”

QUESTION: What do you know about KLADOVIKOV [1]?

ANSWER: I became acquainted with KLADOVIKOV in 1932 at the Regional Supply Department. He gave the impression of a convinced Bolshevik and straightforward party member.

Recorded correctly from my words and read by me — BEREZIN.

INTERROGATED BY — KOGAN.
