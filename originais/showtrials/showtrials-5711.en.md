INTERROGATION RECORD

OF LURIE, Moisei Ilich,

of July 21, 1936.

(also known as EMEL Aleksandr), 1897 y\<ear\> of b\<irth\>, n\<ative\> of the v\<illage\> of Kosarichi, Glusk d\<istrict\>, Byelorussian SSR; residing at 6 Yakimanskaya Embankment, apartment 5 (in the living space of DVOLAITSKY Sholom Moiseevich); a c\<itizen\> of the USSR since 1923. Professor in the Faculty of History at Moscow State University; son of a Jewish elementary religious teacher (melamed). His father died in New York in 1934. Married. Education: Faculty of Philosophy, history and philosophy section, University of Berlin, in 1935. Member of the Communist Party of Germany since 1922; detained and interrogated by the Berlin secret political police, represented by Privy Councillor Gerke, in 1931.

QUESTION: You are still concealing from the investigation your personal organizational role in preparing terrorist attempts against the leaders of the Party and Soviet government. We have sufficiently weighty evidence at our disposal and demand that you give exhaustive, specific testimony about your personal role.

ANSWER: I must acknowledge that until now I have not testified fully enough or frankly enough in the investigation about the concrete terrorist preparations of the fighting group headed by Nathan LURIE, with which I was connected through him.

QUESTION: When and under what circumstances did you learn of the existence of this terrorist fighting group?

ANSWER: I learned about the existence of the terrorist fighting group led by Nathan LURIE in the following circumstances: after my arrival in Moscow from Berlin in late March or early April 1933, I had a lengthy conversation with Nathan Lazarevich LURIE in my room at the Lux Hotel, where I was staying.

QUESTION: What information did you give LURIE during that meeting?

ANSWER: I informed Nathan LURIE in detail about the situation in Germany after Hitler came to power. I told him of Ruth FISCHER's and MASLOV's complete agreement with Trotsky's assessment of the situation in the Comintern, the AUCP(b), and the German Communist Party, and of what conclusions the Trotskyist organization ought to draw from that assessment. I told Nathan LURIE that, whereas before January 30, 1933 (the day Hitler came to power), Ruth FISCHER and MASLOV had considered it necessary to struggle for the concentration of all forces from the extreme right to the extreme left, after January 30, 1933, they characterized the Comintern and the German Communist Party as a decomposing and already decomposed “stinking corpse.” On that basis, Ruth FISCHER and MASLOV came to recognize the historical correctness of Trotsky's entire conception, which he had developed since the end of 1931 and which had reached its final form in 1932. This conception consisted in the need to organize terrorist acts against the leaders of the AUCP(b), above all Stalin, as well as to prepare and organize armed uprisings within the USSR.

QUESTION: Did you tell Nathan LURIE to whom you were to transmit these directives from Trotsky in Moscow?

ANSWER: I was supposed to pass on in Moscow these latest instructions from Trotsky, communicated to me by Ruth FISCHER and MASLOV, to a particularly trusted person of Zinoviev's, Aleksandr Vladimirovich HERTSBERG. However, I did not tell Nathan LURIE about this.

QUESTION: What exactly did Nathan LURIE tell you about his Trotskyist and terrorist activities?

ANSWER: During that conversation, I asked Nathan LURIE whether he still adhered to Trotskyist positions and what his activities in Moscow consisted of. Nathan LURIE replied that he remained a convinced Trotskyist, as before, and told me that since April 1932 a terrorist group, small in numbers but firm in its membership, had existed here in Moscow.

QUESTION: Give testimony on the membership of this terrorist fighting group.

ANSWER: Nathan LURIE told me that, besides himself, the terrorist fighting group comprised his relative by marriage Erik KONSTANT and Pavel LIPSHITS. I barely knew Erik KONSTANT personally, having seen him once or twice. I knew Pavel LIPSHITS better from his time in Berlin, although I had no personal relations with him.

QUESTION: What did Nathan LURIE tell you about the formation of this terrorist fighting group?

ANSWER: When I asked on whose directive and initiative this fighting group had been organized, Nathan LURIE replied that it had been formed by a certain Franz VAITS, with whom the aforementioned Erik KONSTANT had put him in touch. Nathan LURIE gave this answer in response to my question whether he, Nathan LURIE, had been acting on instructions from KAPLINSKY, a former trusted representative of Trotsky in Berlin at the time of the Party's Fifteenth Congress. During that period Nathan LURIE was closely associated with KAPLINSKY and continually worked on his instructions in Berlin.

QUESTION: Who is Franz VAITS, and what do you know about him?

ANSWER: When I asked who Franz VAITS was, Nathan LURIE at first rather reluctantly gave the following answer: Franz VAITS was an active member of the National Socialist Party of Germany and a trusted representative of Himmler (the present head of the GESTAPO in Germany). At that time Himmler headed the “SS” (Black Guards). Nathan LURIE immediately asked whether I, Moisei LURIE, justified his connection with the fascist Franz VAITS. I replied that my personal assessment could not be decisive here, but that in the coming days I would inform a representative of the center about the matter.

QUESTION: What do you know about the plans for terrorist attempts developed by Franz VAITS?

ANSWER: Before Nathan LURIE's arrival in the USSR (April 1932), Franz VAITS, being in contact with KONSTANT and Pavel LIPSHITS, organized and headed this terrorist fighting group. According to VAITS's plans, the group's principal task was to prepare terrorist acts against Stalin, Kaganovich, Voroshilov and Ordzhonikidze. For that purpose the group, then headed by VAITS, carried out work consisting of: 1) tracking and identifying government cars, especially Stalin's car; 2) establishing the hours at which Stalin usually traveled along the Mozhaisk highway to his dacha; 3) establishing contacts with drivers of government cars in order to use them for terrorist activities; 4) establishing contact with a military pilot in order to recruit him to commit terrorist acts; 5) taking measures to gain admission to ceremonial meetings, congresses and conferences to commit terrorist acts against Stalin and other leaders of the Party and government.

QUESTION: What do you know about specific measures taken by the terrorist group of VAITS and Nathan LURIE?

ANSWER: According to Nathan LURIE, I know that on Franz VAITS's instructions Pavel LIPSHITS was to commit a terrorist act against Stalin on Red Square on May 1, 1932. This attempt was not carried out because the Dynamo factory column, where LIPSHITS worked, crossed Red Square at a considerable distance from the mausoleum.

QUESTION: What other attempts at terrorist acts were undertaken by this fighting group?

ANSWER: I also know from Nathan LURIE's words that, after the unsuccessful attempt at a terrorist act on Red Square, Franz VAITS returned to Germany and full leadership of the terrorist fighting group passed directly to Nathan LURIE. During the January session of the All-Russian Central Executive Committee (VTsIK) in 1933, Pavel LIPSHITS, having obtained a ticket to one of the meetings, was to commit a terrorist act against Stalin there on Nathan LURIE's instructions. But the plan was not carried out because LIPSHITS did not encounter Stalin and could not shoot Kalinin, who was chairing the meeting, because the guest seats were too far from the presidium table. I also know that Nathan LURIE personally attended one of the following meetings of that session, determined to attempt to assassinate Stalin there. His attempt likewise failed for the same reasons.

QUESTION: Do you know how Nathan LURIE gained admission to that session?

ANSWER: According to Nathan LURIE, he obtained the ticket from his relative Dr. TAITS, who worked at the Kremlin hospital.

QUESTION: What other terrorist attempts known to you were prepared by Nathan LURIE's group?

ANSWER: I know that on February 23, 1933, the anniversary of the Red Army, Nathan LURIE and Erik KONSTANT decided to assassinate Stalin and Voroshilov. Armed with revolvers, both watched the entrances on opposite sides of the Bolshoi Theatre, but the heavy cordon and guards prevented them from approaching the theatre and carrying out the planned terrorist act.

Nathan LURIE also told me that, on Franz VAITS's instructions, he, KONSTANT and Pavel LIPSHITS organized systematic surveillance of Voroshilov on Frunze Street. The surveillance began in the summer of 1932 and continued until Nathan LURIE left Moscow for Chelyabinsk (July 1933). All three were armed with Brownings. It is needless to speak of the Trotskyists' hatred of Voroshilov, so their desire to kill him is understandable. They established that Voroshilov often walked from the Defense Commissariat to the Kremlin along Frunze Street. They also established the times at which he did so, but for technical reasons they could not carry out a terrorist act.

QUESTION: Until when did Nathan LURIE, KONSTANT and LIPSHITS continue their surveillance of Comrade Voroshilov?

ANSWER: Nathan LURIE, KONSTANT and LIPSHITS carried out surveillance until Nathan LURIE left for Chelyabinsk in July 1933. After his departure KONSTANT and LIPSHITS continued the surveillance, but I do not know until when.

QUESTION: What practical instructions did you give Nathan LURIE after receiving information from him about the fighting activities of the terrorist group he led?

ANSWER: I told Nathan LURIE that the activities of the fighting group he led fully corresponded to the assignments from Trotsky that I had received from Ruth FISCHER on the eve of my departure from Berlin. Later I also informed Nathan LURIE that I had told the person with whom I had a clandestine contact in Moscow about his group's activities. As I have already testified, this person was HERTSBERG, a former personal emissary of Zinoviev's in Berlin until the end of 1931.

QUESTION: What exactly did you tell HERTSBERG about Nathan LURIE's fighting group's activities?

ANSWER: When I informed HERTSBERG in considerable detail about the terrorist preparations of Nathan LURIE's group, HERTSBERG in turn expressed complete satisfaction with its activities and told me he would inform Zinoviev about it.

QUESTION: Did you inform HERTSBERG that VAITS was a member of Germany's National Socialist Party?

ANSWER: Yes, I emphasized that circumstance during our conversation. HERTSBERG showed some embarrassment.

QUESTION: What directives did HERTSBERG give you concerning the use of Nathan LURIE's fighting group?

ANSWER: When I told HERTSBERG about Nathan LURIE and KONSTANT's terrorist fighting group, I said that unfortunately Nathan LURIE would have to leave Moscow because the People's Commissariat of Health was sending him to work in Chelyabinsk. HERTSBERG proposed keeping Nathan LURIE in Moscow at all costs. Knowing from me that Nathan LURIE, as a surgeon, had an opportunity to enter the Kremlin because of his relative TAITS's work in the Kremlin Medical Administration, HERTSBERG said I should convey to Nathan LURIE, as a directive of the center, that he must obtain cancellation of his assignment to Chelyabinsk and enter the Kremlin hospital with the specific aim of having more opportunities to approach Party leaders to commit a terrorist act.

QUESTION: Did you pass that directive from HERTSBERG on to Nathan LURIE?

ANSWER: I passed it on to Nathan LURIE. He tried to carry it out but could not secure cancellation of the assignment. I do not know whether he acted energetically enough, but he came to me and said that it had not been possible to cancel it and that, at least for the time being, he would have to go to Chelyabinsk because refusing to go could negatively affect his transfer into membership in the AUCP(b). N. LURIE's argument was persuasive, and I said he should go to Chelyabinsk for a while.

When I informed HERTSBERG that N. LURIE would nevertheless have to go to Chelyabinsk, HERTSBERG remarked that N. LURIE should at least try to organize a fighting group there.

HERTSBERG pointed out that Ordzhonikidze and other government members might visit the Chelyabinsk Tractor Plant. If such an opportunity arose, it would have to be used to commit a terrorist act. I conveyed this directive from HERTSBERG to Nathan LURIE.

QUESTION: Was Dr. TAITS aware of Nathan LURIE's terrorist intentions?

ANSWER: I think not. I do not know whether Nathan LURIE told TAITS anything.

QUESTION: Did you introduce Nathan LURIE to HERTSBERG?

ANSWER: When I told HERTSBERG about the activities of Nathan LURIE's group, he very much wanted to meet Nathan LURIE in person, check what sort of man he was and whether he could be given an assignment; he wanted to give him an assignment personally. The meeting with HERTSBERG did not take place only for technical reasons, because HERTSBERG had been sent somewhere on assignment between June 1 and July 15, 1933.

QUESTION: Did you meet any leaders of the Trotskyist-Zinovievist organization in Moscow besides HERTSBERG?

ANSWER: Yes, in 1934 I met G. Zinoviev.

QUESTION: How many meetings took place between you and Zinoviev in Moscow in 1934?

ANSWER: There were three meetings in all. All three occurred between April 1933 and early August or late July 1934. The first meeting took place at the editorial offices of the journal Bolshevik; the second, in the conference hall of the Communist Academy during a lecture by KNORIN on the twentieth anniversary of the outbreak of the war; the third, in August 1934, at Zinoviev's apartment on the Arbat.

QUESTION: Did you inform Zinoviev about Trotsky's terrorist directives and Nathan LURIE's terrorist group?

ANSWER: Yes, I informed Zinoviev about this. It was at my last meeting with him, at his apartment. The meeting took place in the following circumstances: Zinoviev was reviewing my book Paths of Betrayal of German Social Democracy. I telephoned him at the editorial offices of the journal Bolshevik. Zinoviev strongly urged me to come to his home if it was convenient. I visited him that same day at nine in the evening.

QUESTION: Did Zinoviev know that you had arrived from abroad?

ANSWER: Yes, he did. I visited him between July 28 and August 5, 1934, because it could have been on the eve of the twentieth anniversary of the outbreak of the war or during the following days.

QUESTION: Did you inform Zinoviev of Trotsky's terrorist instructions given to Ruth FISCHER and MASLOV \[1\]?

ANSWER: Zinoviev's question to me was literally whether I had any new information from Ruth FISCHER and MASLOV and whether their assessment of the situation had changed at all. I told him I had no new information. The last information had been received in October 1933, but the directive I had brought and conveyed to HERTSBERG had not only remained in force but had unquestionably grown stronger.

QUESTION: Which directive were you referring to?

ANSWER: At the beginning of our conversation I approached these questions tactfully, since I personally had no direct connection with Zinoviev. But after he himself asked the question, I thought it possible to speak frankly. I told him that Ruth FISCHER and MASLOV's directive amounted to this: after January 30, 1933, there could no longer be any doubt about the correctness of Trotsky's directives concerning the need to accelerate the organization of armed uprisings in the USSR and terrorist acts against the leadership of the AUCP(b) and the Soviet government, first and foremost against Stalin. Zinoviev entirely agreed with Trotsky's assessment of the situation in the Comintern and the USSR.

When I finished my account, Zinoviev thought for several minutes and then uttered a phrase that was engraved in my memory: “Well, we shall see who has grasped the ‘philosophy of the epoch’ and whether the headless corpse of the dictator (or usurper) will hear the footsteps of the newest history, whose first chapter is already being written.”

Zinoviev said: “I know you. Although we have had only a few conversations, I have known of you since the Sixth Enlarged Plenum of the ECCI (1926), through Ruth FISCHER. HERTSBERG has also spoken of you to me repeatedly.”

QUESTION: Did you tell Zinoviev that Ruth FISCHER and MASLOV's instructions on terrorist struggle against the AUCP(b) and the Soviet government were a direct directive from Trotsky?

ANSWER: Yes, I certainly did.

QUESTION: Who was present at your meeting and conversation with Zinoviev in his apartment?

ANSWER: No one other than Zinoviev and me was present during that conversation.

QUESTION: Did you tell Zinoviev in detail about Nathan LURIE's terrorist group and its membership?

ANSWER: I informed him about it in complete detail. Earlier I had informed HERTSBERG about it, and had also told him of Nathan LURIE's connection with the fascist Franz VAITS. HERTSBERG told me that under no circumstances should I tell Zinoviev about it. But I considered the question so important that it needed to be legitimized personally by Zinoviev himself. Therefore, when Zinoviev asked me from whom Nathan LURIE had received directives for preparing a terrorist act against Stalin, I told him that Nathan LURIE had received them through his relative by marriage Erik KONSTANT from Himmler's representative, the leader of the fascist storm detachments, the fascist Fritz \[2\] VAITS. I awaited his response, but did not have to wait long. Zinoviev said: “What troubles you about this? You are a historian, Moisei Ilich. You know about Lassalle's dealings with Bismarck, when Lassalle wanted to use Bismarck in the interests of the revolution” \[3\].

By this historical parallel Zinoviev wished to convince me of the possibility and necessity of using an alliance with National Socialists in the struggle against the leadership of the AUCP(b) and the Soviet government.

QUESTION: Did Zinoviev tell you about preparations for the murder of Comrade Kirov?

ANSWER: I also remember a phrase Zinoviev used when the discussion turned to where our principal cadres were. He said: “Leningrad was ‘Red Peter,’ and so it remains and shall remain. Do not think that Kirov has crushed our forces in Leningrad. There are still many of our comrades in leading posts, and Kirov will not find it so easy to deal with them, and they know what to do.”

When the discussion turned to Zinoviev's speech at the Seventeenth Party Congress and Kirov's speech about the “baggage-train people” \[4\], Zinoviev said: “Well, we shall yet see who will end up in the baggage train and whether the author of that famous expression about the baggage-train people will even be alive.” These were Zinoviev's exact words.

QUESTION: Did Zinoviev tell you of his terrorist intentions against the leadership of the AUCP(b) and the Soviet government?

ANSWER: Zinoviev tried in every way, invoking Lenin and Marx, to demonstrate to me the historical expediency of terror against the leadership of the AUCP(b). He said: “It is wrong to think that Marx, Engels and Lenin opposed terror in each and every circumstance. Lenin said that his brother's act was wrong because that individual act was not supported by a mass movement and did not cause widespread unrest. Lenin linked individual terror with mass terror. That is precisely what we are striving for, do not forget\! It was Lenin himself, alr\<eady\> in 1897, who posed the question: ‘What heritage are we renouncing?’ And we have never renounced the finest traditions of the People's Will. Recall also Marx's and Engels's positive statements precisely about the terrorist acts of Russian revolutionaries. Here Marxism-Leninism must be taken not as a dogma but as a guide to action. And if we can save living Marxism through individual terror, it is clear that we cannot shrink even from that.”

QUESTION: Against which AUCP(b) leaders did Zinoviev first plan terrorist acts?

ANSWER: On that question Zinoviev told me: “Our individual terror will unquestionably be supported by a mass movement of enormous forces still restrained by the Stalinist regime. Those forces must be released at any cost. That release will come precisely from a successful terrorist act against Stalin and his associates.”

QUESTION: Did Zinoviev warn you of the need to keep secret the meeting and conversation you had with him at his apartment in August 1934?

ANSWER: Yes. Zinoviev made it clear that the meeting was to be kept in the strictest secrecy, and for reasons of conspiracy I never disclosed it to anyone.

QUESTION: After this conversation with Zinoviev, did you meet with HERTSBERG?

ANSWER: Yes, I met HERTSBERG regularly. He and his wife POZDEEVA were close acquaintances and friends of mine.

Moreover, my wife's mother lived in his apartment.

QUESTION: Did HERTSBERG tell you he approved of Nathan LURIE's fighting group's organizational ties with the fascists?

ANSWER: He told me that later, in 1934.

QUESTION: Did you personally meet L. D. Trotsky?

ANSWER: I never met L. D. Trotsky personally. I had only one meeting with his son, Lev SEDOV, in Berlin in the autumn of 1932.

QUESTION: When Ruth FISCHER and MASLOV conveyed Trotsky's terrorist instructions to you, did they say they had received them personally from Trotsky?

ANSWER: They conveyed those instructions to me as a directive from Trotsky. They told me it was Trotsky's latest directive, connected with the events of January 30, 1933, in Germany.

It was clear to me that there was continuing personal contact among Ruth FISCHER, MASLOV and TROTSKY. MASLOV met Trotsky in February 1933. Ruth FISCHER and MASLOV told me that a united center had been created in Berlin, which included, besides themselves, MADYAR, SEDOV, GRILEVICH \[5\] and Ari GOLDBERG (Party alias “Anton”).

MASLOV told me that he had met Trotsky but did not mention the date. Frequent meetings between MASLOV and TROTSKY took place after Papen's coup \[6\] in Prussia (July 20, 1932), before Hitler came to power.

QUESTION: By what means did you inform the Trotskyist center abroad that you had established contact with Zinoviev and HERTSBERG?

ANSWER: I did not tell anyone about the conversation with Zinoviev. But I gave Wilhelm SVENTI, who came to see me in October 1933 on Ruth FISCHER's instructions and had a direct directive to contact Zinoviev, detailed information about the re-establishment of contact with HERTSBERG. Upon leaving he did not come back to see me, because my role as an intermediary between him and Zinoviev had become unnecessary since he was supposed to have a meeting with Zinoviev.

QUESTION: Did you have a password for meeting SVENTI?

ANSWER: I have known SVENTI personally for a very long time, nearly ten years, from our work in the German Communist Party, and he knew me well. He came to me with very detailed greetings from Ruth FISCHER and said: “You are the most decent person in Moscow with whom one can talk about everything.” When leaving Berlin I asked Ruth FISCHER and MASLOV whether anyone would come to see me and exactly who. They answered that it was difficult to say who would come, but someone certainly would. Thus I concluded that SVENTI's visit was a continuation of our conversation with Ruth FISCHER and MASLOV on March 4, 1934.

QUESTION: Was HERTSBERG's wife POZDEEVA aware of Trotsky's terrorist instructions?

ANSWER: Yes, she unquestionably knew of Trotsky's latest terrorist instructions. I do not know whether she was informed in detail of the specific terrorist assignments, but probably so, because she and HERTSBERG had no differences.

QUESTION: Did you speak personally with POZDEEVA about terrorist struggle against the leadership of the AUCP(b)?

ANSWER: In principle, yes. POZDEEVA knew, from me personally and from HERTSBERG, about the latest directives from Trotsky and Ruth FISCHER that I had brought concerning terrorist struggle.

QUESTION: Do you know anything about preparations to murder Comrade Kirov?

ANSWER: Around October or November 1934, Aleksandr Vladimirovich HERTSBERG spent several days in Leningrad and returned in very high spirits. He told me about the very warm reception given to him by the Leningrad comrades, old fighters and like-minded people, and how they wanted, and considered it necessary, for him to move to Leningrad. He also said that the Leningrad Party activists had remained, as before, Zinovievist; that the Leningrad comrades had not forgiven and would never forgive the man who crushed the opposition; and that serious events were to be expected in the near future. HERTSBERG was calling things by their real names at that moment. When I asked, “What do you mean?”, he replied: “The vaults of the Uritsky Palace will not hear the ringing voice of Sergei Mironovich Kirov for much longer.”

QUESTION: Was POZDEEVA present during that conversation?

ANSWER: No, POZDEEVA was not present. After December 1, 1934, I did not meet HERTSBERG. Around the evening of December 20, when I returned home from the university, I found Regina BUDZINSKAYA visiting us. BUDZINSKAYA was very upset by the arrests that had occurred and berated NAUMOV's wife for visiting them after her husband's arrest. BUDZINSKAYA told my wife KOIGEN and me that we needed to be extremely careful. When I asked Regina BUDZINSKAYA, “How do you assess the events of December 1?”, meaning Kirov's murder, she answered, “Well, you know, this is our work.” BUDZINSKAYA knew she could speak frankly to me. She added, “We should expect a number of similar acts in the near future.”

QUESTION: Did you know anything about the existence of terrorist groups in Leningrad before the murder of Comrade Kirov?

ANSWER: In September 1934, I met Professor ZAIDEL in Moscow, in my office at the History Faculty of Moscow State University. ZAIDEL then told me that Professor G. S. FRIDLYAND was in direct contact with L. B. KAMENEV and TER-VAGANYAN, from whom he had received directives on the need to prepare terrorist acts against members of the Politburo of the Central Committee of the AUCP(b), primarily Stalin. In that same conversation Professor ZAIDEL informed me that a terrorist act against Kirov was being prepared in Leningrad.

QUESTION: Did you personally have an organizational connection with Professor G. S. FRIDLYAND?

ANSWER: Yes, I had been organizationally connected with Professor G. S. FRIDLYAND since the summer of 1934.

QUESTION: Did Professor G. S. FRIDLYAND inform you about preparations for a terrorist act against Comrade Kirov in Leningrad?

ANSWER: I saw Professor G. S. FRIDLYAND after December 1, 1934. He stayed at home until December 20, being very ill. In a conversation we had about Kirov's murder, FRIDLYAND told me: “Not only shall we survive this event, but still more serious events lie ahead, on the eve of which the country now finds itself.” From my conversation with FRIDLYAND, I understood that the terrorist act against Kirov had not come as a surprise to him.

QUESTION: Do you know anything about the terrorist activities of ZAIDEL's fighting group after the murder of Comrade Kirov?

ANSWER: In the m\<onth\> of December 1935, in the conference hall of the Communist Academy in Moscow, I met Professor TOMSINSKY. TOMSINSKY told me that in the spring of 1935 ZAIDEL had received a directive from G. S. FRIDLYAND to prepare a terrorist act against Zhdanov. When I asked TOMSINSKY how he knew, he told me that he had learned it in a conversation with ZAIDEL in April 1935. ZAIDEL had received the directive from FRIDLYAND at the People's Commissariat of Education to prepare Zhdanov's murder in Leningrad.

QUESTION: What directives did you give Nathan LURIE when he went to the Chelyabinsk Tractor Plant in 1933?

ANSWER: When Nathan LURIE left in July 1933 to work at the Chelyabinsk Tractor Plant, I instructed him to create a terrorist group. Later, when Nathan LURIE came to Moscow in January 1936, he told me that he had not succeeded in creating a terrorist group there, but that he had twice prepared terrorist acts there: in 1931 against Kaganovich and in 1935 against Ordzhonikidze. Both attempts failed.

QUESTION: What directive did you give Nathan LURIE when he left for Leningrad in January 1936?

ANSWER: Before Nathan LURIE left for Leningrad in January 1936, I met him in Moscow at the Sport Café. During that meeting I instructed Nathan LURIE to prepare and commit a terrorist act against Zhdanov in Leningrad.

QUESTION: Did Nathan LURIE agree to commit a terrorist act against Comrade Zhdanov?

ANSWER: Yes, Nathan LURIE agreed and promised, in the organization's interests, to prepare and commit a terrorist attempt against Zhdanov.

QUESTION: Did you coordinate with anyone the assignment you gave Nathan LURIE to commit a terrorist act against Comrade Zhdanov?

ANSWER: On December 28 or 29, 1935, I met Professor G. S. FRIDLYAND in his office at the History Faculty of Moscow State University. I informed FRIDLYAND that Nathan LURIE, whom I trusted completely, was going to Leningrad and that I considered it necessary to use him there for terrorist purposes. I decided to tell FRIDLYAND about this because by then I no longer had contact with the center and thought it necessary to coordinate my directives to Nathan LURIE with Professor FRIDLYAND. FRIDLYAND promised to give me an answer in a couple of days, since he too wanted to coordinate the matter with someone else.

QUESTION: When did your next meeting with Professor FRIDLYAND occur, and what instructions did you receive from him?

ANSWER: On January 2, 1936, I met Professor FRIDLYAND again in his office at the History Faculty of Moscow State University. At that meeting Professor FRIDLYAND told me the following: “At present I do not know whether ZAIDEL's terrorist group is working well, because ZAIDEL himself is not now in Leningrad. If the man to whom you have decided to give the assignment is a tested comrade, then he must at all costs be put in touch with ZAIDEL's group, and on his own initiative he should organize a second fighting group in Leningrad that would operate in parallel with ZAIDEL's group to carry out a terrorist act against Zhdanov.” In the same conversation FRIDLYAND emphasized that his instructions were based on a directive he had received in 1934 from TER-VAGANYAN and L. B. KAMENEV.

QUESTION: Did your counterrevolutionary Trotskyist-Zinovievist organization have any political platform?

ANSWER: We had no political platform at all. In recent years nobody had worked out a political program. This was because, after 1931, the Zinovievists and Trotskyists were united not by a political program but by the idea of revenge and terrorist attacks against Stalin and other AUCP(b) leaders.

Having long since lost all support and any base in the USSR, our counterrevolutionary organization turned into a handful of political bankrupts and finally degenerated into a terrorist gang.

The foregoing was correctly recorded from my words, read by me, and corresponds to reality — M. LURIE.

\[3\] Ferdinand Lassalle (1825 1864) was a German philosopher, jurist, economist, agitator and political figure who laid the foundations of the Social Democratic Party of Germany. He called upon German workers to establish producers' cooperative associations and to demand universal and direct suffrage for parliament. His struggle against the Progressives led Lassalle to establish particular relations with the Prussian government, which, in Bismarck's person, was not averse to exploiting the struggle between liberalism and social democracy in its own interests. The fact that Bismarck and Lassalle knew each other is entirely reliable: their dealings chiefly concerned the introduction of universal voting.
