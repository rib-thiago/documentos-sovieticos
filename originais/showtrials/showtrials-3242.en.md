INTERROGATION RECORD

conducted by the Assistant Department Head of the GUGB of the NKVD of the USSR — RUTKOVSKY

dated December 13, 1934.

NAUMOV, Ivan Kupriyanovich, born in 1895, a native of Lopino village, Ostashkovo volost, Mozhaysk uyezd, former Moscow province; resides in Moscow, Bolshoy Patriarshy, building No. 8, apartment 13; director of Aircraft Factory No. 20; wife Aleksandra Vasilyevna LEPESHINSKAYA, aged thirty-three; son Leonid, aged eleven. Without property, secondary education, a Party member since 1913, with an interruption from December 1927 to October 1928.

QUESTION: What do you know about the existence in Moscow of a center of former members of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc?

ANSWER: During the Fifteenth Party Congress, ZINOVIEV and a group of people politically close to him decided to return to the Party in an organized fashion in order to avoid organizational destruction. As a result, a counterrevolutionary group formed around ZINOVIEV, consisting of ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, FEDOROV, GERTIK, KHARITONOV, SHAROV, and KUKLIN. Closing in upon itself, this group continued to exist until the present, giving all emerging counterrevolutionary anti-Party groups and organizations to understand that it could be regarded as a force (a reserve held back) capable of being used in the struggle against the Party leadership. This counterrevolutionary group's strong cohesion rests on the aspiration shared by all — to return to the leadership of the Party.

QUESTION: What is the attitude of the counterrevolutionary group's members toward the Party leadership?

ANSWER: The second motive uniting the group over a number of years and until recently is the hatred shared by all its members toward the Party leadership: Comrades STALIN, KAGANOVICH, MOLOTOV, and the late KIROV. Among those most sharply anti-Party in their attitude, I can name BAKAEV I. P., who at one time educated the young people GORSHENIN, BRAVO, RUMYANTSEV, and others in an uncompromising attitude toward the Party leadership.

QUESTION: What can you say about the counterrevolutionary group's activity and meetings?

ANSWER: I know that the places where EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, FEDOROV, SHAROV, BRAVO, and others regularly met were GERTIK's and GORSHENIN's apartments. Such meetings were ordinarily disguised as tea parties, family celebrations, and the like; meanwhile, at these gatherings Party decisions came under attack, political news was discussed, and a political common ground of hostile assessments of Party policy was developed.

QUESTION: With which former members of the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc in Leningrad were members of the Moscow counterrevolutionary group connected?

ANSWER: I know that when visiting Leningrad, GERTIK and FEDOROV met RUMYANTSEV, LEVIN, and others; TARASOV I. I. told me in 1932, or possibly in the spring of 1933, that the Leningrad people continued to meet on the basis of their former membership in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc and talked on anti-Party subjects, particularly about a rupture between town and countryside or, perhaps, a crisis of the dictatorship of the proletariat. —

I. NAUMOV.
