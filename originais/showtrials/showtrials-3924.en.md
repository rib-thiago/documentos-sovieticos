INTERROGATION RECORD

conducted by the Head of Section 4 of the SPO — KOGAN L.

dated December 30, 1934.

GERTSBERG, Aleksandr Vladimirovich.

QUESTION: Specify your role in the counterrevolutionary organization.

ANSWER: As I have already stated, I belonged to a group of persons organizationally connected with members of the organization's center. Around the Moscow center — ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, KUKLIN, GERTIK, and ZALUTSKY — there remained a group of persons who did not break off contact with them until recently. Besides myself, this group included my wife POZDEEVA, GORSHENIN, KOZHURO, KHARITONOV, GESSEN Sergey, PERIMOV, FEDOROV G., BRAVO, and KANATCHIKOV.

Among these “trusted people,” members of the center did not hesitate to speak the truth about their hatred and bitterness toward the Party and Comintern leadership, especially STALIN. They did not hesitate to conduct counterrevolutionary work openly within the Party. This group included people who either directly shared their views or listened silently and thereby gave them reason to suppose they agreed. Both the former and the latter played an equally treacherous role toward the Party.

QUESTION: Inform the investigation of the facts known to you concerning the Zinovievist organization's counterrevolutionary activity.

ANSWER: I returned from abroad, as I have already testified, at the end of 1931. I can therefore cite isolated facts occurring from 1932 onward.

1\. My meeting, together with my wife POZDEEVA, with SAFAROV dates from that time. He came to obtain our impressions of the work of the German Communist Party. I do not remember exactly what I told him, but the conversation was in the nature of condemning the Comintern line, and SAFAROV conducted it in a spirit of bitterness toward the Comintern leadership.

2\. In the summer of 1932 I visited ZINOVIEV at his dacha with KHARITONOV. His son, his wife's sister, who was not a Party member, and someone else (I do not remember who) were there. ZINOVIEV was very pleased that people on whom he could count in his struggle against the Party had come to see him. The general tone of his conversation conveyed resentment and bitterness toward the Party and Comintern. Among particular details of our conversation, I remember that ZINOVIEV spoke about the growth of fascism in Germany. He said that the Comintern's policy was facilitating Hitler's rise to power and that the Comintern could not unite the Communist elements in Germany.

ZINOVIEV complained that the Central Committee was removing old Bolsheviks from responsible work. As an example he cited KRZHIZHANOVSKY's removal (I do not remember in what connection).

3\. In that same year, 1932, I met ZALUTSKY. I cannot report the contents of our conversation — I do not remember. But ZALUTSKY's mood indicated that nothing could be good in the Party while he was outside the leadership. The Party was one thing, he another. This conversation left me with the impression that ZALUTSKY was unquestionably continuing the struggle against the Party and had not lost hope of returning to its leadership.

4\. After GERTIK's return from exile in 1933, he visited our home. Asked why he had been exiled, GERTIK burst into a slanderous tirade against the Party. He said that he had been expelled from the Party for nothing and exiled for nothing, and that this showed the abuses possible under the present state of affairs in the Party. “They,” he said, “suspected that since ZINOVIEV had been exiled he must have left an agent behind, and they regarded me as that agent.” This division into “they” and “we,” and his entire attitude, showed GERTIK's bitterness and hatred toward the Party and its leadership.

5\. During the Seventeenth Party Congress I met KUKLIN. When I asked how he liked the congress, he replied bitterly, “Nothing but chatter,” and made a number of other hostile attacks against the congress. It was the talk of a traitor who hated the Party, the Soviet Union, and its leaders.

Recorded faithfully from my words, read by me, and I sign in confirmation.

GERTSBERG.
