INTERROGATION RECORD

OF ISAEV, German Ivanovich

31 December 1934.

QUESTION: A sealed letter dated 24 January 1931 bearing your signature and addressed to Comrade BOGDANOV, chairman of the Party Collegium of the Regional Party Control Commission, was found during your search. You write: “You will receive this personal letter from me only if the GPU arrests me. I have received a summons to appear there and can expect to be detained,” and so forth. In connection with what circumstances of your activities did you expect arrest?

ANSWER: In January 1930 I was mobilized by the party Central Committee for collective-farm work in the countryside. I worked four months in Burluk village, Kamyshin District of the former Lower Volga Territory.

After returning I was expelled from the party for a Right-Left deviation, as the Central Control Commission characterized my activity.

While working in the countryside I sent anti-Soviet letters to several comrades in Leningrad, writing multiple carbon copies. I emphasized the unbearable and ruinous character of the pace adopted by the party, accused its Central Committee of excesses, tendentiously generalized all negative facts of local life and suppressed achievements.

I sent these anti-Soviet letters to the LSPO and Proletary cooperative cells, where they were read and circulated quite widely. Several party members — BONDARENKO Klavdia, DITRIKH Evgeny, NIKIFOROV Nikolai, PONOMAREV Stepan, MALAYA Berta, LARIONOV Kuzma, BEREZIN Aleksandr, BARABANOV Nikolai and SAMYLOV Gavriil — received party penalties for reading these letters and adopting a conciliatory attitude toward them.

This case of my distribution of anti-Soviet letters received extensive coverage in the Leningrad press (Leningradskaya Pravda, “Correspondence with Friends”).

On the second day after my expulsion by the District Control Commission I received a summons to the OGPU. Since my anti-Soviet letters had become a fairly serious matter and widely publicized, I considered upon receiving the summons that I would be arrested, which is why I wrote to Comrade BOGDANOV at the Control Commission seeking protection.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that by distributing counterrevolutionary letters you sought to assemble a counterrevolutionary group. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: No, that was not my purpose. When leaving for countryside work I promised several comrades from the LSPO and Proletary cooperative cells to report on my work. Among them there was only one Zinovievist — BEREZIN Alfred.

QUESTION: A note from KHANIK Lev dated 20 January 1934 was found during your search, requesting a job for a “Komsomol uncle.” What do you know of KHANIK as a member of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization?

ANSWER: I know KHANIK poorly, having last met him in 1930 at the regional party conference. The “Komsomol uncle” proved to be KHANIK's father, an elderly invalid whom I refused to place in employment. I know KHANIK is an old Zinovievist. I did not meet him for counterrevolutionary work.

I have read the testimony; it has been correctly recorded from my words.

ISAEV.

INTERROGATED BY:

OPERATIONS OFFICER OF THE SPO, GUGB — FEDOROV

Certified correct: A. Svetlova
