INTERROGATION RECORD

conducted on January 19, 1935 by the head of the 1st Section of the SPO —

LULOV.

LELEVICH, Labori Gilelevich, born in 1901, native of Mogilev, residing in Makhachkala, Krasnoarmeiskaya Street, 40, apartment 12, Jewish, citizen of the USSR. Writer. Deputy director of the Institute of National Cultures in Makhachkala. Son of a teacher, an AUCP(b) member. Before the revolution, studied. After the revolution, in party and Soviet work. Married. Wife: LELEVICH-LUKOVSKAYA, Sofya Veniaminovna. Son Varlen, aged 10; they live in Makhachkala. Secondary education. AUCP(b) member since 1917. In January 1928 expelled from the AUCP(b) by the Saratov Provincial Control Commission. In October 1930 reinstated by the Central Control Commission. In October 1928 arrested in Saratov and, by resolution of the Special Conference attached to the OGPU Collegium, exiled for three years to the Urals. Released early in April 1930; senior political personnel. In 1918 participated in partisan combat detachments in Samara. Most recently was deputy chairman of the Dagestan Writers' Union.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that you are a member of the Zinovievist organization. Do you confirm this?

ANSWER: Yes, I confirm it. I am indeed a member of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization.

QUESTION: Name the members of the organization known to you.

QUESTION: Of the members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization personally known to me, I can name: Georgi GORBACHEV, Ivan BAKAEV, Anna KOSTINA, Artem GERTIK, Ivan GORSHENIN, Sergei GESSEN, Vojislav VUJOVIC [1], Henryk DOMBSKY, and Illarion VARDIN. I shall try to recall the other members of the organization. I was connected with the persons listed throughout all the past years on the basis of our joint counterrevolutionary activity. Regina BUDZINSKAYA, who was closely connected with us, knew of the counterrevolutionary activity of a number of members of the organization, especially my own and VUJOVIC's.

QUESTION: What can you testify about the ties of members of the Zinovievist organization with Trotskyists and representatives of other counterrevolutionary groups?

ANSWER: Until 1934 I maintained contact with the Trotskyist Alexander VORONSKY. In 1931 and 1932 I maintained contact with the Trotskyists STRAZH and FREILIKHMAN (printers). They were arrested approximately in 1933. During my visit to Leningrad in 1931 I met with the Trotskyist Viktor BALTSEV, with whom we had served exile together in Solikamsk in 1929–1930. In 1932, at a gathering of members of the Zinovievist organization at BAKAEV's apartment, I also met the Trotskyists UFIMTSEV and SEMASHKO. I shall try to recall other connections between members of the Zinovievist organization and Trotskyists. I know of a connection (possibly an attempt to establish a bloc) between the Shlyapnikovists and ZINOVIEV and VARDIN. In 1932 VARDIN told me that he was meeting MEDVEDEV, that the latter stood fully by his old positions, and that MEDVEDEV and SHLYAPNIKOV were meeting ZINOVIEV.

QUESTION: What do you know about gatherings of members of the Zinovievist organization?

ANSWER: Of the most significant gatherings attended, besides myself, both by members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization personally known to me and by active Zinovievists generally, I now recall two: one took place at the beginning of 1933 at BAKAEV's, and the other in 1932 at VUJOVIC's. Present at the gathering at BAKAEV's were SHAROV, VUJOVIC, BUDZINSKAYA, GORSHENIN, KHARITONOV, myself, LELEVICH, KOSTINA, and also the Trotskyists UFIMTSEV and SIMASHKO. Present at the gathering at VUJOVIC's were VUJOVIC, BUDZINSKAYA, myself, LELEVICH, BAKAEV, KOSTINA, GERTIK, GESSEN, GORSHENIN, and BRAVO. There were some other people; I do not now remember exactly who. These gatherings, like other, smaller meetings systematically practiced in our circle throughout all the past years, were one form of contact and communication between members of the organization. At these gatherings and meetings, topical questions of party policy were usually discussed in a counterrevolutionary spirit. Discussion of these questions was accompanied by counterrevolutionary attacks on the party leadership.

Recorded correctly from my words, read by me —

LELEVICH.

INTERROGATED BY — LULOV.
