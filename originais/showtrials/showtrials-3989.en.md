INTERROGATION RECORD

OF KOSTINA, Anna Porfiryevna

January 2, 1935.

QUESTION: Tell us what you wish to report to the investigation.

ANSWER: I consider it necessary to inform the investigation of the double-dealing, counterrevolutionary behavior in 1932–1933 of VKP(b) member and CC VKP(b) instructor MIRONOV Vladimir.

I personally met MIRONOV in 1931 at the apartment of former Trotskyist UFIMTSEV N.I., with whom he had a long-standing friendship.

MIRONOV frequently visited UFIMTSEV, and since UFIMTSEV's wife, SIMASHKO \[2\], is an old friend of mine, she and I saw each other almost every day. At UFIMTSEV's and SIMASHKO's home, I often met MIRONOV V.

From mid-1932 until February 1933, MIRONOV displayed in conversations an extremely negative attitude toward the CC apparatus, particularly its leadership. I cannot now recall all his specific characterizations and aspects of the CC's work about which he spoke then, but everything came down to the assertion that the CC apparatus was rotten, there was no self-criticism, no independent opinions, and sycophancy and opportunistic adaptation were the principal traits cultivated in the CC VKP(b) apparatus. I remember MIRONOV describing a CC plenum after which Comrade STALIN spoke at a meeting on work in the countryside. In telling this, he said that with us things were always done either before or after a plenum, while the plenum was a rubber-stamping parliament.

I also learned from him about the preparatory work for the resolution on political departments in the countryside. MIRONOV spoke ironically about this as an idea that had suddenly popped up: he had been instructed to write the draft resolution with almost no guidance, since the person assigning the task (I do not remember who) himself essentially did not properly understand the question. There were conversations on other subjects too, but I cannot now recall them. In conversations with MIRONOV, one almost always had to point out his excessive exaggeration, morbid perception of individual, perhaps isolated, cases and consequent incorrect assessment of the CC's entire work—that an apparatus such as he depicted could not pursue a correct political line. To this MIRONOV replied that he was an old apparatchik and knew what was normal and abnormal in apparatus work. MIRONOV's conversations at that time threw UFIMTSEV N.I. off balance, and the latter took them painfully. At the time, I did not inform the appropriate organizations of these conversations because of a liberal, philistine attitude toward them and an underestimation of their counterrevolutionary character. Now, under the influence of the Leningrad events, I think that counterrevolutionary actions may be concealed behind such conversations, and therefore consider it my duty to inform the investigation of this.

Recorded correctly from my words. Read to me—A. KOSTINA.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR—PETROVSKY.

Certified correct: Kazakova
