INTERROGATION RECORD

OF KOTOLYNOV, Ivan Ivanovich

11 December 1934.

QUESTION: Do you know of the existence of a directive letter from ZINOVIEV in 1928 instructing oppositionists to return to the Party for double-dealing purposes?

ANSWER: I know of no such directive; I have never seen it or heard anything about it.

QUESTION: Vladimir RUMYANTSEV's testimony establishes that at a gathering in RUMYANTSEV's apartment in July 1928 you, together with KHANIK and Ivan TARASOV, read and discussed ZINOVIEV's letter of 30/VI/1928, with its instruction: write the application so that it will be considered satisfactory. What do you say to this?

ANSWER: I did not read ZINOVIEV's letter and was not at the gathering in July 1928 at RUMYANTSEV's apartment. In this connection I recall the following episode: in the summer of 1928, I think with BOGOMOLNY, I went to RUMYANTSEV's apartment; I was not allowed in. I remember RUMYANTSEV's brother answering that he was not home, although I could hear his voice. I do not know why this happened or whether anyone was visiting RUMYANTSEV.

I also remember a meeting in the summer of 1928 at IOKHEL's apartment (Vasilievsky Island; I do not remember the exact address). There were about 12 or 13 people at this meeting; I remember SUROV, LUKIN, I. I. TARASOV, F. KUZNETSOV, V. RUMYANTSEV, IOKHEL and myself — I. KOTOLYNOV. At that time RUMYANTSEV, TARASOV and I drew up an application for admission to the Party. At the meeting, with the Party authorities' sanction, we worked to collect signatures for our application. I remember LUKIN and SUROV speaking against it.

At the same meeting a letter from SAFAROV, which I. I. TARASOV had brought from Moscow, was read. In the letter SAFAROV proposed returning to the Party. I. TARASOV brought this letter from Moscow straight to Sestroretsk, where RUMYANTSEV, SUROV and I were on holiday; I do not remember whether KHANIK was there. We read the letter in Sestroretsk and then at IOKHEL's apartment.

QUESTION: How often did you see TARASOV in recent years?

ANSWER: In recent years we met less often. In 1933 he was in Leningrad on business for the Electricians' Central Committee. This time TARASOV and I deliberately agreed not to go anywhere, so as to put an end to our old ties altogether.

In the summer of 1932 I went to the Crimea on holiday via Moscow; I do not remember whether he met me, but I stayed with him — TARASOV — between trains. From TARASOV's apartment we telephoned SAFAROV and then, together with our members, visited SAFAROV. We had no anti-Party conversations.

From 1/XII to 5/XII I was in Moscow on laboratory business and stayed with I. I. TARASOV. Apart from TARASOV, the only oppositionist I saw this time was FAIVILOVICH, at whose home TARASOV and I spent an evening; I then stayed there overnight. —

I. KOTOLYNOV

HEAD OF THE 6TH SECTION, SPO, GUGB — KORKIN
