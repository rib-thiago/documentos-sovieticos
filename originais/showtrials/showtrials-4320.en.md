INTERROGATION RECORD

Of Ivan Feliksovich KASPERSKY

dated January 7, 1935.

KASPERSKY, Ivan Feliksovich, born in 1890, town of Uglich, Yaroslavl Province; Leningrad, Vasilievsky Island, 10th Line, building 23, apartment 15. Russian, USSR citizen. Passport obtained in 1932 at the Leningrad regional militia. Mechanic at the Central Laboratory of Wire Communications of the Low-Current Electrotechnical Trust. Father formerly a musician in Uglich, mother a homemaker, both deceased, father in 1921, mother in 1924; owned a house in Uglich. Before the revolution worked as a mechanic in private workshops and at the former Robert Krug factories in Leningrad (now the “Vpered” factory). After the revolution a mechanic at the fuse factory. Wife Olga Grigoryevna KASPERSKAYA, 37, AUCP(b) member since 1919, secretary of the design bureau of the optical-mechanical trust (VOOMP); son Ivan, 18, nonparty, mechanic at factory No. 4, and three school-age children; two sisters live in Leningrad, one a homemaker, the other a pensioner. Education: completed parish school in 1902. Nonparty; AUCP(b) member from 1917 to 1934, expelled from the Party in 1934 as having automatically dropped out, not having undergone the 1933 purge. In 1927 expelled from the Party by the Fifteenth Congress of the AUCP(b) for belonging to the Zinovievist opposition, reinstated in 1928. Not subjected to repression. In 1934 prosecuted under Article 116 of the Criminal Code. On the military register, military category KP 8. From June 1917 until 1918 in the Red Guard at the fuse factory; from 1918 to 1921 in the Workers' and Peasants' Red Army as assistant regimental military commissar. Did not serve with the Whites. Did not participate in bands, counterrevolutionary organizations, or uprisings.

QUESTION: Tell us which former Zinovievists you maintained ties with and the nature of those ties.

ANSWER: I maintained ties with Moisei YAKOVLEV; Alexei Kirillovich FOKIN; Ivan Ivanovich BELONOGOV; Alexei Fomich SKOROMNY; Semyon Georgievich SEMENOV; Evdokiya KONDRATYEVA; Adam Pavlovich PANKO; and David Samoilovich SHEINBERG. I retained these ties from 1927 until recently.

After the Fifteenth Congress until the end of 1929, our ties were temporarily interrupted because I left Leningrad. From late 1929, through systematic and chance meetings and conversations, we preserved ourselves organizationally until the end of 1934.

QUESTION: Where did your meetings take place, who participated in them, and what was their nature?

ANSWER: Our meetings took place at the apartments of YAKOVLEV, FOKIN, PANKO, BELONOGOV, SKOROMNY, SHEINBERG, and myself. At the meetings we exchanged information on the Zinovievists' situation and held conversations on political subjects, criticizing a number of Party and government measures.

QUESTION: The investigation has information that you are a member of the Zinovievist organization. What can you testify on this question?

ANSWER: All our conversations and meetings had the character of gathering the old Zinovievist cadres.

Recorded from my words and read by me, in confirmation of which I sign: KASPERSKY.

INTERROGATED BY:

Operations officer, 4th SECTION, SPO — KOGAN.

Certified correct: Kazakova
