INTERROGATION RECORD

Of SAFAROV G. I., January 9, 1935.

In addition to my testimony, I inform the investigation of the following:

In his conversations with me in 1930–1934, MAGYAR constantly passed on information about the state of affairs in industry, referring chiefly to his conversations with LAUER, who holds a leading post in the People's Commissariat of Heavy Industry. A picture of insurmountable difficulties in industry was usually painted. Referring to LAUER, MAGYAR declared that the figures for pig iron and steel output were inflated, that “no one believes them,” that they were regarded as “pulled out of thin air,” as not corresponding to the state of affairs and “simply dictated by Sergo.” MAGYAR also related in 1932 that “Ural-Kuzbass has failed because no one thought about transport, and transport cannot carry the loads, there is no fuel, blast furnaces are stopping.” Reporting LAUER's words in 1933 and 1934, MAGYAR said that “there were disagreements in the Central Committee — they projected God knows what output figure, then everyone saw that it could not be left unreduced.” From the same source, MAGYAR reported an “enormous, insurmountable gap between planned construction and the availability of building materials,” and that tens and hundreds of millions were being thrown to the wind. From all these accounts by MAGYAR, it followed that LAUER presented the situation in industry and the state of economic construction in general to him in an anti-Party spirit, and I remember well that at every sharp upswing MAGYAR brought new “difficulties” from this source
