INTERROGATION RECORD

OF NIKOLAEV Leonid Vasilievich

dated 5/XII-1934.

QUESTION: The telephone number of the Latvian Consulate is written in your notebook. Explain how this entry came to be there.

ANSWER: I decided to contact the Latvian Consulate for the purpose of conducting counterrevolutionary work. This was a few days before the experimental gas attack was carried out in the city. I obtained the consulate’s telephone number and address from the information bureau. I went straight to the consulate, which was located on Herzen Street. The consul received me in his office on the third floor of the building. As I understood it, the consul lived there too; I inferred this because some rooms of the consulate clearly looked like living quarters.

During the conversation with the consul, his secretary, who spoke Russian well, was present in the office.

QUESTION: What conversation did you have with the consul?

ANSWER: As a precaution, I did not want to tell the consul the real purpose of my visit at once. I began by saying that relatives of mine lived in Riga and that I was due to receive an inheritance from them. The consul asked what kind of inheritance it was: real estate or money; I answered that it was money. I told the consul that I corresponded with relatives in Riga and that my mother lived in Siberia; I left him with the impression that I was Latvian and spoke Russian haltingly; incidentally, just in case, I had brought my grandfather’s Latvian passport with me. At my first meeting with the consul, I hoped to receive financial assistance from him.

The conversation with the consul ended at that point because his secretary, of whom I was wary, was present in the office; I decided to return another time to continue the conversation with the consul.

QUESTION: What exactly did you intend to discuss with the consul at your next meeting?

ANSWER: I intended to introduce myself as a Red Professor, say that I had valuable materials to forward abroad, and ask the consul for money.

QUESTION: Did a second meeting with the consul take place?

ANSWER: No, it did not. This is what happened: one of the following days I called the consul and asked him to receive me; he answered, “I cannot now; come tomorrow,” and specified the visiting hours. When I arrived at the consulate building, a car with license plate No. 10168, marked with a red stripe on the plate, was standing at the entrance; as I understood it, the car belonged to a military department. I became suspicious that preparations were being made to arrest me; therefore I went up only as far as the consulate premises and decided not to enter. I never went to the consulate again and decided to establish contact with the German Consulate.

QUESTION: What precise material did you intend to hand over to the consul?

ANSWER: I intended to give the consul some material and an article, critical and anti-Soviet in character, about the country’s internal situation.

QUESTION: Describe the features by which the consul could be identified.

ANSWER: The consul was tall, completely gray-haired, with his hair combed back.

Recorded accurately from my words and read back to me.

L. NIKOLAEV
