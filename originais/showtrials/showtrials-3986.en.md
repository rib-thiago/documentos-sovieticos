INTERROGATION RECORD

January 2, 1935.

of the accus\<ed\> GORSHENIN Iv\<an\> St\<epanovich\>

Question: Tell us in greater detail what you know about the member of your organization, prosecutor SAKHOV Boris.

Answer: The member of our organization SAKHOV Boris remained organizationally connected with it until recently, that is, until its liquidation in December 1934.

When visiting me, KUKLIN A.S., BRAVO and ROZNER, he, SAKHOV, constantly expressed a critical attitude toward the party leadership and the policy pursued by the CC of the VKP(b), particularly regarding the methods and pace of dekulakization and eviction of the kulaks.

SAKHOV personally visited me (in my apartment) only twice: once in 1933 after returning from an inspection trip to the Ukhta-Pechora concentration camp, and a second time in early 1934 after returning from Eastern Siberia, where he had traveled with a Special Commission of the CC of the VKP(b) inspecting the East Siberian Territory.

On both occasions, SAKHOV informed me in considerable detail about the results of these commissions' inspections, which, in his words, had uncovered a series of outrageous abuses demonstrating the unquestionable degeneration of local party organizations and organs of Soviet power.

He characterized these organizations as powerless to combat the phenomena discovered because, according to him, the central party and Sov\<iet\> organizations, instead of providing real assistance to localities, sent inexperienced and unsuitable people there who essentially assisted the elements of degeneration.

Regarding his inspection trip to the Ukht\<a-\>Pech\<ora\> concentration camp, SAKHOV told me that something nightmarish was going on there since, in his words, a gigantic number of people had been driven there who, owing to the absence of elementary conditions for keeping prisoners, were dying in droves every day.

Concerning his trip to Eastern Siberia (Irkutsk, Chita), SAKHOV told me he had been a member of a special commission appointed by the Politburo of the CC of the VKP(b), which uncovered a number of criminal and political offenses on the spot, including widespread infiltration of local party and Sov\<iet\> organizations by socially alien persons, including outright White Guards.

He told me that, as a result of the inspection, disagreements arose among individual Commission members when determining punishments for the guilty leaders of local organizations; during these disagreements he, SAKHOV, took an absolutely clear position on the necessity of the strictest punishment for everyone regardless of person, but allegedly the chairman of this commission, I think YAROSLAVSKY, disagreed with his arguments and essentially glossed over and concealed all these crimes from the party.

SAKHOV directly linked his removal from work in Moscow in the Republic's Prosecutor's Office with his work and conduct on the above-mentioned CC Commission.

He told me, among other things, that although his superiors in the Prosecutor's Office were trying in every way to retain him for work in Moscow, nothing came of it, since such was the CC's decision.

Question: Did SAKHOV know of your membership and role in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization?

Answer: Yes, he certainly knew. He undoubtedly also knew that I was close to ZINOVIEV and other members of the Moscow Center.

Question: What do you know about KARTASHEV, a former participant in the Zinovievist opposition living in Leningrad?

Answer: KARTASHEV Grigory, a member of the VKP(b) since 1918, a graduate of the IKP, now professor of Sov\<iet\> Law at the Leningr\<ad\> Inst\<itute\> of Sov\<iet\> Law, is also a member of our organization. He was personally connected with me, knew of the existence of the Moscow Zinovievist center and made use of my information about the sentiments of ZINOVIEV and other center members. In his political sentiments and convictions, KARTASHEV, like all of us, remained on the old Zinovievist positions of rejecting the current policy of the CC of the VKP(b) and the party leadership.

I sign to confirm the correctness of my testimony; I have read the record—GORSHENIN.

Interrogated by: DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO OF THE UGB—STROMIN.
