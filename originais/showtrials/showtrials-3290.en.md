TESTIMONY

of NAUMOV, Ivan Kupriyanovich, born in 1895, a native of Lopino village, Ostashkovo volost, Mozhaisk district, former Moscow province; residing in Moscow, 8 Bolshoi Patriarshy, apartment 13; director of aircraft factory no. 20; wife: Aleksandra Vasilievna LEPESHINSKAYA, aged 33; son Leonid, aged 11. Without property, secondary education, a member of the VKP(b) since 1913, with a break from December 1927 to October 1928.

The more deeply one analyzes the existence of the anti-Party counterrevolutionary group, the more indisputable becomes my contention that, in submitting a declaration of return to the Party during the Fifteenth Party Congress, this group sought not honest disarmament but its organizational preservation by deception, and preservation for the same counterrevolutionary Trotskyist purposes, that is, to return to the leadership by any means.

I maintain that, until very recently, the members of this group fully confided both their sentiments and their actions to me. Therefore, everything I say here about their activities, sentiments and instructions is true and can always be confirmed and substantiated by me in every way.

I turn to facts illustrating the counterrevolutionary activities and anti-Party conduct of the group as a whole and its individual members, illustrating their boundless hatred for the entire present Leninist leadership, especially comrade STALIN, and the continued existence of secret instructions to carry out the Zinovievist slogan of a bloc of all former groupings, including TROTSKY, SHLYAPNIKOV, UGLANOV and the like, through to TOMSKY, BUKHARIN, RYKOV and others inclusive.

First fact: On returning to Moscow I was invited to KAMENEV's to celebrate the New Year (1928), and saw for myself that the Right Zinovievist group maintained an organized existence, extending to internal collections of money for such a “celebration”. All those who had signed the declaration of the “23” were present (I do not remember only comrade MINICHEV) \[i\], as well as SOKOLNIKOV and BELA KUN \[1\]. This “celebration” must frankly be called a plenum of the group's Center, with sympathizers participating, to review the year and outline prospects for 1929. At so broad a plenum there were no direct speeches against the Leninist leadership, and particularly STALIN, but remarks were made aloud to neighbors, especially by GORSHENIN, KAPITONOV and GESSEN, about Lazar Moiseevich's allegedly fictitious revolutionary past, about comrade MOLOTOV's slow-wittedness; they did not hesitate to speak about his supposedly poor appearance, or to repeat the old tales about comrade STALIN's rudeness and so on. Interestingly, parallels were drawn with sympathetic assessments of TOMSKY, BUKHARIN and RYKOV, who supposedly only needed a little correction and reinforcement from the Left. An atmosphere of hostility to the leadership was felt throughout the evening.

Second fact: or rather, dozens of facts during 1929. During this period I personally met ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV more than once, and always, whatever they spoke about or one discussed with them, the conversation inevitably turned to the leadership, to inquiries about whether there were disagreements, little cracks and so forth among the Party leaders. Having nothing to oppose to the Party's general line except the old Trotskyist rubbish of the former platform, ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV, especially ZINOVIEV, could think of nothing but returning to the leadership. I well remember this conversation with him:

I: TROTSKY, of course, is permanently excluded from any future leadership, since his counterrevolutionary activities are clear.

ZINOVIEV: No, I cannot say that; you are oversimplifying; in politics processes develop in more complicated ways.

Interestingly, much later, around 1932, I heard the same attitude toward the possibility of TROTSKY's return to the leadership from S. YUDIN, after YUDIN renewed his connections with ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV.

Furthermore, already in the period following the last Party purge, GORSHENIN, in discussing SHLYAPNIKOV's expulsion, did not characterize him as a man of counterrevolutionary disposition, which is precisely what SHLYAPNIKOV is.

I return to 1929. The Right Zinovievist group began reaching beyond its own circle, seeking rapprochement with the Right-Left bloc. This happened before my eyes. B. KUSHNER represented the Right-Leftists; BAKAEV and FEDOROV represented ZINOVIEV; citizens KAMINSKY and LOMINADZE also attended. I also know that S. GESSEN was repeatedly given special authorization to negotiate with Moscow residents, and he himself told me of meetings with LOMINADZE and SHATSKIN and said that, on attitudes toward the kulak and on Party democracy, he could reach agreement with LOMINADZE. When I myself encountered LOMINADZE, SHATSKIN, STEN and KOSTROV, I observed the greatest sympathy on their part toward the former oppositionists. SEF also told me in Tiflis of the sympathetic attitude of LOMINADZE and others toward the former Leningrad people.

The interest of ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, SHAROV, FEDOROV and others in the Right-Left bloc reached a particular height when they learned that SYRTSOV, chairman of the Sovnarkom \[2\], had turned against the Party; then KUSHNER once reported that not only SYRTSOV but a number of military men were against STALIN, and that BLYUKHER in particular was allegedly in an unfavorable mood. Things then reached the point where ZINOVIEV, in my presence at his apartment (as he said, jokingly, but he was serious), considered whom to appoint where. Himself as chairman of the Communist International's Executive Committee; KAMENEV as chairman of the Sovnarkom; BAKAEV as chairman of the OGPU; EVDOKIMOV as Central Committee secretary; SOKOLNIKOV as people's commissar of Finance; and so on and so forth. He also envisaged BUKHARIN's participation; it would only be awkward, said ZINOVIEV, if he again dragged SLEPKOV and company along with him, though supposedly agreement had also been reached about SLEPKOV.

KAMENEV talked of nothing but “how to form a government”.

The Zinovievists reached beyond their own group not only toward the Right-Left bloc, but also sent their representatives into UGLANOV's camp. These representatives were SHAROV and FEDOROV; besides UGLANOV himself, Yakov RAVINSKY conducted negotiations on his behalf. The whole Party knows that these negotiations succeeded from the case of the counterrevolutionary SLEPKOV–UGLANOV–RYUTIN group.

Third fact: again, an entire series of facts. This concerns the organization of a deliberately directed connection between the Moscow and Leningrad groups. GERTIK told me more than once that he visited Leningrad and met RUMYANTSEV, LEVIN and MANDELSHTAM. Citizen FEDOROV told me the same. Furthermore, I know of ELKOVICH's journeys during his leave from Sverdlovsk through Moscow to Leningrad, when he had neither business nor family reasons to go there. He himself told me (in 1932 in Sverdlovsk) that he had gone to see his boys. Moreover, I maintain that, but for the special organizational work of the Right Zinovievists through GERTIK, SHAROV and FEDOROV, there would have been no group of RUMYANTSEV, KOTOLYNOV, MANDELSHTAM, LEVIN and others. There would have been none because I myself came at the time (1928) to dissolve this group of former supporters of SAFAROV, TARKHANOV, VARDIN and myself, and it was dissolved, as RUMYANTSEV and KOTOLYNOV assured me as early as 1929. Therefore the Zinovievists reestablished it through GERTIK, FEDOROV and SHAROV.

One of the former supporters of the so-called Zinovievist Left, I. I. TARASOV, who visited Leningrad comparatively recently (late 1932 or early 1933), told me with surprise that RUMYANTSEV, TSEITLIN and MANDELSHTAM gathered for drinking sessions and discussed such questions as whether we had a rupture of the link between town and countryside or a crisis of the dictatorship of the proletariat, and other similar questions. They talked about the leadership, about the desirability of ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV and the others returning to the leadership. But why rely on TARASOV's account? Take LILINA's funeral \[3\]. In its own way, this was a test of their strength, like the separate demonstration in the October days of 1927. All the Zinovievists were mobilized to agitate for acquaintances and former supporters to attend, almost with their families. During the procession SHAROV and LEVIN openly agitated, saying that it was wrong to bury her in the Aleksandr Nevsky Lavra, that it should have been on the Field of Mars, that the present Leningrad leadership showed its incompetence even in this funeral, and that surely we would not have organized it this way. After the funeral a meeting took place at SHAROV's apartment, where ZINOVIEV spoke directly about preserving their forces and rallying their supporters. Much later, at Dina GERTIK's funeral in 1933, the same planned and organized attempt to stage a demonstration was made. GORSHENIN organized this demonstration. Not only did he invite me twice, giving the precise assembly time, address and so forth, but he specifically checked why I had not attended. A whole campaign was built around Dina GERTIK's death, alleging that she was a victim of STALIN's regime, that Dina GERTIK had been abandoned without any help, and that Anya KOSTINA had provided an example of Party solidarity. BAKAEV, BRAVO and GORSHENIN were especially active in spreading this agitation.

The entire story of Dina GERTIK's illness and funeral once again attests to the Zinovievists' organizational work, to their use of every occasion to struggle against the Party leadership in order to secure leadership for themselves.

Fourth fact: MOLOTOV's appointment as chairman of the Sovnarkom was met with outright hatred. At GERTIK's apartment, where citizens FEDOROV, BRAVO, M. POZDEEVA and others were present, when I mentioned Vyacheslav Mikhailovich's name, GORSHENIN they threw out \[4\] the remark: “Ah, you mean BEETHOVEN?” “Why?” I asked. “Why?” answered GORSHENIN. “SKRYABIN!” Everyone present began, each in his own way, to justify his disagreement with comrade MOLOTOV's new appointment. They then began recounting who had heard what and where about alleged disagreements between comrade MOLOTOV and KAGANOVICH. BRAVO and GORSHENIN were particularly assiduous. That evening gave me the impression of a gathering of spiteful internal émigrés, and any person in a morbid, counterrevolutionary frame of mind could have found inspiration among the Zinovievists for any act against the leadership, against those who appointed such an unsuitable chairman of the Sovnarkom, from their point of view. The sentiments I saw that evening at GERTIK's apartment made such an impression on me that I simply could never go there again; meanwhile, meetings of the Zinovievist group's participants continued both at GERTIK's apartment and at GORSHENIN's, and work on finding ways to return to the leadership continued at them; there the group's members worked out tactics on arising issues. For example, when I met EVDOKIMOV after KOSTINA's expulsion and told him of my negative attitude toward her speech, EVDOKIMOV let slip this phrase: “We too advised her not to speak.”

Fifth fact: Shortly after ZINOVIEV's expulsion from the Party, I met VARDIN on Arbat Square and, answering his question about what I thought of ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's expulsion, said that I considered it absolutely correct. VARDIN flew into a rage, abused me and declared that I attributed greater guilt to ZINOVIEV than “the Stalinists themselves”. Later I learned that VARDIN had begun seeing ZINOVIEV, and this phrase indicates the existence of non-Stalinists, a Zinovievist rather than Stalinist attitude toward counterrevolutionary dealings with the RYUTIN–SLEPKOV group and others.

My conclusion: What we have is not only an attempt to preserve themselves as an organized group by returning to the Party through deception, but also the reestablishment of the group of RUMYANTSEV, KOTOLYNOV and others, and repeated forays beyond the group's boundaries in search of allies to return to the leadership in any composition, provided it is not the present Leninist composition headed by comrade STALIN. We see the group's degeneration into a closed, embittered organization, under whose influence any counterrevolutionary sentiments can arise and any methods of struggle against the Party leadership suggest themselves, including terrorist acts like the abominable murder of Sergei Mironovich KIROV. And I declare directly: political responsibility for comrade KIROV's death falls upon the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist group.

NAUMOV.
