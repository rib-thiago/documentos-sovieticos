INTERROGATION RECORD

OF DMITRIEV, Timofei Dmitrievich, dated 10/1-35.

QUESTION: What further testimony can you give about the Rostov Zinovievist group's ties with the Leningrad organization of Zinovievists?

ANSWER: I have already testified that from my departure from Leningrad in 1930 until 1934 I maintained correspondence and personal contact with members of the center of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization in Leningrad — LEVIN Vladimir, MYASNIKOV, and others. Correspondence with LEVIN Vladimir and others aimed to strengthen organizational ties with the Leningrad organization. In his letters LEVIN supplied me and GORDON, a member of the Rostov group, with information about the situation and sentiments of like-minded people in Leningrad and Moscow. He informed me about his contacts and the sentiments of center members BAKAEV, KUKLIN, and ZINOVIEV. Our organization's tactical directives for “growing into” the party in the interests of the organization's aims were also formulated by LEVIN Vl. in a letter to me (“…G. [that is, ZINOVIEV] sits at home doing nothing. I think this harms both him and others. One ought to insist on work. One ought without fail to seek conversations on this subject”). In the interests of maintaining organizational ties, strengthening links between the old Zinovievist cadres, and obtaining the appropriate information, I corresponded with a number of other people, particularly GAIDEROVA Z. In 1933 she informed me of her contact with GERTIK (a member of our organization's center), about LEVIN Vl., KORSHUNOV, and MYASNIKOV. She supplied the latter's address for contact. In one letter to me GAIDEROVA emphasizes the need to maintain contact with ZINOVIEV, informs me about his latest printed works, and ironically comments in an anti-party, double-dealing spirit on the so-called “repentances” (“Acknowledging one's mistakes is now such a business that everyone is ready for it, whether one has done anything or not”). The anti-party, counterrevolutionary character of my correspondence with GAIDEROVA and her political outlook are vividly enough illustrated by the following passage from her letter to me: “After all, one is not going to solemnly retell the columns of news from ‘Leningradskaya Pravda,’ or ardently develop and debate the brilliant thoughts of brilliant articles by today's arch-brilliant writers… That is beyond us.” A certain Ilya also informed me by letter of sentiments in Leningrad, about KORSHUNOV, GAIDEROVA, and ZINOVIEV's situation. I cannot recall who he is. I have already testified that LEVIN Vladimir informed me in his letters of “Leningrad affairs.” In one letter LEVIN tells me: “…I. I. [I. I. KONDRATIEV] left the Regional Executive Committee; in his place Abram IVANOV came from Moscow.” LEVIN comments on this news in a spirit of discontent: “No suitable candidates for this work ‘were found’ in Leningrad.” At the same time LEVIN informs me of GERTIK's arrival in Leningrad (a member of the Zinovievist organization's center), about PETERS, about all sorts of transfers of leading workers in Leningrad, and gives his assessments of certain political events. A. TOLMAZOV also supplied me with information from Leningrad.

QUESTION: A counterrevolutionary libel directed against the party leadership was found during the search of your home. Where did this document come from, to whom does it belong, and for what purpose did you keep it?

ANSWER: The counterrevolutionary libel entitled “Penitential,” directed against the party and discovered in my possession during the search, belongs to me. It was given to me in 1927 by the former oppositionist Olga YURGENS and was used by us in the struggle against the party.

QUESTION: In your previous testimony you stated that for a number of years, until 1934, as a member of the Zinovievist organization, you conducted practical work in Leningrad, Rostov, and Moscow to rally the Zinovievist cadres, establish contact between the Rostov group and the Moscow center, and that all this work was subordinated to the task of bringing ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV into the leadership.

How do you assess this activity of yours?

ANSWER: All my activity in the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization from the Fifteenth Congress until 1934, as well as the activity of the entire organization as a whole (including the Leningrad organization, the Rostov group, and the Moscow center), about which I testified in the records of my interrogations of 24, 25, and 30 December 1934, was from beginning to end a struggle against Soviet power, work to undermine the foundations of the proletarian dictatorship; in this struggle we joined the entire camp of Soviet power's bitterest enemies.

My guilt, like that of all members of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization, is further aggravated by the fact that, having returned to the party for maneuvering purposes, we conducted the struggle against the party and Soviet power from within the party, using for counterrevolutionary purposes the confidence shown us. Our struggle against Soviet power was therefore treacherous in character.

Recorded correctly from my words:

DMITRIEV.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE 1ST SECTION OF THE SPO — LULOV.

Certified true: Khvatov
