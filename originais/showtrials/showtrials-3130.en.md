INTERROGATION RECORD

OF KULISHER ROMAN MARKOVICH, DATED DECEMBER 17, 1934.

QUESTION: What prompted your request to be summoned for interrogation?

ANSWER: I wish to testify as follows:

On July 24, Leonid NIKOLAEV and I were traveling by train from Sestroretsk to Leningrad. Leonid NIKOLAEV first began asking me what existing internal Party groupings I knew of in general and, in particular, what I knew of the existence and activity of the members of the SYRTSOV–LOMINADZE group. I replied that I knew only that LOMINADZE himself worked at a factory in the Urals and had spoken at the Seventeenth Congress. Continuing the conversation, Leonid NIKOLAEV insistently asked whether I knew of a group of former oppositionists whose aim was to carry out terrorism against the Party leadership. I replied that terrorist groups had existed among the oppositionists and mentioned the group of SHLYAPNIKOV's so-called “Left Workers' Opposition,” which had existed in 1928. Leonid NIKOLAEV then asked what I knew of instances of attempts being organized on STALIN's life. I told him what I knew about the arrest in 1931–1932 of a terrorist group of the so-called “Peasant Party,” which had been preparing an attempt on STALIN.

QUESTION: What did Leonid NIKOLAEV tell you about the anti-Party organization of former oppositionists which he knew to exist in Leningrad?

ANSWER: Leonid NIKOLAEV told me that through his previous work in the regional Control Commission and Workers' and Peasants' Inspectorate he had encountered representatives of the former Zinovievist opposition, naming several surnames, including EVDOKIMOV's, and also mentioned, without naming surnames, the “FAT MEN,” as he put it. Leonid told me that this group still existed and was carrying on work hostile to the Party. As far as I remember, Leonid NIKOLAEV added that it would be interesting to establish contact and speak with someone from this group. I expressed my supposition that the group to which EVDOKIMOV belonged had broken up; NIKOLAEV objected and said that this was untrue and that the group was still active.

QUESTION: What conversation did you and Leonid NIKOLAEV have on that same occasion about an attempt on Comrade STALIN's life?

ANSWER: Answering Leonid NIKOLAEV's questions, as he continued to ask me about instances of attempts on STALIN's life, I told him of a case I knew from the newspapers: a terrorist had twice gone to the Kremlin, waiting for STALIN to leave; on first seeing STALIN the terrorist had not fired, and the second time, when he was at the Kremlin to shoot and kill STALIN, he had not managed to do so because he was arrested.

I told Leonid NIKOLAEV that access to the Kremlin had formerly been relatively easy and that STALIN's protection had not been arranged securely enough for him. Now, however, judging by the restrictions and strictness governing admission to the Kremlin, it was very difficult to get inside. As evidence, I related my visit to the Kremlin in April or May 1934, when I went to see Comrade LEVICHEV in the Secretariat of the RSFSR Council of People's Commissars at his summons: even then, on the first day I was summoned, I could not gain immediate entry until my admission to the Kremlin had been cleared with the GPU, as I was told at the People's Commissariat of Heavy Industry.

The entire meaning and content of Leonid NIKOLAEV's conversation with me on the train was directed toward finding out from me how possible it was to enter the Kremlin with the objective of making an attempt on STALIN's life.

Recorded correctly from my words, read by me.

KULISHER

INTERROGATED BY:

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE OO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR — SOSNOVSKY

Certified true: M. Koroleva
