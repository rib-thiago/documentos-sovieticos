INTERROGATION RECORD

OF KULISHER R. M., dated December 17, 1934.

QUESTION: What can you add to or clarify in your previous testimony concerning what Leonid NIKOLAEV said to you and what you said to him about the possibilities of carrying out a terrorist attack on Comrade Stalin?

ANSWER: I acknowledge that between October 12 and 15 of this year, Leonid NIKOLAEV, walking with me from 31 Tchaikovsky Street to the corner of Liteiny Avenue and being in a state of extreme bitterness against the Party (about which I testified earlier), directed this bitterness specifically toward the leadership of the Leningrad Regional Committee and the leadership of the entire Party — the Central Committee. Leonid NIKOLAEV told me that he had already decided to embark on active struggle against the Party leadership and was ready to kill KIROV.

In the same embittered, excited, and determined tone, Leonid NIKOLAEV went on to tell me that he would not stop short of making an attempt on STALIN's life.

I regarded these words of Leonid NIKOLAEV's as the result and expression of his personal nervous disturbance and agitation, since he connected them with his personal dissatisfaction with life and his own situation.

QUESTION: It has been established that earlier, before this conversation with you, Leonid NIKOLAEV was determined to go to Moscow. What do you know about this from him?

ANSWER: Leonid NIKOLAEV did indeed tell me on September 27 that he needed to go to Moscow as soon as possible to obtain a voucher for a health resort there. He tried to persuade me to go to Moscow with him, but I said that I could not go at present and would have the opportunity in December. Leonid NIKOLAEV then asked me to arrange for him to stay in Moscow with one of my acquaintances. I said that I usually stayed with the BIRMANs (they are old acquaintances of mine; Raisa Efimovna BIRMAN lives on Vorontsovskaya Street), but that I felt it would be improper for me to send him, Leonid, to them. During the same conversation Leonid NIKOLAEV told me that he intended to take his brother Pyotr NIKOLAEV with him to Moscow.

QUESTION: What can you add to or clarify in your previous testimony concerning Leonid NIKOLAEV's participation in the counterrevolutionary group of the former Zinovievist opposition?

ANSWER: Our conversation with Leonid NIKOLAEV on the train on July 24, about which I testified earlier, began with Leonid NIKOLAEV telling me that there was a group of former Zinovievists in Leningrad and that this group was still carrying on work hostile to the Party. Leonid informed me of this in the following terms: “The group of the former Zinovievist opposition, now gathering around EVDOKIMOV (among others, Leonid named one other member of the group whose surname I have now forgotten — a blind former partisan commander), has lain low for the time being, but at the right moment it will yet show what it can do.” Throughout this conversation Leonid NIKOLAEV spoke so definitely and clearly about the existence of this group of former Zinovievists that I had no doubt that Leonid NIKOLAEV not only knew of its existence but was himself connected with its members and fully informed of its present activity and its significance for the counterrevolutionary struggle against the Party in the future, whenever they had the opportunity.

In addition, Leonid NIKOLAEV told me that he knew of the existence in Leningrad, apart from Evdokimov's group, of several other groups representing different strands of the former opposition, and that these groups, situated on the outskirts of Leningrad and rallying around individual prominent figures of the former opposition, had not withdrawn into themselves, sometimes did not even maintain contact with one another (group with group), but could become active at a suitable moment.

Recorded correctly from my words, read by me —

KULISHER

INTERROGATED BY:

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE OO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD — SOSNOVSKY
