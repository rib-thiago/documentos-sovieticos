INTERROGATION RECORD

OF BAKAYEV Ivan Petrovich—

January 3, 1935.

Question: Do you acknowledge that you belonged to an anti-party group based in Moscow and consisting of former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc?

Answer: I acknowledge that such a group did indeed essentially exist in Moscow and that it included former participants in the Zinovievist-Trotskyist bloc.

The group included ZINOVIEV, KAMENEV, EVDOKIMOV, GORSHENIN, KUKLIN, FYODOROV, GERTIK, SHAROV, PERIMOV and myself.

Each of us, when meeting in the years after the Fifteenth Congress of the VKP(b), felt cut off from the party and not integrated with it.

Below I shall try to characterize the anti-party profile of each member of the group.

ZINOVIEV—he considered, together with all of us, that the decisions of the Fifteenth Congress of the VKP(b) removed the disagreements between the opposition and the party, that these decisions were adopted to supersede proposals previously advanced by the opposition, and that their adoption had been influenced by the struggle the opposition had waged within the party. ZINOVIEV expressed doubts as to whether the congress's decisions would be implemented and, when implemented, whether they would be distorted in practice.

ZINOVIEV characterized the results of the Five-Year Plan's implementation—January 1933—by saying that overhead costs had been excessively high during its execution; it followed that another possibility existed for transforming the country without such overhead costs, and that the party leadership was to blame for this; that is, this assertion contained old opposition refrains about the leadership's incapacity.

On collectivization, ZINOVIEV said in conversations with me that it was being carried out coercively, that the excesses occurring were the fault not only of localities but also of the party leadership; regarding the latter, he pointed out that STALIN's letter on dizziness from success had appeared late and that collectivization in the forms in which it was being carried out led to the countryside's ruin.

Furthermore, he repeatedly developed the idea that the party leadership did not inform the party of the real state of affairs in the country.

ZINOVIEV sowed doubts about the possibility of implementing the party's decision to raise the working people's material standard two- or threefold as a result of implementing the Five-Year Plan; naturally, statements of this sort were directly anti-party in character.

ZINOVIEV usually characterized the struggle against the Right within the party as clearly insufficient, saying that resistance to the Right was indecisive. This was expressed in terms casting doubt on how sincerely the party was fighting the Right.

From conversations with ZINOVIEV, I understood that he considered the most important resolutions to have been adopted by the party CC without observing inner-party democracy.

Thus, for example, unless I am mistaken, the resolution on grain deliveries, adopted in 1932 by a CC decision immediately after a plenum, was regarded by ZINOVIEV as a decision that should have been made by the plenum.

I knew from ZINOVIEV about LOMINADZE's sentiments; ZINOVIEV said that the latter disagreed with a number of the party's most important decisions on internal policy. I concluded that ZINOVIEV was holding political meetings with representatives of the Right-Left bloc.

ZINOVIEV sharply condemned the state of affairs in the ECCI, considering that despite the rise of the revolutionary movement it was being left without help from the leadership, and that MANUILSKY and PYATNITSKY were not fulfilling their roles.

ZINOVIEV expressed dissatisfaction with the leadership of the VKP(b), particularly STALIN, of whom he spoke in indecent terms.

ZINOVIEV and we, the group's other members, considered that the party was isolating us former oppositionists and that our position within the party was difficult.

Recorded correctly from my words; I have read the record—I. BAKAYEV.

Interrogated by: ASSISTANT HEAD OF THE EKO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR—DMITRIYEV.

Certified correct: Biryukov
