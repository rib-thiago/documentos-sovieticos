INTERROGATION RECORD

of G. E. ZINOVIEV, conducted on January 18, 1935.

In addition to the testimony I gave the court, I consider it necessary to communicate the following facts, some of which I have recalled, while I told you about others before the trial:

1. In 1932 ZOF came to my dacha in Ilinskoye; he had at one time belonged to the so-called Leningrad Zinovievist opposition. Moreover, I was personally connected with him because in 1917 he organized my shelter at Razliv near Sestroretsk when I was in hiding.

Officially ZOF withdrew from the Leningrad opposition in 1927. The occasion for meeting me in 1932 was that, being friendly with old EMELYANOV, he wanted to talk to me about the proposed publication of the latter's memoirs. During this meeting ZOF showed himself to be very anti-party in his sentiments. In the course of our conversation he said, in particular: “Wait a year. The Central Committee will not withstand all the contradictions of the economic situation, the party regime, and so on for more than a year.” In this connection he refers to the prevalence of such sentiments among economic administrators; he named no specific surnames.

2. ROZIN came to my dacha together with ZOF. I had known ROZIN since 1917. He frequently visited me in Moscow in 1928 and 1929, when he worked in the Moscow Committee of the AUCP(b). At that time ROZIN passed on all sorts of gossip, rumors, and information gleaned from the summaries to which he had access in the Moscow Committee.

ROZIN's political character is defined by his connection with SHLYAPNIKOV. ROZIN did not participate in my conversation with ZOF, as though leaving us to talk face to face.

I met ROZIN again in 1934 in Kislovodsk. During this chance meeting ROZIN, who works in Smolensk at Military District Headquarters, told me the following about the situation in the army: that a large army was concentrated in the frontier areas and that, since there was no war, the uncertainty of the situation created tense

sentiments. In connection with this, there was demoralization among the commanders, suicides, and so on. From my conversation with ROZIN I understood that he remained on his old political positions.

3). I met TRIFONOV, KAMENEV's former deputy at Glavkontsesskom, at KAMENEV's; he himself was also close to SMILGA.

TRIFONOV had been a Trotskyist since either 1921 or 1923. In 1932 he held particularly anti-party sentiments. I knew this from KAMENEV and from TRIFONOV personally. I remember that, before going to the Central Control Commission in connection with the Ryutin case, I found TRIFONOV at KAMENEV's. He was indignant at our being called to account and said: “They will not dare expel you. Most likely you will soon be called into the leadership.” As I recall, TRIFONOV also told us about SOLTS's anti-party sentiments. To my characterization of TRIFONOV I must add that, although personally not very active, he is very persistent in his anti-party sentiments. I must particularly note that, reading Trotsky's “bulletins,” I noticed that the information in the “Letters from Moscow” section coincided closely with what I had heard from TRIFONOV and VAGANIAN [1], or what others told me on the authority of both.

4. VAGANIAN — I met him in 1932. He was then full of all sorts of rumors and gossip and was the main breeding ground of anti-party sentiments.

5). In his first conversations with myself and KAMENEV, STEN said that “it is now clear that Trotsky was right about everything, including your group (the Zinovievists). In all honesty, this must be admitted.” STEN said that this view of Trotsky was the assessment of the SHATSKIN–LOMINADZE group. When I expressed a wish to meet them, STEN said I would hardly learn anything new from a conversation with them, since he reflected their views.

During negotiations with myself and KAMENEV, on the question of our disagreements with the Trotskyists, STEN put forward the following idea as a compromise: the question of disagreements with Trotsky was not topical and did not prevent joint action.

6. MAGYAR — in addition to what I testified about him, I wish to report that at our last meeting he told me that terrorists had entered our country across the Minsk frontier. MAGYAR said that there were four terrorists, that three of them had been caught, and that several thousand Communists had been mobilized to capture them. MAGYAR also reported that there had allegedly been attempts by the terrorists to climb over the Kremlin wall. This conversation with MAGYAR was a chance one in the street; he said he had heard about it in Comintern circles.

7. V. ROMM — our correspondent in America, was a Trotskyist. When our bloc with the Trotskyists broke up, ROMM told me in 1928 that he was moving from Trotskyist positions to ours. ROMM was a friend of my former secretary BOGDAN. I have not seen ROMM since 1928.

8. PUTNA — a military official, was a Trotskyist. He visited me in Moscow at the beginning of 1929. The ostensible occasion for the meeting was his handing me a photograph of LILINA that he had taken shortly before her death. PUTNA then told me that he had moved from Trotskyist to Zinovievist positions. I never met him again.

9. BITKER — worked abroad, was a Trotskyist. During the period of the bloc with the Trotskyists, Trotsky personally told me of BITKER that he considered him trustworthy and would not want anyone to know this, since anything could always be sent abroad through him. BITKER was a clandestine contact of Trotsky's, concealed even from the joint center.

10. POTEMKIN — a diplomatic official. During the period of the bloc with Trotsky I knew him as a Trotskyist on the basis that Trotsky then told me he considered him one of his own. I did not meet him personally.

11. In addition, I knew as a Trotskyist a diplomatic official who is now in either Uruguay or Paraguay, in any event in South America. I do not know his surname; he is tall, with dark hair.

I also recall that in 1927 Trotsky spoke to me of a concealed Trotskyist who was then either head of Social Insurance or a prominent Social Insurance official.

G. ZINOVIEV.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE EKO, GUGB, NKVD OF THE USSR — MIRONOV.

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO, GUGB, NKVD OF THE USSR — LYUSHKOV.

Certified true:
