To comrade I. V. Stalin

Just now (December 16, at 7:30 in the evening), comrade Molchanov and a group of Chekists have arrived at my apartment and are conducting a search.

I tell you honestly, comrade Stalin: from the moment I returned from Kustanai on the Central Committee's instructions, I have not taken a single step, spoken a single word, written a single line or had a single thought that I should have had to conceal from the Party, from the Central Committee or from you personally. I thought of only one thing: how to earn the trust of the Central Committee and your trust personally, how to persuade you to include me in the work.

I have nothing, and can have nothing, except old archives (everything accumulated over more than thirty years, including the years of opposition).

I am guilty of nothing, nothing, nothing before the Party, before the Central Committee or before you personally. I swear to you by everything that can be sacred to a Bolshevik; I swear to you by the memory of Lenin.

I cannot even imagine what could arouse suspicion against me. I implore you to believe this honest word. I am shaken to the depths of my soul.

G. Zinoviev

RGASPI, F. 17, Op. 171, D. 199, sheet 45.

Published in: Izvestiya TsK KPSS, 1989, no. 7, pp. 70–71.
