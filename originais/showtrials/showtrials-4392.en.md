INTERROGATION RECORD

Of SMILGA Ivar Tenisovich, dated January 10, 1935.

SMILGA I.T., b\<orn\> in 1892, member of the VKP(b) since 1907, with an interruption from December 1927 to January 1930; was expelled for factional activity. In January 1928 he was exiled by the OGPU Collegium to the Narym region for three years for participation in counterrevolutionary Trotskyist work. In June 1929, following a declaration of his break with Trotskyism, he was released early.

QUESTION: The investigation possesses information that until recently you waged a struggle against Soviet power and the VKP(b)?

ANSWER: Beginning in 1926 I was one of the leaders of the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist organization and an author of counterrevolutionary documents of that organization.

In 1929 I submitted a declaration of withdrawal from the opposition. From 1929 until recently I did not conduct a factional struggle in the form in which I had conducted it before submitting the declaration.

QUESTION: In what form did you conduct the struggle after 1929?

ANSWER: I did not sever ties with certain former participants in the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist organization. When meeting them throughout 1930–1934, on different occasions and on various issues, I expressed my anti-Soviet, anti-party views to them and listened to their anti-Soviet propositions, and thus in fact conducted counterrevolutionary work against Soviet power and the VKP(b).

QUESTION: What counterrevolutionary views did you express to the former participants in the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist organization with whom you maintained ties from 1930 until the day of your arrest?

ANSWER: Basically, these counterrevolutionary views amounted to the following:

1\) the assertion that the party was incorrectly planning capital investment in the national economy;

2\) the assertion that there was a lack of coordination and disproportions in the national economy;

3\) the assertion that administrative measures in the national economy were excessive at the expense of economic measures;

4\) discrediting the leadership of the VKP(b).

QUESTION: With which former participants in the Trotskyist organization did you maintain ties during this period (1930–1934)?

ANSWER: I cannot recall all the persons over this entire period.

QUESTION: Can you recall them for the period from 1932 until the day of your arrest?

ANSWER: Over the last three years I maintained ties with the following persons, former participants in the counterrevolutionary Trotskyist organization: 1) PREOBRAZHENSKY E. (works, I believe, in the People's Commissariat of State Farms), 2) ALSKY (Karabugaz), 3) MALTSEV B. (works at some aircraft factory), 4) KOZLOVSKY Ch. (I do not know what he does), 5) EMANUEL (I do not know his workplace), 6) GAEVSKY D. (I have not seen him since 1932), 7) VERZHBLOVSKY D. (lost contact in 1932), 8) RIVASH T. (last saw him in 1934), 9) DELIBASH N. (works at a theater studio), 10) SMIRNOV I.N. (arrested), 11) VUJOVICH V. (works in Tashkent as director of an institute), 12) KAMENEV L.B. (arrested), 13) VORONSKY A. (writer, works in Moscow), 14) ROBINSON, 15) BLISKAVITSKY N. (arrested), 16) ABRAMOVICH I. (works in the South at some metallurgical plant), 17) DROBNIS Ya. (met him in late 1933 or early 1934 in Tashkent; I do not know where he works now), 18) RAFALKES S. (works in Tashkent in the public education administration).

In addition, during this period I believe I met DREITSER and OKHOTNIKOV, but I cannot vouch for the accuracy of these recollections of meetings with them.

QUESTION: Were the ties you mentioned with the persons listed above merely personal ties, or ties with people conducting clandestine work?

ANSWER: I had no organizational ties with them. Whether they had organizational ties, I do not know.

QUESTION: Did you maintain ties with KORNITSKY (arrested for clandestine Trotskyist work), SAFAROV and KAPITONOV (arrested in the Leningrad case)?

ANSWER: In 1932 I did indeed maintain ties with KORNITSKY, KAPITONOV, SAFAROV and the now deceased BOGDANOV. I expressed to these persons my counterrevolutionary views on particular issues of the policy of the party and Soviet power.

QUESTION: What was your conversation with ZALUTSKY, who visited you at your apartment in 1932, about?

ANSWER: I do not remember the content of the conversation.

QUESTION: What did your conversations on political topics with ZINOVIEV amount to?

ANSWER: I do not remember; I spoke with ZINOVIEV shortly after returning from exile.

QUESTION: You had Trotsky's book “The History of the Russian Revolution” in German. Where did you obtain it?

ANSWER: In 1931 I received from BOGDANOV (who worked under me at Gosplan, a former Trotskyist who died in 1932) the first volume of TROTSKY's “The History of the Russian Revolution” in German. This book was in my possession for a few hours.

QUESTION: To whom did you pass it?

ANSWER: I returned it to BOGDANOV; I do not know what he did with it.

QUESTION: From whom did you learn in 1932 of the existence of an illegal platform?

ANSWER: In 1932 L.B. KAMENEV, in Moscow or Gagra, told me he had heard of the existence of some right-wing platform. I did not see it.

QUESTION: Do you acknowledge that, by concealing from the party the fact that an illegal platform was circulating, you committed a crime?

ANSWER: I consider that I made a mistake.

Recorded correctly from my words and read by me.

(I. SMILGA)

INTERROGATED BY:

(G. MOLCHANOV)

RGASPI F. 671, Op. 1, D. 127, fols. 198–202.
