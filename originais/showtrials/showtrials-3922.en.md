INTERROGATION RECORD

Of SAFAROV G. I., December 30, 1934.

On the questions put to me by the investigation, I testify:

A. TOIVO, who worked at Khlebotsentr until its liquidation, belonged to the Zinovievist group and fought very actively against the Party. EVDOKIMOV, KUKLIN, and BRAVO also worked there, and he was connected with them. I met him in the winter of 1931–1932; he was proud of the “good company” assembled at Khlebotsentr, and from his words one could understand that he continued to hold anti-Party Zinovievist views. He himself is a person with a very strong veneer of philistinism, but eager for gossip, rumors, and the like, and fond of spreading them.

MIRONOV — a former participant in the Fourteenth Congress, who, as far as I remember, previously worked in Pskov; a Zinovievist. I cannot say precisely, but I believe that in the first half of 1932 he was in Leningrad and sought contact with Zinovievists, as, I believe, SEREDOKHIN told me. MIRONOV is an old Zinovievist who retained his sympathies for the anti-Soviet Zinovievist group.

MIKHAILOV, who worked in the Volodarsky district and was also the organizer of one of the Party collectives and a member of the district committee in 1925, is an active Zinovievist. I last saw him in 1928, after the return from Achinsk. He sharply condemned acceptance of the Party line, was indignant at the “inconsistency,” and then became close to V. RUMYANTSEV and S. MANDELSHTAM in accordance with his anti-Party orientations.

LOBACHOV — chairman of the factory committee at the “BOLSHEVIK” plant during the period when Konst. SOLOVYOV was Party organizer there, one of the chief organizers of the Trotskyist-Zinovievist counterrevolutionary bloc in the Volodarsky district. In 1925–1927, as can be judged from V. RUMYANTSEV's reports relating to that period, he displayed a sharply hostile attitude toward Party policy. Personally, he was quite close to K. SOLOVYOV.

KORSHUNOV — from the former City district; later, as far as I remember, he worked at the “MARTI” plant. From a meeting with him in Kislovodsk in the summer of 1931, I remember that he said then that he still considered himself to belong to the Zinovievist group.

LEBEDEV — from the Vyborg district, a close friend of A. KUKLIN, with whom the latter met regularly until recently; an active Zinovievist who had “weight” in the Zinovievist group thanks to his connections in the Vyborg district.

LIZDIN — signed the platform and refused to return to the Party, considering such a return “ruinous.” He remained in this position of hostility toward the Party until recently, as I also judge from the account of Moisey YAKOVLEV, who told me about this in 1934.

Ivan KASPERSKY — from the Vasilyevsky Ostrov district, belonged among the active Zinovievists. Having lost his Party card after reinstatement, he showed no desire to restore his standing in the Party and took to endless drunkenness.

ZARGE — an active Trotskyist who, I believe, returned from exile in 1932. Meeting me on Kominterna Street in mid-1932, he said: “Everything could have been different if the Zinovievists had not betrayed” (the Trotskyists are meant).

GOLODETS — a rabid Trotskyist who was with me in exile in Achinsk. There he connected himself with a group of Jewish speculators who ensured the forwarding of his letters and his contacts with other Trotskyists throughout Siberia. A sharply hostile anti-Soviet element. In 1932, I caught a glimpse of him near the Comintern in Moscow, but did not speak to him because our personal relations had broken off during our time in Achinsk.

LOBASTOV — a former Party member in Achinsk, later expelled for drunken brawls. In Achinsk, during my stay there, he considered himself one of the “leaderless.” In 1928, he came to see me in Saratov on the pretext that he had been left without money after a trip to Moscow to the Central Control Commission, where he had gone to request reinstatement in the Party. In reality, as someone later told me in Moscow — I do not remember who — he had gone to Rakovsky, who was then in Saratov, to obtain Trotskyist directives and contacts for exiled Trotskyists.

Regina BUDZINSKAYA — VUYOVICH's wife, maintained close contact with VAGANYAN \[i\] and LELEVICH throughout. When we met in the summer of 1934 in a Gort store, she said that VAGANYAN, who had moved to Moscow, frequently visited them. She gave no details of these meetings.

Pyotr KIKODZE — I believe, editor of “Literaturnaya gazeta” in Tiflis, a close friend and pupil of VARDIN. He spent his days and nights at the latter's home in 1932, and VARDIN boasted that he was “his man” in every respect.

NASONOV — formerly worked in the Comintern and is now head of a Political Department in the Vinnitsa region; an active “leftist” who, in conversations in 1932, viciously abused the Party leadership and Party policy. He was closely connected with LOMINADZE and MAGYAR, whom he visited on all his trips to Moscow until 1934.

TATAROV — a very nimble double-dealer who invariably tried not to show it, but was connected with the Zinovievists. At meetings until recently, he always took an interest in “Grigory Yevseyevich's health and mood,” emphasizing his “secret” solidarity with the “wronged.” At the same time, in public he sought just as zealously to demonstrate his “devotion” to the Party line.

ZINOVIEV mentioned the YEMELYANOVS, the old man and his sons, when I met him at Ilyinskoye in July 1932. He said approximately the following: “Look, even old YEMELYANOV is indignant at the situation, and his sons are good fellows, fully sympathetic to us.

It is significant that such an old Bolshevik family is with us.”

Written in my own hand — SAFAROV.

INTERROGATED BY:

Head of the 1st Section of the SPO of the GUGB of the NKVD of the SSOR — PETROVSKY.

Certified true: V. Stoyko
