TESTIMONY OF TARASOV, Ivan Ivanovich, January 6, 1935.

In response to the questions put to me by the investigation, I testify:

V. SOBOL. I have not seen him for a very long time, about seven years. I recall him as a man of extreme attitudes.

GURALSKY. Before the 15th Congress he was one of the most active oppositionists and, as far as I remember, apparently had international opposition contacts. I know nothing about him since the 15th Congress.

KOZHURO. GURALSKY’s wife. Took an active part in the opposition throughout. I have heard nothing about her since the 15th Congress.

IOKHEL. I last saw him around the end of 1933 at YAKOVLEV, Nikolai’s apartment in Moscow, where I learned that during the latest party purge IOKHEL had been expelled because at the purge itself he defended the idea that the Zinovievist opposition was much better than the Trotskyists and that they should not be confused with one another. Such a statement undoubtedly demonstrates that the man is still entirely in the grip of counterrevolutionary Zinovievist ideas. I also heard that before the purge he worked in some fishing association. Throughout the period after the 15th Congress I saw him in 1928 in Leningrad, and then at YAKOVLEV’s for the second time. I do not know where he is now.

MAGYAR. To put it briefly, he is the secret counselor of all anti-party factions and groupings; this is precisely how he impressed himself on my memory from the time of the Zinovievist opposition before the 15th Congress and in the first period (in 1928) after the 15th Congress. He was always connected with almost every anti-party current and could tell in detail what was happening among the “pure” Trotskyists and simultaneously what was happening at Zinovievist headquarters. In the party’s eyes he somehow always remained irreproachable. I have not seen MAGYAR since 1928–29.

DMITRIEV, Nikolai. A year to a year and a half ago he was quite definitely in favor of unconditionally bringing ZINOVIEV into the party leadership; hostility toward the party leadership could be sensed in him.

BULAKH, ROZOVSKY. In the first period after the 15th Congress they apparently visited ZINOVIEV (see my testimony).

GRINBERG. I believe he was connected with ZINOVIEV until the most recent period (see my testimony).

VUJOVIC. I have not seen him for about five years. During the opposition he was among the most active oppositionists, with extensive international contacts.

BUDZINSKAYA (VUJOVIC’s wife). An active oppositionist, with international contacts before the 15th Congress. I have not seen her for about five or six years either.

DOMBSKY, a Pole. I remember him as an active oppositionist with international contacts. He was closely connected on a daily basis with a Polish woman (I do not remember her surname) who lived on Malaya Lubyanskaya Street. This Polish woman was apparently called Zosya.

Both DOMBSKY and Zosya were closely connected with VUJOVIC and BUDZINSKAYA. I have not seen either DOMBSKY or Zosya for about six years.

KUSHNER, Boris. A friend of Musya NATANSON and NAUMOV. During the Trotskyist-Zinovievist opposition he apparently did not formally belong to it, but sympathized and was connected with it through NATANSON. Later, as I heard from someone (probably NAUMOV), he was connected with the Right Opposition.

I. TARASOV.

INTERROGATED BY:

HEAD OF THE 6TH SECTION OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB OF THE NKVD OF THE USSR — KORKIN.
