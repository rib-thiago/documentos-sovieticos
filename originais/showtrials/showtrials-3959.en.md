INTERROGATION RECORD

OF A.K. SEREDOKHIN, January 1, 1935.

Question: During the search of your apartment, a pocket notebook was seized containing numerous addresses of Zinovievists living in Leningrad and Moscow. Do you acknowledge the notebook now presented to you as your property?

Answer: The notebook presented to me by the investigation does in fact belong to me and was seized during the search of my apartment; I personally handed this notebook to the NKVD officers conducting the search.

Question: Please enumerate all the addresses of Zinovievists living both in Moscow and in Leningrad entered in this notebook.

Answer: 1) ABRAMOVICH Efim Konstantinovich, 2) ALEKSANDROV Aleksandr, 3) BRODSKY Villi, 4) GRIGORYEV Aleksandr, 5) GLUMAKOV Fyodor Alekseyevich, 6) TREIFA Pyotr Adamovich, 7) EVDOKIMOV Grigory Yeremeyevich, 8) KUKLIN Aleksandr Sergeyevich (Moscow), 9) KOTOLYNOV Ivan, 10) KORSHUNOV Efim Konstantinovich, 11) KATSNELSON Mulya, 12) LEIKIN, 13) LOBASHEV Gennady Ivanovich, 14) LUKIN Vasily, 15) LEVIN Vladimir, 16) LEGKOV Nikolai, 17) MINAYEV Semyon, 18) MATTEI G.I., 19) MANDELSHTAM Sergei, 20) NAUMOV Ivan Kupriyanovich (Moscow), 21) NALIVAIKO Foma, 22) NIKANOROV, 23) RUMYANTSEV Vladimir, 24) REM, 25) SAFAROV G.I. (Moscow), 26) SAFRONOV Pyotr, 27) SOLOVYOV Konstantin Stepanovich, 28) TARKHANOV Sergei, 29) TOLMAZOV Andrei, 30) FAIVILOVICH Leonid Yakovlevich (Moscow), 31) FADEYEV Fyodor, 32) FYODOROV Aleksandr, 33) KHANIK Lev Osipovich, 34) KHALUPO Boris, 35) TSEITLIN Yakov, 36) SHE\<I\>NBERG.

Question: For what purpose did you keep track of and take an interest in the places of residence of former Zinovievists?

Answer: I met most of them. We met by chance; I wrote down their addresses without any particular purpose—out of simple interest in my old friends from our common struggle in the ranks of the Zinovievists.

Question: Which of the above persons did you meet recently, and under what circumstances did this occur?

Answer: I met the following persons:

EVDOKIMOV, KUKLIN and SAFAROV in Moscow in 1931 (in December), during my stay in Moscow on official business; FAIVILOVICH in 1934, also in Moscow. In Leningrad recently, I also happened to meet and talk with approximately the following persons: 1) ALEKSANDROV—through our work together in the regional Supply Department, 2) BRODSKY—at Smolny, 3) GRIGORYEV Aleksandr, 4) EVDOKIMOV G.E., in Moscow in 1931 and in Leningrad at Smolny in 1933, 5) KUKLIN in 1934 at Smolny, 6) KATSNELSON through our work together, 7) LEIKIN in the Business Club dining room, where we often had lunch, 8) LOBASHEV—at the Leningrad Soviet, 9) NALIVAIKO—on Lensnab premises, 10) LEVIN Vladimir at Smolny, 11) RUMYANTSEV in various places, 12) REM—at Smolny, 13) SOLOVYOV in the Business Club dining room, 14) TOLMAZOV—we met in various places since we were friends, 15) FADEYEV—in various places, 16) KHALUPO—at Smolny, 17) TSEITLIN—at Istomol. I do not remember other meetings now. Our meetings were always accidental and at different times. Our meetings took place during the past year.

Question: What exactly did you discuss with SAFAROV in 1931, and what purpose did you pursue by visiting him?

Answer: I had no purpose at all. We talked about various subjects, including ZINOVIEV and the work of the Comintern. SAFAROV told me that ZINOVIEV had begun to stray again and that he should not have associated himself with KAMENEV L.B. Regarding the work of the Comintern, SAFAROV told me about the achievements that exist, or rather existed at that time, in the German Communist Party and some fraternal Communist parties of the East.

Question: From whom did you obtain EVDOKIMOV's and KUKLIN's addresses, where did your meetings with them take place, and who besides yourself was present during these conversations?

Answer: I obtained EVDOKIMOV's address from TOLMAZOV Andrei, who in turn received it from SHATSKY \[1\]. I learned where KUKLIN lived from SAKHOV. TOLMAZOV and SAKHOV were also present during my conversation with EVDOKIMOV, while the same persons and KUKLIN's relatives were present during my conversation with KUKLIN.

Question: The investigation has information that, while visiting KUKLIN, you informed him of the situation of certain Zinovievists living in Leningrad; what can you say about this?

Answer: I do not remember this. I do not keep a record of my conversations and cannot remember it.

Question: What exactly did KUKLIN tell you about ZINOVIEV?

Answer: He told us that ZINOVIEV had published a book, “Marxism and War,” and strongly recommended that we read it, since he regarded it as a worthy, good book.

Question: Did KUKLIN tell you about the expulsion from the party of BAKAYEV's wife, KOSTINA? How did he assess this fact?

Answer: Yes, he did tell us about it and said in this connection that the decision was wrong because, in his words, one must not resort to expulsion from the party because a party member expresses an opinion on one subject or another. Our conversation came down to the existence within the party of a strict regime that prevents people from speaking freely and violates the elementary conditions of inner-party democracy. Regarding KOSTINA herself, he said that she had spoken at a party meeting at her workplace and made a speech against the decision of the CC of the VKP(b) to expel ZINOVIEV from the party. —

SEREDOKHIN.

INTERROGATED BY:

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO OF THE UGB, STROMIN.

Certified correct: Churbanova
