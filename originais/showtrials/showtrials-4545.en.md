INTERROGATION RECORD

of ZORIN (GOMBARG), Sergei Semyonovich

January 21, 1935.

ZORIN (GOMBARG), Sergei Semyonovich, born in 1890 (45 years old), city of Kirovsk (Elisavetgrad), father a teacher, Jewish, USSR citizenship. Moscow, Leontyevsky Lane, 8, apartment 20. Director of the Scientific Research Institute for the Industrialization of Housing Construction. Wife Antonina Pavlovna MALINOVSKAYA, aged 35, non-party, head of paperwork at the “Gastronom” store; daughters Natalia, aged 15, a student, and Olga, aged 9, live with his former wife in Moscow, Nikitskaya Street, house number I do not remember. His former wife Elizaveta Yakovlevna VOLGINA lives there, an AUCP(b) member, a writer, working at “Izvestia.” Without property. Elementary education. AUCP(b) member since 1917; from 1919 to 1921 secretary of the Leningrad Party Committee; secretary of the Bryansk Provincial Committee in 1921; secretary of the Ivanovo-Voznesensk Provincial Committee from 1923 to 1925; elected a candidate member of the AUCP(b) Central Committee at the Thirteenth Congress. No previous convictions. Did not serve with the Whites.

Question: The investigation has information about your participation in the Zinovievist organization. Do you acknowledge this?

Answer: After the Fifteenth Congress I retained my old opposition views and therefore did not return to the party. I returned to the party in the first half of 1930, without fully overcoming my former counterrevolutionary views. After returning to the party I continued maintaining ties with ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV. I met with them during 1930, 1931, and 1932. In the course of these meetings I had a number of political conversations of an anti-party character with ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV.

The principal conclusion to be drawn from these meetings is that ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV remained on their former counterrevolutionary positions on the most essential questions of economic and political life. Without objecting to the policy of industrialization, both ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV considered that industrialization was being carried out at colossal cost, resulting from the incompetence of the AUCP(b) leadership. KAMENEV demonstrated that industrialization was proceeding with colossal production costs through a number of figures he possessed and specially selected materials. The meaning of ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's statements on the country's industrialization amounted to saying that, if they, ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV, were carrying it out, the results would be entirely different.

On collectivization, ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV considered that it should not be conducted on such a large scale, that collective farms should initially have been built only from the poor, and that gradually, as the collective farms grew stronger, the less prosperous middle peasants should have been selectively drawn into them. In doing so, ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV completely evaded the question of eliminating the kulaks as a class.

From ZINOVIEV's and KAMENEV's statements on collectivization it followed that, since a battle was taking place within the party on this question between the Central Committee and the Right, ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV should remain on the sidelines of this struggle so as to be able, if necessary, to establish contact with the Right.

On the material situation of the working class, ZINOVIEV's favorite statement was that the workers' situation was deteriorating, that somewhere workers were already striking, and so on.

Both ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV pictured the future situation of the Union of Republics in the darkest colors, considering that as a result of the AUCP(b)'s unskillful and incorrect leadership the country faced colossal difficulties with which the AUCP(b) leadership would be unable to cope.

I acknowledge that, by maintaining political ties with ZINOVIEV and KAMENEV during 1930, 1931, and 1932, by sharing certain of their political views, I was a double-dealer.

Recorded correctly from my words, read by me —

ZORIN.

INTERROGATED BY:

SECTION HEAD, EKO, GUGB, NKVD OF THE USSR — CHERTOK

Certified true: V. A.
