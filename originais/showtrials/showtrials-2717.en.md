INTERROGATION RECORD

OF ZVEZDOV, Vasily Ivanovich,

dated 9 December 1934.

ZVEZDOV, Vasily Ivanovich, aged 32 (year of birth 1902), son of a worker, born in Tver, Russian, USSR citizen. Leningrad, 11 Mira Street, apartment 8. Final-year student at the Industrial Institute (Mechanical Faculty), works at the “Elektrosila” factory (diploma internship). Married. Wife: Anastasia Grigorievna, née GLUSHENKO, 26, VKP(b) member since 1926, final-year student; son: Vilen, 2; mother: Daria Iudovna, 52, homemaker; stepfather: Konstantin Osipovich BELEVICH, 47, worker at the “Oktyabrskaya” factory (mechanical workshop). Owns no property. His parents also never owned anything. Higher education; upon graduating from the Institute will be a process engineer. Working on his diploma project. VKP(b) member since 1923. Party organiser of Mechanical Faculty students undertaking internships at the “Elektrosila” factory. Never tried or interrogated. Private. Did not serve with the Whites.

QUESTION: What do you know about Leonid NIKOLAEV, a former Komsomol member in Vyborg District?

ANSWER: I have known Leonid Vasilievich NIKOLAEV since 1924. We met during my work in the Vyborg District VLKSM Committee, during my work in the Luga Uezd Committee (1925), and during my visit to the Istomol House of Party Activists in 19343 (May). When he worked in the Vyborg District Committee, I scarcely spoke to him: he did registration and clerical work, while I headed the District Committee's Economic Department. I came to know him more closely later, when I was assigned to the Luga Uezd VLKSM Committee in 1925, where NIKOLAEV was then Head of the General Department. At the beginning of our work together I shared a room with NIKOLAEV in the Luga House of Soviets. During this period I could form a more or less definite impression of NIKOLAEV, since I observed him for a longer time, sharing a room with him for about three weeks. He was always taciturn, sought to keep apart from the other Komsomol lads and, if anything happened at work in the Uezd Committee, NIKOLAEV reacted morbidly. Having a very high opinion of himself, he endured personal troubles alone. He moved out of my room upon marrying. He then met Milda DRAULE, an employee of the Uezd Party Committee, and married her. Milda DRAULE had, in my opinion, the same peculiarities as NIKOLAEV; she was as withdrawn as he was, but at the same time he seemed to me to be under her influence.

QUESTION: What was NIKOLAEV's relationship to the Zinovievist opposition?

ANSWER: At the outset of the opposition struggle NIKOLAEV played no active role. At the Uezd Komsomol Committee Bureau, where opposition-related matters were discussed, NIKOLAEV, who attended the Bureau meetings as secretary, did not speak against the opposition. My assertion that NIKOLAEV sympathised with the opposition is based on the fact that the entire Uezd Committee Bureau, except for one future head of the Organisational Department, shared the opposition's views. Later, individual Bureau members and then the entire Bureau, except for myself and Matvey IVANOV, broke with the opposition. Matvey IVANOV broke with the opposition at the first Uezd Committee plenum. As for Leonid NIKOLAEV, his attitude remained indefinite, and it was impossible to determine his relationship to the opposition in the later period. In general, by March 1926 only two or three people remained from the entire Komsomol organisation, namely myself, ZVEZDOV, and SAVAITIS; the latter voted for the resolution against the opposition proposed at the Uezd Committee plenum. At this plenum I was removed from my job and placed at the disposal of the Central Committee's Northwestern Bureau in Leningrad.

QUESTION: How long were you in the town of Luga?

ANSWER: About a year, from January or February 1925 to March 1926.

QUESTION: When was your next meeting with NIKOLAEV after leaving Luga?

ANSWER: It was in the spring of 1934, on the premises of the House of Party Activists, in the room of the Leningrad Komsomol “Combat Veterans' Association”. We happened to meet and discussed various subjects concerning study, work and family circumstances. He told me he was working at the Institute of VKP(b) History as a travelling instructor, that he had been excluded from the Communist Academy because he lacked industrial work experience and because of staff reductions there. NIKOLAEV then asked whether it would be difficult for him to study at a higher technical institution, saying he had decided to enter a higher educational institution. In addition, NIKOLAEV told me he now had two children and that his wife Milda DRAULE worked in some trust.

I asked NIKOLAEV, among other things, how to find the staff of the “Combat Veterans' Association”, since I had come intending to apply for membership. NIKOLAEV, who knew all the association's staff, gave me the necessary explanations, and we parted. That was our last meeting.

QUESTION: Did NIKOLAEV not tell you that he had been expelled from the Party or was dissatisfied about anything?

ANSWER: We did not discuss this, although one undoubtedly sensed that NIKOLAEV was dissatisfied with his situation and that his exclusion from the Communist Academy, in particular, had hurt him.

QUESTION: Which former Zinovievists do you meet?

ANSWER: I meet the following persons who once participated in the Zinovievist opposition: 1. Nikolai ANTONOV, who studies in the same faculty as I do; 2) Mikhail NADEL, also a student at the same institute; and 3. Ivan KOTOLYNOV [1], who studies with me in the Physics Faculty; these persons (were) meet me in our institute's library, and once ANTONOV and NADEL visited my apartment.

All our meetings are accidental.

QUESTION: For what purpose do you keep anti-Party documents relating to the Zinovievist opposition at your apartment?

ANSWER: I had no purpose; nevertheless I consider that I had no right to keep such documents and acknowledge this act of mine as unquestionably anti-Party. The opposition materials found during the search of my premises:

1\. The platform of the Zinovievist opposition;

2\. a statement by a group of oppositionists to the VLKSM Provincial Committee;

3\. V. I. LENIN's testament;

4\. the statement of the “four” to the VKP(b) Central Committee Politburo;

5\. the appeal of the “83” to the VKP(b) Central Committee;

6\. the appeal “To All Party Members”, signed by ZINOVIEV, EVDOKIMOV, BAKAEV, PETERSON, SMILGA and TROTSKY;

7\. “On V. I. LENIN's Testament” — a study by SAFAROV;

8\. an appeal to the Central Committee signed by a group of oppositionists, dated 14/XII/1927, kept by me since 1927.

QUESTION: Did you give these materials to anyone to read?

ANSWER: I gave them to others to read only while I was in the opposition, until 1927, before the decision of the Fifteenth Party Congress. Since breaking with the opposition, I have given nothing to anyone to read.

QUESTION: Why did you continue keeping opposition literature after your final break with the opposition?

ANSWER: I paid no attention to it.

Taken down in the record from my words — read —

ZVEZDOV.

DEPUTY HEAD OF THE SPO, UNKVD FOR LENINGRAD REGION — STROMIN
