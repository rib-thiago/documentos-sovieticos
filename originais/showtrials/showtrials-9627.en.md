RECORD

of the supplementary interrogation of MUSHTAKOV, Andrei Andreevich,

dated December 18, 1934.

Question: For what purpose did you keep the poems of counterrevolutionary content found at your home, and who wrote these poems?

Answer: The counterrevolutionary poems found at my home, written and circulated in early 1926 — “On Earth, the Whole Human Race” and “The Central Committee Members' Efforts Are in Vain” — were written by TARKHANOV, now in the Far Eastern Army; and the song “In Moscow City, in the Stone Kremlin” was sung by the Leningrad people after the Fourteenth Party Congress; I do not know the author of the latter. I did not remember that these poems were kept at my home.

Question: State where and when you acquired the weapons found at your home, and whether you had permission from the appropriate authorities to possess such a large quantity of weapons.

Answer: The weapons found at my home belong to me, with the exception of Mauser no. 3, which citizen DRIGA, F. I., a member of the VKP(b) currently working at Glavmorsevput, left in my safekeeping. I acquired the weapons as follows: the carbine and small-caliber Nagant were given to me in 1932 by the sponsored tank regiment stationed at Strelna; the short Mauser was given to me by the Vladivostok District GPU Department in 1927; I purchased the Walther in 1928 or early 1929 in Vladivostok; the small Walther was given to me in 1932 by comrade MEDVED; and Browning no. 1 was given to my wife by S. S. LOBOV in 1918. In 1929 I had a permit to possess the Walther; I have no authorization from the appropriate authorities for the other weapons.

Recorded correctly from my words and read to me: MUSHTAKOV.

Interrogated by: head of the 1st Section of the SPO of the GUGB — PETROVSKY.
