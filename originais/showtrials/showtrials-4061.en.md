INTERROGATION RECORD

Of YAKOVLEV, Moisei Nakhimovich,

dated January 4, 1935.

QUESTION: Inform the investigation about the political positions of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization you headed in the Vasilyevsky Ostrov district?

ANSWER: Common to all participants in the organization were dissatisfaction with the party regime, a critical attitude toward the party leadership, and an especially hostile attitude toward Comrade STALIN; but gradually this hostility toward Comrade STALIN begins to soften, and participants in the organization come to appreciate Comrade STALIN’s outstanding role, while the critical attitude toward the leadership of the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) in general remains until the most recent period.

On questions of party policy in the countryside, there was a negative attitude toward the pace of collectivization; we considered the pace adopted by the Central Committee beyond our capacity and capable of leading to an acute agricultural crisis.

We displayed the same negative attitude toward the country’s pace of industrialization, which was regarded as beyond our capacity.

Widespread among us was the thesis of the worsening situation of the working class, difficulties of subsistence, and the deteriorating food situation; all this was linked to the Central Committee’s leadership.

QUESTION: Describe the counterrevolutionary role and activities of individual participants in the organization?

ANSWER: The counterrevolutionary organization in the Vasilyevsky Ostrov district was formed from old Zinovievist cadres, former active participants in the struggle against the party; its main core consisted of workers, although I also had contacts in another milieu.

NOTMAN, Konstantin, a researcher and teacher at LILI. A very active, longstanding Zinovievist. After the 15th Party Congress and until the beginning of 1929 he was an active participant in the illegal Zinovievist organization and a regular participant in illegal meetings at my apartment. During this period he displayed great hostility toward the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) and personally toward Comrade STALIN. During the 15th Congress he was in the opposition troika of the Vasilyevsky Ostrov district, replacing BOGOMOLNY. Together with other participants he went to establish contact with oppositionists at various plants in the Vasilyevsky Ostrov district.

Recently he withdrew from the group.

FOKIN, Alexei — a worker at the Elektroapparat plant. A longstanding active Zinovievist who spoke successfully at workers’ meetings. After the 15th Congress he was readmitted to the party but did not change his sharply negative attitude toward the Central Committee’s line.

He was a regular participant in illegal meetings. In the organization he was considered an active person until the most recent period.

I heard that he has outside contacts.

SEMYONOV, Semyon — a former worker at the Kazitsky plant, now deputy director of some laboratory. An active Zinovievist. A rather capable person who spoke successfully at meetings against the party line. After the 15th Congress he was expelled and reentered the party for tactical maneuvering.

He is considered an active person in the organization, participated in all illegal meetings, and went to establish contact with other oppositionists and explain the need to join the party.

KONDRATYEVA, Yevdokia (wife of SEMYONOV, S.), a lover of political gossip with persistent counterrevolutionary attitudes. Hostile toward Comrade STALIN, very sympathetic toward ZINOVIEV and always interested in him. Together with her husband, a regular participant in illegal meetings. She occupied no active position in the organization.

She has contacts in Komsomol circles in the Vasilyevsky Ostrov district.

SEMYONOV (“Skobar”) — an old worker at the cable plant, an active Zinovievist; after the 15th Congress he was not expelled from the party but remained in it for tactical maneuvering, constantly displaying dissatisfaction with Central Committee policy.

A regular participant in illegal meetings at my apartment. He had some contacts at the cable plant.

Recently he has taken to drinking.

PANKO, Adam — a worker, now an office employee; he was not expelled from the party. Displays constant dissatisfaction with “difficulties,” linking this to Central Committee policy. A regular participant in illegal meetings, where, although he spoke rarely, he was considered an active person.

Three months ago participants in the organization gathered at his apartment for SHEINBERG’s funeral [1]. He is closely connected with the Zinovievist LEIKIN.

KASPERSKY, Ivan Feliksovich — a metalworker, a longstanding active Zinovievist. One of the active participants in our organization. During the 15th Congress he headed the opposition troika of the Vasilyevsky Ostrov district. Non-party (did not pass the purge). Recently he has had sharply anti-party attitudes.

QUESTION: Was SKOROMNY, Alexei Fomich, a participant in your organization?

ANSWER: SKOROMNY, A. F., did not belong to the organization I headed, but was close to some of its members, such as FOKIN and PANKO. During the 15th Party Congress SKOROMNY belonged to the general opposition group of the Vasilyevsky Ostrov district.

I last saw SKOROMNY at SHEINBERG’s funeral and became convinced of his counterrevolutionary attitudes.

QUESTION: Inform the investigation about your organizational contacts at the Academy of Sciences, as well as your activities there in the interests of the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization, in particular what your connection with GORBACHEV consisted of.

ANSWER: My organizational contacts at the Academy of Sciences were only with GORBACHEV, G. E., and KAREV, N., and were mainly along the lines of the struggle on the theoretical front.

I approved of and assisted GORBACHEV in his literary struggle, supported the “Napostovist Left,” which pursued the Zinovievist counterrevolutionary line in its publications (GORBACHEV, LELEVICH, BEZYMENSKY). I supported GORBACHEV in his anti-party conduct when he turned Pushkin House of the Academy of Sciences into a base for Litfront.

I was sympathetic to KAREV’s constant anti-Soviet attacks on the philosophical leadership.

I also took an anti-party attitude toward Comrade STALIN’s historic letter to “Proletarian Revolution,” assessing it as the disorganization of historical thought (conversations with GORBACHEV and KAREV). I considered the party’s ideological line to be stifling initiative on the ideological front.

QUESTION: Did GORBACHEV know about the counterrevolutionary Zinovievist organization you headed?

ANSWER: GORBACHEV knew of the existence of this counterrevolutionary organization in 1928–29 and may even have attended its illegal meetings sometimes.

I have read the testimony; it has been recorded correctly from my words —

M. YAKOVLEV

INTERROGATED BY:

OPERATIONS OFFICER OF THE SPO OF THE GUGB — FEDOROV.
